FINDINGS
[serious] [delays urgent help] §17 Red flags and referral, list of what you might see; Current: "What you might see in the sports this unit covers, each going by one of those two rows: an athlete who seems faint, unwell or unusually tired around weigh-ins,"; Problem: an athlete who is faint or acutely unwell around a weigh-in (a possible effect of rapid weight loss, which §6 says is common in young combat athletes) is sent to the referral rows: a same-day pass to the person for medical concerns. The unit never gives an emergency route. It nowhere mentions emergency services, first aid, collapse or the competition's medical staff. A coach who follows the unit to the letter refers the athlete when they should get immediate care; Fix: "An athlete who faints, collapses, is confused or seems acutely unwell around a weigh-in, a session or a competition gets the competition's medical staff, the club's first-aider or emergency services at once; the referral rows come after that. What you might see otherwise in the sports this unit covers, each going by one of those two rows: an athlete who seems unusually tired around weigh-ins,"
[should-fix] [delays urgent help] §14 Competition-day weigh-ins, "Before"; Current: "you say that the class can be changed at the next review and that their medical contact is the person to talk to, and you pass on the worry by SP9's route if it suggests restriction."; Problem: when an athlete is worried about making weight on competition day, they may be restricting food or drink at that moment. This line pushes the class question to "the next review", lets the coach decide whether the worry "suggests restriction", and sets no time limit. It also does not say that withdrawing today is an option; Fix: "you tell them that their health comes before today's weigh-in and that they may withdraw today, that the class can be changed at the next review, and you pass on the worry the same day by SP9's route; if they seem unwell, the competition's medical staff see them now."
[should-fix] [confidentiality with a minor] §2, closing line; Current: "You do not ask about periods; if an athlete or parent tells you, you pass it on with their agreement."; Problem: making the pass-on depend on the athlete's agreement means that a minor who says "don't tell anyone" stops a referral that the unit elsewhere requires "the same day" (§2 and §3). It amounts to a promise of confidentiality that a coach cannot keep with a minor; Fix: "You do not ask about periods; if an athlete or parent tells you, you pass it on the same day, telling them that you are doing so and to whom; you do not promise to keep it to yourself."
[should-fix] [past the coach's part] §11 Who decides, opening paragraph; Current: "Where a concern about eating, weight or body is already open, a choice that needs no restriction may be recorded and the medical contact is told of it; any choice that could need restriction waits for them."; Problem: the line does not say who judges whether a choice "needs no restriction". Under Deliverable D the coach does not know the athlete's weight, so in practice the judgement falls to the coach or to the athlete's own answer. That is a clinical judgement, made while an eating concern is open, about an athlete who may play down restriction; Fix: "Where a concern about eating, weight or body is already open, only a move to a higher class may be recorded before the medical contact has seen the athlete, and the medical contact is told of it; any other choice waits for them."
[should-fix] [burden on the child] Deliverable A, Dani's protocol, questions row; Current: "3. To compete in that class, would you need to eat less, drink less or train extra at any point?"; Problem: a 14-year-old with an open eating concern is asked, in front of her coach and parents, to declare whether she would restrict, and her answer decides whether medical input is needed ("Required before any answer to question 3 that is 'yes' is recorded"). That puts the burden of disclosure on the child, and a "no" can let a risky choice be recorded; Fix: "3. Is that the class you are in now, the one above, or another? (Any choice other than moving up goes to the medical contact before it is recorded; nobody here asks how you would make a weight.)"
[should-fix] [abuse definition differs from cited consensus] §12 table, row "Repeating it after a no"; Current: "Psychological abuse is defined as a pattern of repeated behaviour in a power-differentiated relationship"; Problem: the 2016 IOC consensus statement (Box 1), which the row cites through DD-SP1, defines psychological abuse as "A pattern of deliberate, prolonged, repeated non-contact behaviours within a power differentiated relationship". The row drops "deliberate", "prolonged" and "non-contact", so the definition it states differs from the source; Fix: "Psychological abuse is defined as \"a pattern of deliberate, prolonged, repeated non-contact behaviours within a power differentiated relationship\" (2016 statement, Box 1, as DD-SP1 reports it)"
[minor] [abuse definition differs from cited consensus] §12 table, row "Watching what an athlete eats"; Current: "Repeated, it is the pattern of non-contact behaviour in a power-differentiated relationship that defines psychological abuse."; Problem: same shortening as the row above ("deliberate, prolonged" dropped), and it states as fact that a behaviour meets the definition, which §12 itself says is not the coach's to decide about others; Fix: "Repeated, deliberate and prolonged, it fits the pattern of non-contact behaviours in a power-differentiated relationship by which the 2016 statement defines psychological abuse; in yourself, stop; in anyone else, the welfare officer decides."
[minor] [burden on the child / route around a parent] Deliverable A, Dani's protocol, question 5; Current: "Is there anything you would rather say without me in the room?"; Problem: the question offers only the coach leaving. If a parent is the source of weight pressure, the child has to ask on her own to speak without her parents, in front of them; Fix: "Is there anything you would rather say without me, or without your parents, in the room?"
[minor] [route for a concern about a parent] Deliverable C6, parent pushes for the lower class; Current: "Can we put it on the agenda for the review on [date]?\""; Problem: the script sends every parent push to the season review. A parent's push that includes restricting the child's food or drink, watching what she eats or weighing her at home is a concern for the same day, which §12 sends to the welfare officer; the script does not say so; Fix: "Can we put it on the agenda for the review on [date]?\" (If the parent describes restricting her food or drink, watching what she eats or weighing her at home, that goes the same day to the welfare officer by DD-SP1's route, and the eating side to the person for medical concerns by SP9's.)"
[minor] [weight-loss method named] §6, definition of rapid weight loss; Current: "The unit names no method."; Problem: the unit does name one, dehydration, in §6 ("so that they are not tempted to dehydrate") and §18 (quoting British Taekwondo). It names it only as the risk a rule guards against and gives no instruction, but the statement is false as written; Fix: "The unit describes no method; where a federation rule below names dehydration, it is named only as the risk that rule guards against."
