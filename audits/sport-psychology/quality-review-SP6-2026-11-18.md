## Confirmed

- Oudejans & Pijpers (2009):
  - 17 expert basketball players, five weeks, with or without anxiety. 17 expert dart players throwing from high or low on a climbing wall. Only the groups that trained with anxiety held up.
  - The "acclimatizes" explanation is the authors'.
  - QJEP 62(8):1631–1647. https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/training-with-anxiety-has-a-positive-effect-on-expert-perceptual--2/
- Low et al. (2021):
  - Design: the definition, the inclusion criteria and "physically practiced as much as".
  - Sample: 14 studies, 394 participants (Table 1 sums to 399); 10 sport and 4 law-enforcement studies; the sports listed; Bell et al.'s elite youth cricketers; no ages.
  - Pooled effects: g = 0.85 (0.37–1.34), I² = 78.4%. Without the outlier, g = 0.67 (0.41–0.94 in the results, 0.43–1.12 in the abstract), I² = 17.1%.
  - Subgroups: sport 0.72 (0.45–1.00, k = 10); law enforcement 0.63 (−0.14 to 1.39, k = 3); novices 0.77 (k = 5); experienced 0.61 (k = 8); dose 0.73 (k = 6), 0.72 (k = 4) and 0.42 crossing zero (k = 3). No significant moderators.
  - Bias: Egger P = 0.12, funnel "relatively symmetrical". Risk of bias mostly unclear, with one study high on several items.
  - Other: one retention study; Bell et al. measured competition performance; the stated limits (combined stressors, longer programmes, transfer).
  - Manipulation counts: judgement 8, rewards 6, forfeits 4, environmental 3 (Table 1).
  - Bibliography: author order Low, Sandercock, Freeman, Winter, Butt, Maynard; SEPP 10(1):149–163.
  - Sources: https://repository.essex.ac.uk/26305/3/PressureTrainingForPerformanceDomains.pdf ; https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/pressure-training-for-performance-domains-a-meta-analysis/ ; https://repository.essex.ac.uk/26305/
- Kent et al.:
  - 82 male players aged 11 to 18, mean 14.12, randomly allocated. Three pressure sessions over 18 weeks: 2v2, crowd noise, a leaderboard, rewards for the top players, dice-roll consequences for the bottom, sprints for ages 15 to 18.
  - Three 30-minute workshops (15 minutes for ages 17 and 18) and reflective diaries.
  - χ² = 7.15, p = .028; skill execution not significant. 68 retested at 6 weeks and 26 at 12. "Partial support".
  - Ethics: protection from harm; results not used for contract decisions.
  - JASP 34(4):691–712, online 25 Feb 2021. https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/9339/1/9339-Kent-(2021)-Implementing-a-pressure-training-program.pdf ; https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/10413200.2020.1868618 ; https://idrottsforum.org/journal-of-applied-sport-psychology-volume-34-2022-issue-4/
- Kegelaers et al. (2021):
  - Group means 18.37 and 17.76; non-random switching-replications design; a resilience workshop; eight sessions of about 30 minutes over three weeks.
  - Content: a last-two-minutes simulation with a referee, a deficit and limited time-outs; free throws watched, capped, with team sprints after misses; a coach-led reflection after every session.
  - Results: individual resilience (CD-RISC) not significant; team vulnerabilities (CREST) interaction p = .013.
  - Players' reports: stress, frustration, unfairness, panic. A coach saw younger players react more to consequences falling on teammates. "Never fully re-create" competition.
  - JASP 33(2):151–172; author order. https://core.ac.uk/download/387931251.pdf ; https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/a-mixed-methods-evaluation-of-a-pressure-training-intervention-to/
- Stoker et al. (2016): 11 elite coaches; task, forfeit and judgement; demands and consequences; individual differences; the definition of pressure; IJSP 47(3):262–281; authors. https://ijsp-online.com/abstract/view/47/262
- Stoker et al. (2019):
  - Six GB disability shooters (five rifle, one pistol) aged 20 to 41; seven conditions in random order; the condition details; required score = mean of the last three competitions.
  - Perceived-pressure means 1.83, 2.8, 4.5 and 4.9, with the other conditions not reported. Cognitive anxiety up under forfeit and judgement; judgement debilitative; scores lower under judgement and the time limit; reward not significant.
  - The sample size is named as a limitation.
  - TSP 33(3):221–227; authors. https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/13095/1/Effect%20of%20pressure%20training%20manipulations%20pressure.pdf ; https://repository.essex.ac.uk/25648/

  - Raise pressure gradually, by analogy with graded exposure.

  - Once a month is too rare. Stopping before competition may protect confidence. A new skill is learned before pressure is added.
  - The two debrief questions; limiting repetitions; the netball study.
  - Authors; JSPA 15(1):3–18, online 18 Jan 2023. https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/18698/3/Pressure%20Training%20From%20Research%20to%20Applied%20Practice.pdf ; https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/pressure-training-from-research-to-applied-practice/
