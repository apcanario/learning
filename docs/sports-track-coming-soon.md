### 2 · Running a Squad

- **Reader:** the coach or team manager of a club squad, in an individual or a team sport. The same reader as Sport Psychology.
- **Scope:**
  - Groups: cohesion, roles, cliques, leadership among athletes, how a group becomes a team and what threatens one, and how a training group in an individual sport differs from a team.
  - Individuals inside the group: selection and deselection, playing time, the star and the bench, conflict.
  - The coach as leader: one-to-one meetings and group meetings as formats, with what is known about each.
  - Parents: expectations, communication, the difficult parent, the parent who coaches from the stands.
  - Staff and club: assistants, volunteers, the board, the coach's own boundaries.
  - Logistics: the season calendar, travel with minors, competition entries, budgets and fees, communication channels.
- **Where the evidence pushes back:** group dynamics and parenting in sport have peer-reviewed research; logistics has almost none. Those units are graded practice throughout and built as checklists and templates, labelled so.
- **Builds on:** Sport Psychology's units on climate, parents, competition day and the coach's line.

### 3 · Managing an Athlete's Career

- **Now:** syllabus written 21 Nov 2026, set up 23 Nov 2026 (row S-AC0), 0 of 18 units written. Where the scope below differs from `docs/athlete-career-syllabus.md`, the syllabus wins; the lines below are the record of 19 Nov.
- **Reader:** a coach of an athlete in an Olympic or federation sport, below the agency level, who also sponsors the athlete through a business of their own, helps them unpaid as representative, or both. In the preamble. A public course: the single-reader rule does not apply.
- **Scope:** what an agent does; sponsorship (what a sponsor buys, how to find one and pitch it); making an athlete marketable; contracts and image rights as questions to ask; prize money and funding; the career after sport.
- **Where the evidence pushes back:**
  - It is industry practice, sponsor-side data and platform documentation, not science. It is sourced like the personal-brand course, with anecdote and vendor data labelled.
  - Agent work is licensed in some sports, and the rules differ by sport and country. The course says what to check, never what the rules are.
  - Marketing a minor is a safeguarding question before it is a commercial one. It gets its own unit, tied to Sport Psychology's deep dive on the coach's line, and covers the conflict of interest when the coach is also the manager.
- **Builds on:** the public personal-brand course, for method.

## Not courses

- **Talent identification:** the evidence is mostly that early prediction is unreliable; that is a unit, and Sport Psychology's unit on the developing athlete already holds it.
- **Nutrition:** a dietitian's field; a coach's version is mostly referral, which Sport Psychology covers.
- **Safeguarding:** held by Sport Psychology's deep dive on the coach's line.
- **Performance analysis and data:** sport-specific in practice.
- **Skill acquisition** (how athletes learn technique): proposed and set aside on 19 Nov 2026 in favour of the two courses above. It has the strongest experimental evidence of any candidate, so it stays on this list as a possible fourth.
