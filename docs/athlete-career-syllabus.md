How each was read:

- **Public course.** The reader is described by position, never by sport or product. The case gives two fictional coaches a small label each: one in running kit, one in training wear for combat-sport clubs.

- **Sources.** The preamble already held the Project's floor. Added: a law firm's note or a press report is never the source for what a text says, and an interested party's page is evidence only of what that party says, offers or requires.

**Where the evidence pushes back**

Every law, rule, ruling and paper named in this list was seen in web search results on 21 Nov 2026 and not read in full. They show where the syllabus needed shaping; the units read the texts.

- **Nothing was found on coach-sponsors.** One search returned sport-ethics work on role conflict among support staff (*British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 1999) and governing bodies' rules that tell coaches and officials to avoid or declare conflicts of interest (World Athletics' Conflicts of Interest rules; the ski federation's checklist for coaches). AC2 builds on the general literature on conflicts of interest and on those rule texts, grades its own reasoning as practice, and says plainly if no study of a coach who sponsors their own athletes exists.
- **Most of the rest is practice too.** Peer-reviewed research exists on athlete brand image, endorsers, sponsorship effects, athletes' finances and career transitions. It studied famous athletes, large sponsorships and consumers. For an unknown athlete and a small sponsor almost nothing was measured.
- **The reviews say so themselves.** Cornwell and Kwon's systematic review of sponsorship research (1996 to 2017) reports a surplus of work on audience response and a shortage on how sponsorship is managed.
- **Rules differ by sport and country and they move.** The EU Court of Justice ruled on football's agent regulations in July 2026 (C-209/23, C-428/23); World Athletics has run central rules for athletes' representatives since 2023; Portugal changed its patronage regime in August 2026 (Decreto-Lei n.º 167/2026). The course teaches the reader to open the text and never states their rule.
- **Unpaid help may still be regulated.** Portugal's Lei n.º 54/2017 defines the *empresário desportivo* as an accredited person who represents or intermediates in sports contracts. Whether unpaid help with sponsorship falls inside that is a question AC2, AC13 and DD-AC3 put to the text and to a professional.
- **The sponsor's tax position depends on what the payment is.** Portuguese guidance separates sponsorship (a business expense, with VAT as a service) from a donation under the patronage regime. AC8 and AC9 put both as questions for the business's accountant.
- **A minor changes everything.** Portugal's Lei n.º 105/2009 makes a minor's participation in advertising subject to authorisation by or notification to the CPCJ; France and Illinois have laws on children's online earnings. AC4 comes before any unit about money, and in the case the coach's own label is one of the two approaches to the child.
- **Survivorship.** Athlete success stories are the visible minority. DD-AC1 requires an independent record for each case and at least one athlete who stopped.

## 3 · Goal and outcomes

**Goal.** By the end of the course you can help an athlete you coach on the business side without harming the athlete or the coaching: as their sponsor through a business of your own, as their unpaid representative to other sponsors and funders, or both, with the conflicts between those roles written down and managed. You know which rules bind what the athlete may sell, you have found the funding the athlete is entitled to and built a season budget, you can say what the athlete offers a sponsor and prove it, run your own sponsorship of athletes fairly, find other sponsors, agree terms and keep them, read a contract well enough to know what to ask and when to pay a lawyer, tell when a professional should take over, and plan the career alongside and after sport, with a one-year plan written for your own athlete.

- **Outcome 1 · Stand on safe ground.** A function map, a roles declaration, mandate and conflict register, a rules register, and a safeguard protocol where the athlete is a minor (AC1 to AC4).
- **Outcome 2 · Fund the season and sell honestly.** A season budget and funding log, an asset inventory, a marketability plan and athlete profile, a sponsor table, a sponsorship policy and agreement for your own business, a prospect list with a written proposal and pipeline, a servicing calendar and sponsor report, checked against documented cases and published numbers (AC5 to AC11, DD-AC1, DD-AC2).
- **Outcome 3 · Sign, hand over and plan.** A contract question sheet, a representation decision, a dual-career and exit plan, the one-year career plan, and the Portugal register (AC12 to AC15, DD-AC3).

Each outcome is measured by its deliverables existing for the reader's own athlete.
