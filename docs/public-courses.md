## 3 · Rules every writing row follows

1. **One unit per chat, in syllabus order.** The writing order is the reading order. A unit starts only when every earlier unit of its course is filed.
2. **Opus 5.5 writes; a separate Opus 5.5 context checks.** No Fable audit or Fable fresh check follows (S-NOAUDIT, 15 Nov 2026). The chat is a thin main chat on Sonnet 5.5 (S-BW1, `docs/method.md` §4): it only stages, launches agents, runs scripts, builds, publishes and records, and never opens a unit; the applier and the filer are Opus 5.5 agents too.
3. **The brief is never reworded.** It is built with `kit/mdcourse.py`'s `open_brief`: the preamble, the Aim, Takes and Gives lines, the unit brief.
4. **Source units are material, never authority.** The writer is given the private units its Takes line names, with their fresh-check reports. It re-verifies every claim it carries over at the claim's source. Nothing rests on a source unit alone. Sport Psychology for Coaches has no source units, so this rule does not apply to it. Nor does it to Supply Chain Execution for Product Teams, which has none either.
5. **Mistakes are corrected where they sit.** The writer lists every mistake it finds in a source unit; the fresh checker verifies that list. A verified mistake is fixed in the private unit as an exact replacement, with a dated note in that unit and a line in its hub's changelog. A mistake found in a filed public unit is fixed the same way. Each is named in the reply.
6. **Contradictions and needs are flagged, never silently absorbed.** The writer lists (a) where its evidence contradicts a filed unit of its course or the brief of an unwritten one, and (b) what the unit needed that the syllabus does not give, or what a later unit must now cover or avoid. The chat appends each to the course's `syllabus-flags.md` with the units affected and a proposed change. No brief is changed by the chat that raises a flag. A flag that names a later unit is passed to that unit's writer by `kit/next_prompt.py`. If a flag means the next unit cannot be written as briefed, the reply says so in its first line, and the next prompt is a syllabus review instead of a unit.
7. **A syllabus review** is its own row, added when Pedro asks for one or a flag blocks a unit: it reads every open flag, proposes each brief change as an exact replacement, and applies only what Pedro confirms; `units_data.py` changes only there. It runs on the best model the session has.
8. **No private term reaches a public course.** `python3 kit/private_terms.py` must print OK before filing; `verify.py` runs it on the vault, the data folder and the built pages (but each course's generated "How it was built" page, which quotes `method.md` and links the private repository on every course: P24). The terms are `kit/private-terms.txt`. A writer never refers to the private course or its units. Supply Chain Execution for Product Teams (syllabus §6): its pages never name the Project's owner, his employer or his employer's products, except that the employer may appear as one entry of a dated list taken from a named analyst report or an audited filing; `kit/private_terms.py` allows the employer's terms (its `employer:` lines) on that course's pages only inside such a list, a Markdown list or table whose lead-in line names the analyst firm or the filing and a year, and fails them anywhere else.
9. **The case stays consistent.** PB: every fact a unit invents about Mara is appended to `case-facts.md` at filing. BW: a unit takes facts about Academy only from `case-academy.md`; a fact it needs and the file lacks is a need, added to the file from the Project's records by the filing chat, never by a writer. SP: as PB, every fact a unit invents about Dani, Milan or Sara is appended to the table "Added by units" of its `case-facts.md` at filing. SCE: as SP, every fact a unit invents about the case operations or the fictional vendors is appended to the table "Added by units" of its `case-facts.md` at filing (unit, operation, fact).
10. **Reliefs wait.** The two courses are not yet in the outline library (it needs ten outlines that a course's own texts name). Until row G-R registers them (row G-R-SP for Sport Psychology for Coaches, row G-R-SCE for Supply Chain Execution for Product Teams), a public unit's page shows no relief; the writer's `Object:` line is kept in the changelog row for G-R.
11. **Everything is pushed.** Each chat commits and pushes `apcanario/learning`, publishes Academy to its artifact once, runs `deploy.sh` for GitHub Pages, rewrites the Project copies, and ends with the next row's prompt in a code block.
12. **Proposals are dealt with in the chat that raises them** (Pedro, 12 Nov 2026, 21:22 and 21:31). Nothing a chat uncovers is left as a proposal for a later chat: every correction to a filed unit or a record, and every kit fix it needs, is made in that chat before it closes. A writer's contradiction whose remedy is a change to a filed unit's text, not a brief, is such a correction. Three limits keep a correction from adding errors or pulling the chat off its unit: only the text it names changes; a fresh Opus 5.5 agent checks each change against its source and against the syllabus (the briefs of the units it touches and of every unwritten unit that relies on it, the filed units, the register) before it is applied, its report saved first; a change that fails, needs a brief changed or would contradict another filed unit is not applied and becomes a flag for the next syllabus review (rule 7). What the check turns up is worked the same way, once more, in the same chat. Only two kinds stay *proposed*: a brief change, which Pedro confirms in a syllabus review, and a task only Pedro can do. A correction still open after that, or left by a row that stopped, goes into the Corrections section of `docs/BACKLOG.md`, and the next prompt works it first. `kit/next_prompt.py` prints these steps (1b and 7b) into every prompt.
13. **Quality review when flags pile up** (Pedro, 17 Nov 2026, 21:38). A unit named by more than 5 syllabus flags raised before it is written, or by more than 8 counting those raised before and after, is reviewed for quality: one fresh Opus 5.5 agent, with live search, not shown the writing or any earlier check, runs the fresh check's seven passes on the whole unit, checks the brief, the deliverable and every flag the unit was given, and lists what it could not verify; its report is saved first, its findings are applied under rule 12's limits, and what it could not verify is cut or marked unverified in the unit. The rule is recorded as a flag row whose text begins "Quality review" (open until done, then resolved; a resolved row restarts the count). `kit/next_prompt.py` counts the flags (`--quality`) and prints the review into the prompt: after the fresh check for the unit being written, at step 1b for a filed unit.
