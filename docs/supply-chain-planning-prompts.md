## 1 · Writer rules

The writer is one fresh Opus 5.5 agent with its packet and nothing else.

1. **Open before citing.** Every reference is found by a search and read at its publisher's page, its DOI address or the issuing body's own page before it carries a claim. The seed bibliography says only that an entry exists.
2. **Source log.** `out/sources.md`: one row per reference with the address opened, what was read (abstract, reprint or full text), the date, and the claims it carries. A reference that could not be opened is listed as not opened and carries nothing. A title is the article's own title, never a slug; a figure is cited to the paper that reports it, not to one that repeats it; each Sources entry is checked against the publisher's record before hand-in.
3. **Fewer, better.** Prefer one source read in full to three read in abstract. Do not cite to decorate. If a section's support is one textbook's account, grade it practice and say so.
4. **Grade every claim** by SCP1's key, and state the kind of evidence of every finding (forecasting competition, field experiment, laboratory experiment, simulation, single-company case study, survey) with its data, setting and year. From SCP2 on, the key arrives in the packet inside SCP1.
5. **Vendors.** Assess, rank, compare or recommend no real vendor, and map no real vendor's product line. Name a real vendor only to attribute a dated public statement to it, graded vendor-reported, or as an entry in a dated list from a named analyst report or an audited filing, every vendor on the list alike. Worked product examples use Lathmoor, Ebbfield and Varnby Software, from the case register, labelled fictional. Never say or hint which real company a fictional one resembles.
6. **Analyst reports.** Say how each was read. If only the abstract could be opened, the unit states only what the abstract states.
7. **No private figures.** No price, revenue, margin or win rate of a private company unless it published it. Where a brief asks for a cost line with no public figure, write "no public figure".
8. **The case** comes from `case-facts.md` only; invented facts go in the notes.
9. **Scope.** No formula and no worked calculation: a method is given by what goes in, what comes out, what it assumes and when it fails; a quantity by what moves it and in which direction. Statistical, machine-learning and optimisation methods are treated on published evidence; nothing treats generative AI, assistants or agents, and a vendor's word "AI" is reported as the vendor's word. No interview framing. No craft method taught. Nothing about the Project's owner, his employer, his role or his work.
10. **The two closing sections** before the deliverable: "In the planning meeting" and "Self-check", every answer drawn from the unit's sourced text.
11. **Flags and corrections.** A contradiction with a filed unit or a brief, a mistake noticed in a filed unit, or a need the syllabus does not meet, is written to `out/notes.md` (a contradiction or a mistake under **Contradictions**, a mistake with the quoted passage, what is wrong, the source and the exact replacement; a need under **Needs**), naming every unit it affects and with a proposed change, so that the filer writes it into `syllabus-flags.md` in that file's form. It is carried by section 4. The writer never rewords a brief.
12. **Hand-in.** The unit, `out/unit.md` (front matter, the unit, the deliverable, Sources); `out/sources.md`; `out/notes.md` with three lists, each item naming the unit codes it touches, an empty list written as "none": **Case facts** (rule 8), **Contradictions** and **Needs** (rule 11); the figures rendered; `out/unsure.md`, up to ten claims it is unsure of, closing with the `Object:` line; a reply of six lines at most (its model, the files it wrote, the counts of its lists, the number of references opened and not opened). `out/stats.json` is not the writer's: the chat writes it from these files with `python3 kit/thin.py stats`.
13. **Credentials.** The source log records for every reference its kind of venue (peer-reviewed journal, academic or professional publisher's book, forecasting competition, association, analyst firm, audited filing, standards body, vendor) and any commercial interest its authors have in what they describe; a real company's practice comes only from a peer-reviewed or published account with named authors or its own audited filing.
14. **Length.** The 2,000 to 6,000 word guide covers the body; the deliverable is not counted.

Rules 1 to 3, 5 to 8 and 11 to 12 are the Execution course's as its rows corrected them (its rule 2's last sentence and rules 11 and 12 set by S-SCE0's registration check, 19 Nov 2026), with this course's vendors in rule 5. Rules 4, 9 and 10 changed and rules 13 and 14 are new, from the design (`prompts-changes.md`, "Writer rules that change").

## 2 · The fresh checker's seven passes

One fresh Opus 5.5 agent that has not seen the unit written. It writes findings (error, should-fix, minor) and edits nothing. A reference that does not exist stops the row. It also runs the unit against Part 9 of `docs/sourcing-standard.md` line by line.

1. **References exist and match.** Every Sources entry against its publisher's or Crossref's record: authors, title, journal, year, volume, pages, DOI. The source log's venue and interest columns are present for every reference and agree with the record, and no account written by the sellers of a method or co-written by a company about itself is cited without that label.
2. **Claims against sources.** Every cited claim opened and read against its source; "unverifiable" where the source will not open, never a pass.
3. **Grades.** Each grade against SCP1's key; a vendor's statement never carries more than vendor-reported; an abstract-only reading is said to be one. A finding whose kind of evidence is not stated fails, and so does a simulation or a model result presented as a field result.
4. **Vendors.** No real vendor assessed, ranked, compared, recommended or mapped; each real vendor named only as writer rule 5 allows; every list complete and alike; no private company's unpublished figure; no fictional vendor tied to a real one.
5. **The case.** Every case fact against `case-facts.md`; labelled fictional; never used as evidence.
6. **Scope and tone.** No formula and no worked calculation; no method only named, without what goes in, what comes out, what it assumes and when it fails; nothing on generative AI, assistants or agents; no interview framing, no method teaching, nothing about the Project's owner or his employer; the preamble's tone rules; terms defined on first use.
7. **Deliverable and figures.** The deliverable against the brief's wording, built in full and then as a template; "In the planning meeting" and "Self-check" present and agreeing with the body; every figure's labels and numbers traced to the text.

Passes 1, 3, 6 and 7 changed from the Execution course's, from the design (`prompts-changes.md`, "Fresh checker's passes that change"): the design's "pass on sources" (venue and interest columns, sellers' and co-written accounts labelled) is joined to pass 1, which checks the Sources entries.

## 4 · The carry-forward rule (Pedro, standing; `docs/method.md` §9, in full)

Nothing found goes unrecorded or waits for a later audit. It applies to every row of the course, the last included.

1. **What counts.** Any error, mistake, contradiction, unverified claim, missing source or unmet need, found before, during or after a unit is written: by the writer, the fresh checker, the applier, the filer, the corrections checker, a script, or Pedro.
2. **About a unit already filed.** It goes into the next row's prompt as a correction, with the unit, the passage, what is wrong and the source. The next chat fixes it first, before it writes its own unit: each change is checked by a fresh agent against its source and the syllabus, applied as an exact replacement, and recorded.
3. **About a unit not yet written.** It becomes a flag in the course's `syllabus-flags.md` naming every unit it affects, and it is printed in each of those units' prompts as a guideline for the writer. A flag that would change a brief is proposed, never applied by the chat that raised it.
4. **About the unit being written.** It is fixed in the same chat before filing. What cannot be fixed (a source that will not open) is cut or marked unverified in the unit, and carried as in 2.
5. **Every delivery ends with the next prompt.** The reply of every row closes with the prompt for the next unit in a code block, as `kit/next_prompt.py` prints it (a bundle's one reply ends with the course's starter line, §4), already holding: every correction still open on a filed unit, every open flag that names the next unit, and any guideline of the course's writing template that the row showed needs updating, with the update made. A row that finds a rule of the template wrong or unworkable corrects it in the course's prompts document (`docs/<course>-prompts.md`), says what it changed, and the next prompt carries the corrected text.
6. **Nothing is dropped silently.** A correction or flag leaves the next prompt only when it is resolved, with the row and the report that resolved it written beside it.
7. **The last row** (the course's last unit in its writing order) delivers a closing corrections prompt in place of a next unit's, listing every correction and flag still open.

For this course, item 7: the last row (W-DD-SCP2) works the closing corrections itself, in its own chat, by the correction route, and delivers a closing corrections prompt only for what is still open after that, saying why. Item 5's prompts document is this one, `docs/supply-chain-planning-prompts.md`.
