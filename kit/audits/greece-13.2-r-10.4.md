## 3 Disputes
1. Sisyphus slander (section 1): real question, but no source or scholar on either side; "folklore the epic suppressed" against "classical invention" is stated without a name. One side unnamed, in fact both.
2. Palamedes' absence from Homer (section 2): real. "Ancient critics" (Philostratus, Heroicus 25.13; schol. Od. 11.547 would do) against "most modern scholars": no names on either side.
3. The duals in Iliad 9 (section 3): real and well known. Positions fairly stated (two-man version with Phoenix inserted; loose dual; pointed exclusion of Odysseus) but no names (Page 1959; Hainsworth 1993; Nagy 1979). Both sides unnamed.
4. Astyanax (section 4): real, ancient against ancient, all three sources named with references. Fair; the closing sentence draws the common thread without leaning.
5. The end of the Odyssey and the maids (section 6): two disputes in one box. The first names Aristophanes and Aristarchus and states the two readings fairly (original end or fulfilment) but does not give the scholion. The second (how to render dmōiai) names Wilson and Atwood against "older translations", a fair statement of a real shift; not strictly a scholarly dispute.
6. Teiresias' prophecy and the Telegony (section 6): stated as "most scholars" against an unnamed alternative; the alternative is not really a party (nobody argues that Teiresias promises death by stingray). As framed it is an open question, not a dispute. Unnamed.
7. The Philoctetes Odysseus (section 7): real (sophist and operator against servant of the gods' will), fairly summarised, but "some critics … others". Both sides unnamed.

Missing: the date of Ajax (the report's "probably 440s" is one side of an open question, late 450s to 440s); the modern debate over the Doloneia is handled in the body without names (Danek 1988 for its deliberate difference, Hainsworth 1993 for the loose fit) and would be the natural box for section 3; whether Odysseus in Ajax 1332–1373 acts from pity or from calculation (Knox 1961 against Whitman 1951) is the one dispute a reader of this topic most needs and is not raised.
