## The three Rome dimensions

| # | Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | Source criticism | Regal or early-Republican events narrated as fact with no reporter named, or Livy and Dionysius cited as if contemporary; a number from Valerius Antias passed on bare. | The report says once that the sources are late and then narrates; Livy is dated but Dionysius and Plutarch are not; the derivative sources are named but not placed against the annalists they used. | Every pre-300 claim carries its reporter and the gap: 1.4 "Livy 1.3.10–16.8, written about 27–25 BC, seven centuries after the date he accepts; Dionysius … published from 7 BC; and Plutarch's Romulus, about AD 100"; the annalist behind a detail is named where known (1.4: Piso's Tarpeia, Fabius' Antho, Antias' 527 women); and the report draws the consequence (0.1: "Their agreement is one tradition reported twice; Dionysius' extra detail is usually extra rhetoric"). |
| 10 | Cross-reference integrity | A Greek module the prompt points to is retold, or contradicted, or the pointer misdescribes it ("Greek module 7.3 covers Pyrrhus' Sicilian campaign" when it does not). | Pointers present and accurate but the report still retells a paragraph of what it points to; or a pointer to a Rome neighbour that has not been opened. | Pointers are exact and the material is left there: 0.1 "Greek module 0.1 covers those sources, and they are not repeated here", and in the Ovid trap "Greek module 0.1 warned against treating Ovid as Greek; the Roman trap is the mirror image"; 1.4 hands Theseus to Greek 3.3, the kings to 1.5, the Lupercalia of 44 to 5.4, each in a clause. Every pointer checked against its target holds. |
| 11 | Names and forms | Zeus for Jupiter, Ulysses beside Odysseus in one report; "Augustus" before 27 BC or "Octavian" after; years without BC/AD where the century is ambiguous; Greek forms for Roman people (Marcus Tullius as "Kikero"). | Standard forms with one or two slips (one "Augustus" for 29 BC; Dio's book number given in one numbering without the other). | Latin gods, English forms for people and places, Octavian until 27 and Augustus after (1.4: "Octavian's first consulship in 43 … In 27 he passed over the name Romulus for Augustus … as Augustus he displayed"), BC and AD given wherever the century could be mistaken, Cicero's works by the spec's titles (0.1: On the Orator, On the Laws, On the Republic, Brutus, Against Catiline). |

Scoring rules added for Rome:
- Dimension 9 is scored on the regal and early-Republican claims only; a report with no such claims (an imperial module) scores it on whichever derivative source it leans on (the Historia Augusta, Dio's epitomators, Zosimus) and whether it says so. A module with nothing of the kind gets "n/a" and is averaged over ten dimensions.
- Dimension 10 counts Rome-to-Rome pointers as well as Greek ones: open every target. A false pointer costs two points by itself.
- Dimension 11 slips are listed in the errata table with the fix; three or more count as one factual error on dimension 1.
