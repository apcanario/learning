## 5 Depth
Strongest:
1. The closing h2: the circularity point (Schliemann's II, Dörpfeld's VI, Blegen's VIIa each "confirmed" by the excavator who wanted them) and the distinction between what oral poetry keeps (names, places, former greatness) and what it loses (events) give the methodological lesson the prompt asked for as an argument, not a maxim.
2. The Hittite section's cumulative paragraph: four letters are reduced to what they jointly show (a Mycenaean power active for two centuries; Wilusa attacked, a king lost, hostility between two great kings) and no more; the Sausgamuwa erasure is used as a terminus.
3. "Wind, current and the economics": the epithet, the north-easterly, the one-to-three-knot current and the square rig are made to explain Beşik Bay and the toll economy, then turned on the poem ("needs no stolen queen", Hdt 1.1–5).
Weakest:
1. "Finley's scepticism": one sentence of three clauses; nothing on why Finley put Homeric society in the tenth and ninth centuries (the gift exchange, the oikos, the absence of the polis), which is the argument Latacz had to answer.
2. "Dörpfeld's walls": describes the masonry and stops; the reason Dörpfeld's VI displaced Schliemann's II (Mycenaean imports in VI, none in II) is not stated, though it is the hinge of the whole identification sequence.
3. Box 5 (Appaliunas): three assertions without a name or a reason, where the body had set up a real question (a restored word, a Greek-looking name at a Hittite vassal court).
