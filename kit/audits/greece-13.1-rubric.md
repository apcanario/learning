# Greek 13.1: the rubric, calibrated on the core course

Run with Fable 5.1, 27 October 2026. Baseline reports: Greek 2.1 (Cosmogony: Chaos to Zeus, target 2,000), 4.2 (The Iliad, 3,000), 6.3 (The Peloponnesian War, 3,000), 8.1 (Ritual practice, 2,500), all written with Fable 5.1 and read in full against their prompts. Every quoted anchor below is from one of the four.

## The rubric

| # | Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Factual accuracy | Three or more wrong dates, names, numbers or attributions, or one error that the report builds an argument on. | One or two small slips that change nothing downstream (a wrong year in passing, a number given to the wrong ancient author). | Every checkable claim checks out; where it cannot be checked the report says so. 2.1: Kronos's children "reversed order, which is why Hestia, swallowed first, is 'first and last' in cult (Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite 22–23)"; the detail, the reason and the citation all hold. |
| 2 | Citation precision | Author only, or "sources say"; figures and quotations with no reference; a reference that does not contain what it is cited for. | Author and work throughout, book or line numbers for the main points only; or one citation that gives a number its source does not give (6.3 puts Plutarch's "some 3,000 prisoners" under Xenophon, Hellenica 2.1.20–32, which has no figure). | Author, work and book or line for every claim that has one, so a reader can open the text: 2.1 cites the Theogony by line throughout and the table gives the line range of each Titan's catalogue; 8.1 gives inscriptions by corpus number (IG I³ 35, SEG 9.72, ML 30). Modern works carry title and date. |
| 3 | Prompt coverage | Two or more named items missing, or one treated in a sentence when the prompt asks for a section. | Every item present but one or two thin: 4.2's Nestor and Paris get a table row each where the prompt wanted "what each represents". | Every item the prompt names has its own paragraph or table row and the order of the prompt is visible in the headings: 6.3 walks the prompt's list from Corcyra to the amnesty of 403 and then takes "Thucydides as historian" as its own section. |
| 4 | Depth and argument | Narrates in sequence; "what" without "why"; no sentence a handbook would not contain. | Explains the main turns but states its interpretations rather than earning them; a strong section beside a flat one. | Each section ends on a reason: 2.1 "Zeus rules by distributing rather than hoarding: that is the poem's political theology, and the reason his reign is not another turn of the wheel"; 4.2 "The omissions are the design"; 6.3 "the Dialogue closes Book 5 and Sicily opens Book 6, so the logic of power without limit expounded at Melos is tested at Syracuse." Contrasts are drawn (Gigantomachy "different in four ways"; "what is Greek shows by contrast"). |
| 5 | Handling of disputes | Boxes absent or decorative: one side unnamed, "some scholars", or a dispute that is not real. | Real disputes with names on each side, but the report leans without saying so, or leaves out the one dispute a reader of this topic needs. | Every box names a source or scholar on each side and says what remains open: 6.3 on causes sets de Ste. Croix (1972) against Kagan (1969) and Badian (1993) and ends "The weight of the Megarian Decree remains uncertain"; 4.2 on fate gives three readings and names the crux ("The Sarpedon scene is the crux and supports all three"). Ancient against ancient and modern against modern both appear. |
| 6 | Self-test quality | Questions answerable from general knowledge; answers wrong or contradicting the body. | Mostly understanding, some recall; every answer right. 8.1 Q10 (which month the Mysteries fall in) is recall; Q8's answer adds "sweeping the house", which the body does not say. | Questions ask why or how and need the report: 2.1 Q6 "Why does Zeus win the Titanomachy, in Hesiod's telling?", Q7 "three reasons the Gigantomachy is a different event"; 4.2 Q6 "What does the Sarpedon episode show about Zeus and fate?". Answers match the body and carry the citation. |
| 7 | Carry-forward usefulness | Summaries of the report, or truisms. | Three true ideas, but one is a summary rather than something the reader needs later. | Three ideas phrased as instruments for later modules: 2.1 "Titanomachy, Typhon and Gigantomachy are three distinct events … later Greek and Roman writers blur them, and you should not"; 8.1 "judge later material … by asking what people did, not what they professed"; 6.3's third names what the fourth century begins with. |
| 8 | House style | Preamble or closing filler, US spelling, mannered phrasing, broken markup, or body more than 25 percent off target. | Clean prose and markup; body outside the −10/+15 percent band but under 25 percent off; or one or two mannered sentences. | British spelling, begins with the first h2, plain prose, valid structure (6–10 h2, boxes, 10-question quiz, 3 carry items), body within the band. 4.2 (+10 percent) and 6.3 (+6 percent) are the models. |

Scoring rules used throughout the audit:
- Dimension 1 is scored on the errata table of module 13.2 / Rome 14.2: 5 for none, 4 for one slip, 3 for two, 2 for three or four, 1 for five or more or one load-bearing error.
- Dimension 8 counted the length band with check.py's numbers (body words before the Self-test heading; −10 to +15 percent) in the audits run on 27 October 2026 up to Rome 14.4. **From then on length does not score** (Pedro's rule, 27 October 2026: the target is a guide, a topic takes the words it needs); dimension 8 is spelling, filler, mannered phrasing and markup only, and padding, not length, is the fault. The baseline scores below keep their historical 8s (2.1's 2 and 8.1's 4 were length); on the new rule 2.1 is a 5 and the baseline mean 4.9. A proper title in US spelling (The Orientalizing Revolution) is not a spelling fault.
- A dispute box with one side unnamed costs a point on dimension 5 by itself; a dispute that is not real (the two sides do not actually differ) costs two.
- Verdicts follow the prompt: pass is no dimension below 4 and no factual error; revise is a list of edits fixes it; rewrite is two or more dimensions below 3 or more than two factual errors.
