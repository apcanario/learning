# Rome 14.6: the syllabus reviewed

Run with Fable 5.1, 27 October 2026. Read in full: every unit rationale, title, why line and prompt in `roman-syllabus.html` (85 modules), the Rome course copy in `courses.py:ROME`, and, by the same method, the Greek syllabus's unit 12 (deep dive III) and the renumbered audit (unit 13), with the Greek course copy in `courses.py:GREECE`. The Opus 5.5 syllabi review of 25 October (`reviews/2026-quality-pass/syllabi.md`, units 12 to 14 and Greek 12 to 13) was re-read; every one of its applied changes was found in the current text and is confirmed. This pass therefore concentrated on units 0 to 11, which that review did not cover.

## 1. Structure

- **Order.** Sources first (unit 0), then the land and peoples with the founding legends read beside the archaeology (unit 1), the religion before the Republic that ran on it (unit 2), then history in period order with the myths inside the periods that wrote them (3 to 8), then the standing institutions (9) and literature, art and reception (10) closed by the capstone. History and myth stay interleaved throughout; the sources-first rule holds. No change.
- **Missing, duplicated, misplaced.** Nothing major is missing. Two threads are thin and spread across modules rather than given a home: the economy (coinage, trade, the grain fleet, the villa) sits in 4.4, 7.5 and 9.4; engineering and science in 9.2, 9.4 and 10.2. Both match the Greek course's own silences and need no new module. The duplications found are all prompt-level (section 3); none is a module in the wrong unit.
- **The three deep dives.** The fall of the Republic, the Aeneid and Roman philosophy mirror the Greek course's three (Troy, Alexander, philosophy) and extend the course's three densest threads. The one candidate not chosen, Christianity and late antiquity, is carried adequately by 2.5 and 8.3 to 8.6. Keep.

## 2. Facts inside the prompts

Every checkable claim in the 85 prompts, the why lines and the rationales was checked: dates, chapter and line citations, titles and authors of modern works, counts. All hold, including the ones a reader might doubt: Livy 142 books, 35 surviving (0.1); seven reigns for 244 years (753 to 509; 0.3 and 1.5); Tacitus, Histories 3.72 and Pliny 34.139 for Porsenna taking Rome (1.6); Varro's 170 imageless years at Augustine, City of God 4.31 (2.1); the lectisternium of 217 (Livy 22.10.9; 2.2); Gellius 10.15 on the Flamen Dialis (2.3); Polybius 6.53–54 (2.4); Livy 39.8–19 and the Tiriolo bronze (2.5); Gaius' Institutes in the Verona palimpsest of 1816 (9.1); Juvenal 10.81 (9.4); the Augustan exhibition of 1937 (10.4); the 54-module count in the capstone (55 content modules in units 0 to 10 less the capstone itself); the lex Villia annalis of 180 (11.1); Petrarch's find of 1345 (11.5); Cicero, Brutus 262 and Suetonius, Julius 56 (11.6); and all the unit 12 and 13 references the earlier review listed as confirmed. Greek unit 12: Theaetetus 152a–179b, Cratylus 385e–386a, Memorabilia 1.2.12–48, Strabo 13.1.54, Principal Doctrines 2, Agathias 2.30–31 all right.
