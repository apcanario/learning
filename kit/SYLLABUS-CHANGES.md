**Status: applied in the session of 25 October 2026.** Kept as the record of the specification. Where verification required, the prompts as written differ from this draft: in 13.1, Athenodorus Cordylion (Cato's) and Athenodorus son of Sandon (Augustus') are two men (Strabo 14.5.14), the decree of 161 is a pointer to module 4.5, which covers it, and the censors' edict of 92 BC is added; in 13.8, 'at Carnuntum' stands between books 2 and 3, not at the head of book 2; Greek 12.2 leaves the minor Socratics to 12.5 to avoid a duplicate; and each new prompt points back to the written reports that already cover its ground (Greek 5.6, 6.5, 7.2, 7.4, 8.4, 11.1; Rome 4.5, 5.4, 5.6, 6.3).

Drafted 25 October 2026 with Opus 5.5. The session that writes Rome unit 7 makes these changes **first**, so that the unit 7 writers' lane notes can name the new modules. This file is a specification. The session writes the actual prompts in the house formula: "Give me an in-depth report on … Cover: …; Format: about N words, headed sections, name sources, flag disagreements, end with a 10-question self-test with answers and the three ideas I must carry forward."

Every prompt must be checked against the reports already written before it goes in. Where a written report already covers something, the prompt says "extending module X" and points back instead of asking for it again. The facts in these specs were checked when drafting, but a prompt is a contract, so verify every date, citation and modern title before you write it into a syllabus.

### 1b. New unit 13, Deep dive III: Roman philosophy, the Stoics and Marcus Aurelius

Unit rationale (draft): *Philosophy reached Rome as an embassy and a scandal and became the inner life of its governing class. Eight modules on how Romans used the Greek schools, above all the Stoa, ending with the emperor who wrote philosophy to himself.*

| Module | Title | Target | Extends | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13.1 | Philosophy comes to Rome | 2500 | 4.5, 5.6; Greek 7.2 | The expulsions of philosophers and rhetors (the decree of 161 BC in Suetonius, *On Rhetoricians* 1, and Gellius 15.11; Athenaeus 12.547a on two expelled Epicureans). The embassy of 155 and Carneades in one sentence (module 4.5 covers it). Panaetius with Scipio Aemilianus; Posidonius at Rhodes and his Roman visitors (Cicero, Pompey). Greek philosophers in Roman households: Philodemus with Piso, Diodotus with Cicero, Athenodorus with Cato and later with Augustus, Arius Didymus. Cicero's Latin philosophy as a pointer to 5.6. Roman suspicion and Roman use: why Romans took the ethics and left the theory. Tacitus, *Agricola* 4, on a mother curbing her son's zeal for philosophy. |
| 13.2 | Stoicism made Roman | 2500 | Greek 12.7 | Point back to Greek 12.7 for Zeno to Chrysippus. Here: the doctrine as Romans received and reshaped it. Virtue the only good; preferred and dispreferred indifferents; *oikeiosis* and Hierocles' circles; the passions as false judgements; providence and the cosmic city; fate and responsibility (Chrysippus' cylinder, Cicero, *On Fate* 42–43, Gellius 7.2). Panaetius' four *personae* and Cicero's *On Duties* as a Roman ethic of role and office. The critics: Cicero, *Pro Murena* 61–66, mocking Cato's paradoxes; Horace's satires on the Stoic sage. |
| 13.3 | The rival schools at Rome | 2500 | 5.6; Greek 12.5, 12.6, 12.8 | Epicureans after Lucretius (point back to 5.6): Philodemus and the Villa of the Papyri at Herculaneum, Cassius, Atticus, Horace's "pig from Epicurus' herd" (*Epistles* 1.4.16). The New Academy and Cicero the sceptic. Peripatetics. Nigidius Figulus and the Neopythagoreans. Cynics under the empire: Demetrius, friend of Seneca and Thrasea; Lucian's *Peregrinus*. Why the Stoa won the senatorial class. Marcus Aurelius' endowed chairs at Athens in 176. |
| 13.4 | Cato and the Stoic opposition | 2500 | 5.4, 7.1, 7.2 | Cato the Younger as a Stoic life (Plutarch, *Cato Minor*; Utica only as a pointer to 5.4). Cato after death: Seneca's Cato, Lucan books 2 and 9. Thrasea Paetus, his *Life of Cato* (used by Plutarch) and his death in 66, where the *Annals* break off (16.21–35). Arria's "Paete, non dolet" (Pliny, *Letters* 3.16). Helvidius Priscus against Vespasian (Suetonius, *Vespasian* 15; Epictetus, *Discourses* 1.2.19–24). The expulsions of philosophers under Vespasian and Domitian. Arulenus Rusticus and Herennius Senecio killed for praising Thrasea and Helvidius (Tacitus, *Agricola* 2). The debate over whether there was a "Stoic opposition" at all (Brunt, "Stoicism and the Principate", 1975; MacMullen, *Enemies of the Roman Order*, 1966). |
| 13.5 | Seneca | 3000 | 7.1, 10.2 | Life: Corduba and the elder Seneca; exile to Corsica, 41–49, and the *Consolation to Polybius*; recall by Agrippina; tutor and minister to Nero; *On Clemency*; wealth and the charge of hypocrisy (Suillius in Tacitus, *Annals* 13.42; Dio 61.10); withdrawal. Works: the *Moral Letters to Lucilius*, the dialogues (*On Anger*, *On the Shortness of Life*, *On Tranquillity of Mind*, *On the Happy Life*), *Natural Questions*. The death in 65 as a Socratic performance (*Annals* 15.60–64). Quintilian's verdict on his style (10.1.125–131). The Christian Seneca: the forged letters to Paul, Tertullian's "often ours" (*On the Soul* 20). The tragedies stay with module 10.2. |
| 13.6 | Musonius Rufus and Epictetus | 2500 | 13.2 | Musonius' lectures as a pupil recorded them: women should study philosophy, marriage, simple living; exile to Gyaros. Epictetus: slave of Nero's freedman Epaphroditus, lame, freed, expelled from Rome under Domitian, school at Nicopolis. Arrian's *Discourses* and *Handbook*. What is up to us (*Handbook* 1); *prohairesis*; roles and duties. His reach: Marcus Aurelius received Epictetus' notes from Rusticus (*Meditations* 1.7); the *Handbook* was adapted for Christian monks; modern cognitive therapy claims him. |
| 13.7 | Marcus Aurelius: a life | 3000 | 7.3 | Birth in 121 as Marcus Annius Verus; family; Hadrian's "Verissimus"; the adoptions of 138; marriage to Faustina the Younger; the long apprenticeship under Antoninus (*Meditations* 1.16). Teachers: Fronto (the letters recovered from palimpsests by Angelo Mai from 1815), Herodes Atticus, Junius Rusticus and the turn to philosophy. Accession in 161 with Lucius Verus. The Parthian war. The Antonine plague (Galen). The Marcomannic wars and the years at Carnuntum and Sirmium. The rain miracle on the column against the Christian versions. Avidius Cassius' revolt of 175 and the rumours about Faustina; her death at Halala. Commodus made Augustus in 177. The martyrs of Lyon in 177 (Eusebius, *Church History* 5.1). Death in 180. Sources: the *Historia Augusta*'s *Marcus*, Dio's epitomators, Herodian book 1, Fronto, the column and the equestrian statue. Modern biography: Birley, *Marcus Aurelius* (1966, revised 1987). | 13.8 | The Meditations and the afterlife of Roman Stoicism | 2500 | 13.6, 13.7 | The text: *To Himself*, in Greek, twelve books. Rutherford, *The Meditations of Marcus Aurelius: A Study*, 1989; Brunt, "Marcus Aurelius in his Meditations", 1974). Themes: impermanence, the view from above, the inner citadel, duty, the cosmic city, death. The debt to Epictetus. Transmission: Arethas about 900, the *Suda*, the first edition by Xylander in 1558 from a manuscript now lost. Afterlife: Lipsius' neo-Stoicism (*On Constancy*, 1584), Montaigne, Frederick the Great, Matthew Arnold's essay of 1863, modern popular Stoicism. Was the author a philosopher or a tired emperor talking himself into his duty? |

## 2. Greece: add a philosophy deep dive, move the audit

The Greek core treats philosophy in three survey modules. 5.6 pairs the Presocratics with the lyric poets. 6.5 puts the Sophists, Socrates, Plato and Aristotle into one report. 7.2 gives the Hellenistic schools a section. 7.4 covers Neoplatonism in a paragraph. The new unit takes each to the depth of the Troy and Alexander deep dives. Inserting a core unit would renumber written reports, so this is a deep dive.

### 2a. New unit 12, Deep dive III: Greek philosophy

Unit rationale (draft): *From Thales to the closing of the Academy: the arguments, the schools and the lives, at the depth the survey modules 5.6, 6.5 and 7.2 could only outline.*

| Module | Title | Target | Extends | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12.1 | The Presocratics | 3000 | 5.6 | The Milesians; Xenophanes; Pythagoras and the Pythagoreans; Heraclitus; Parmenides, Zeno's paradoxes and Melissus; Empedocles; Anaxagoras; Leucippus and Democritus. Each one's argument, not just his slogan. The evidence problem: fragments and doxography (Aristotle, *Metaphysics* A; Theophrastus; Simplicius), Diels' *Die Fragmente der Vorsokratiker* and DK numbering, the Derveni papyrus, the Strasbourg Empedocles. |
| 12.2 | The Sophists and Socrates | 2500 | 6.5 | Protagoras (man the measure, in Plato's *Protagoras* and *Theaetetus*); Gorgias (*On Not Being*, *Helen*); Prodicus, Hippias, Thrasymachus; Antiphon's papyrus on law and nature. Socrates: the Socratic problem (Plato, Xenophon, Aristophanes' *Clouds*, Aristotle), the elenchus, the *daimonion*, the trial of 399 and its politics (Critias, Alcibiades, the amnesty), the *Phaedo*'s death scene, the minor Socratics. 6.5's outline is the floor: go deeper, don't repeat. |
| 12.3 | Plato | 3000 | 6.5 | Life and the Seventh Letter's authenticity; Sicily; the Academy. The dialogue form and the chronology debate (stylometry). The Forms; recollection and the immortal soul (*Meno*, *Phaedo*); the *Republic* beyond 6.5's outline; love in the *Symposium* and *Phaedrus*; the *Timaeus*' cosmos; the *Laws*; the unwritten doctrines; Plato's myths (Er, Atlantis), with pointers to the Greek modules that use them. |
| 12.4 | Aristotle | 3000 | 6.5; 11.1, 11.7 | Life: Stagira; twenty years in the Academy; Assos and Lesbos; tutor to Alexander (point back to the Alexander deep dive); the Lyceum. The corpus as lecture notes and its survival (Strabo 13.1.54 on Neleus' cellar; Andronicus' edition). Logic and the syllogism; *Physics* and the four causes; *Metaphysics*; the biology (*History of Animals*, the Lesbos lagoon); *On the Soul*; *Nicomachean Ethics* beyond the mean (friendship, *eudaimonia*, the contemplative life); *Politics* (natural slavery, the constitutions); *Poetics* and *Rhetoric*. The afterlife in outline: Arabic transmission and Aquinas. |
| 12.5 | Cynics, Cyrenaics and the other Socratic schools | 2500 | 7.2 | Antisthenes; Diogenes of Sinope and the anecdote tradition (Diogenes Laertius book 6); Crates and Hipparchia; Aristippus and the Cyrenaics on pleasure; the Megarians and dialectic. Diogenes Laertius as a source and his limits. The Cynic as social critic, and the road from Diogenes to Zeno. |
| 12.6 | Epicurus and the Garden | 2500 | 7.2 | Life and the Garden; the three letters and the *Principal Doctrines* (Diogenes Laertius book 10); the fourfold remedy; atoms and the swerve; pleasure as the absence of pain; friendship; "death is nothing to us". Diogenes of Oenoanda's wall inscription. The Herculaneum papyri. The hostile tradition (Plutarch, *Against Colotes*). Point to Rome 5.6 and 13.3 for Lucretius and the Roman Epicureans. |
| 12.7 | The Stoa from Zeno to Posidonius | 2500 | 7.2 | Zeno of Citium and the Painted Stoa; Cleanthes' *Hymn to Zeus*; Chrysippus' system (propositional logic, *pneuma*, the conflagration, the ethics). The sources: von Arnim's *Stoicorum Veterum Fragmenta*, Diogenes Laertius book 7, Stobaeus, Cicero. Panaetius and Posidonius as the hinge to Rome; hand off to Rome deep dive modules 13.1 and 13.2. |
| 12.8 | Sceptics and Neoplatonists: the late schools to 529 | 2500 | 7.2, 7.4 | Pyrrho and Timon; the Academy's sceptical turn (Arcesilaus, Carneades); Sextus Empiricus; Middle Platonism; Plotinus (the *Enneads*, Porphyry's *Life*), Porphyry, Iamblichus, Proclus, Hypatia. Justinian's measure of 529 (point back to 7.4) and the argument over what it did. Simplicius and the philosophers who went to the Persian court (Agathias 2.30–31). |
