# Rome 12.7: Augustan or anti-Augustan?: sources

Written 1 November 2026 (Opus 5.5) with the report. Line numbers of the *Aeneid* follow the standard Oxford text (Mynors); "(not verified)" marks a reference given from memory that could not be confirmed this session.

## Ancient sources
- Virgil, *Aeneid* (OCT, ed. R. A. B. Mynors, 1969): 1.279; 1.286–296 (1.294–296); 3.280; 6.791–793; 6.851–853 (6.853); 6.860–886 (6.861–862, 6.869–871, 6.882–883, 6.885–886); 6.893–898; 7.759–760; 8.626–731 (8.666–670, 8.678–679, 8.685–688, 8.698–700, 8.704–706, 8.714–728, 8.730, 8.731); 10.441–443; 10.517–520; 10.524; 12.930–938; 12.938–941; 12.946–947; 12.949; 12.950–952; book 4 generally, with the curse 4.607–629.
- Aelius Donatus, *Life of Virgil* (the Suetonius–Donatus life): 21 (the design), 32 (Octavia at the reading).
- Ovid, *Tristia* 2.533–536 (*tuae Aeneidos auctor*); *Heroides* 7 (Dido's letter); *Metamorphoses* 14.78–81 (Dido), 14.581–608 (Aeneas deified).
- Servius, *Commentary on the Aeneid* (ed. Thilo and Hagen): preface (*intentio Vergilii haec est, Homerum imitari et Augustum laudare a parentibus*, as quoted by R. D. Williams 1967); on 1.286 (Julius Caesar, as given in module 6.3); on 2.557 (Pompey, as given in module 12.2); on 6.893 (the gate of ivory: the poet wants all he has said understood as false) (not verified); on 12.940 (pietas in both the hesitation and the killing, as paraphrased by Gaskin 2020).
- Macrobius, *Saturnalia* (ed. Kaster, Loeb, 2011): books 3–6 (Virgil as authority); 1.24.11 (Virgil's letter to Augustus); 5.17.4–6 (Eustathius on the fame of Dido's story, text as quoted on aeneid4.theclassicslibrary.com).
- Lactantius, *Divine Institutes* 5.10 (Aeneas not pious; tr. W. Fletcher, Ante-Nicene Fathers 7, via bkv.unifr.ch).
- Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* 1125b31–32 (the man angry at the right things, as and for as long as he ought).
