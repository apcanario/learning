# Rome 4.5: Polybius, Cato and the Greek invasion of Rome: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Caecilius Statius, compared with his Menandrian original (known through Gellius, *Attic Nights* 2.23; the report's wording).
- Cato the Elder, *Origines* (lost; fragments only). Fragments: Chassignet, Budé (1986); Cornell (ed.), *The Fragments of the Roman Historians* (2013).
- Cato the Elder, *On Agriculture*, preface 1–4. Standard edition: Goujard, Budé (1975).
- Cato's advice to his son on the Greeks, as quoted in Pliny, *Natural History* 29.14.
- Cicero, *Brutus* 60; 71; 72; 79.
- Cicero, *On Ends* 1.9.
- Cicero, *On Old Age* 26.
- Cicero, *On the Republic* 2.1–2; 5.1; and book 3 (the Carneades speeches, known chiefly through Lactantius, *Divine Institutes* book 5). Also *On Friendship* and *On Duties*, named without locators.
- Cicero, *Tusculan Disputations* 1.3.
- Dionysius of Halicarnassus, *Roman Antiquities* 1.11.1.
- Ennius, *Annales* (fragments; cited for the Homeric dream, the "three hearts" and the line *moribus antiquis res stat Romana virisque*, the last quoted in Cicero, *On the Republic* 5.1). Standard edition of the fragments: Skutsch, *The Annals of Q. Ennius* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1985).
- Gellius, *Attic Nights* 2.23; 3.3.15; 15.11.1; 17.17.1; 17.21.45; 18.9.5.
- Horace, *Epistles* 2.1.69–71; 2.1.156–157; 2.1.157–163.
- Lactantius, *Divine Institutes* book 5 (for Cicero, *On the Republic* 3).
- Livius Andronicus, *Odusia* (fragment, first line *Virum mihi, Camena, insece versutum*, preserved in Gellius 18.9.5).
- Livy, *History of Rome* 7.2; 39.6.7; 39.40–44; 40.29.
- Lucilius (satirist, served at Numantia; mocked affected Greek; cited through Cicero, *On Ends* 1.9).
- Naevius, *Bellum Punicum* and his plays (lost; known through Gellius 3.3.15 and 17.21.45).
- Nepos, *Cato* 1.4.
- Pausanias, *Description of Greece* 7.11.
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* 17.244; 29.14.
- Plautus, *Trinummus* 19; *Mostellaria* (named for *pergraecari*, without a line number).
- Plutarch, *Cato the Elder* (*Cato Maior*) 2; 3; 9; 12; 16–19; 22; 23.
- Plutarch, *Philopoemen* 21.
- Polybius, *Histories* 1.1.5; 3.4; 3.8–9; 6.2; 6.3–10; 6.10; 6.11–18; 6.11.11; 6.15–17; 6.27–32; 6.37–39; 6.42; 6.43–44; 6.48–50; 6.51–52; 6.53–54; 6.53.10; 6.56; 6.57; 28.6; 31.11–15; 31.23–24; 31.25; 38.21–22; 39.5. Standard edition: Loeb (Paton, rev. Walbank and Habicht).
- Pseudo-Lucian, *Long Lives* (*Macrobioi*) 22.
- Sallust, *Catiline* 10.
- Suetonius, *Life of Terence* (Caesar's verdict; Terence's birth at Carthage).
- Tacitus, *Annals* 4.33.
- Terence, *Adelphoe* 15–21; *Andria* 9–21.
- Velleius Paterculus 1.13.3.
- Valerius Antias and Calpurnius Piso Frugi, lost annalists cited for dates (Livy 40.29 on the Numa books; Pliny, *Natural History* 17.244 on 154).
- Menander, *Dis Exapaton* (Oxyrhynchus papyrus; see Handley below), compared with Plautus, *Bacchides*.
