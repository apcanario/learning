# Rome 10.2: Imperial literature, Seneca to Apuleius: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Ammianus Marcellinus, *Res Gestae* 14.6; 28.4 (digressions on the Roman nobles and plebs).
- Apuleius, *Metamorphoses* (the *Golden Ass*) 1.1; 3.24–25; 4.28–6.24 (Cupid and Psyche); book 9; book 11. The *Apology* is named without a passage.
- Augustine, *City of God* 18.18.
- Augustine, *Confessions* 1.13; 3.4.7; 8.12.29.
- Ausonius, *Mosella* and the wedding cento (*Cento nuptialis*), named without passages.
- Boccaccio, *Decameron* 7.2, taking the lover in the tub from Apuleius 9.5–7.
- Claudian, *Rape of Proserpina* (*De raptu Proserpinae*), named without a passage.
- Dante, *Purgatorio* 21–22 (Statius as a secret Christian).
- [Lucian], *Onos* (the *Greek Ass*), named without a passage.
- Fulgentius, *Mythologies* 3.6.
- Hesiod, *Works and Days* 109–201.
- Historia Augusta, *Hadrian* 11.3.
- Jerome, *Chronicle* (Quintilian as first paid rhetorician); *Commentary on Zechariah* 3.14 (thirty books of Tacitus' histories); *Letters* 22.30 (the dream of being flogged as a Ciceronian).
- Juvenal, *Satires* 1.30; 1.49–50; 1.79; 1.170–171; 10.356; 15.27; Satires 3 and 6 by number only. Standard text: W. V. Clausen (Oxford Classical Texts, 1959; 2nd edn 1992).
- Lucan, *Civil War* (*Pharsalia*) 1.1; 1.33–66; 1.128; 1.135–157; 6.507–830; 9.985–986; 10.546.
- Lucius of Patrae, *Metamorphoses* (lost), known through Photius, *Bibliotheca* codex 129.
- Martial, *Epigrams* 1.4.8; 10.4.10; books 13–14 (Saturnalia gift-tags) and *On the Spectacles*, named without passages.
- Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 1.89–150.
- Persius, *Satires* 1.2; 5.
- Petronius, *Satyricon* 1–2; 26.7–78 (Trimalchio's dinner); 35; 40; 41–46; 49; 71–78; 111–112; 119–124; 139.2.
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* preface 17; 33.1–3; books 33–37 on metals and stones.
- Pliny the Younger, *Letters* 1.1; 2.17; 3.5; 3.7.5; 3.21; 4.19; 7.27; 10.94. Standard text: R. A. B. Mynors (Oxford Classical Texts, 1963).
- Quintilian, *Institutio oratoria* 10.1.46–131 (with 10.1.90; 10.1.93; 10.1.112); 12.1.1. The lost book on the causes of corrupted eloquence is mentioned without a passage. Standard text: M. Winterbottom (Oxford Classical Texts, 1970).
- Seneca the Younger, *Letters* 114.1.
- Seneca the Younger, tragedies: *Medea* 910; *Thyestes*; *Oedipus*; the eight accepted plays and *Hercules Oetaeus* named without line numbers. Standard text: O. Zwierlein (Oxford Classical Texts, 1986).
- [Seneca], *Octavia*, named without a passage.
- Silius Italicus, *Punica* (book 15, the Choice of Scipio).
- Statius, *Thebaid* 8; 12; 12.811–812. *Silvae* 2.7; 5.4. *Achilleid*, named without a passage.
- Suetonius, *Augustus* 9; *Vespasian* 18 (the Hippo Regius inscription is under Not resolved).
- Tacitus, *Agricola* 3.1. *Annals* 1.1.3; 1.9–10; 11.11.1; 15.49.3; 15.70.1; 16.18–19. *Dialogue on Orators* 28–35 and 36–41 (28–41). *Germania* and *Histories*, named without passages.
- Valerius Flaccus, *Argonautica* book 1 (Jupiter's speech on the passing of empire).
- Virgil, *Aeneid* 6 (the model for Lucan's book 6); *Eclogue* 4 (Statius' conversion in Dante).
