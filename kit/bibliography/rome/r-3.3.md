# Rome 3.3: Veii, the Gauls and Camillus: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Aristotle on the Celts' capture of Rome and "one Lucius" who saved it: known only through Plutarch, *Camillus* 22.4 (fragment; no other locator).
- Aulus Gellius, *Attic Nights* 16.13.7 (Caere and *civitas sine suffragio*).
- Cicero, *Pro Balbo* 53 (the bronze column of the Latin treaty).
- Diodorus Siculus, *Library of History* 14.93.2; 14.93.3–5; 14.102.4; 14.113; 14.114; 14.115.6; 14.116; 14.116.7; 14.116.8–9; 14.117.5; 14.117.7. Book 14 is also relied on generally as the sparer account.
- Dionysius of Halicarnassus, *Roman Antiquities* 6.95 (terms of the Cassian treaty). The report notes that his account after 443 survives only in excerpts. Standard edition: Loeb (Cary).
- Heraclides Ponticus on a Hyperborean army taking "Rome": known only through Plutarch, *Camillus* 22.2.
- Justin, *Epitome of Pompeius Trogus* 20.5.
- Livy, *History of Rome* 1.44; 1.44.3; 2.33; 2.41; 3.15–18; 4.17–20; 4.26–29; 4.59; 4.59–60; 5.1–23; 5.2; 5.4.11; 5.15–16; 5.19; 5.19.1; 5.21.1–3; 5.21.8–9; 5.22.1; 5.22.3–7; 5.23.5–6; 5.30.8; 5.32.7–9; 5.33–35; 5.33.2–4; 5.34; 5.34–35; 5.35–36; 5.37–38; 5.39–55; 5.40.7–10; 5.41; 5.42–43; 5.46.1–3; 5.47; 5.48.8–9; 5.49; 5.49.1–6; 5.49.7; 5.50.3; 5.50.7; 5.51–54; 5.55.1–2; 5.55.2–5; 6.1.2; 6.1.10; 6.1.11; 6.2; 6.4.4; 6.5.8; 6.9.4; 6.12.2–5; 6.25–26; 6.32.1; 7.12; 8.3–6; 8.7–10; 8.11; 8.13; 8.14; 8.14.12. Livy books 2–8 are also relied on generally.
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* 3.57 (Theopompus on the capture by Gauls); 29.57 (dogs crucified); 35.157 (Vulca).
- Plutarch, *Camillus* 1; 15; 22; 22.2; 22.4. Plutarch, *On the Fortune of the Romans* (geese carried in state; no passage given). Standard edition of the *Lives*: Loeb (Perrin).
- Polybius, *Histories* 1.6.1–2; 2.17; 2.18.2; 2.18.3; 2.22.5; 3.24.
- Propertius 4.10.27–30.
- Strabo 5.2.3.
- Suetonius, *Augustus* 7.2; *Tiberius* 3.2.

## Corpora and inscriptions
- The Tabula Veliterna (third-century Volscian bronze from Velitrae): cited by name only; edited in E. Vetter, *Handbuch der italischen Dialekte* (Heidelberg, 1953) no. 222 (from memory, not verified as the report's source).

## Not resolved
- "Stephen Oakley calls it the largest single increase in Roman territory": no work given (the *Commentary on Livy Books VI–X* is the likely source but the report does not say so, and I could not find the passage).
- "Lily Ross Taylor reckoned it added about half": no work given (probably *The Voting Districts of the Roman Republic*, 1960; not verified).
- "Marijke Gnade's Dutch team" at Satricum: excavation, no publication cited.
- "In 1916 Giulio Quirino Giglioli found the Apollo" at Portonaccio: a discovery, no publication cited.
- "Early excavators took a layer of burnt roof tiles in the Comitium for the Gallic fire": attributed to Platner and Ashby (1929) as listed above; the excavation reports behind it are not cited.
- The "Caeretan tablets" (the censors' list of disfranchised citizens): named without a source.
