# Rome 10.4: Reception: how Rome reached you: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Caesar, *Gallic War* 5.44 (Vorenus and Pullo, the centurions of the television series *Rome*).
- Cassius Dio 56.30 (Livia and the poisoned figs).
- Eusebius, *Life of Constantine* 4.61–62 (baptism near Nicomedia in 337).
- Jerome, *Chronicle* (Constantine baptised by Eusebius of Nicomedia), cited by pointer to module 8.3; no year or passage given.
- Livy 2.5 (Brutus and his sons); 2.32 (Menenius' fable of the belly); the first ten books as the subject of Machiavelli's *Discourses*.
- Plutarch, *Antony* 26 (the barge on the Cydnus); *Coriolanus* 6 (with the whole life for Volumnia and Virgilia). The Lives as the source of Shakespeare's Roman plays.
- Polybius book 6 (the mixed constitution), as Machiavelli's model.
- *Res Gestae Divi Augusti*, displayed on the pavilion wall beside the reassembled Ara Pacis (no chapter cited).
- Tacitus, *Annals* 1.5.
- Virgil, *Eclogue* 4.5 (the source of *novus ordo seclorum* on the Great Seal).

## Not resolved
- "Lorenzo Valla ... demolished it in 1440 on philology": no work named (presumably *De falso credita et ementita Constantini donatione*; not verified).
- "Nicholas of Cusa doubted it in 1433": no work named (presumably *De concordantia catholica*; not verified).
- "Otto III's chancery called it a fabrication in 1001" and "renovatio imperii Romanorum on his seal in 998": no source cited; Charlemagne's *renovatio* formula likewise.
- "Anthony Mann's *The Fall of the Roman Empire* (1964)" and the other films: listed without a reference apart from director and year.
- "Cola di Rienzo's letters" and Petrarch's letter hailing him as a new Brutus: no letter cited.
- "Thomas Platter, 21 September 1599" (the Swiss traveller at the Globe): no edition of his account cited.
- The Howard University decision and the op-ed titles: see the Modern works entry; the New York Times Magazine and Washington Post items are given only as the report describes them.
- "Orson Welles staged *Julius Caesar* in Fascist uniforms in New York in 1937", "Mostra Augustea della Romanità (September 1937)", "the Via dell'Impero (28 October 1932)", "Mussolini's proclamation of 9 May 1936": events, no publication cited.
