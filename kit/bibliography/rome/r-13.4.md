# Rome 13.4: Cato and the Stoic opposition: sources

Written 1 November 2026 (Opus 5.5) with the report. "(not verified)" marks a reference given from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Augustine, *City of God* 1.23 (Cato's suicide as weakness; his advice to his son) (not verified).
- Cicero, *Letters to Atticus* 12.4.2; 12.21.1; 12.40.1; 13.46.2 (the *Cato*, Brutus' *Cato*, Hirtius' pamphlet, Caesar on the two) (section numbers not verified).
- Cicero, *On Duties* 1.112 (Cato had to die); 2.86 (Antipater of Tyre's recent death).
- Cicero, *Pro Murena* 61–66.
- Dio, *Roman History* 65(66).12 (Helvidius and Vespasian); 65(66).13 (Mucianus against the Stoics; the expulsion; Demetrius and Hostilianus); 67.13 (Senecio). Loeb book numbers with the traditional (margin) number in brackets.
- Epictetus, *Discourses* 1.2.19–24 (Helvidius and Vespasian), from Arrian's text.
- Gellius, *Attic Nights* 15.11 (the decree under Domitian; Epictetus to Nicopolis).
- Juvenal, *Satires* 5.36–37 (birthdays of Brutus and Cassius); 6.338 (the *Anticato* in two books) (not verified).
- Lucan, *Civil War* 1.128; 2.234–325; 2.326–391; 2.380–383; book 9, especially 9.580 and 9.601–604 (line numbers not verified).
- Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 1.14.
- Martial, *Epigrams* 1.13.
- Pliny the Younger, *Letters* 3.11.2–3; 3.12.3; 3.16.2–10; 7.19.4–6.
- Plutarch, *Caesar* 54.
- Plutarch, *Cato Minor* 4; 6; 10; 11; 16–18; 25; 37; 52; 65–70 (25 and 37 checked in the Perseus English translation).
- Plutarch, *Cicero* 39.
- Seneca the Younger, *Letters* 11.10; 24.6–8; 71; 73.1 (not verified).
- Seneca the Younger, *On Providence* 2.9–12.
- Seneca the Younger, *On the Constancy of the Wise Man* 2.2.
- Suetonius, *Domitian* 10.3.
- Suetonius, *Julius* 56.5.
- Suetonius, *Vespasian* 13; 15.
- Tacitus, *Agricola* 2.1–2; 42.4; 45.1.
- Tacitus, *Annals* 14.12.1; 14.48–49; 14.57; 15.60–64; 15.70; 16.21–35 (especially 16.21.1, 16.22, 16.26, 16.27–29, 16.33, 16.34, 16.35.1).
- Tacitus, *Histories* 4.5–6; 4.43.

## Modern works
- Ahl, Frederick M., *Lucan: An Introduction* (Ithaca: Cornell University Press, 1976). His reading of Cato confirmed through Tim Stover's abstract "Thirty Years' War: Lucan's Cato since 1988" (Society for Classical Studies), not from the book.
- Brunt, P. A., "Stoicism and the Principate", *Papers of the British School at Rome* 43 (1975) 7–35 (volume not verified; pages from a sehepunkte review); reprinted in his *Studies in Stoicism*, ed. Miriam Griffin and Alison Samuels (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2013).
- Leigh, Matthew, *Lucan: Spectacle and Engagement* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1997). The phrases summarised in the report come from Stover's abstract.
- MacMullen, Ramsay, *Enemies of the Roman Order: Treason, Unrest, and Alienation in the Empire* (Cambridge, Mass.: Harvard University Press, 1966). Chapter titles checked on the Fulcrum contents page.
