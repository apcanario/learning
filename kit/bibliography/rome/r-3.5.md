# Rome 3.5: Pyrrhus and the Greek south: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Aelian, *On Animals* 1.38 (the squealing pigs against the elephants).
- Appian, *Samnite History* 7; 9; 10. The work survives in fragments.
- Aulus Gellius, *Attic Nights* 3.8 (Valerius Antias and Claudius Quadrigarius on the poisoning offer); 4.8 (Fabricius and Cornelius Rufinus).
- Cicero, *Brutus* 61; 72.
- Cicero, *On Divination* 2.116 (Ennius' "aio te, Aeacida").
- Cicero, *On Duties* 1.38; 3.86–87.
- Cicero, *On Old Age* 16.
- Cicero, *Pro Balbo* 55.
- Cicero, *Tusculan Disputations* 1.89.
- Dionysius of Halicarnassus, *Roman Antiquities* 1.6.1 (Hieronymus and Timaeus); 19.5; 19.11; 19.13–18; 20.1; 20.4; 20.9; 20.12; 20.14. Books 19–20 survive in Byzantine excerpts; standard edition of the whole work: Loeb (Cary).
- Ennius, *Annales* book 6 (the Pyrrhic War): lost; the oracle verse is known through Cicero, *On Divination* 2.116, Pyrrhus' refusal of ransom through Cicero, *On Duties* 1.38, and two lines of Appius Claudius' speech through Cicero, *On Old Age* 16. No fragment numbers are given in the report.
- Eutropius, *Breviarium* 2.11; 2.14.
- Frontinus, *On Aqueducts* 6; *Stratagems* 4.1.14.
- Justin, *Epitome of Pompeius Trogus* 18.2.
- Livy, *History of Rome* 28.28; 29.18; 34.4.6; 35.14.8; 42.47. *Periochae* 11; 12; 14; 15 (books 12–15 are lost). The *Periochae* are in vol. 14 of the Loeb Livy (Schlesinger and Geer).
- Lycophron, *Alexandra* 1226–1230; 1435–1450.
- Orosius, *Histories against the Pagans* 4.3.1–2.
- Pausanias 1.12.1.
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* 3.105; 7.214; 8.16; 33.44; 34.26.
- Plutarch, *Pyrrhus* 14; 15; 16; 17; 18–19; 19; 20; 21; 23; 25. The report treats Plutarch as following Hieronymus of Cardia and Pyrrhus' own memoirs (both lost). Standard edition: Loeb (Perrin, *Lives* IX).
- Plutarch, *Cato Maior* 2.
- Polybius, *Histories* 1.5.1; 1.7.6–7; 1.7.6–8; 1.7.10–13; 1.7.12; 1.10.4; 1.20; 1.20.14; 3.25; 6.27–42.
- Valerius Maximus, *Memorable Deeds and Sayings* 1.8.6; 2.7.15; 3.7.10; 4.3; 4.3.9; 6.5; 6.6.5.
- Varro, *On the Latin Language* 7.39.
- Velleius Paterculus 1.14.7.
- Virgil, *Aeneid* 6.836–840.
- Zonaras, *Epitome of Histories* 8.2; 8.5; 8.6; 8.7 (the twelfth-century epitome of Cassius Dio's lost books; standard edition Büttner-Wobst, Bonn, 1897, from memory). Florus is named as a handbook without a passage.
- Claudius Quadrigarius, Valerius Antias, Hieronymus of Cardia, Timaeus: lost; known here through Gellius 3.8, Plutarch and Dionysius 1.6.1 as cited.

## Modern works
- Andrew Burnett, "The first Roman silver coins", *Numismatica e Antichità Classiche* 7 (1978) 121–142 (pages from memory, not verified).
- *Historia Numorum: Italy*, ed. N. K. Rutter (London: British Museum Press, 2001) (cited by the report for the early date of the first Roman silver; contributor not named in the report).
- Simon Hornblower, *Lykophron: Alexandra: Greek Text, Translation, Commentary, and Introduction* (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2015) (identified from context: the report says "Simon Hornblower (edition, 2015)").
- Patrick Kent, *A History of the Pyrrhic War* (Abingdon: Routledge, 2020).
- Arnaldo Momigliano, "Terra Marique", *Journal of Roman Studies* 32 (1942) 53–64.

## Corpora and inscriptions
- M. H. Crawford, *Roman Republican Coinage* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1974): RRC no. 13 (the earliest didrachm, helmeted Mars and horse's head, 280–276 in Crawford's dating); no. 20 (Hercules and the she-wolf, 269–266).
- *Sylloge Inscriptionum Graecarum*, ed. W. Dittenberger, 3rd edn (Leipzig, 1915–24): Syll.³ 392 (Pyrrhus' dedication to Zeus at Dodona).
- *Fasti Triumphales*: cited by name for 272 and 266 BC; standard edition A. Degrassi, *Inscriptiones Italiae* XIII.1 (Rome, 1947) (not verified as the report's source).

## Not resolved
- "Beloch ... date the poem to about 190" and "like Gruen": no work given for Beloch or Gruen.
- "The excavations at Morgantina showed the denarius began about 211": no publication cited.
- The painted plate from Capena with an elephant and calf (Villa Giulia), the silver stater of Locri with Roma and Pistis, and the didrachm with Achilles and Thetis: described, with no catalogue reference.
