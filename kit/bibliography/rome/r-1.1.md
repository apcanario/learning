# Rome 1.1: The peoples of Iron Age Italy: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Cato the Elder, *Origines*, the fragment on the dedication of the grove at Aricia by Egerius Baebius (preserved in Priscian); the "Latin dictator" and the eight peoples; Cato's date for the Etruscan foundation of Capua (reported by Velleius); Cato on the Ligurians "illiterate and liars" (quoted by Servius on *Aeneid* 11, no line given in the report). The standard collection of the fragments is T. J. Cornell (ed.), *The Fragments of the Roman Historians* (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2013); the report does not say which edition it used.
- Cato the Elder, *On Agriculture* 141 (lustration prayer).
- Cicero, *Pro Balbo* 53 (the Cassian treaty on a bronze column behind the Rostra).
- Festus, quoting the antiquarian Cincius, on the Latin meetings at the grove of Ferentina down to 340 BC. The report gives no locator. Festus survives in W. M. Lindsay's Teubner text (1913).
- Dionysius of Halicarnassus, *Roman Antiquities* 1.74.1 (Timaeus on Carthage and Rome, 814/813); 4.49 (Tarquinius Superbus and the Latin festival, forty-seven peoples); 6.95 (the Cassian treaty of 493). Loeb (Cary).
- Herodotus 7.170 (Messapians and Tarentines).
- Homer, *Iliad* 11.632–637 (compared with the Nestor cup).
- Livy 1.1 (Antenor and the Veneti); 1.7.8 (Evander and letters); 1.13.5 (Quirites from Cures); 1.13.8 (Titienses); 1.18.4 (Numa and Sabine discipline); 2.16.4–5 (Attus Clausus, 504); 4.37 (Capua, 423); 4.44 (Cumae, 421).
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* 3.69 (the *populi Albenses*).
- Plutarch, *Romulus* 14.1 (Fabius Pictor on the seizure of the Sabine women).
- Polybius 3.22 (first treaty with Carthage, dated 508/7).
- Strabo 5.4.2 (Picentes and the woodpecker); 5.4.9 (Pithekoussai); 5.4.12 (Samnites and Hirpini).
- Tacitus, *Annals* 11.14 (Demaratus, Evander and the alphabet).
- Thucydides 6.2.6 (Phoenicians round Sicily); 6.3 (Naxos 734, Syracuse 733).
- Varro, *On the Latin Language* 5.74 (Tatius' altars "smell of the Sabine tongue"); 5.144 (Lavinium and "our Penates").
- Velleius Paterculus 1.7.2–4 (Capua).

## Corpora and inscriptions
- *Tabulae Iguvinae* (the Iguvine Tables), tablets VIa–VIb cited, no line numbers. Standard edition: J. W. Poultney, *The Bronze Tables of Iguvium* (Baltimore: American Philological Association, 1959) (not verified; the report cites no edition).
- Inscriptions named without a corpus number: the Nestor cup (Pithekoussai, tomb 168); the Lavinium plaque to Castor and Pollux (*Castorei Podlouqueique qurois*); the Praeneste fibula (*Manios med fhefhaked Numasioi*); the Marsiliana d'Albegna alphabet tablet; the Osteria dell'Osa graffito (*eulin*); the Nora stone; the Poggio Sommavilla flask. The report gives no editions or numbers for any of them.

## Not resolved
- "Edilberto Formigli (2011)" and "Daniela Ferro and Edilberto Formigli (2011)" on the Praeneste fibula: the report gives no title. A search turned up only a Formigli entry, "La Fibula Prenestina", *Bullettino di Paletnologia Italiana* 99 (2011–14), cited in Wikipedia; whether that is the work meant could not be confirmed.
- "Gert-Jan Burgers and Jan Paul Crielaard" on L'Amastuola: no year or title in the report. Candidates are their "The Migrant's Identity: 'Greeks' and 'Natives' at L'Amastuola, Southern Italy", in L. Donnellan, V. Nizzo and G.-J. Burgers (eds), *Conceptualising Early Colonisation* (Turnhout: Brepols, 2016) 225–238, and an earlier L'Amastuola article of 2007 (not confirmed).
- "Andrea Cardarelli", "Luigi Pigorini", "Renato Peroni", "Giovanni Colonna", "Albert Nijboer and Johannes van der Plicht", "Giacomo Devoto", "Helmut Rix", "Eugenio La Rocca", "Ferdinando Castagnoli", "Paolo Sommella", "Georges Dumézil" (the Sabine war as myth): named without any work, year or title.
- "Giacomo Boni", "Giovanni Gozzadini", "Alessandro Visconti" (1817): excavators or publishers named with a date only; no publication is cited.
- "Timaeus" (814/813) and "Fabius Pictor": known only through Dionysius and Plutarch as cited above.
