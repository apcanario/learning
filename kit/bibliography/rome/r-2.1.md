# Rome 2.1: What Roman religion was: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Augustine, *City of God* 4.8; 4.27; 4.31; 6.4; 6.5; 6.6–8 (Varro's Antiquities survives chiefly through these books).
- Cato the Elder, *On Agriculture* 132; 139; 141.
- Cicero, *De domo sua* 1.
- Cicero, *De haruspicum responso* 23.
- Cicero, *On Divination* 2.51; 2.70; 2.74; 2.148–149.
- Cicero, *On the Laws* 2.31.
- Cicero, *On the Nature of the Gods* 1.4; 1.116; 2.8; 2.10–11; 2.72; 3.5–6; 3.95.
- Dionysius of Halicarnassus, *Roman Antiquities* 2.18–19.
- Festus, *On the Meaning of Words* (on *sacra publica*; under *ordo sacerdotum*; no book or page cited). Standard edition: W. M. Lindsay (Teubner, 1913).
- Gaius, *Institutes* 2.4.
- Lactantius, *Divine Institutes* 4.28.
- Livy, *History of Rome* 1.31.4; 7.2.2; 7.3; 21.62; 21.63; 22.9.7; 22.10; 33.44; 34.44; 43.13 (incl. 43.13.1–2).
- Julius Obsequens, *Book of Prodigies* (cited generally; survives from 190 BC into Augustus' reign).
- Ovid, *Fasti* 6.295–298.
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* 28.11.
- Plutarch, *Coriolanus* 25.3; *Marcellus* 8; *Numa* 8.
- Polybius, *Histories* 6.56.6–12.
- Servius, commentary on Virgil, *Aeneid* 1.292; on *Georgics* 1.21 (the latter citing Fabius Pictor).
- Varro, *On the Latin Language* 7.85.
- Varro, *Antiquities of Human and Divine Things*: lost; known only through Augustine (above).
