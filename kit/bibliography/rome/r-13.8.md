# Rome 13.8: The Meditations and the afterlife of Roman Stoicism: sources

Written 1 November 2026 by the report's writer (Opus 5.5). Passages are those the report cites; section numbers of the *Meditations* follow Gataker's divisions as printed in modern editions. "(not verified)" marks a reference given from memory that could not be checked in this session.

## Ancient and medieval sources
- Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* (*To Himself*): 1.1–4; 1.5; 1.6; 1.7; 1.8; 1.9; 1.10; 1.11; 1.12; 1.14; 1.15; 1.16; 1.17; 2.1; 2.2; 2.17; 3.1; 4.3; 4.4; 4.32; 4.33; 4.41; 4.43; 5.1; 6.13; 6.30; 6.44; 8.9; 8.48; 9.3; 9.6; 9.30; 11.33–38; 12.36. Edition consulted for the place note at the end of book 2: C. R. Haines, Loeb Classical Library (1916). Other passages from memory of the standard text (not verified passage by passage).
- Epictetus, *Discourses* (Arrian's record): 1.1.21–25 (write down and rehearse daily); 3.2.1–2 (the three fields of training).
- *Historia Augusta*, *Avidius Cassius* 3 (Marcus' three days of lectures on his precepts); *Marcus* 27.7 (Plato's saying on philosophers and rulers) (chapter numbers not verified).
- Themistius, *Orations* 6 (*On Brotherly Love*, to Valens, 364: no need of Marcus' exhortations).
- Seneca the Younger, *On Leisure* 4.1 (the two commonwealths; pointer to Rome module 13.2).
- Arethas of Caesarea, letter to Demetrius, archbishop of Heraclea (before 907), on his old copy of Marcus' book; known to me through the modern accounts below.
- *Suda* (late tenth century), entry on Marcus: a guide to his own life in twelve books; about thirty quotations.
- John Tzetzes (c. 1150), quotations of books 4 and 5.
- Nicephorus Callistus, *Ecclesiastical History* (fourteenth century): the book composed for the instruction of Marcus' son.
