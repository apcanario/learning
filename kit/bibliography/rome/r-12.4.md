# Bibliography: Rome 12.4, Dido

## Ancient sources
- Virgil, *Aeneid* (Latin text after R. A. B. Mynors, Oxford Classical Text, 1969; translations the writer's own): 1.265–266, 1.340–368, 1.482; 2.557–558; book 4 throughout, especially 4.1–2, 4.23–29, 4.31–55, 4.68–73, 4.86–89, 4.90–128, 4.136–150, 4.165–172, 4.173–194, 4.198, 4.206–218, 4.223–237, 4.259–278, 4.279–303, 4.305–387, 4.393–395, 4.416–449, 4.450–498, 4.534–552, 4.569–570, 4.596–602, 4.607–629, 4.644–662, 4.669–671, 4.675–685, 4.693–705; 5.1–7; 6.450–476; 8.709; 11.72–75; 12.819–840.
- Timaeus of Tauromenium, FGrHist 566 F 82 (via module 4.1).
- Pompeius Trogus, in Justin's epitome 18.4–6 (via module 4.1).
- Servius, commentary on the *Aeneid*, on 4.682 (Varro on Anna; via module 4.1).
- Macrobius, *Saturnalia* 5.17.5–6 (Dido's story known to be false); 5.19 (Cornutus and the lock; Euripides' *Alcestis*).
- Euripides, *Alcestis* (Death and the lock, as Macrobius cites it).
- Homer, *Iliad* 16.33–35 (Patroclus to Achilles); *Odyssey* 11 (Ajax's silence).
- Catullus 64.154–157 (Ariadne to Theseus); 66.39 (the lock of Berenice, via module 12.1).
- Horace, *Epodes* 9 (via module 5.5); *Odes* 1.37.9–10.
- Ovid, *Heroides* 7, especially 7.195–196; *Metamorphoses* 14.78–81; *Fasti* 3.523–696; *Tristia* 2.533–536.
- Dionysius of Halicarnassus, *Roman Antiquities* 1.64.4–5 (Aeneas by the Numicus; via module 1.3).
- Appian, *Punica* 136 (the refounding of Carthage).
- Augustine, *Confessions* 1.13.20–22, 5.8.15 (Latin text as in the CSEL edition, read at bkv.unifr.ch); *City of God* 9.4.
- Dante, *Inferno* 5.61–63, 5.85; *Monarchia* 2.3.
- Christopher Marlowe and Thomas Nashe, *The Tragedy of Dido, Queen of Carthage* (London, 1594).
- Nahum Tate, libretto of Henry Purcell's *Dido and Aeneas* (printed libretto for the Chelsea performance, usually dated 1689); Tate, *Brutus of Alba* (1678).
- Hector Berlioz, *Les Troyens* (libretto 1856, score 1858; *Les Troyens à Carthage*, Théâtre-Lyrique, Paris, 1863; first complete staging Karlsruhe, 1890).
