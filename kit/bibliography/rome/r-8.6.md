# Rome 8.6: The Rome that did not fall: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient and medieval sources
- Anna Komnene, *Alexiad* book 10.
- *Annals of Lorsch* (named for the claim that the imperial name had lapsed among the Greeks, no passage).
- Attaleiates, Michael (named as an eyewitness of Manzikert; the work is not named in the report, no passage).
- *Chronicon Paschale* (named, no passage).
- *Council of Constantinople (381)*, canon 3.
- Einhard, *Life of Charlemagne* 28 (the report calls it *Life of Charles*).
- George of Pisidia, court poems on Heraclius (named, no passage).
- George of Trebizond, letter to Mehmed II (named, no passage; paraphrased from memory, per the writer's note).
- John Lydus, *On the Magistracies* (cited by work, on the retreat of Latin).
- Kekaumenos (named for the figure of 14,000 blinded prisoners, no passage).
- Kritoboulos of Imbros, history of the reign of Mehmed II (named, no passage; title not given in the report).
- *Liber Pontificalis* (named for the coronation ceremony of 800, no passage).
- Liudprand of Cremona, *Embassy* (to Constantinople, 968), cited by work.
- Niketas Choniates (named as the author of the lament on 1204; the work is not named in the report, no passage).
- Paul the Silentiary, verse description of Hagia Sophia (named, no passage).
- Philotheus of Pskov, letter to Vasily III (named, no passage).
- Procopius of Caesarea, *Wars* 1.24.33–37; 1.24.54; 2.22–23. *Buildings* 1.1. *Secret History* 19.7. Standard edition: Loeb (Dewing, Dewing and Downey). The writer's note says 1.24.33–37 and 1.24.54 are section numbers from memory, and that Wars 2.22–23 and Buildings 1.1 are cited by chapter only.
- Psellos, Michael (named as a Doukas partisan blaming Romanos IV; the work is not named in the report, no passage).
- *Royal Frankish Annals* (named for the acclamation of 800, no passage).
- Skylitzes, John (named for the blinding of 15,000 prisoners after Kleidion in 1014; the work is not named in the report, no passage).
- Theophanes the Confessor, *Chronicle* (named for the anointing of Charles, Greek fire and Heraclius's wars, cited without annus mundi entries, per the writer's note).
