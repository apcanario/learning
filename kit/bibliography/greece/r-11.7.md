# Greece 11.7: The sources for Alexander: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

Verification note: web search returned titles and venues only. Items marked "(partly verified)" had their title and venue confirmed by a search hit; everything else completed from memory is marked "(not verified)".

## Ancient sources
- Alexander Romance (Pseudo-Callisthenes) 3.30–33 (the embedded *Liber de Morte*). Recensions named: alpha; Julius Valerius (early 4th century); Leo of Naples, *Historia de Preliis* (10th century); the Syriac *Alexander Legend* (c. 630); Armenian and Ge'ez versions.
- Arrian, *Anabasis* preface; 1.8.1; 1.12; 2.3.7; 3.18.11–12; 4.7–14; 4.10–14; 4.14.3; 6.2.3; 7.25–26; 7.28–30; 7.29.4. Standard edition: Loeb (Brunt).
- Arrian, *Indica* 18–42 (the voyage of Nearchus). Standard edition: Loeb (Brunt, vol. 2).
- Athenaeus, *Deipnosophistae* 10.434b (the Ephemerides); 13.576d–e (Cleitarchus on Thaïs and Persepolis).
- Curtius Rufus, *Historiae Alexandri Magni* 4.6.25–29; 5.7; 9.5.21; 10.9.1–6. Standard edition: Loeb (Rolfe).
- Diodorus Siculus, *Library of History* 17.12; 17.72; 18.8. Standard edition: Loeb.
- Diogenes Laertius 6.84.
- Justin, *Epitome of Pompeius Trogus* books 11–12.
- Lucian, *How to Write History* 12; *Macrobii* 22.
- *Liber de Morte Alexandri* and the Metz Epitome (anonymous, late antique Latin; lost Metz manuscript, destroyed in 1944).
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* 6.61; 35.110.
- Plutarch, *Alexander* 1.2; 38; 46; 53–55; 55.9; 76–77. Standard edition: Loeb (Perrin).
- Plutarch, *On the Fortune or Virtue of Alexander* (*Moralia* 326d–345b).
- Quintilian, *Institutio Oratoria* 10.1.74.
- Strabo, *Geography* 14.1.22.
- Quran 18:83–98; Ferdowsi, *Shahnameh* (c. 1010); Nizami (12th century); Walter of Châtillon, *Alexandreis* (c. 1180); the Old French *Roman d'Alexandre*. All cited without passages as stages of the Romance tradition.
- The lost historians (Callisthenes, Ptolemy, Aristobulus, Nearchus, Onesicritus, Chares, the Ephemerides, Cleitarchus, Marsyas, the bematists) are cited through the surviving authors. Collected in Jacoby, FGrH II B, beginning at no. 117.

## Corpora and inscriptions
- FGrH: Jacoby, F., *Die Fragmente der griechischen Historiker*, II B, from no. 117 (Berlin: Weidmann, 1929).
- P. Oxy. 4808, *The Oxyrhynchus Papyri* vol. 71 (London: Egypt Exploration Society, 2007) (the report says "published 2007"; volume number from memory, not verified).
- Babylonian astronomical diaries for 331 and 323 BC (the report names no edition). Standard edition: A. J. Sachs and H. Hunger, *Astronomical Diaries and Related Texts from Babylonia*, vol. 1 (Vienna: Österreichische Akademie der Wissenschaften, 1988) (not verified).
- The Priene dedication of the temple of Athena Polias (now in the British Museum): usually *Inschriften von Priene* 156 (not verified; the report gives no number).

## Not resolved
- "Hammond ... defended a genuine court journal" (Ephemerides): the report names Samuel and Hammond with no year; Hammond's own paper on the Ephemerides could not be identified.
- "The letter to Chios (c. 332)", "Mytilene's reconciliation decree" and "the Tegea inscription": the report gives no corpus numbers. They are normally found in Tod, *Greek Historical Inscriptions* II, and Rhodes and Osborne, *Greek Historical Inscriptions 404–323 BC*; I did not confirm the numbers.
- "Tarn (1948) was partisan": see above; no page cited.
