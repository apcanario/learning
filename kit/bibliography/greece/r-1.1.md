# Greece 1.1: Minoan Crete: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Apollodorus (Pseudo-), *Library* 1.1.6 (Dicte); 1.9.26 (Talos); 3.1.1 (Europa); 3.1.3–4 (Pasiphae and the Minotaur). Apollodorus, *Epitome* 1.12–15 (Daedalus and Icarus).
- Apollonius of Rhodes, *Argonautica* 4.1638–88 (Talos).
- Callimachus, *Hymn to Zeus* 8–9.
- Diodorus Siculus 4.76–9 (Daedalus); 5.70 (Ida and the Kouretes).
- Euripides, *Cretans* (lost; cited for the Minotaur's name Asterios; no fragment number given).
- Herodotus, *Histories* 3.122; 7.170–1 (Minos' death at Camicus).
- Hesiod, *Theogony* 477–84 (birth of Zeus at Lyktos).
- Homer, *Iliad* 13.450–3 (Idomeneus' descent from Minos); 14.321–2 (Europa daughter of Phoenix); 18.590–2 (Daedalus' dancing-floor).
- Homer, *Odyssey* 11.321–5 (Ariadne killed on Dia); 11.568–71 (Minos as judge of the dead); 19.172–7 (peoples of Crete, including Eteocretans); 19.178–9 (Minos reigning in nine-year periods); 19.188 (cave of Eileithyia at Amnisos).
- Moschus, *Europa* (no lines given).
- Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 2.833–75 (Europa); 8.183–235 (Daedalus and Icarus).
- Philochorus (rationalised version of the Minotaur, as reported by Plutarch); FGrH 328 (standard number, not given in the report).
- Plato, *Laws* book 1 (opening, Minos as lawgiver); no Stephanus numbers given.
- Plutarch, *Theseus* 15–19.
- Thucydides, *Histories* 1.4 (Minos' navy).

## Modern works
- Jan Driessen and Colin F. Macdonald, *The Troubled Island: Minoan Crete Before and After the Santorini Eruption*, Aegaeum 17 (Liège and Austin: Université de Liège and University of Texas at Austin, 1997). Confirmed by catalogue records.
- Arthur Evans, *The Palace of Minos at Knossos*, 4 vols (London: Macmillan, 1921–35).
- Walter L. Friedrich and others, "Santorini Eruption Radiocarbon Dated to 1627–1600 B.C.", *Science* 312 (2006) 548 (the report gives "Friedrich et al. 2006" for the olive branch; identified from context).
- Cathy Gere, *Knossos and the Prophets of Modernism* (Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 2009).
- Yannis Hamilakis (ed.), *Labyrinth Revisited: Rethinking 'Minoan' Archaeology* (Oxford: Oxbow Books, 2002). Confirmed by catalogue records.
- J. Alexander MacGillivray, *Minotaur: Sir Arthur Evans and the Archaeology of the Minoan Myth* (New York: Hill and Wang, 2000).
- Martin P. Nilsson, *The Mycenaean Origin of Greek Mythology* (Berkeley: University of California Press, 1932).
- Charlotte L. Pearson and others, "Annual radiocarbon record indicates 16th century BCE date for the Thera eruption", *Science Advances* 4 (2018) eaar8241 (the report gives "Pearson et al." and "Work since 2018"; identified from context).
- Colin Renfrew, *The Emergence of Civilisation: The Cyclades and the Aegean in the Third Millennium BC* (London: Methuen, 1972).

## Corpora and inscriptions
- Linear B tablets from Knossos, cited by series and number as in Ventris and Chadwick, *Documents in Mycenaean Greek* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1956; 2nd edn 1973), and the later Knossos corpus edition: KN Gg 702 (the "Lady of the Labyrinth", da-pu2-ri-to-jo po-ti-ni-ja); KN Gg 705 (Eleuthia at Amnisos); KN Fp 1 (Dictaean Zeus). The report does not say which edition it takes the readings from.
- Linear A: the report cites the corpus in general (about 1,400 documents; the Hagia Triada archive; libation-table formula (j)a-sa-sa-ra-me) without naming an edition.

## Not resolved

- "Maximilian Mayer ... 1892" (labyrinth from labrys): no title given.
- Named scholars with a claim but no work or year: Jan Driessen, Ilse Schoep (palace as "court compound"; Driessen's own works other than the 1997 volume); Alan Peatfield (survey of peak sanctuaries); Nanno Marinatos (theology of the gold rings); Yannis and Efi Sakellarakis (Anemospilia); Peter Warren ("Room of the Children's Bones"); Barry Molloy (violence in Minoan art); Spyridon Marinatos (1939 thesis on Thera and Akrotiri excavation from 1967); Leonard Palmer and John Boardman (Knossos tablet date; Palmer also for Linear A as Luwian); John Younger (Linear A values); Cyrus Gordon, Jan Best, Gareth Owens (Linear A language proposals); Halbherr and Pernier (Phaistos); Nikolaos Platon (Zakros, palatial scheme); Christian Doll, Piet de Jong and the Gilliérons (Evans's restorations).
