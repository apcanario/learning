# Greece 3.1: What a hero is, and Perseus: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Apollodorus (Pseudo-), *Library* 2.2.1 (Proetus and Tiryns); 2.4.1–5 (Perseus, the Perseids). Loeb (Frazer) is the standard edition.
- Herodotus, *Histories* 1.67–68 (bones of Orestes); 2.44 (two cults of Heracles); 2.91; 5.67 (Adrastus and Melanippus); 6.53–54; 7.61; 7.150.
- Hesiod, *Theogony* 270–283 (Gorgons, Pegasus); 276–277; 278–279; 280–283. Hesiod, *Works and Days* 156–173 (the heroes, fourth race). Hesiod (Pseudo-), *Shield of Heracles* 216–237 (Perseus in flight).
- Homer, *Iliad* 12.23 (hemitheon genos andron); 14.319–320 (Danae and Perseus); 19.95–133 (Hera, Eurystheus and the oath).
- Hyginus, *Fabulae* 63–64; *Astronomica* 2.9–12.
- Josephus, *Jewish War* 3.420 (rock of Andromeda at Joppa).
- Lucian, *Dialogues of the Sea-Gods* 14.
- Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 4.604–5.249, with 4.608–614 (Acrisius denies Jupiter's paternity); 4.663–5.235 (Andromeda, Phineus); 4.782–786; 4.794–803 (Medusa and Neptune in Minerva's temple); 5.236–249 (variant ending: Proetus and Polydectes petrified).
- Pausanias 2.16.2, 2.16.3 (Mycenae named from the mykes); 2.16.2–6; 2.18.1 (Perseus' cult on Seriphos; Dictys and Clymene at Athens); 4.35.9 (Andromeda at Joppa); 5.13.4–6 (shoulder-blade of Pelops); 6.6.7–11 (hero of Temesa).
- Pherecydes of Athens, fragments quoted in the scholia to Apollonius of Rhodes, *Argonautica* 4.1091 and 4.1515: FGrH 3 F 10–12 (Perseus narrative; F 10, F 11 cited individually).
- Pindar, *Nemean* 3.22 (heros theos); 10.4. Pindar, *Pythian* 5.93–95 (Battus of Cyrene); 10.29–48; 10.47–48; 12.6–27; 12.12; 12.17–18.
- Plutarch, *Theseus* 36 (return of Theseus' bones from Skyros).
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* 5.69 (Andromeda at Joppa).
- Simonides, fr. 543 PMG (Danae in the chest). Standard edition: D. L. Page, *Poetae Melici Graeci*.
- Sophocles, *Antigone* 944–950 (chorus compares Antigone to Danae).
- Strabo 10.5.10 (Seriphos' stones); 16.2.28 (Andromeda at Joppa).
- Aeschylus, *Phorcides*; Sophocles, *Andromeda*; Euripides, *Andromeda* (412 BC): lost tragedies named without fragment numbers.

## Modern works
- Carla M. Antonaccio, *An Archaeology of Ancestors: Tomb Cult and Hero Cult in Early Greece* (Lanham, MD: Rowman & Littlefield, 1995). (not verified)
- Walter Burkert, *Structure and History in Greek Mythology and Ritual* (Berkeley: University of California Press, 1979). Confirmed by catalogue records.
- Joseph Campbell, *The Hero with a Thousand Faces* (New York: Pantheon Books, 1949). (not verified; the report gives "Campbell's monomyth (1949)")
- J. N. Coldstream, "Hero-Cults in the Age of Homer", *Journal of Hellenic Studies* 96 (1976) 8–17 (the report names Coldstream without title or year; identified from context; the article's existence and journal are confirmed, the page range is not verified).
- Gunnel Ekroth, *The Sacrificial Rituals of Greek Hero-Cults in the Archaic to the Early Hellenistic Period*, Kernos Supplement 12 (Liège: Centre International d'Étude de la Religion Grecque Antique, 2002). The report abbreviates the title. Confirmed by catalogue records.
- Johann Georg von Hahn, *Sagwissenschaftliche Studien* (Jena: Mauke, 1876). (not verified; the report gives only "Hahn's 'exposure-and-return formula' (1876)", so the work is identified from context)
- Gregory Nagy, *The Best of the Achaeans: Concepts of the Hero in Archaic Greek Poetry* (Baltimore: Johns Hopkins University Press, 1979). The title is as given in the report's short form plus subtitle. (not verified for publisher details)
- Otto Rank, *Der Mythus von der Geburt des Helden* (Leipzig and Vienna: Deuticke, 1909). The report gives only "Otto Rank (1909)"; identified from context. English translation, *The Myth of the Birth of the Hero* (1914). (not verified for publisher details)
- Lord Raglan (FitzRoy Richard Somerset), *The Hero: A Study in Tradition, Myth and Drama* (London: Methuen, 1936). Confirmed by catalogue records.
- Erwin Rohde, *Psyche: Seelencult und Unsterblichkeitsglaube der Griechen* (Freiburg: Mohr, 1890–94). The report gives "Psyche (1894)"; the date is that of the completed work. (not verified)

## Corpora and inscriptions

- PMG: *Poetae Melici Graeci*, ed. D. L. Page; Simonides fr. 543.
