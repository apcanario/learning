# Greece 6.1: The Persian Wars: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Aeschylus, *Persians* (produced 472; the report cites the play as a whole, with no line numbers).
- Aristotle, *Constitution of the Athenians* 22 (ostracisms, 487 to 482); 22.7 (the hundred talents lent for triremes).
- Ctesias, *Persica*: named as a Greek doctor at the Persian court, surviving only in epitome; no locator given.
- Diodorus Siculus 11.9–10 (Thermopylae, the night attack); 11.29 (the Oath of Plataea). Book 11 is described as depending on Ephorus.
- Herodotus, *Histories*: 1.1 (Persian logioi); 1.84–86 (fall of Sardis); 1.143 (Ionians); 1.188–191 (fall of Babylon); 3.70–88 (the seven conspirators); 3.80–82 (the constitutional debate); 3.89–97 (satrapies and tribute); Book 4 (the Scythian expedition); 5.28; 5.30–35 (Aristagoras and Naxos); 5.49–51; 5.78; 5.97; 5.100–102 (the burning of Sardis); 5.105 ("Master, remember the Athenians"); 6.7–17 (Lade); 6.18–20 (Miletus); 6.43–45 (Mardonius' expedition); 6.101; 6.102; 6.105–106 (Philippides and the Spartans); 6.109; 6.111–117 (Marathon); 6.120; 6.121–131 (the Alcmaeonids); 6.132–136 (Miltiades); 7.8–18; 7.10; 7.22–24 (the Athos canal); 7.33–36 (the Hellespont bridges); 7.60; 7.104; 7.139; 7.141; 7.141–143 (the wooden wall); 7.144 (the fleet); 7.152; 7.173; 7.184–186; 7.202–205; 7.213–218; 7.220; 7.223; 7.226; 7.228; 7.229–231; 8.1–18 (Artemisium); 8.4–5; 8.41; 8.48; 8.51–53; 8.62; 8.75; 8.79; 8.87–88; 8.94; 8.109; 8.113–120; 8.143–144; 8.144 (Hellenic identity); 9.28–30; 9.63; 9.78–79; 9.81 (the Serpent Column); 9.100–101 (Mycale); 9.114–121 (Sestos).
- Lucian, *On a Slip of the Tongue* (*Pro lapsu inter salutandum*) 3 (the runner Philippides).
- Lycurgus, *Against Leocrates* 80–81 (Oath of Plataea).
- Pausanias 10.13.9 (the Phocians melt the Delphic tripod).
- Plutarch, *Aristides* 7 (the illiterate voter); 19. Plutarch, *On the Glory of Athens* 347c (the runner from Marathon). Plutarch, *On the Malice of Herodotus*: named, no locator.
- Simonides, Plataea elegy: see Corpora (P.Oxy. 3965).
- Thucydides 1.20–22 (method); 1.73–75 (the Athenian envoys at Sparta); 1.93 (Themistocles and the Piraeus); 1.95–96 (the Delian League); 1.132 (Pausanias and the Serpent Column).
- Theopompus: named as having called the Oath of Plataea a forgery; no locator (see Not resolved).

## Corpora and inscriptions
- Behistun (Bisitun) inscription of Darius I: cited as "his own Behistun inscription"; no section numbers given. (Standard edition: R. G. Kent, *Old Persian*, 2nd edn, New Haven 1953, DB.)
- Decree of Themistocles, the inscription found at Troezen in 1959: cited without a publication reference.
- Oath of Plataea: Lycurgus, *Against Leocrates* 80–81; Diodorus 11.29; the fourth-century stele from Acharnae (no publication reference given).
- P.Oxy. 3965: Simonides' Plataea elegy, published 1992 (*The Oxyrhynchus Papyri*, vol. 59, ed. P. J. Parsons). The volume number and editor are from memory. (not verified)
- Serpent Column, Constantinople (Istanbul): the names of the thirty-one cities, cited from the monument itself.
