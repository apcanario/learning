# Greece 6.7: Alexander: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Aristotle, *Politics* 1311b (Philip's assassin).
- Arrian, *Anabasis*: 1 pref. (method; Ptolemy and Aristobulus); 1.7; 1.8; 1.9; 1.11; 1.13–16; 1.15.8; 2.3; 2.6–11; 2.14; 2.14.5; 2.18–24; 3.3–4; 3.7.6; 3.11–15; 3.16; 3.18, with 3.18.11–12; 3.21; 3.26, with 3.26.3–4; 3.29–30; 4.5–6; 4.8–9; 4.10–12; 4.13–14; 4.14.3; 4.17; 4.19; 5.8–19; 5.25–29; 6.9–11, with 6.11.8; 6.22–26; 7.4; 7.6; 7.9–10; 7.11.9; 7.14; 7.14.7; 7.16–17; 7.23.6; 7.25–26; 7.27. Arrian's debt to the lost Ptolemy and Aristobulus is stated in the report.
- Athenaeus 12.538b–539a (Chares on the Susa marriages); 13.557b–e (Philip's marriages, quoting Satyrus).
- Curtius Rufus, *History of Alexander* 3.8–12; 4.4.17; 4.6.29; 5.7; 5.8–13; 6.7–11; 8.1–2; 8.3; 8.4; 8.6–8; 8.8.21; 8.13–14; 9.2–3; 10.5; 10.6–10; 10.9.3–6 (the allusion used to date Curtius). Books 1 to 2 are lost, as the report notes.

- Lucian, *Macrobii* 22 (Aristobulus' age).
- Pausanias 8.7.7.
- Plutarch, *Alexander* 2–3; 5; 7; 9; 10; 27; 33; 38; 45; 47; 48–49; 50–51; 54; 55; 60; 66; 72; 76; 77. Plutarch, *On the Fortune of Alexander* 329c–d.
- Strabo 1.4.9; 15.2.5; 17.1.43 (Callisthenes quoted).
- The lost contemporary historians (Callisthenes, Ptolemy, Aristobulus, Cleitarchus, Nearchus, Chares) and the *Ephemerides*, or Royal Diaries: named and used only through Arrian, Plutarch and others; no fragment numbers cited. The *Alexander Romance* is named without locator.

## Modern works
- Ernst Badian, "Alexander the Great and the Unity of Mankind", *Historia* 7.4 (1958) 425–444. Confirmed by the bibliography in the Wikipedia article on Badian.
- Ernst Badian, "The Loneliness of Power", *AUMLA: Journal of the Australasian Universities Language and Literature Association* (1962) (the full title of the journal paper is "Alexander the Great and the Loneliness of Power"), reprinted in *Studies in Greek and Roman History* (Oxford: Blackwell, 1964) 192–205. The journal and year, the longer title and the reprint pages come from a scholars' forum post and one search result; the AUMLA volume number and pages are not confirmed. (not verified)
- A. B. Bosworth, *Conquest and Empire: The Reign of Alexander the Great* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1988). (not verified for subtitle and publisher)
- A. B. Bosworth, *Alexander and the East: The Tragedy of Triumph* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1996). The report cites the title without subtitle. (not verified for subtitle and publisher)
- Johann Gustav Droysen, *Geschichte Alexanders des Grossen* (Hamburg: Perthes, 1833). The report writes only "J. G. Droysen ... made Alexander the founder of Hellenism"; identified from context. (not verified)
- Philip O. Spann, "Alexander at the Beas: Fox in a Lion's Skin", in Frances B. Titchener and Richard F. Moorton Jr. (eds), *The Eye Expanded: Life and the Arts in Greco-Roman Antiquity* (Berkeley: University of California Press, 1999). The report names "Spann" without title or year; the article title and the containing volume's title are confirmed by a forum post, but the editors, publisher, year and pages are from memory. (not verified)
- W. W. Tarn, *Alexander the Great*, 2 vols (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1948). (not verified for publisher)

## Corpora and inscriptions
- Babylonian astronomical diaries: cited for the lunar eclipse of 20 September 331 (fixing Gaugamela to 1 October) and for the king's death on 29 Aiaru (11 June 323). No tablet or edition is named in the report.
- P.Oxy. LXXI 4808 (published 2009): the papyrus calling Cleitarchus tutor to Ptolemy IV. *The Oxyrhynchus Papyri*, vol. 71.

## Not resolved

- "Badian saw a political signal to Greece ... Bosworth thought a sober decision could hide behind the party; Hammond followed Arrian" (the burning of Persepolis): no works named.
