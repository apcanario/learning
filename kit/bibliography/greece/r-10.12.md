# Greece 10.12: Troy after antiquity: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Cassius Dio, 62.18.
- Dares the Phrygian, *De excidio Troiae historia* (chs 12–13, portraits of the main figures; with the pseudo-letter of Cornelius Nepos to Sallust). Dictys of Crete, *Ephemeris belli Troiani* (Latin version by Lucius Septimius); the Greek original in *P.Tebt.* 2.268; used by John Malalas. Both Latin texts cited without edition.
- Dionysius of Halicarnassus, *Roman Antiquities* 1.72 (reporting Hellanicus).
- Ennius, *Annales*; Livius Andronicus, *Odusia*; Naevius, *Bellum Punicum*: fragments cited through Servius and Macrobius, without fragment numbers.
- Euripides, *Trojan Women* (415 BC), named for its date and subject; no passage cited.
- Homer, *Iliad* 5.9; 19.282–300; 20.302–308; 24.723–776. *Odyssey* 4; 22.
- *Ilias Latina* (first-century Latin abridgement of about 1,070 hexameters); no edition cited.
- Isidore of Seville, *Etymologies* 1.42.1.
- Juvenal, *Satires* 8.
- Lucretius, *De rerum natura* 1.117–126.
- Macrobius and Servius (on Naevius and Virgil; Servius on *Aeneid* 5.370 for Nero's *Troica*): cited generally.
- Petronius, *Satyricon* 89.
- Suetonius, *Divus Julius* 39.2; *Divus Augustus* 43.2; *Nero* 38.2.
- Tacitus, *Annals* 15.39.
- Virgil, *Aeneid* 1.242–249; 1.267–288; 2.49; 5.370 (via Servius); 5.545–603; 12; 12.819–828.
