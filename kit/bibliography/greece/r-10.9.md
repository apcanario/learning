# Greece 10.9: The gods at Troy: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Aeschylus, *Psychostasia* (lost), as reported in Plutarch, *How the Young Man Should Listen to Poems* 17a.
- Aristotle, *Poetics* 25, 1460b35–1461a1.
- *Cypria* fr. 1 West, as quoted in the scholia on *Iliad* 1.5; Proclus' summary of the *Cypria*; *Aethiopis* (the weighing of souls, as the likely source of the scales motif).
- Euripides, *Helen* 36–41; *Orestes* 1639–42; *Trojan Women* 987–90.
- Gorgias, *Encomium of Helen* (cited generally).
- Heraclitus the Allegorist, *Homeric Problems* 17–20, and the opening (on impiety); also the readings of the plague of Book 1 and of Hera hung between anvils.
- Hesiod, *Catalogue of Women* fr. 204.95–123 Merkelbach–West.
- Homer, *Iliad* 1.5; 1.33–52; 1.188–222; 1.207; 1.213–14; 1.357–427; 1.396–406; 1.495–530; 1.508–10; 1.524–30; 1.599; 2.1–4; 2.1–40; 2.155; 3.164; 3.173–80; 3.373–420; 4.31–67; 4.160–8; 5.1–8; 5.311–430; 5.446; 5.825–63; 5.832–4; 5.890; 6.344–8; 6.357–8; 7.452–3; 8.1–27; 8.68–74; 8.473–7; 9.410–16; 14.153–353; 14.201; 14.214–17; 14.246; 14.249–62; 14.267–9; 14.315–28; 14.317–27; 14.346–51; 15.18–24; 15.59–71; 16.384–92; 16.431–61; 16.443; 16.443–9; 16.459–61; 16.658; 16.780; 16.786–806; 17.198–208; 17.321; 17.441–7; 18.54; 18.369–617; 19.86–7; 19.87; 19.137–8; 19.223–4; 20.30; 20.33–40; 20.54–66; 20.67 (scholion); 20.293–308; 21.211–382; 21.385–513; 21.389–90; 21.403–8; 21.410–14; 21.441–60; 21.462–7; 21.470–513; 21.489–513; 21.497–501; 22.168–85; 22.208–13; 22.214–47; 24.23–30; 24.25–30; 24.33–54; 24.77–140; 24.334–467; 24.527–33.
- Homer, *Odyssey* 1.7; 1.32–43; 1.68–75; 8.266–366; 8.326; 8.579–80; 9.526–36; 13.125–87; 13.330–2; 17.485–7; 24.351–2.
- Metrodorus of Lampsacus (allegorical interpreter; cited without fragment).
