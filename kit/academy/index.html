How every course works

the constants
  1. One reader. Every course is written for the same person and read in order, on a phone, with the citations checked.
  2. Sources first. A unit names its sources and says where they disagree; a claim that cannot be sourced is marked unsourced or left out.
  3. One unit per chat, built in full. Each unit is written from a published brief or prompt, in its own chat, with its closing artefact: a self-test and carry-forward ideas for the history courses, a shipped deliverable for Brand + AI.
  4. Checked before filing. Every new unit is checked by a fresh reader that has not seen it written: each cited claim against its source, the deliverable against its brief. A unit written with live search is checked again six months on. The model that wrote each unit is recorded.
  5. An open syllabus. Every prompt and brief is published with its course, so any unit can be regenerated, extended or checked.

Why history and myth together

the method of the track
μῦθοςmythos

In Homer, simply what someone says with authority: a speech, a command, a tale. By the fifth century it had come to mean the story you cannot check.

λόγοςlogos

A word, a count, an account. By the same century it meant the argued explanation, the one that gives its reasons and invites you to test them.

The Greeks drew that line themselves. Herodotus calls his work a historiē, an inquiry, and still reports what the Persians and Egyptians told him. Thucydides warns that his history leaves out the mythōdes, the storylike, and will seem less pleasant for it (1.22). Plato expels the poets from his ideal city and then ends the Republic with a myth of his own.

The tidy story of a clean progress "from mythos to logos" (the title of Wilhelm Nestle's book of 1940) is now itself treated as a modern myth: the Greeks never stopped telling myths; their philosophers used them, their historians argued with them, and their cities were governed by them. So each course reads a culture's myths and its history together, asks who told each story and what they gained by it, and keeps the evidence in view. The aim is not to debunk the myths but to understand them as well as the history. Later courses point back to earlier ones where they touch: Rome to Greece's units 7 and 10, Persia to units 6 and 11, Egypt to units 1 and 7.

  • Every later module grades its claims by EG1's key.
  • EG2 fixes the site names and EG3 the chronology later modules follow.

Procedure

  1. One fresh writer per module, in code order, four modules to a chat. The chat stages a packet for the writer: the module's brief from this course's Briefs page, never reworded; the sourcing standard; the seed bibliography, courses/egypt/notes/bibliography.md; every module of this course already filed; and, where a brief points back, the Greek or Roman module named. The course has no source units and no case register.
  2. The writer opens every reference before citing it and keeps a source log with the address opened, what was read and the date; cites an Egyptian text by its standard designation and the translation opened; gives every date before 664 BC as approximate with its chronology; grades every claim by EG1's key; puts every disagreement in a dispute box with a named source or scholar on each side; and reports its contradictions with a filed module or a brief, and the needs the syllabus does not meet.
  3. A fresh checker that has not seen the writing runs seven passes: every Sources entry against its publisher's or Crossref's record, every cited claim against its source, the grades, time and number, names and positions, lane, pointers and tone, and the closing material and figures. Its report is filed as audits/egypt/fresh-check-<code>.md before any fix; findings are applied as exact replacements.
  4. Anything found wrong in a filed module goes into the next module's prompt and is fixed there first; anything that bears on a module not yet written is appended to courses/egypt/notes/syllabus-flags.md and printed in that module's prompt. A flag that would change a brief is proposed, never applied by the chat that raised it; a writer never rewords a brief.
  5. The module is filed at courses/egypt/<code>-<slug>.md with the front matter of method.md §7; this data file is updated; Academy is rebuilt, verified and published, the artifact and then GitHub Pages, before the next module begins.
  6. A read by the Project's owner sits on a bundle boundary (after bundle 1 and after bundle 15); starting the next writing chat is his go. There is no gate row.

Models

  • Syllabus: Fable 5.2, as the Project sets for course design.
  • Writing: Opus 5.5 for every module. The deliverable is written in the same context as its module.
  • Fresh check: Opus 5.5, in a separate context.
  • Not Sonnet or Haiku for modules or checks.

Review rule

Every new module gets its fresh check before filing: every reference against its record, every cited claim against its source, the deliverable against the brief, every figure against the text. Modules with the search flag on (EG18, EG46, EG55, EG56, DD-EG5) state facts that can change and are re-checked for staleness six months after writing.

Procedure

  1. Make a Project ("Courses — Brand & AI"). Add to its knowledge: this page, the research report, the five notes files. Project instructions, three lines: who the reader is; this page holds every brief; each unit is written in full, not described.
  2. One chat per unit. Copy the unit's brief from its Briefs page, paste it into a new chat in that Project. Never reuse a chat across units; leaked context is how a course starts repeating itself.
  3. Save the output to the vault as courses/brand-ai/<code>-<slug>.md (e.g. B1-mental-model.md), same shape as the history course. Hand it to the assistant: it commits, adds the unit to Learn, moves the counters, adds a changelog line.
  4. Units that need live facts (A8, B2, B8, DD-A1, DD-B3) are written with web search on; the briefs say so. Everything else without.

Models

  • Writing: Opus 5.5 for all remaining units. The quality gap shows most on long-form synthesis, which is this.
  • The shipped deliverable is written in the same chat as its unit, never separately, so it inherits the unit's reasoning.
  • Checking: a fresh context that has not seen the writing checks every cited claim against its source before the unit is filed (Fable 5.1 to A4, Opus 5.5 from A5).
  • Not Sonnet or Haiku for units or checks. They are the right tools for turning a finished unit into posts, a Shorts list or an email; that is B7's pipeline.

Review rule

Every unit gets a fresh check before filing: every cited claim against its source, the deliverable against the brief. Units written with live search (the Search column of courses-index.md lists them) are re-checked for staleness six months after writing.

Every new unit gets its fresh check before filing: every cited claim against its source, the deliverable against the brief. Pick

SP1 first: the scope line and the grade key every later unit uses.

  • Every later unit grades its claims by SP1's key.
  • It settles what a coach may and may not do.
  • It clears the popular claims before they recur.

Override if: none; the course is cumulative.

Procedure

  1. One chat per unit, in syllabus order. The chat stages a packet for a fresh writer: the unit's brief from this course's Briefs page, never reworded; the sourcing standard; the fictional case register, courses/sport-psychology/notes/case-facts.md; the seed bibliography, courses/sport-psychology/notes/bibliography.md; and every unit of this course already filed. The course has no source units.
  2. The writer opens every reference before citing it and keeps a source log, and reports three lists with the unit: facts it invented about the case athletes, contradictions with a filed unit or with the brief of an unwritten one, and needs the syllabus does not meet.
  3. A fresh checker that has not seen the writing checks every cited claim against its source, every Sources entry against its publisher's record, the grades, the samples, the scope and safety rules and the spread of sports, the deliverable against the brief and every figure against the text. Its report is filed as audits/sport-psychology/fresh-check-<code>.md before any fix; findings are applied as exact replacements. SP9, DD-SP1 and DD-SP2 also get a safety reader, a third context that reads only for scope and safety.
  4. Contradictions and needs are appended to courses/sport-psychology/notes/syllabus-flags.md; a brief is changed only by a syllabus review that the Project's owner confirms. The case register gains the facts a unit invented, with the unit's code.
  5. The unit is filed at courses/sport-psychology/<code>-<slug>.md with the front matter of method.md §7; this data file is updated; Academy is rebuilt, verified and published, the artifact and then GitHub Pages.

Models

  • Syllabus: Fable 5.2, as the Project sets for course design.
  • Writing: Opus 5.5 for every unit. The deliverable is written in the same context as its unit.
  • Fresh check and safety reader: Opus 5.5, each in a separate context.
  • Not Sonnet or Haiku for units or checks.

Review rule

Every new unit gets its fresh check before filing: every cited claim against its source, the deliverable against the brief, every figure against the text. Units written with live search (SP9, DD-SP1, DD-SP2) are re-checked for staleness six months after writing.

Override if: none; the course is cumulative.

Procedure

  1. One fresh writer per unit, in writing order, up to four units in one chat. The chat stages a packet for the writer: the unit's brief from this course's Briefs page, never reworded; the sourcing standard; the fictional case register, courses/athlete-career/notes/case-facts.md, which agrees with Sport Psychology for Coaches' register; the seed bibliography, courses/athlete-career/notes/bibliography.md; and every unit of this course already filed. The course has no source units.
  2. The writer opens every reference before citing it and keeps a source log with the address opened, what was read and the date, grades every claim by AC1's key, gives every rule as its issuing body's dated text followed by the question for the reader's own body, names the conflict between the reader's roles wherever the coach or the coach's business has a stake, and reports three lists with the unit: facts it invented about the case, contradictions with a filed unit or with the brief of an unwritten one, and needs the syllabus does not meet.
  3. A fresh checker that has not seen the writing runs seven passes: every Sources entry against its publisher's, Crossref's or its issuing body's record, every cited claim against its source, the grades, the rules and the Portugal sections, safety (minors, anti-doping, real people, the roles conflict, money figures), the case, scope and tone, and the deliverable and figures against the brief and the text. Its report is filed as audits/athlete-career/fresh-check-<code>.md before any fix; findings are applied as exact replacements.
  4. Anything found wrong in a filed unit goes into the next unit's prompt and is fixed there first; anything that bears on a unit not yet written is appended to courses/athlete-career/notes/syllabus-flags.md and printed in that unit's prompt. A flag that would change a brief is proposed and listed in the chat's reply; a writer never rewords a brief. The case register gains the facts a unit invented, with the unit's code.
  5. The unit is filed at courses/athlete-career/<code>-<slug>.md with the front matter of method.md §7; this data file is updated; Academy is rebuilt, verified and published, the artifact and then GitHub Pages, before the next unit begins.
  6. A read by the Project's owner sits on a bundle boundary; starting the next writing chat is his go. There is no gate row.

Models

  • Syllabus: Fable 5.2, as the Project sets for course design.
  • Writing: Opus 5.5 for every unit. The deliverable is written in the same context as its unit.
  • Fresh check: Opus 5.5, in a separate context.
  • Not Sonnet or Haiku for units or checks.

Review rule

Every new unit gets its fresh check before filing: every reference against its record, every cited claim against its source, the deliverable against the brief, every figure against the text. Units written with live search (every unit but AC6, AC7, AC10 and AC15) are re-checked for staleness six months after writing.
