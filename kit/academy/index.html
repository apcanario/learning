Why history and myth together

the method of the track
μῦθοςmythos

In Homer, simply what someone says with authority: a speech, a command, a tale. By the fifth century it had come to mean the story you cannot check.

λόγοςlogos

A word, a count, an account. By the same century it meant the argued explanation, the one that gives its reasons and invites you to test them.

The Greeks drew that line themselves. Herodotus calls his work a historiē, an inquiry, and still reports what the Persians and Egyptians told him. Thucydides warns that his history leaves out the mythōdes, the storylike, and will seem less pleasant for it (1.22). Plato expels the poets from his ideal city and then ends the Republic with a myth of his own.

The tidy story of a clean progress "from mythos to logos" (the title of Wilhelm Nestle's book of 1940) is now itself treated as a modern myth: the Greeks never stopped telling myths; their philosophers used them, their historians argued with them, and their cities were governed by them. So each course reads a culture's myths and its history together, asks who told each story and what they gained by it, and keeps the evidence in view. The aim is not to debunk the myths but to understand them as well as the history. Later courses point back to earlier ones where they touch: Rome to Greece's units 7 and 10, Persia to units 6 and 11, Egypt to units 1 and 7.

How every course works

the constants
  1. One reader. Every course is written for the same person and read in order, on a phone, with the citations checked.
  2. Sources first. A unit names its sources and says where they disagree; a claim that cannot be sourced is marked unsourced or left out.
  3. One unit per chat, built in full. Each unit is written from a published brief or prompt, in its own chat, with its closing artefact: a self-test and carry-forward ideas for the history courses, a shipped deliverable for Brand + AI.
  4. Checked before filing. Every new unit is checked by a fresh reader that has not seen it written: each cited claim against its source, the deliverable against its brief. A unit written with live search is checked again six months on. The model that wrote each unit is recorded.
  5. An open syllabus. Every prompt and brief is published with its course, so any unit can be regenerated, extended or checked.

Procedure

  1. Make a Project ("Courses — Brand & AI"). Add to its knowledge: this page, the research report, the five notes files. Project instructions, three lines: who the reader is; this page holds every brief; each unit is written in full, not described.
  2. One chat per unit. Copy the unit's brief from its Briefs page, paste it into a new chat in that Project. Never reuse a chat across units; leaked context is how a course starts repeating itself.
  3. Save the output to the vault as courses/brand-ai/<code>-<slug>.md (e.g. B1-mental-model.md), same shape as the history course. Hand it to the assistant: it commits, adds the unit to Learn, moves the counters, adds a changelog line.
  4. Units that need live facts (A8, B2, B8, DD-A1, DD-B3) are written with web search on; the briefs say so. Everything else without.

Models

  • Writing: Opus 5.5 for all remaining units. The quality gap shows most on long-form synthesis, which is this.
  • The shipped deliverable is written in the same chat as its unit, never separately, so it inherits the unit's reasoning.
  • Checking: a fresh context that has not seen the writing checks every cited claim against its source before the unit is filed (Fable 5.1 to A4, Opus 5.5 from A5).
  • Not Sonnet or Haiku for units or checks. They are the right tools for turning a finished unit into posts, a Shorts list or an email; that is B7's pipeline.

Review rule

Every unit gets a fresh check before filing: every cited claim against its source, the deliverable against the brief. Units written with live search (the Search column of courses-index.md lists them) are re-checked for staleness six months after writing.

Procedure

  1. Make a Project ("Courses — Brand & AI"). Add to its knowledge: this page, the research report, the five notes files. Project instructions, three lines: who the reader is; this page holds every brief; each unit is written in full, not described.
  2. One chat per unit. Copy the unit's brief from its Briefs page, paste it into a new chat in that Project. Never reuse a chat across units; leaked context is how a course starts repeating itself.
  3. Save the output to the vault as courses/brand-ai/<code>-<slug>.md (e.g. B1-mental-model.md), same shape as the history course. Hand it to the assistant: it commits, adds the unit to Learn, moves the counters, adds a changelog line.
  4. Units that need live facts (A8, B2, B8, DD-A1, DD-B3) are written with web search on; the briefs say so. Everything else without.

Models

  • Writing: Opus 5.5 for all remaining units. The quality gap shows most on long-form synthesis, which is this.
  • The shipped deliverable is written in the same chat as its unit, never separately, so it inherits the unit's reasoning.
  • Checking: a fresh context that has not seen the writing checks every cited claim against its source before the unit is filed (Fable 5.1 to A4, Opus 5.5 from A5).
  • Not Sonnet or Haiku for units or checks. They are the right tools for turning a finished unit into posts, a Shorts list or an email; that is B7's pipeline.

Review rule

Every unit gets a fresh check before filing: every cited claim against its source, the deliverable against the brief. Units written with live search (the Search column of courses-index.md lists them) are re-checked for staleness six months after writing.

Pick

IA1 first, once the kit can draw the course's figures.

  • IA2 fixes the notation; IA1 only needs the kinds to exist.
  • The four levels (IA5 to IA8) are the course; everything before them is what they rest on.
  • Deep dives last, DD-IA1 and DD-IA2 with search on.

Override if: the figure kinds take more than one chat to add; then IA1 and IA2 are written with the four existing kinds and their figures redrawn when the kinds land.

Procedure

  1. One chat per unit, in the Learning Project. Copy the unit's brief from this course's Briefs page and paste it into a new chat; never reuse a chat across units. An isolated agent context counts as a chat (method.md §4, P31).
  2. The unit is written in full with its deliverable and its figures, each figure a fenced viz block of a kind kit/viz.py can draw. Every Perqo fact the unit invents is labelled and kept consistent with the Perqo facts already filed.
  3. A fresh checker in a new context that has not seen the writing (Opus 5.5, skills/audit-units §3) checks every cited claim and every figure's content against the unit, and the deliverable against the brief; findings are applied as exact replacements and the report filed as audits/ux/fresh-check-<code>.md.
  4. File the unit at courses/ux/<code>-<slug>.md with the front matter of method.md §7 (skills/file-unit); update this data file, rebuild, verify, publish Academy.

Models

  • Writing: Opus 5.5 for every unit. The deliverable is written in the same chat as its unit.
  • Checking: Opus 5.5, a fresh context each time, before filing; the model that checked each filed unit is on How it was built.
  • Not Sonnet or Haiku for units or checks.

Review rule

Every new unit gets its fresh check before filing: every cited claim against its source, the deliverable against the brief. Figures count as claims: each figure is checked for whether its labels and numbers match the unit's sourced text. Units written with live search (DD-IA1, DD-IA2) are re-checked for staleness six months after writing.

Pick

SP1 first: the scope line and the grade key every later unit uses.

  • Every later unit grades its claims by SP1's key.
  • It settles what a coach may and may not do.
  • It clears the popular claims before they recur.

Override if: none; the course is cumulative.

Procedure

  1. One chat per unit, in syllabus order. The chat stages a packet for a fresh writer: the unit's brief from this course's Briefs page, never reworded; the sourcing standard; the fictional case register, generator/data/sport-psychology/case-facts.md; the seed bibliography, generator/data/sport-psychology/bibliography.md; and every unit of this course already filed. The course has no source units.
  2. The writer opens every reference before citing it and keeps a source log, and reports three lists with the unit: facts it invented about the case athletes, contradictions with a filed unit or with the brief of an unwritten one, and needs the syllabus does not meet.
  3. A fresh checker that has not seen the writing checks every cited claim against its source, every Sources entry against its publisher's record, the grades, the samples, the scope and safety rules and the spread of sports, the deliverable against the brief and every figure against the text. Its report is filed as audits/sport-psychology/fresh-check-<code>.md before any fix; findings are applied as exact replacements. SP9, DD-SP1 and DD-SP2 also get a safety reader, a third context that reads only for scope and safety.
  4. Contradictions and needs are appended to generator/data/sport-psychology/syllabus-flags.md; a brief is changed only by a syllabus review that the Project's owner confirms. The case register gains the facts a unit invented, with the unit's code.
  5. The unit is filed at courses/sport-psychology/<code>-<slug>.md with the front matter of method.md §7; this data file is updated; Academy is rebuilt, verified and published, the artifact and then GitHub Pages.

Models

  • Syllabus: Fable 5.2, as the Project sets for course design.
  • Writing: Opus 5.5 for every unit. The deliverable is written in the same context as its unit.
  • Fresh check and safety reader: Opus 5.5, each in a separate context.
  • Not Sonnet or Haiku for units or checks.

Review rule

Every new unit gets its fresh check before filing: every cited claim against its source, the deliverable against the brief, every figure against the text. Units written with live search (SP9, DD-SP1, DD-SP2) are re-checked for staleness six months after writing.
