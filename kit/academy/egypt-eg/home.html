Egyptian history and mythology

Ancient Egypt from the first kings to the closing of the last temple, told from Egyptian evidence first and from Greek and Roman observers second, with every claim sourced to an opened text and graded on the seven-grade key EG1 sets. Fifty-seven core modules in twelve syllabus units, then three deep dives of seven modules each: the pyramids, Amarna, and the afterlife books. What you will be able to do

To know ancient Egypt from the first kings to the closing of the last temple, its history, its gods and its dead, well enough to place any monument, text or claim in its period and to say what kind of evidence it rests on.

  • Outcome 1. Place any king, monument or text within its period and dynasty, and say how firm the date is; measured by EG57's examination, sections 1 and 2, and the anchor-date table of EG3 from memory (EG2 to EG35).
  • Outcome 2. Give the main gods, the creation accounts, the Osiris cycle and the afterlife as Egyptian sources state them, naming the earliest source for each element; measured by EG57, section 3, and the source table of EG39 (EG36 to EG49, DD-EG15 to DD-EG21).
  • Outcome 3. Grade a claim about Egypt on the seven-grade key, and name what would change the grade; measured by EG57, section 4, and the claims audit of DD-EG7 (EG1, EG31, EG56, DD-EG1 to DD-EG14).
  • A fourth, smaller outcome. Read a royal cartouche and the offering formula on sight (EG53, EG54).

Every module is sourced to Egyptian texts in named, opened translations, objects by museum and accession number, classical authors by book and chapter, peer-reviewed articles, academic monographs, the UCLA Encyclopedia of Egyptology, excavation reports and museum records, each opened before it is cited and logged; grades every claim (attested, later tradition, consensus, contested, scientific, announced, conjecture); gives every date before 664 BC as approximate with its chronology; cites no translation by Budge; and ends with its deliverable, then the Self-test and Carry forward.

Frame

Pick

A Personal Mythology course at Rome's scale, registered as a markdown course, written under the source-credibility floor, in twenty chats of four modules after one setup chat.

  • Rome's scale: 57 core modules and 21 deep-dive modules.
  • The floor applies: sources opened and logged, claims graded.
  • Bundles of four cut 78 chats to 20, from one two-line starter.

Override if: bundle 1 shows writers could not open enough sources to cover a brief; then the access rule is changed before bundle 2 is started.

  • What the course is for. To know ancient Egypt from the first kings to the closing of the last temple, its history, its gods and its dead, well enough to place any monument, text or claim in its period and to say what kind of evidence it rests on. It is the third course of Personal Mythology, after Greece and Rome, and points back to both.
  • Egyptian evidence first. Egypt is told from Egyptian evidence first and from Greek and Roman observers second; every statement says what kind of evidence carries it and how far that evidence stands from the event.
  • Where the evidence pushes back. Greece and Rome were written from knowledge with search off. Egypt cannot be: the floor forbids a claim on an unopened reference, and Egyptology's core is in books, many not open. The course leans on what is open and peer-reviewed: the UCLA Encyclopedia of Egyptology, the open publications of the Chicago institute (ISAC), the Metropolitan Museum and the IFAO, journal articles at their DOI, museum records. A writer who cannot open a standard book names the view through an opened reporter or leaves the name out.
  • Dates are a model. Egyptian chronology before 664 BC is a model, not a record. Every early date is approximate and names its chronology (EG3 sets the convention).
  • No connected myth. Egyptian myth has no connected native narrative. The course says so (EG36, EG39) and marks Plutarch as what he is.
  • Claims under examination. The subject attracts more unsourced claims than any other in the series. They are named as claims under examination and graded, never repeated as fact and never mocked (DD-EG7).
