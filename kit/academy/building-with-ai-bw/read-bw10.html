01What a subscription measures, and what it does not publish

BW2 §6 compared Anthropic's plans and the API on 15 November 2026. Read again on 16 November 2026, Anthropic's pages describe what a subscription meters in four parts.

A session window. "The period starts when you send your first message, and your limit resets in full five hours later" (Claude Help Center, How do usage and length limits work?, labelled "Updated over a week ago", read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). The pricing page calls it "a rolling five-hour session window", on which "paid plans add weekly limits on top" (Claude, Pricing, read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). The same page lists Ultra with "No 5-hour limit"; the help article says verified Ultra accounts do not have the session limit. Anthropic's own article on Ultra refused the fetch for this unit and was not checked.

A weekly limit. "Weekly limits apply across all models" (How do usage and length limits work?). On Max and Ultra, Fable models "draw from your plan's regular weekly usage limits and use them faster than other Claude models", and "You can use up to 50% of your weekly usage limits on Fable models at no extra cost"; on Pro, "Fable models run on pay-as-you-go usage credits from the start" (Claude Help Center, Claude Fable models on your plan, labelled "Updated this week", read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). The usage-limits article says some plans also have a separate weekly limit for Fable; the Fable article, updated more recently, describes a share of the regular limit and no separate one. Two of the vendor's pages differ here; the Fable article is the more specific.

One pool across surfaces. "Your activity across Claude on web, desktop, mobile, and Claude Code all draws from the same pool" (Pricing). Scheduled tasks in Claude Code "draw down subscription usage the same way interactive sessions do" (Anthropic, Automate work with scheduled tasks, read 16 Nov 2026; vendor).

Credits past the limit. On paid plans you can "turn on usage credits to keep working at standard API rates" (Pricing). The credits page adds that "Usage credits are billed at standard API rates", that you can "Set a maximum amount you're willing to spend on usage credits each month" under Settings > Usage with "Adjust limit", that credits cover Claude Code in the terminal as well as chat, and that credits bought on or after 7 December 2026 generally expire twelve months after purchase (Claude Help Center, Manage usage credits for paid Claude plans, labelled "Updated this week", read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). Settings > Usage shows "progress bars showing how much of your five-hour session and weekly usage limits you've consumed" (Claude Help Center, Usage limit best practices, labelled "Updated over 2 weeks ago", read 16 Nov 2026; vendor).

What draws the limit down. Anthropic lists eight factors: "Message length", "File attachment size", "Current conversation length", "Tool usage (e.g., Research, web search)", "Model choice", "Effort level", "Artifact creation and usage", and "Multi-step tasks, like running code, creating files, or browsing websites" (Usage limit best practices). The pricing page sums it up: "How much you can do depends on the length and complexity of your conversations, the model you choose, and the features you use, so there's no fixed message count."

What is not published. None of the pages read gives:

  • the number of tokens a five-hour window or a week allows on any plan. The one worked example is still the Pro article's: around 45 messages every five hours for short conversations, often more depending on capacity (Claude Help Center, About Claude's Pro Plan Usage, labelled "Updated over a week ago", read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). The same article keeps the right to "limit your usage in other ways, such as weekly and monthly caps or model and feature usage, at our discretion";
  • the rate at which each model draws the limit (BW2 §6 found the same on 15 Nov 2026), beyond Fable using it "faster";
  • the size of the weekly limit;
  • how much less a cached token counts against a plan (§5);
  • how long a claude.ai cache lasts (§5);
  • how much memory is loaded into a given chat (§6).

The window itself is published: in the claude.ai chat on paid plans, Fable 5.2, Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5, Opus 5, Sonnet 5.5, Sonnet 5 and Haiku 5.5 have 1M tokens; Fable 5, Opus 4.6 to 4.8 and Sonnet 4.6 have 500K; and "Outside of these models, Claude’s context window size is 200K" (Claude Help Center, How large is the context window on paid Claude plans?, labelled "Updated this week", read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). The usage-limits article now agrees ("the newest models support up to a 1M token context window, while others support 500K or 200K tokens"), and states the split this unit relies on: "Length limits relate to Claude's context window", while usage limits govern how much you use over time.

The matrix sorts what a subscriber can read from the vendor against what has to be inferred.

What follows (stated opinion, on the pages above). You cannot budget a subscription in tokens, because the exchange rate is unpublished. You can budget it in what enters each window, which you control (BW1 §1), and read the result on the usage bars. Every rule in this unit is of that kind.

The case. Academy (Academy case), a library of self-study courses built by one person with Claude in October and November 2026, is this course's running example: one project, an anecdote, never evidence about AI tools in general. Its record does not say which plan the project runs on, whether memory was on, or any token or window measure for a unit (Academy case file §7), and it holds no measure of hours or money spent per unit (§9). So the case cannot show a usage figure. What it can show is the shape of its work, which decides what enters each window: each unit is written in a new chat or a fresh agent context that sees only its brief and the units already filed (§2).

02What burns usage for no gain

Each of the four habits below spends one of Anthropic's eight factors (§1) and buys nothing the task needed.

Re-reading large files. A file read into a chat sits in the history from then on and is re-sent on every later turn (BW1 §2). Reading it again adds a second copy. Anthropic's Pro article names the plain case: "Don't re-upload files within the same conversation" (About Claude's Pro Plan Usage). In Claude Code, the documented remedies are to read less in the first place: specific requests let Claude "work efficiently with minimal file reads", and a hook that filters a 10,000-line log to its error lines cuts the context "from tens of thousands of tokens to hundreds" (Anthropic, Manage costs effectively, read 16 Nov 2026; vendor; documented practice). BW4 §2 gave the general form: a file a chat is pointed to by name loads once, when needed, and nothing else arrives.

Giant pastes. Message length is the first factor on the list, and a paste is re-sent like a file. Anthropic's advice runs the other way for material the task does need: "Provide all relevant data in a single, well-structured message" (Usage limit best practices), and BW3 §5 gave the evidence that a brief given in pieces does worse than the same brief given whole. The waste is the paste the task did not need: a full export when one column is used, a whole transcript when one section is used, the same document pasted again three turns later. The test before pasting (stated opinion): does the model need this text to produce this output?

Chats that never end. BW1 §2's illustrative arithmetic: at about 2,000 tokens an exchange, the first 30 turns send about 930,000 tokens of input in all. Claude Code's page says the same in plan terms: "a one-line question in a session that has been open all day still draws usage for the whole conversation" (Manage costs effectively). On claude.ai a chat long enough to be summarised costs more again: "Longer conversations that trigger automatic context management consume more of your usage limit", and the article's advice is to "Try starting a new conversation if you're approaching your usage limit in a longer chat" (How do usage and length limits work?). The tokens buy worse answers as well, by context rot (BW1 §2).

Research run twice. "Research is subject to the same limits as standard Claude conversations", and research sessions "can use up your limits faster due to Claude retrieving multiple sources" (Claude Help Center, Use research on Claude, labelled "Updated over 2 weeks ago", read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). The only research multiple Anthropic has published is for its 2025 system: agents use "about 4× more tokens than chat interactions" and "multi-agent systems use about 15× more tokens than chats" (Hadfield, Zhang, Lien, Scholz, Fox and Ford, How we built our multi-agent research system, Anthropic, 13 Jun 2025, read 16 Nov 2026; vendor, measured internally, 2025 system). No newer figure was found for this unit; 15× is a 2025 system's figure, and no current rate is published. Running the same question again pays it again, and every run brings fresh claims to check (BW6 §1, §5). The remedy is BW4 §4's: a finding worth keeping is written to a file with its source and date, and the next chat reads the file.

Three more items on the list spend usage when left at a setting the task does not need.

  • Effort above the task's need. Effort level is one of the eight factors; BW2 §3 sets effort per task. In Claude Code, thinking tokens "are billed as output tokens", and lowering effort is the documented way to cut them on models whose thinking cannot be switched off (Manage costs effectively).
  • Tools and connectors left on. "Tools and connectors are token-intensive", and the article's advice is to "Turn off apps you've connected when a conversation doesn't need them" (How do usage and length limits work?). BW4 §1 gives the On demand setting.
  • Work that runs while you are away. In Claude Code, a session-scoped scheduled task "fires on its interval even while the session is idle, sending your full context each time"; goal check-ins, subagents and agent teammates send requests of their own, and each teammate "keeps consuming tokens until it exits" (Manage costs effectively). BW9 §4 costs agents; this unit adds that background work keeps spending.

The case (anecdote). The case's method avoids the first and third habits by construction: one unit, one context, which sees its brief and the filed units (Academy case file §2). It does not record whether any of its chats grew long, or whether any research was run twice (§7 note: no token or window measure). A usage review of the owner's chats, 53 chats over six days, produced ten ranked changes, each tied to a count (§8); the case file does not list them.

03When a new chat is cheaper

BW4 §4 set out when to start a new chat for quality and independence, and when not to. This section adds the cost side.

The mechanism. Continuing a chat re-sends its history on every turn (BW1 §2). A new chat sends the standing layers (instructions, skill descriptions, memory, project files while small: BW4 §2) plus what you hand it. The standing layers are paid on every turn of every chat either way, so they drop out of the comparison; what differs is the history.

The arithmetic (illustrative, unmeasured, uncached). On BW1 §2's assumption of about 2,000 tokens an exchange, 30 turns in one chat send 2,000 × (1 + 2 + … + 30) = 930,000 tokens of history. The same 30 turns as three chats of 10, each later chat opening with a 2,000-token handoff note, send 110,000 + 130,000 + 130,000 = 370,000 (turn k of a later chat sends the note plus 2,000 × k). That is about 60% less, provided the later turns needed only what the note carries.

What caching changes. While a cache is warm, re-sent history costs less (§5), so inside one working session a long chat costs less than the uncached arithmetic says. After a break the comparison flips back. For project content on claude.ai, "If you come back to a project after a long break, your first message counts that content in full again" (Usage limit best practices). In Claude Code, "your first message after a break longer than the cache lifetime misses the cache and reprocesses your full context" (Manage costs effectively). The cache lifetime there is one hour for the main conversation on a subscription within the plan's included usage, and five minutes on usage credits (Anthropic, How Claude Code uses prompt caching, read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). claude.ai publishes no lifetime: "Caches expire after a period of inactivity" (Usage limit best practices).

Trimming, in Claude Code (documented practice; How Claude Code uses prompt caching, Manage costs effectively):

  • /rewind truncates to an earlier turn: "Rewinding truncates back to a prefix that is already cached, rather than building a new one as compaction does." It is the cheap way off a wrong path.
  • /compact replaces the history with a summary. The summarising request reads the whole conversation, cheaply while the cache is warm and at full price after a break. The page's tip: "Pick your model and effort level at the top of a session, then save /compact for natural breaks between tasks."
  • /clear starts fresh: "When you want a fresh start instead of continuity, /clear costs nothing", and "Stale context wastes tokens on every subsequent message" (Manage costs effectively).
  • If you leave a long conversation idle in a terminal on the one-hour cache, "Claude Code can compact it shortly before the cache expires" (How Claude Code uses prompt caching): the tool writes its own handoff note while the history is still cheap to read.

On claude.ai no help article read for this unit describes a way to trim a chat by hand; the one trimming mechanism is automatic context management, which costs more of the limit (§2).

Switching model or effort mid-chat. In Claude Code, "each model has its own cache", and switching models "recomputes the entire request even when the content is identical". Changing effort does the same on most models; on Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5 and Fable 5.1, with an API key or a Claude subscription, "the cache stays intact by default" (How Claude Code uses prompt caching). The page does not list Fable 5.2 among those models. On claude.ai the effect is undocumented; BW2's rule 1 already sets both dials before the first message, and that rule costs nothing.

The rule (stated opinion; each branch traces to a source above). First ask: does the next step need this chat's history? If it does not, start a new chat (Claude Code: /clear). If it does, ask: has the chat been idle longer than the cache lifetime? If it has, start a new chat with a handoff note, since the next message pays for the whole history anyway and the note is smaller. If it has not, ask: is the chat on a wrong path? If it is: Claude Code: /rewind. claude.ai: new chat, since claude.ai documents no hand trim. If it is not: stay; /compact only between tasks. On claude.ai the lifetime is unpublished, so this unit treats a break of over an hour as expired, by analogy with Claude Code's one-hour figure (stated opinion). This refines BW4 §4's third reason to stay ("The pause is short"): short means inside the cache lifetime.

The case (anecdote). The case's chats end by writing the prompt for the next chat (Academy case file §2), and a lost writer's work was resumed by a third writer from the second's working files (§7): the handoff lived in files, so a new context could pick it up. Whether any of its chats were continued across a break is not recorded.

04A token budget per task type

Since no allowance per window is published (§1), a budget here is a ceiling on what may enter the window for one task, and the point at which its chat stops. It is written in what enters, not in tokens, because no page read gives the exchange rate between tokens and a plan's limit (§1). BW2's routing table gave each recurring task a tier and an effort, and left a "How often" column for this unit (BW2, Deliverable Part 2). Three readings (arithmetic and stated opinion, on the table).

  1. Standing layers are a per-turn tax. Twenty installed skills at about 100 tokens each add about 2,000 tokens to every turn of every chat where they are installed (arithmetic on Agent Skills; BW4 §2 made the same count). A light task run inside a heavy project pays the project's standing files on every turn, which is why rows 1 to 3 run outside it.
  2. The dearest rows are the ones that multiply. Research, agent runs and agent teams all multiply a chat's tokens by a published factor (about 15× and 4× on Anthropic's 2025 research system, §2; about 7× for agent teams in plan mode, the table above), each against its own baseline (BW9 §4), and each is the row most worth running once and filing.
  3. On a subscription, watch two numbers. Settings > Usage shows the session and weekly bars (§1); in Claude Code on Pro, Max, Team or Enterprise, /usage also attributes recent usage to "skills, subagents, plugins, and individual MCP servers", and recommends changes for any behaviour behind 10% or more of it (Manage costs effectively). The spend cap for a Claude Code run on the API is --max-budget-usd, which the costs page names; BW9 gives the rest of an agent's guardrails.

05Caching, as far as it is documented

Caching is the one mechanism that makes a re-sent history cheaper. How much is documented depends on where you work.

On the API. "By default, the cache has a 5-minute lifetime"; a one-hour lifetime costs more. Cache writes cost 1.25 times the base input price for five minutes and 2 times for one hour; cache reads cost 0.1 times, with exceptions: 0.05 times on Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, 0.025 times on Fable 5.2 and Fable 5.1. The shortest cacheable prompt on the newest models is 512 tokens (Anthropic, Prompt caching, read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). The page does not mention claude.ai or subscriptions.

In Claude Code. The cache is a prefix: "The match is exact, so a change anywhere in the prefix recomputes everything after it" (How Claude Code uses prompt caching). On a Claude subscription within the plan's included usage, the main conversation gets one hour and almost everything else five minutes: "Subagents fall outside the main-conversation TTL bucket, so they get five minutes even on a subscription". Once a session draws on usage credits, the main conversation drops to five minutes (same page). Actions that break the cache include switching models, changing effort on most models, turning on fast mode, connecting or removing an MCP server when tool search is off, and compacting; actions that keep it include editing files, invoking skills and rewinding (same page). A fork "inherits the parent's system prompt, tools, and conversation history exactly, so its first request reads the parent's cache"; a fresh subagent does not (same page).

That last fact has a cost side BW9 §4 did not need. A fork is the cheaper way to hand off a side task, because it reads a warm cache; it is also a reviewer that has seen the writing (BW9 §4). When a check must be independent (BW6 §4), pay for a fresh subagent, a new session or a custom subagent: the independence is what you are buying (stated opinion).

On claude.ai. Three sentences are all that is published. "Content in projects is cached and counts less against your limits when reused" (Usage limit best practices); "cached tokens count less than uncached portions toward your usage limits" (About Claude's Pro Plan Usage); and "Caches expire after a period of inactivity" (Usage limit best practices). The best-practices page also lists "Claude remembers context from earlier in the conversation" under caching, without a rate or a lifetime. How much less a cached token counts, how long a claude.ai cache lives, and whether a new chat in the same project reads the project's cache are not stated (BW4 §4 found the last unstated on 15 Nov 2026).

What follows (stated opinion). Caching rewards three habits you already have reasons for: fixed standing layers at the top of the window (BW4 §1), model and effort chosen before the first message (BW2, rule 1), and work done in one sitting. It punishes a long chat resumed after a break, which is §3's case for a new chat with a handoff note.

06What the vendor keeps, for how long, and the settings that govern training

All facts below are Anthropic's, for its consumer plans unless marked, as read 16 Nov 2026.

Chats. The retention page covers Free, Pro and Max and Claude Code used through those accounts, and is dated 1 July 2026. "Anthropic retains your personal data for as long as reasonably necessary"; a deleted chat leaves your history at once and is "Deleted from our back-end storage systems within 30 days". With the model-improvement setting on, new or resumed chats and coding sessions may be kept de-identified "for up to 5 years in our model training pipelines". A chat flagged by the trust and safety systems keeps its inputs and outputs "for up to 2 years" and its classification scores for up to seven. Data tied to a thumbs up or down, or a bug report, is kept five years. "Your Incognito chats are not used to improve Claude" (Anthropic Privacy Center, How long do you store my data?, 1 Jul 2026, read 16 Nov 2026; vendor).

The training setting. It is a toggle under Settings > Privacy. Off, "we will not use any new chats and coding sessions you have with Claude for future model training"; and "Your data will still be included in model training that has already started"; chats flagged by safety classifiers may still be used to improve internal trust and safety models (Anthropic Privacy Center, How do I change my model improvement privacy settings?, 3 Aug 2026, read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). The terms change that created it: "These updates apply to users on our Claude Free, Pro, and Max plans"; they "do not apply to services under our Commercial Terms"; retention is five years if you allow training and "our existing 30-day data retention period" if you do not, for new or resumed chats; and "If you delete a conversation with Claude it will not be used for future model training" (Anthropic, Updates to Consumer Terms and Privacy Policy, 28 Aug 2025, read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). The 2026 retention page does not restate the 30 days for chats you keep; it gives 30 days only for deleted chats.

Who can read them. "Access is limited to a small number of personnel involved in model training", and "We automatically de-link your data from your user ID (like your email address) before any review". The same article asks for care with financial details such as card and bank numbers, health records, passwords and login credentials, and confidential business or personal documents; it covers Free, Pro, Max and Ultra and Claude Code on those accounts (Claude Help Center, I would like to input sensitive data into my chats with Claude. Who can view my conversations?, labelled "Updated over 2 weeks ago", read 16 Nov 2026; vendor).

Memory. "Claude saves memory as a set of individual topics as you chat, rather than summarizing conversations after they end." "Each project has its own memory"; a project's chats use that memory and your memory from chats outside projects, never another project's, and "Use account memory" on the project's page separates them fully. By default memory does not store personal or sensitive subjects such as health, politics or religion, unless you turn on "Include sensitive topics in memory". "Some information is never saved to memory, even if you ask": government ID numbers, criminal history, financial account numbers and immigration status. When a chat is deleted, including under a retention period, the memory entries generated from it are not removed; you delete them yourself under Settings > Your Claude, "Manage" next to "Your memory". An organisation's custom retention period for chats also applies to memory, project memory excepted. The article does not say how much memory is loaded into a chat (Claude Help Center, Use Claude's chat search and memory to build on previous context, labelled "Updated this week", read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). On plans: "Memory is on by default on Free, Pro, Max, and Ultra plans across web, desktop, and mobile"; an undated update note on the same post says memory is now on by default for members of most Team and Enterprise organisations created on or after 31 October 2026, and older organisations are unchanged (Anthropic, Claude's memory works everywhere, and you decide what's in it, 25 Aug 2026, read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). The help article says owners control whether memory is available on Team and Enterprise, and "Owners can't turn memory on for individual users".

Projects. "Context is not shared across chats within a project unless the information is added into the project knowledge base." The article describes sharing a project on Team and Enterprise and deleting one, and says nothing about retention, training or what happens to the files after deletion (Claude Help Center, How can I create and manage projects?, labelled "Updated over a week ago", read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). The retention page does not mention projects, memory or uploaded files either. So no retention period for project files is published on the pages read.

Incognito. Incognito chats are not saved to your history or to memory, are kept 30 days by default, "are not used for training", are "currently only available for chats outside of projects", and "Once closed, incognito chats cannot be reopened"; they are available on every plan (Claude Help Center, Use incognito chats, labelled "Updated over 2 weeks ago", read 16 Nov 2026; vendor).

Commercial plans. Anthropic's privacy centre states that by default it does not use inputs or outputs from its commercial products, such as Claude for Work and the API, to train models, unless you send feedback or otherwise opt in; feedback stores the conversation for up to five years (Anthropic Privacy Center, Is my data used for model training?, 18 Aug 2026, read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). The page gives no general retention period for commercial chats.

The deletion sequence (stated opinion, on the table). Deleting a chat that held private material is three steps: delete the chat; delete the memory entries it wrote, since the chat's deletion leaves them; and know that if training was on when the chat ran, a de-identified copy may already be in a training run that has started. Rating a chat with a thumb keeps its data five years, so do not rate chats that hold private material.

07Which material belongs in memory, in a project, in a private store, or nowhere

Four places can hold what you work with. Each has properties from §6 and from earlier units that decide what belongs there.

  • Memory is written by a model about your chats, loaded in amounts nobody publishes, reaches every chat outside projects and, unless a project's "Use account memory" is off, every project's chats, and survives the deletion of the chat it came from (§6; BW1 §3). With memory on, a project's own memory can also be referenced in chats outside projects (Claude Help Center, Use Claude's chat search and memory to build on previous context, read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). BW4 §1 put it as the wrong home for a rule you need held exactly.
  • A project's knowledge loads into every chat in that project while the project is small, and is searched once it is large (BW4 §1, §3); its retention is not stated (§6). Its output goes wherever the project's work goes.

08Work material and your employer's policy: a question for you

This section gives the facts the vendors publish and the questions they leave to you. It is information, not legal advice.

The facts (vendor, read 16 Nov 2026). Anthropic's consumer terms change of 2025 applies to Free, Pro and Max and does not apply to services under its Commercial Terms (§6). Its commercial products do not train on inputs or outputs by default (§6). Its sensitive-data article asks for care with confidential business documents (§6). Google's Gemini Apps Privacy Hub says that with a work or school account, use of Gemini Apps "may be subject to different data handling terms", and asks: "Please don’t enter confidential information that you wouldn’t want a reviewer to see" (Google, Gemini Apps Privacy Hub, last updated 26 Oct 2026, read 16 Nov 2026; vendor). OpenAI's Data Controls FAQ points professionals who need more control of their data to its business plans and their privacy policies (OpenAI, Data Controls FAQ, labelled "Updated over 4 months ago", read 16 Nov 2026; vendor).

What they leave to you. Which terms govern the account you are using is a fact about your account; whether your employer allows its material on that account is a fact about your employer. No vendor page can answer the second. Before any work material enters a chat, answer five questions in writing:

  1. Does my employer have a written policy on AI tools, and which tools and plans does it approve?
  2. Is the account I am using a personal consumer account or one my employer provides under its own terms?
  3. On a personal account, is the training setting off, and does the policy allow any work material there at all?
  4. Does the material include other people's personal data, client material or anything under a confidentiality agreement?
  5. Who do I ask when the policy is silent, and have I asked?

If any answer is "I don't know", the material goes nowhere in your account (§7) until it is answered (stated opinion). The case file holds no fact about an employer, so the case cannot illustrate this section.
