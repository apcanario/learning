COURSE SP · Sport Psychology (public) · IN PROGRESS

Sport Psychology for Coaches

For a coach in any sport with no training in psychology: what changes in an athlete between 10 and 25, the climate your own behaviour creates, what anxiety is and how it is kept going, the skills a coach may teach and how strong the evidence is for each, training under pressure, reading and responding to an athlete, competition day, the signs that mean referral, and a season plan. Three deep dives: the coach's line with minors, weight and who decides, and the techniques an athlete carries into competition. Worked on three fictional athletes in three sports. Ten core units in four parts and three deep dives.

What you will be able to do

By the end of the course you can adjust what you ask of an athlete to their age and stage; teach a small set of evidence-graded psychological skills in practice and use them on competition day; respond to an athlete's state from their baseline and their own words; keep your language, your handling of weight and your authority on the right side of a line you can state; and recognise the signs that mean referral, with a season plan written for your own squad.

  • Outcome 1 · Know the athlete and the evidence. An age-and-stage table, a climate audit of your own coaching, and a claim-check you can run on anything you are told (SP1, SP2, SP3).
  • Outcome 2 · Skills in practice and on the day. An anxiety profile, a skills block, a pressure-training block, a response table, a competition-day protocol, a pocket toolkit and a season plan (SP4 to SP8, SP10, DD-SP3).
  • Outcome 3 · Wellbeing and the coach's line. A red-flag table and referral plan, a conduct code and comment test, and a protocol for who decides a weight class (SP9, DD-SP1, DD-SP2).

Every unit is sourced to consensus statements, meta-analyses and peer-reviewed studies, grades each recommendation by its evidence, names the sample behind each finding, says where a coach's part ends, and ends with a deliverable built in full on three fictional athletes and then as a template.

Frame

Pick

A coach's course, not a psychologist's. Climate, skills, noticing, referring.

  • Coaches are with athletes daily; clinicians rarely are.
  • A stated scope keeps a public course safe to follow.
  • Graded evidence is what separates it from online advice.

Override if: the readers turn out to be mostly sport psychology students; then the course adds theory and assessment and drops the scope limits.

  • Where the evidence pushes back. Most studies are small. Most samples are university students or adult athletes, not 13-year-olds who win national titles. Several of the techniques coaches most want (talking to yourself by name, reading nerves as readiness, if-then plans) were tested outside sport. Prevalence figures for abuse come from adults remembering their childhood. The course grades every recommendation and names the sample behind it, so a reader sees "moderate" and "weak" often.
