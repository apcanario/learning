Scope note. The Greek text of the decree could not be read: PHI (packhum text 301891) was never returned by a search, the Warburg OGIS scan failed at its site, a Cambridge chapter fetch was denied, and two other candidate PDFs were refused (login / access denied). The only Greek of the decree seen is Mommsen's (OCR-garbled) quotation of its opening clause. The verdicts below rest on (1) the Antikensammlung Berlin's own catalogue entry for the stone, inventory IvP 249, and (2) the Oxford CHANGE database's line-by-line summary of I.Pergamon 249. Both agree with the module's clause. The ratification phrase in Greek (expected "δεῖ δὲ ἐπικυρωθῆναι τὴν διαθήκην ὑπὸ Ῥωμαίων") was NOT seen in any source read and is not quoted as verified.

## Place 1 (dispute box)
Current:
```
**Where sources disagree.** The letter of Mithridates in Sallust, *Histories* 4.69 M, alleges the will was forged by Rome. The Pergamene decree *OGIS* 338, passed within weeks of Attalus' death, cites the will and says that Rome had still to ratify it, which most historians take as decisive; the motive, probably to pre-empt a succession war, remains conjecture.
```
Verdict: CONFIRMED (for the clause "cites the will and says that Rome had still to ratify it")

Check against the source: PASS, on the curatorial description and the CHANGE summary, not on the Greek.
- https://smb.museum-digital.de/pdf/object/270184.pdf?lang=de (Staatliche Museen zu Berlin, Antikensammlung, "Oberer Teil einer Stele: Volksbeschluß nach dem Tode Attalos' III.", inventory IvP 249, description by S. Brehme): the decree refers to the king's testament, which left the city free with added territory, "da aber das Testament von den Römern noch bestätigt werden muss" (as returned by the fetch); also "Die häufig bezweifelte Echtheit des Testaments wurde erst durch den Fund dieser Inschrift bewiesen."
- https://change.csad.ox.ac.uk/inscriptions/item/item-4.1268 (CHANGE, I.Pergamon 249, PHI 301891, TM 868412, "Decree granting citizenship and resident foreigner status to various groups after death of Attalos III", 133 BCE): ll. 3-10 summarised as the will of Attalos III granting the city its territory and freedom. No Greek on the page.
- https://elekpub.bib.uni-wuppertal.de/download/pdf/7523621.pdf (Mommsen, "Senatsbeschluss über Pergamon", Gesammelte Schriften, Historische Schriften I, 1906, p. 65 n.): cites IvP 249 for "ἀπολέλοιπεν τὴ[ν] πατρ[ί]δα ἡμῶν ἐλευθέρα[ν]" (OCR as returned: "απολέλοιπεν τή[μ πατρ\ίδα ημών ελενΰέρα"). This is the will clause's wording; it does not reach the ratification phrase.
- Greek words relied on: only "ἀπολέλοιπεν τὴν πατρίδα ἡμῶν ἐλευθέραν" (via Mommsen). The ratification clause in Greek was not read.

Outside the brief, not checked: "passed within weeks of Attalus' death". No source read gave the decree's date relative to the death beyond "133 BCE"/"after the death". Flag for a later check.
