## Figure 3 (matrix): FIX

Row order: Proba (l. 23), sortes (l. 31), Roman d'Eneas (l. 41), Dante (ll. 45–47), Tate and Purcell, Berlioz (l. 68), Broch (l. 75), Le Guin (l. 77), Mantua monument (l. 81). Nine rows, in section order from "The Christian Virgil" to "Virgil in Fascist Italy and after", as the caption claims.

Trace, row by row:

- **Proba's Cento**: "lines and half-lines" / "Virgil's verses". Text, l. 23: "built entirely of lines and half-lines from Virgil"; "a cento on Christ made of Virgil's verses". Supported. Caption "without a passage" correct (the proem line 23 and Etymologies 1.39.26 are cited for other things).
- **Sortes Vergilianae**: "one line as an answer" / "opened at random". Text, l. 31: "opened the poem at random and read the first line the eye fell on as an answer". "one line" is a paraphrase, not the text's words, and it misleads: every example the text gives draws a passage of several lines (6.808–812, 6.847–853, 4.615–620). **Fix needed** (see 1).
- **Roman d'Eneas**: "the story, its weight moved: second half a love story" / "Virgil's story". Text, l. 41: "It keeps Virgil's story and moves its weight ... the second half becomes a love story Virgil never wrote". Supported. Caption "without a passage" correct for this row.
- **Dante**: "the poet as guide" / "book 6; the fourth Eclogue". Text, l. 45: "the choice of Virgil himself"; l. 47: the reasons are four (poet of the providential empire, author of "the one classical descent into the underworld", prophet "through the fourth Eclogue", the school's master of style). The cell gives two, which fits a "where from" column naming parts of Virgil's work; "book 6" is the text's "descent into the underworld" (the text ties book 6 to the Inferno at l. 45). Acceptable. The caption's passage clause is wrong (see 2).
- **Tate and Purcell**: "Dido; Rome dropped out" / "book 4 alone". Text, l. 68: "Tate's libretto takes book 4 alone and ends with the queen: the founding of Rome has dropped out". The cell drops the text's qualifier: it is the founding of Rome that drops out, not Rome. **Fix needed** (see 3).
- **Berlioz**: "Troy and Carthage" / "books 1, 2 and 4". Text, l. 68: "the fall of Troy from book 2 ... and Carthage from books 1 and 4". Supported.
- **Broch**: "the order to burn it" / "Donatus' life". Text, l. 75: "Its subject is Donatus' story of the order to burn the poem (module 12.1)". The text never says the story is in Donatus' life (the life is mentioned only at l. 13, separately, as surviving with the preface). The cell says more than the text and contradicts the caption's own "Donatus' story". **Fix needed** (see 4).
- **Le Guin**: "the silent bride's voice" / "Lavinia's silence". Text, l. 77: "gives a voice to the woman Virgil left silent"; l. 127: "the bride with no lines"; l. 41: Lavinia "never speaks". Supported. The caption misattaches a passage to it (see 5).
- **Mantua monument**: "Anchises' charge to Rome" / "6.851–853". Text, l. 81: "carries Anchises' charge to Rome ... (6.851–853), with lines from the fifth Eclogue and the Georgics' praise of Italy". Supported as far as it goes; the row omits the other two inscriptions the text names (see 6, recommended).

Caption, item by item:

- Proba, sortes, Roman d'Eneas, Tate and Purcell, Berlioz "without a passage" / "without passages (deep dive module 12.4 has more)": correct (l. 68 sends Tate, Purcell and Berlioz to 12.4; Memoirs ch. 2 is cited for Berlioz's boyhood, not for the opera).
- Dante: "(Inferno 1.70–72 and 1.85–87, where Dante calls him 'my master and my author')". The "where" clause covers both passages, but the text (l. 45) attaches 1.70–72 to Virgil naming himself by his date and only 1.85–87 to "my master and my author". **Fix needed** (see 2). "with book 6 and the fourth Eclogue given as reasons without a passage": correct. "the Eclogue rendered into Italian in Statius' speech (Purgatorio 22.67–73)": correctly attached (l. 47), though it describes something the drawing does not show; harmless as a pointer, kept.
- Broch: "after Donatus' story of the order to burn the poem (module 12.1)": verbatim from l. 75.
- Le Guin: "(the text cites Aeneid 12.64–69 for Lavinia's silence in its account of the Roman d'Eneas)". The text (l. 41) cites 12.64–69 for her single blush: "Lavinia blushes once (12.64–69) and never speaks". The silence is said without a passage. **Fix needed** (see 5).
- Mantua: "Anchises' charge to Rome (6.851–853)": correct.

Kind and drawing: a matrix fits nine items against two attributes; headers "WHAT IT TAKES" and "WHERE FROM" read correctly over their columns; no cell is clipped or overlapping; both themes readable. On phone the figure scrolls sideways across two frames, the caption with it (house rule).

### Fixes

1. Sortes row, "What it takes": change "one line as an answer" to "the first line the eye fell on, as an answer" (text l. 31).
2. Caption, Dante: change "(Inferno 1.70–72 and 1.85–87, where Dante calls him "my master and my author")" to "(Inferno 1.70–72, where the shade names himself by his date, and 1.85–87, where Dante calls him "my master and my author")".
3. Tate and Purcell row, "What it takes": change "Dido; Rome dropped out" to "Dido; the founding of Rome dropped out" (text l. 68).
4. Broch row, "Where from": change "Donatus' life" to "Donatus' story" (text l. 75; matches the caption).
5. Caption, Le Guin: change "(the text cites Aeneid 12.64–69 for Lavinia's silence in its account of the Roman d'Eneas)" to "(in its account of the Roman d'Eneas the text cites Aeneid 12.64–69 for Lavinia's one blush and says without a passage that she never speaks)".
6. Recommended, not required: Mantua row, "What it takes": "Anchises' charge to Rome, with lines from the fifth Eclogue and the Georgics" (text l. 81), so the row does not imply the charge is the monument's only text; "Where from" stays "6.851–853" for the charge.
