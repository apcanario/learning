## Figure 2 (matrix): FIX

**Rows, passages, order.** The ending (12.950–952), Marcellus (6.860–886), The shield (8.626–731), Dido (book 4). The first three ranges match the section headings exactly. Dido's heading has no range; "book 4" comes from "Module 12.4 reads book 4", and the caption says the text gives no line range. The rows follow the text's order. The headers PASSAGE / AUGUSTAN READING / PESSIMISTIC READING are correct. A matrix suits four cases against two readings.

**Cells.**

- The ending, Augustan: "just anger, punishment". The cited sentence (Test case: the ending, Where sources disagree) reads: "Galinsky (1988) reads it as justified anger in Aristotle's sense, the punishment of Pallas' killer". "Just anger" appears in the text too ("Roman moralists did not condemn just anger"). The cell says no more than the text and passes. "justified anger, punishment of Pallas' killer" would match the cited sentence more closely (optional).
- The ending, pessimistic: "triumph of furor, breach of Anchises' precept". Text: Putnam "reads the killing as the triumph of furor and a breach of Anchises' precept". Word for word. Correct.
- Marcellus, Augustan: "the dynasty's grief made the nation's". Text: "The Augustan reading hears the dynasty's grief made the nation's". Word for word. Correct.
- Marcellus, pessimistic: "the parade ends at a grave". Text: "The pessimistic reading hears the parade of glory end at a grave" (self-test: "the parade of glory ending at a grave"). **Fault:** the cell restates the sentence but drops "of glory", which is the point of the reading (glory against grave), so it is not in the text's own words.
- The shield, Augustan: "his descendants' fame carried as pietas". Text: "The Augustan reading takes the same line as a climax of pietas: as he carried his father out of Troy, he now carries on his shoulder the fame and fates of his descendants (8.731)". **Fault:** "carried as pietas" is the drawing's own phrase. The text calls the line "a climax of pietas" and has Aeneas carry "the fame and fates" of his descendants. The cell paraphrases instead of using the text's words, and drops "fates".
- The shield, pessimistic: "rejoicing without knowing the events". Text: "Aeneas lifts the shield rejoicing in the images without knowing the events" (8.730), one of three details the pessimists press. In the text's words, with "in the images" dropped and the meaning unchanged. Passes.
- Dido, Augustan: "pietas obeyed at great cost". Text: "Aeneas' departure is pietas obeyed at great cost". Word for word. Correct.
- Dido, pessimistic: "the mission's first great victim". Text: "Dido is the mission's first great victim". Word for word. Correct.
