- Aristotle, Rhetoric 1411a24–25 (= SSR V B 184) is confirmed as the oldest Diogenes testimony (Peitho abstract), but the wording ("the Dog" calling the taverns the common messes of Attica) is from memory.
- Sinope's coins: tampering in the mid-fourth century, with Branham (1996) and Navia (2005) giving the story a historical core, comes from Wikipedia's summary and the IEP, not the originals. The inference "if the coins date the exile, Diogenes reached Athens after Antisthenes was dead" is my own; the IEP says only that the dates make the Antisthenes link doubtful.
- Philodemus, On the Stoics, rebuking Stoics who disowned Diogenes' Republic: from memory, no column cited. Knucklebones were dropped from the 6.72 summary because I was unsure they are in DL rather than Athenaeus.
- Goulet-Cazé, L'ascèse cynique (1986), as a commentary on DL 6.70–71 that finds a reasoned theory of training, and Julian, Orations 6 and 7, defending Cynicism as a universal philosophy: from memory.
- Cyrenaic box: Tsouna's position (founder no ethical hedonist, no epistemology), her rejection of Rowe (2015), and Mondolfo's assignment to the grandson all come from the SEP entry "The Cyrenaics" (Tsouna). Rowe's chapter itself was not seen. Irwin (1991) and Zilioli (2012) were removed after the SEP showed they belong to the realist/anti-realist debate, not the founder question.
- Cosmopolitanism box: Kleingeld and Brown (SEP "Cosmopolitanism") is the cautious reading. I did not name Moles or Schofield because I could not confirm which side each takes.
- Section numbers from memory or located only by position in the Perseids text: DL 6.10–11, 6.45, 6.58, 6.69 (both eating in the agora and parrhēsia), 2.88 and 2.93, 2.106, 2.115; Xenophon, Memorabilia 2.1.13.
- Plutarch, Against Colotes 1119c–1120b for Stilpo on predication: Wikipedia gives only chapters 22–23, and the Stephanus range is my conversion. Seneca, Letters 9.18–19 is from memory.
- Cicero, On Duties 1.148 for "the Cynic way to be rejected entirely", with Panaetius as the probable source; Sextus, Against the Mathematicians 10 for Diodorus on motion; "Boethius reports" for Diodorus' definition of the possible, given with no page: all from memory.
- The claim that Prior (1955) and Gaskin (1995) "supply the missing steps differently" is a general characterisation, not checked against either work. Reading the "lying argument" of the Athenaeus 9.401e epitaph as the Liar is Stock's interpretation, which is why the text says "perhaps".
