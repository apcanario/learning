# Rome 13.7 writer notes (Opus 5.5, 1 November 2026)

- Oliver and Palmer 1955 on the *trinqui* and Lyon: the link was taken from the Judaism and Rome database summary (which also names Keresztes) and the Hesperia listing; the article itself not read. The decree's date ("late 170s", 176/177) and "priests of the Gallic provinces" are from memory.
- Hadot (1984) and Görgemanns on Aristo of Chios, Champlin and Birley on Titius Aristo: from Görgemanns' footnote 3 (RhM 134); Görgemanns' year 1991 is not printed on the download.
- Barnes 1978: his view (Eusebius' date may be wrong; persecution possibly after 180) is from the same database summary, not the paper.
- Kovács 2009 (171 against the literary 174; no coin tied to the miracle) from Fear's BMCR review only.
- Dio section numbers: checked on LacusCurtius, whose section markers are garbled; I cite chapters only where unsure. 71.1 and 71.1.2 (Lucilla; Sextus) are approximate.
- From memory, not checked: Galen, *On Prognosis* (excused from the campaign, 168); Claudian, *Sixth Consulship* (inclines to the emperor's virtue); *Verus* 10–11 for the poisoning rumours; *Acts of Justin* and Rusticus as prefect c. 165; Melito at Eusebius 4.26.5–6; *Meditations* 11.3 on the Christians and its possible gloss; Ucubi as the modern form of Succuba.
- From memory: Lucilla's wedding at Ephesus (164); Commodus Augustus in 177 attested by coins and inscriptions; the Faustinian girls refounded; equestrian statue moved to the Capitol in 1538 and "the only bronze equestrian statue of a Roman emperor to survive whole".
- Marcus' age at the adoption given as sixteen (born April 121, adopted February 138) against the *Historia Augusta*'s eighteen (5.1): my arithmetic, not a cited modern view.
- Domitia Lucilla's brickworks and Fronto's letters' content (grape harvest, sore throat, grandson's death, the letters on eloquence, Lucius asking for a history) are from general knowledge; no letter numbers given.
- "At least fourteen children" is attributed to modern reconstructions; the count follows standard modern lists, not an ancient source.
