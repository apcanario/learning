Checked against `text.txt`, `captions.md` and all twelve images: desktop and phone frames, dark and light. House rules were applied as given. Phone frames show part of each figure because the figure keeps its natural width, and that is not counted as a fault.

## Figure 1 (tree): FIX

**Trace**
- "Arria the Elder, wife of Caecina Paetus": supported. The text says "Thrasea's wife Arria was the daughter of a more famous Arria … the elder Arria's husband, Caecina Paetus" (Letters 3.16.2; 3.16).
- "(died 42)": not supported as drawn. The text says Caecina Paetus "was condemned" in 42, and that "she stabbed herself, drew out the dagger and handed it to him" (Dio 60.16; the words are at 3.16.6). The self-test puts the dagger "in 42 (3.16.6)". The text does not say in so many words that either of them died. The parenthesis also sits directly after "Caecina Paetus", so it reads as his death date, not hers.
- "Arria the Younger, wife of Thrasea Paetus; exiled in 93": supported. The text says "The younger Arria … was exiled in 93 (Letters 3.11.3)". It calls her "Thrasea's wife Arria".
- "Fannia, wife of Helvidius Priscus; exiled in 93": supported. The text calls her "her daughter Fannia, Helvidius' wife … banished a third time herself on his account (7.19.4)". It says she was "Charged in 93" (7.19.4–6). The Domitian section counts "Arria and Fannia exiled" in 93 (Letters 3.11.2–3).
- Kind and order: a three-step line from mother to daughter, in the text's order (elder Arria, then the younger Arria, then Fannia). A tree fits.
- Readability: clear in both themes, with nothing clipped or overlapping on desktop. On the phone each box is split between frames 1 and 2. This is the house rule for natural width, so it is not counted.

**Caption**
- "Three generations … mother to daughter, with each woman's husband" matches the drawing.
- "Thrasea as her son-in-law" under 3.16 is supported (3.16.10).
- 3.11.3 for the younger Arria is supported.
- The caption leaves out two passages:
  - The text gives the dagger scene to Dio 60.16, with Pliny 3.16.6 for the words. The caption names only Pliny.
  - The text supports Fannia's exile in 93 with Letters 3.11.2–3 as well as 7.19.4–6. The caption names only 7.19.4–6.

**Fix**

  - If you want to keep "died", the text needs the death stated. Change the sentence in "Arria and the women of the house" to: "When he hesitated to die, Dio says (60.16), she stabbed herself, drew out the dagger and handed it to him with the words Paete, non dolet (3.16.6): Paetus, it does not hurt; both died." From the section Arria and the women of the house: Pliny, *Letters* 3.16 (the line from the elder Arria to her granddaughter Fannia, 3.16.2; the elder Arria's dagger in 42, 3.16.6, with Dio 60.16; Thrasea as her son-in-law, 3.16.10), 3.11.3 (the younger Arria exiled in 93), and 7.19.4–6 with 3.11.2–3 (Fannia, Helvidius' wife, charged and exiled in 93)."

## Figure 2 (timeline): FIX

**Trace**
- "46 BC, Cato dies at Utica": the year and place are supported, but the month is missing. The text says "at Utica in April 46 … (Cato Minor 66–70)".
- "AD 42, Arria: Paete, non dolet": supported. The text says "In 42 … Paete, non dolet (3.16.6)", and the self-test says "the dagger in 42 (3.16.6)".
- "AD 59, Thrasea walks out": supported. The text says "when Nero's letter accused Agrippina in 59, Thrasea walked out (Annals 14.12.1)".
- "AD 65, Seneca and Lucan die": half supported. The text says "Seneca had staged the same model a year earlier, in 65 (15.60–64 …), and Lucan had died reciting his own verse (15.70 …)". The year 65 is attached to Seneca only. For Lucan the text has only the pluperfect "had died", with no year, so the label dates Lucan's death more precisely than the text does.
- "AD 66, Thrasea dies; Helvidius banished": supported. The section heading reads "the death in 66". The text says "Helvidius Priscus and Paconius Agrippinus were banished from Italy (16.33)", and the death scene is at 16.34–35. The Helvidius section says "Banished in 66".
- "AD 93, Rusticus and Senecio killed; philosophers expelled": the events are supported (Agricola 2.1–2; Suetonius, Domitian 10.4; Dio 67.13; Pliny, Letters 3.11.2–3). The qualifier on the date is missing. The text says "The year was 93, after Agricola's death in August."
- "VESPASIAN, 69–79" and "DOMITIAN, 81–96": supported. The text gives "Vespasian's reign (69–79)" and "under Domitian (81–96)", both without a passage. Each span label carries its years, so it reads as a period.
- Not drawn: Helvidius' death ("The year of his death is not given; modern accounts place it in the mid-70s") and Vespasian's expulsion ("Modern dates for the expulsion vary, from 71 into the mid-70s"). Leaving these out is correct.
- Kind and order: a timeline fits. The events are in chronological order and the spans sit at the right positions on the axis.
- Readability: legible in both themes, with no clipping. The dots for 65 and 66 almost coincide, so the 65 dot is half hidden behind the 66 dot. The label "Seneca and Lucan die" ends a few pixels short of the 66 leader line. Both are tight but not overlapping, and the separate leader lines and date labels keep them apart. See the RENDERER note below.
