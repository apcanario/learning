## Findings
1. [error] [accuracy] Section "Monarchy as necessity: Cassius Dio".
   Current: "After narrating the Ides, Dio pauses to judge (44.2)."
   Problem: Dio's reflection on democracy and monarchy comes before his narrative of the murder, not after it: 44.1–2 is the digression, 44.3 opens "It happened as follows", and the killing is at 44.19.
   Fix: "Before narrating the Ides, Dio pauses to judge (44.2)."
   Basis: Dio 44.1–3, Cary's Loeb translation on LacusCurtius (https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cassius_Dio/44%2A.html); 44.2 text at https://www.lexundria.com/dio/44.2/cy.

2. [should-fix] [attribution] Section "Gruen: a Republic that was not dying".
   Current: "His judgement in short was that civil war caused the fall of the Republic, not the fall the war."
   Problem: The sentence is Gruen's own, and famous ("Civil war caused the fall of the Republic, not vice versa", 1974, p. 504); the paraphrase garbles it ("not the fall the war"), and the paragraph's last sentence then presents the words as Morstein-Marx's paper title without saying he was quoting Gruen.
   Fix: "His judgement, in a sentence that became famous, was that \"civil war caused the fall of the Republic, not vice versa\" (p. 504)."
   And replace: "where Robert Morstein-Marx gave his paper the title \"Civil War Caused the Fall of the Republic, not Vice Versa\"." with: "where Robert Morstein-Marx took Gruen's sentence as the title of his paper."
   Basis: University of Minnesota event page for Morstein-Marx's talk, calling the title "the famous judgment of Erich Gruen" (https://cla.umn.edu/premodern/news-events/events/civil-war-caused-fall-republic-not-vice-versa); LibreTexts course page citing Gruen 1974, p. 504 (https://espanol.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Ciencias_Sociales/Aspectos_de_la_historia_del_Imperio_Romano/01%3A_La_transformacion_de_la_Republica/1.02%3A_Tres_teorias_modernas_influyentes); Berkeley programme (https://ahma.berkeley.edu/news/last-generation-roman-republic-50-years). The page number is from a secondary citation, not the book.

3. [should-fix] [pointer] Section "From Mommsen to Syme: the saviour and the oligarchy".
   Current: "his rule lasted five years after the Rubicon and ended in murder by his own officers (11.7);"
   Problem: Deep dive module 11.7 makes the opposite point of mixed leadership: two leaders were Caesar's own marshals (Decimus Brutus, Trebonius) and two were pardoned enemies (Brutus, Cassius). "His own officers" misdescribes the target.
   Fix: "his rule lasted five years after the Rubicon and ended in murder by his own marshals and men he had pardoned (11.7);"
   Basis: rome/r-11.7.html, section on the conspirators ("Two leaders were pardoned enemies; two were Caesar's own marshals").

5. [should-fix] [accuracy] Section "Flower: which republic fell?".
   Current: "Sulla's republic, the one Gruen found working, was a different and more fragile order, imposed by a victor and dismantled within a decade of his death (module 5.3)."
   Problem: Gruen's span is 78 to 49; Flower's Sulla's republic is 81 to 60, so only half of Gruen's years fall in it, and the 50s, Gruen's main evidence, are in Flower's post-republican phases (the report's own next sentence says republican politics ended in 60). Module 5.3 also says Sulla's system was partly undone in 70 (Senate, courts and colonies survived), not dismantled.
   Fix: "Sulla's republic, which held the first half of the years Gruen found working, was a different and more fragile order, imposed by a victor and partly undone within a decade of his death (module 5.3); the 50s, the rest of Gruen's span, Flower counts as no republic at all."
   Basis: Flower's periods from BMCR 2010.02.69 (https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/?p=27392); rome/r-5.3.html ("Sulla's enlarged Senate, standing courts and colonies survived; his government without tribunes did not"); the report's own Gruen section ("from Sulla's death to 49").

6. [should-fix] [figure] Section "Italy, the empire and the army".
   Current: "and Brutus could lend to a provincial city at 48 per cent with a governor's cavalry behind his agent (11.5)."
   Problem: The 48 per cent is Cicero's figure; the sentence gives only a pointer, not whose number it is.
   Fix: "and Brutus, Cicero's letters show, could lend to a provincial city at 48 per cent with a governor's cavalry behind his agent (<em>Letters to Atticus</em> 5.21.10–13; 11.5)."
   Basis: rome/r-11.5.html, Salamis paragraph (Letters to Atticus 5.21.10–13, 6.1.5–7, 6.2.7–9).

7. [minor] [accuracy] Section "Italy, the empire and the army".

   Basis: rome/r-5.2.html (Cinna won over the army at Nola; Rome surrendered late in 87); rome/r-11.2.html (Sulla "crossed in 83 and took Rome in November 82").

10. [minor] [figure] Section "Flower: which republic fell?", timeline caption.
   Current: "the dividing laws of 180 and 139 are deep dive module 11.1's."
   Problem: The dividers are Flower's own (the lex Villia annalis of 180 and the ballot of 139 mark her fourth and fifth republics); 11.1 describes the laws. As written the caption reads as if the division were 11.1's. The writer's doubt on this point is resolved: the dates are right.
   Fix: "the dividing laws of 180 and 139 are Flower's; deep dive module 11.1 describes them."
   Basis: BMCR 2010.02.69 (https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/?p=27392): "180 BC and the lex Villia annalis mark the beginnings of a fourth republic"; the ballot "marks the end of this fourth republic".
