## Findings
1. [error] [attribution] Section "Cornelia: the mother of the Gracchi, made and remade".
   Current: "Filippo Coarelli ("La statue de Cornélie, mère des Gracques, et la crise politique à Rome au temps de Saturninus", 1978) dated the statue to the popular politics of about 100 BC and took the base's text for a later re-cutting that set the Scipionic daughter before the Gracchan mother, in his view probably in the Sullan reaction. Whether the surviving lettering is Republican or belongs to the statue's move into Octavia's portico under Augustus is disputed, and who first erected the statue, and when, is uncertain; that its text was changed is not."
   Problem: Kajava, the article the writer could not open, reports that Coarelli argued the surviving base is the original and that its first text was erased and the present one cut under Augustus. He gives no Sullan date; the "conservative reaction" wording seems to come from a Wikipedia sentence that cites Fantham et al. (1994). Both Coarelli and Kajava date the present lettering to Augustus, so "Republican or Augustan" is not in dispute between them. As written, the box also has no one named on the second side.
   Fix: "Filippo Coarelli ("La statue de Cornélie, mère des Gracques, et la crise politique à Rome au temps de Saturninus", 1978) dated the statue to the popular politics of about 100 BC and argued that the surviving base is the original, its first text erased and the present one cut under Augustus, when the statue stood among Octavia's works. Mika Kajava ("Cornelia Africani f. Gracchorum", <em>Arctos</em> 1989) accepted the Augustan lettering but thought a date near 121 BC possible for the statue, and doubted that its first text can be recovered from Plutarch. Who first erected the statue, and when, is uncertain; that its text was changed is not." Add to the bibliography: Kajava, Mika, "Cornelia Africani f. Gracchorum", *Arctos* 23 (1989) 119–131.
   Basis: Kajava, *Arctos* 23 (1989) 119–131 (academia.edu/2414984): "a perfectly preserved inscription of Augustan date"; Coarelli held that "the original base was never replaced: the Augustan base is in fact the original one", with the inscription "replaced by a new one in Augustan times"; Kajava suggests "around the year 121 BC"; Sulla is not mentioned. Wikipedia, "Cornelia (mother of the Gracchi)", for the likely source of the Sullan reading.

   Basis: not checked: standard chronology.
