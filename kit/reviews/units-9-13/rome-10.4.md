## Findings
1. [should-fix] [attribution] Section "Petrarch, Cola di Rienzo and the measured ruins".
   Current: "What else, he asked, is all history but the praise of Rome?"
   Problem: The quotation has no source and follows straight on the citation of <em>Familiares</em> 24.3–4, so a reader would take it to be from the letters to Cicero. It comes from Petrarch's invective against Jean de Hesdin of 1373: "Quid est enim aliud omnis historia, quam Romana laus?"
   Fix: "What else, he asked, is all history but the praise of Rome (<em>Invectiva contra eum qui maledixit Italie</em>, 1373)?"
   Basis: Ronald Witt, "Francesco Petrarca and the Parameters of Historical Research", <em>Religions</em> 3 (2012) 699, quoting the Latin and citing the <em>Invectiva contra eum qui maledixit Italie</em> in David Marsh's edition (Harvard, 2003), p. 417: https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/3/3/699/htm

2. [should-fix] [accuracy] Section "Shakespeare's Roman plays".
   Current: "its Volumnia and Vergilia are Plutarch's names, not Livy's (module 3.2)"
   Problem: Livy does have a Volumnia: she is Coriolanus' wife, and his mother is Veturia (2.40.1). Shakespeare took from Plutarch the mother Volumnia and the wife Vergilia, which module 3.2 sets out. In the play the wife is spelt Virgilia, as in North.
   Fix: "its mother Volumnia and wife Virgilia follow Plutarch, where Livy has Veturia and Volumnia (module 3.2)"
   Basis: Livy 2.40.1 ("ad Veturiam matrem Coriolani Volumniamque uxorem"); Plutarch, <em>Coriolanus</em> 33–34; rome/r-3.2.html, paragraph on the matrons (Livy's Veturia and Volumnia; Plutarch's Volumnia and Vergilia).

3. [should-fix] [register] Section "Self-test", question 5.
   Current: "even the names Volumnia and Vergilia are Plutarch's, not Livy's."
   Problem: The answer repeats the slip in finding 2: Livy uses the name Volumnia, for the wife.
   Fix: "even the mother Volumnia and wife Virgilia follow Plutarch, where Livy's pair are Veturia and Volumnia."
   Basis: as finding 2.

4. [minor] [accuracy] Section "Rome without an emperor: popes, Franks and a forged gift".
   Current: "had his tutor Gerbert take the name Silvester II"
   Problem: Otto III secured Gerbert's election in 999, but the sources credit Gerbert with choosing the papal name. "Had him take the name" makes it Otto's choice.
   Fix: "made his tutor Gerbert pope in 999 as Silvester II"
   Basis: Catholic Encyclopedia, "Pope Sylvester II" (elected "through the influence of the emperor"; Gerbert "took the name of Sylvester"): https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14371a.htm
