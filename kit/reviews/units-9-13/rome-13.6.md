## Findings
1. [should-fix] [attribution] Section "The lectures: what Lucius wrote and Stobaeus kept".
   Current: "Martha Nussbaum called it "The Incomplete Feminism of Musonius Rufus, Platonist, Stoic, and Roman" (2002): an equal capacity for virtue is argued in principle, while the household's roles in Lecture 4 stay Roman. Gretchen Reydams-Schils, introducing the reissue of Lutz's translation (<em>That One Should Disdain Hardships</em>, 2020), calls his view of marriage as a partnership for the good life significant and revolutionary; reviewing it, Gregory Hays read the apparent proto-feminism as a rhetorical device aimed at male promiscuity (exchange in the <em>New York Review of Books</em>, 29 April 2021)."
   Problem: "Significant and revolutionary" comes from Reydams-Schils' letter in the 2021 NYRB exchange, not (as the sentence implies) from her 2020 introduction, and she applies it to relationships between women and men as a vehicle of the good life, with marriage as the focus. Hays' reading is given only in her words ("a mere rhetorical ploy"); his original review was not checked (the writer's note says so), and his reply in the exchange does not restate it. The box should report the view as hers about him and give his own reply. Also, "called it" has no clear antecedent.
   Fix: "Martha Nussbaum's title gives her verdict, "The Incomplete Feminism of Musonius Rufus, Platonist, Stoic, and Roman" (2002): an equal capacity for virtue is argued in principle, while the household's roles in Lecture 4 stay Roman. Gretchen Reydams-Schils, who introduced the reissue of Lutz's translation (<em>That One Should Disdain Hardships</em>, 2020), called his view that relations between women and men, above all marriage, can be a vehicle of the good life significant and revolutionary, and took Gregory Hays' review to dismiss the apparent proto-feminism as a rhetorical ploy against male promiscuity; Hays answered that she was struck by how far Musonius transcended his time, he by how far he did not (exchange in the <em>New York Review of Books</em>, 29 April 2021)."
   Basis: NYRB, "Which Musonius?", 29 April 2021, https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2021/04/29/which-musonius/ (Reydams-Schils: "significant and revolutionary"; her summary of Hays: "a mere rhetorical ploy to challenge socially accepted patterns of promiscuity in the sexual behavior of men"; Hays' reply: "She is struck by the degree to which he transcended his time, I by the degree to which he didn't").

2. [minor] [attribution] Section "Musonius Rufus: a knight under Nero".
   Current: "whom Juvenal calls his friend and pupil (3.116–117)"
   Problem: Juvenal does not name Celer: his old Stoic informer who killed Barea, "his friend and pupil", is identified with Egnatius Celer by the scholiast and modern commentators. As written, the clause reads as if Juvenal names him; the double "his" is also ambiguous (the writer's own note inverts it, making Celer Barea's pupil, whereas Juvenal makes Barea the informer's pupil).
   Fix: "and whom Juvenal's unnamed old Stoic informer, usually taken to be Celer, killed though Barea was his friend and pupil (3.116–117)"
   Basis: Juvenal 3.116–118 ("Stoicus occidit Baream delator amicum / discipulumque senex"), from knowledge; identification as Celer standard in commentaries; not web-checked.
