# Rome 12.5 writer: claims to check first

1. Norden (1903) traced Anchises' doctrine to a single source he took to be Posidonius: from memory; a search did not confirm it (no fetch reached Norden's text). Remove the name if unconfirmed.
2. Gates dispute: Everett 1900, Otis 1959, Boyle 1986, Austin 1977 ("not true shades") and Norden following Everett in 1926 are taken from search abstracts (Reed, CQ 1973; a Sydney journal article), not the works themselves; article titles for Everett and Otis in the bibliography are from memory.
3. Golden bough dispute: Brooks 1953, Segal 1965–68, Thomas 2001 (Servius' rebuttal shows the reading was current) from a Wikipedia summary; Segal's and Brooks' titles and journal details are from memory.
4. "Does every soul return": Williams 1972 and Austin 1977 (pure souls stay), Page (all reborn) and Conington's "hardest passage" come from a 2005 CAMWS abstract (McIntosh), not the commentaries.
5. Lloyd-Jones' early epic of Heracles' descent: from the Clark 2001 abstract; the *Maia* 1967 article named in the bibliography is from memory.
6. Propertius 4.11.39 for Achilles as Perseus' ancestor: line number from memory.
7. Servius on 6.609 quoting the Twelve Tables' curse on the patron, and Servius on 6.211 rebutting the "hesitation" reading: from memory (the second supported by the Wikipedia summary).
8. Augustine, *City of God* 14.5 quotes 6.719–721 (confirmed in an English translation); 21.13 quotes the purgations (confirmed text, chapter as the translation numbers it).
9. Dante line numbers (*Inferno* 2.13–15, 3.91–93, 3.124–126, 8.13–24, 9.37–48) are from memory, not checked against Petrocchi.
10. Bacchylides 5 as the intermediate for the leaves simile and Pausanias 10.28.2 for Charon in Polygnotus after the *Minyas*: from memory.
