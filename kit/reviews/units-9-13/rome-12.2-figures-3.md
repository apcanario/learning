## Figure 2 (flow): FIX

**Kind.** A linear route of 13 stages, drawn as a serpentine flow with arrows. Fits.

**Order.** Antandros → Thrace → Delos → Crete → Strophades → Actium → Buthrotum → Italy sighted → Etna → Drepanum → Carthage → Sicily again → Cumae. This matches the text: book 3 as l. 63–69, then "The storm of book 1 followed" (l. 69), the Libyan harbour (l. 26), the return to Sicily (l. 83), Cumae (l. 96). The row-turn connectors go from the last box of each row down to the first box of the next, with the arrowhead on the next stage. They read correctly.

**Stage labels**
- Antandros, "fleet built, 3.1–12": "The fleet is built under Ida at Antandros (3.1–12)" (l. 63). ✓
- Thrace, "Polydorus, 3.19–68" (l. 63). ✓
- Delos, "the ancient mother, 3.96": antiquam exquirite matrem (3.96) (l. 63). ✓
- Crete, "Pergamea, 3.121–191" (l. 63). ✓
- Strophades, "Harpies, 3.209–269" (l. 63). ✓
- Actium, "games, 3.280" (l. 65). ✓
- Buthrotum, "Helenus, Andromache, 3.300–351": the body cites only 3.302, 3.325–332, 3.349–351 and so on (l. 67). The self-test gives "Andromache and Helenus live in a little Troy … (3.300–351)" (l. 158). The range is therefore in the text and attached to Helenus and Andromache, and the caption says where it comes from. ✓
- Italy sighted, "3.523" (l. 69). ✓
- Etna, "Achaemenides, 3.588–654" (l. 69). ✓
- Drepanum, "Anchises dies, 3.707–715" (l. 69). ✓
- **Carthage, "after the storm, 1.157–169": fault.** The text attaches 1.157–169 to "a harbour on the Libyan coast" (l. 26: "Seven ships reach a harbour on the Libyan coast (1.157–169)"), not to Carthage. The text reaches Carthage later, when Aeneas watches the city rising (1.430–436) and says o fortunati (1.437) (l. 34). The node puts the harbour's lines under a place the text does not attach them to.

  The caption, correctly, describes this stage as "the landing at a harbour on the Libyan coast … 1.157–169". So caption and drawing disagree on what the stage is. The caption's "that leads to Carthage" is also an inference: the text never says the harbour leads to Carthage, though it is narratively implied.
- Sicily again, "5.8–41": "A storm turns the fleet back to Sicily and to Acestes (5.8–41)" (l. 83). The fleet was earlier at Etna and Drepanum, so "again" is fair. ✓
- Cumae, "6.1–101": "The fleet lands at Cumae. Aeneas visits Apollo's temple … and the Sibyl … (6.1–101)" (l. 96). ✓
