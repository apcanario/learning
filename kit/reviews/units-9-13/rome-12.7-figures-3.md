## Figure 2 (matrix): FIX

**Rows, passages and cells traced to the text.** The rows follow the text's order: ending, Marcellus, shield, Dido. The headers PASSAGE / AUGUSTAN READING / PESSIMISTIC READING read correctly.

- **The ending, 12.950–952.** The passage is from the section heading "Test case: the ending (12.950–952)".
  - Augustan cell, "just anger, punishment": "Galinsky (1988) reads it as justified anger in Aristotle's sense, the punishment of Pallas' killer". The text also says "Roman moralists did not condemn just anger" and "The killing is punishment". The cell is correct.
  - Pessimistic cell, "triumph of furor, breach of Anchises' precept": "Putnam (The Poetry of the Aeneid, 1965; The Humanness of Heroes, 2011) reads the killing as the triumph of furor and a breach of Anchises' precept". This is verbatim.
- **Marcellus, 6.860–886.** The passage is from the heading.
  - Augustan cell, "the dynasty's grief made the nation's": "The Augustan reading hears the dynasty's grief made the nation's". Verbatim.
  - Pessimistic cell, "the parade of glory ends at a grave": "The pessimistic reading hears the parade of glory end at a grave". Verbatim apart from inflection.
- **The shield, 8.626–731.** The passage is from the heading.
  - Augustan cell, "a climax of pietas: he carries his descendants' fame and fates": "The Augustan reading takes the same line as a climax of pietas: … he now carries on his shoulder the fame and fates of his descendants (8.731)". The cell is correct.
  - Pessimistic cell, "rejoicing without knowing the events": **fault, see below.**
- **Dido, book 4.** The text gives no line range for the case, only "Module 12.4 reads book 4".
  - Augustan cell, "pietas obeyed at great cost": "Aeneas' departure is pietas obeyed at great cost". Verbatim, and it is the head claim of the reading.
  - Pessimistic cell, "the mission's first great victim": "Dido is the mission's first great victim". Verbatim, and the head claim.

**Caption.** The caption is correct except where the shield fault reaches it:
- Galinsky (1988) is attached to the ending's Augustan cell, and Putnam (1965; 2011) to its pessimistic cell, as the text attaches them.
- The passages 12.950–952, 6.860–886 and 8.626–731 are each cited for their own row.
- "Book 4, for which the text gives no line range" is correct for the Dido case as a whole. The text cites the curse at 4.607–629 only inside the readings, and no cell draws on the curse.
- "Without a named critic for either side" is true of Marcellus, the shield and Dido.

**Fault.** The text does not give the pessimistic reading of the shield as one claim. It says "Pessimistic readers press three details instead":
1. "Catiline hangs in Tartarus while a Cato, probably Caesar's enemy, gives laws to the righteous (8.666–670)".
2. "Actium, a civil war, is drawn as a foreign one".
3. "Aeneas lifts the shield rejoicing in the images without knowing the events … (8.730)".

The cell shows only the third detail, as if it were the whole pessimistic reading, so the caption's "the two readings as the text states them" does not hold for this cell. The cell also drops the text's "in the images", which leaves "rejoicing" without an object. Every other cell in the matrix carries a reading's head claim. This one carries one of three coordinate details, and the reader cannot tell.

**Fix.** Replace the shield's pessimistic cell with the three details in the text's words:

> Cato giving laws to the righteous; Actium, a civil war, drawn as a foreign one; rejoicing in the images without knowing the events

Then cite the lines in the caption by changing "the shield (8.626–731)" to:

> the shield (8.626–731), whose pessimistic cell gives the three details the text says pessimists press (Cato, 8.666–670; Actium drawn as a foreign war, no line given; Aeneas' rejoicing, 8.730) and whose Augustan cell gives the reading of the same line that follows it (8.731)

The cell will wrap to three lines. That is legible at this column width, and the phone frames already scroll sideways.

If a shorter cell is preferred, keep the single detail but restore the text's wording, "rejoicing in the images without knowing the events". In that case the caption must say so:

> the shield (8.626–731), whose pessimistic cell gives the third of the three details the text says pessimists press, Aeneas' rejoicing at 8.730, set against the Augustan reading of 8.731

**Kind and layout.** The matrix suits four cases set against two readings. Every cell is legible and unclipped in both themes. Across the two phone frames each cell can be read whole. The row tints only separate the rows, carry no meaning and need no legend.
