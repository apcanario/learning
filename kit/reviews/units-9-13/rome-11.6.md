1. [error] [attribution] Bibliography file `bibliography/rome/r-11.6.md`, entry for Henige.
   Current: "David Henige, "He came, he saw, we counted: the historiography and demography of Caesar's Gaul", *Annales de démographie historique* (1998) (title and journal not verified)."

   Fix: "David Henige, "He Came, He Saw, We Counted: The Historiography and Demography of Caesar's Gallic Numbers", *Annales de Démographie Historique* 1998, no. 1."
   Basis: the bibliography of "Caesar's Violence in Gaul", *Crossings* 5 (2025), https://crossingsjournal.ca/index.php/crossings/article/download/347/123/2793. Page numbers not checked: Persée blocks fetching.

2. [should-fix] [attribution] Section "Counting the dead, and the charge of genocide".
   Current: "David Henige (1998) argued that none of these figures can be checked and that they should be read as rhetoric, not data."
   Problem: The writer says this summary is from memory, and no source available here confirms it. What can be traced to Henige is narrower. He argued against the Helvetian census of 1.29: a migrating people could hardly take such a census or carry its tablets, the exact quarter of fighting men is oddly convenient, and the figures were more likely invented than counted. The report already uses his "exactly a quarter" point two paragraphs earlier without naming him.
   Fix: "David Henige (1998) doubted that a migrating people could have taken such a census or carried its tablets, found its exact quarter of fighting men oddly convenient, and judged Caesar's Helvetian figures more likely composed than counted."
   Basis: Wikipedia, "Battle of Bibracte" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bibracte), which summarises Henige 1998. This is a secondary summary; the article itself could not be fetched.

3. [should-fix] [attribution] Section "Counting the dead, and the charge of genocide", dispute box.
   Current: "and mass enslavement, though it could dissolve a people, falls outside the legal definition."
   Problem: Taylor's published summaries do not support "falls outside the legal definition". The Cambridge chapter abstract says the instances of mass enslavement would have the same effect as genocide by dispersing a people, which leans the other way. The rest of the sentence is confirmed: only the Eburones are genocidal, and the Meuse and Avaricum are framed as retribution.
   Fix: "though he notes that the mass enslavements could have had the same effect as genocide, by dispersing a people."
   Basis: Taylor, CA 2012 abstract (https://rune.une.edu.au/web/handle/1959.11/13306); Cambridge World History of Genocide vol. 1 (2023), ch. 11 abstract (https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-world-history-of-genocide/caesars-gallic-genocide/11AFBD8BC6FA6B90BA41B24AD439F0BA).

4. [should-fix] [figure] Section "Counting the dead, and the charge of genocide".
   Current: "In 57 the Nervii are reduced from 60,000 fighting men to scarcely 500 (2.28); in 54 they are besieging Quintus Cicero's camp in force (5.39–49)."
   Problem: At 2.28.2 the 60,000 and 500 are figures the surviving Nervian elders give when they surrender, as Caesar reports them, not Caesar's own count. The distinction matters in a paragraph about Caesar contradicting himself. The BMCR reviewer of Welch and Powell makes this point against Wiseman's use of the passage: the attribution let Caesar keep deniability.
   Fix: "In 57 the surrendering Nervian elders, as Caesar reports them, say their fighting men have been cut from 60,000 to scarcely 500 (2.28); in 54 they are besieging Quintus Cicero's camp in force (5.39–49)."
   Basis: Caesar, *Gallic War* 2.28.2; BMCR 1999.04.16 (https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/1999/1999.04.16/).

5. [should-fix] [register] Section "Writing, publication and Hirtius' eighth book", dispute box.
   Current: "Hirtius' "easily and quickly" was long read as evidence that Caesar wrote all seven books at once, in the winter of 52 to 51, and published them as a block to support his candidacy for a second consulship."
   Problem: The box names a scholar only for the year-by-year side (Wiseman). The single-publication view is held by nobody named. Matthias Gelzer is a standard named holder of that view.
   Fix: "Hirtius' "easily and quickly" was long read, by Matthias Gelzer (<em>Caesar: Politician and Statesman</em>, 1968) among others, as evidence that Caesar wrote all seven books at once, in the winter of 52 to 51, and published them as a block to support his candidacy for a second consulship." Then add to the bibliography: "Matthias Gelzer, *Caesar: Politician and Statesman*, trans. Peter Needham (Oxford: Blackwell, 1968)."
   Basis: CAMWS 2015 abstract "Bellum Gallicum publication" (https://camws.org/sites/default/files/meeting2015/Abstracts2015/205.BellumGallicumPublication.pdf), which cites Gelzer p. 104 and Meier for unitary publication and Wiseman and Krebs for serial publication.

6. [should-fix] [figure] Section "Self-test", question 4.

   Fix: "reduced, by their own envoys' account, to scarcely 500 fighting men in 57 (2.28)"
   Basis: Caesar, *Gallic War* 2.28.2.
