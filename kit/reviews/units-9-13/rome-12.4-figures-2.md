## Figure 4 (timeline): FIX

The drawing traces correctly:

- "about 397–400 / Augustine, Confessions": the text says "The Confessions (about 397–400)". The dot sits on about 397, the range's first year (house rule).
- "early fourteenth century / Dante, Inferno 5": the text says "the Inferno, written in the early fourteenth century". It cites 5.61–62 and 5.85, so "Inferno 5" is the text's canto. The dot sits at about 1308, inside the stated span; at this scale it implies no year the text does not give.
- "1594 / Marlowe's Dido printed": the text says "printed in 1594". The label rightly says printed and not written ("probably written between 1587 and 1593").
- "1689 / Dido and Aeneas at Chelsea (traditional date)": the text says "a performance by the girls of Josias Priest's boarding school in Chelsea, usually dated 1689" and "The traditional account … makes the opera a school piece of 1689". The qualifier is carried.
- "1863 / Les Troyens à Carthage staged": the text says "staged … as Les Troyens à Carthage at the Théâtre-Lyrique in 1863".

The dots are in chronological order and placed correctly against the ticks: 1594, 1689 and 1863 measure within two years of their positions. The stacked labels do not overlap in either theme. The pulled-in "1900" tick label and the phone scroll are house rules.

Fault, in the caption: "the traditional 1689 for the Chelsea performance of Purcell's opera, disputed by Pinnock and Wood (Early Music, 1992)" attaches the wrong dispute to the wrong item.

- Per the text, Pinnock and Wood disputed the account that makes the opera a school piece of 1689. They argued it began earlier as a court work, perhaps as early as 1684, "with the Chelsea performance a revival". The text does not say they disputed the Chelsea performance's date.
- What the text sets against the 1689 date for the Chelsea link is the letter of 1688: "usually dated 1689, though a letter of 1688 already links the opera to the school".
- The figure itself shows only "(traditional date)", not Pinnock and Wood, so naming them also claims more than the figure shows.

Ovid's Heroides are undated there and not drawn.
