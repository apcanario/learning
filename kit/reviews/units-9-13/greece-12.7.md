## Findings
1. [error] [pointer] Section "Diogenes of Babylon and Antipater of Tarsus".
   Current: "Carneades' charge of circularity is module 12.8's."
   Problem: Written module 12.8 says nothing about Carneades' attack on the Stoic end or any charge of circularity. Its Carneades paragraph covers the persuasive impression, theology, fate and the embassy. "Circularity" appears there only among Agrippa's modes. Module 12.8's prompt does not list this topic either, so the pointer is false.
   Fix: "A. A. Long (\"Carneades and the Stoic <em>Telos</em>\", 1967) reads both restatements as answers to Carneades." (or: delete the sentence)
   Basis: greece/r-12.8.html read in full (no "Antipater", no discussion of the telos); 12.8 prompt in greek-syllabus.html; A. A. Long, "Carneades and the Stoic Telos", Phronesis 12 (1967) 59–90 (from knowledge).

2. [should-fix] [pointer] Section "Panaetius and Posidonius: the hinge to Rome".
   Current: "included the satire on Athenion, the philosopher who took Athens over to Mithridates in 88 (Athenaeus 5.211d–215b; EK F253), the prelude to Sulla's siege in module 7.3."
   Problem: Module 7.3 names the tyrant who took Athens to Mithridates as Aristion ("Athens under the tyrant Aristion joined him"), following Plutarch and Appian. A reader following the pointer finds a different name with no explanation. Whether Posidonius' Athenion and Aristion are the same man, or two tyrants in quick succession, is unresolved.
   Fix: "included the satire on Athenion, the Peripatetic who took Athens over to Mithridates in 88 (Athenaeus 5.211d–215b; EK F253), the prelude to Sulla's siege in module 7.3; whether he is the tyrant Aristion of Plutarch and Appian, as module 7.3 names him, or Aristion's predecessor is disputed."
   Basis: greece/r-7.3.html ("Mithridates, Sulla and Pompey"); Athenaeus 5.211e (Athenion a Peripatetic); Wikipedia, "Aristion" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristion): "no universally accepted resolution", two successive tyrants the commonest explanation. About +22 words, still inside the band.

3. [minor] [accuracy] Section "Chrysippus: propositions and arguments".
   Current: "the strictest view, usually credited to Chrysippus,"
   Problem: Sextus (Outlines 2.110–112) lists four criteria. The fourth, by "emphasis", is stricter than connection: it makes even "if it is day, it is day" false. Connection is therefore not the strictest.
   Fix: "a stricter view, usually credited to Chrysippus,"
   Basis: Sextus, Outlines of Pyrrhonism 2.110–112 (from knowledge of the text).
