# Rome 13.6, Musonius Rufus and Epictetus: figure check 2

Checked against `text.txt` and `captions.md` only, with every desktop and phone image looked at in both themes. Phone frames that show only part of a figure, and the house timeline style, are not counted as faults.

## Figure 1 (timeline): PASS

Every label traced to the section "Musonius Rufus: a knight under Nero":

- **AD 60, "Rubellius Plautus sent to Asia".** Text: "whom Nero in 60 told to withdraw to his estates in Asia (Tacitus, Annals 14.22)". Caption: 14.22 (60). Matches.
- **AD 62, "Plautus killed; Musonius had counselled constancy".** Text: "When the killers came in 62 ... urged him to wait for death with constancy (14.59)". Caption: 14.59 (62). Matches.
- **AD 65, "Pisonian conspiracy; Musonius exiled to Gyaros".** Text: "After the conspiracy of Piso in 65 Musonius was himself sent away ... (Annals 15.71). He went to Gyaros". The text places Musonius on Gyaros through Life of Apollonius 7.16 (the spring "there"). Annals 4.30 is about Tiberius refusing the island, not about Musonius, so leaving it out of the caption is right. Caption: 15.71 (65), 7.16 (Gyaros). Matches.
- **AD 67, "Isthmus canal begun; Philostratus' Musonius digs".** Text: "Nero's canal through the Isthmus of Corinth, begun in 67", with the scene cited to 5.19. The text gives the year 67 without a passage, and the caption says so. The label keeps the text's doubt about the source by writing "Philostratus' Musonius" (the text calls it "the same romance" and "a novelistic life"). Matches.
- **"December 69", "Musonius lectures the Flavian soldiers".** Text: "In December 69 ... lectured them on the blessings of peace (Histories 3.81)". The month is kept. Caption: 3.81 (December 69). Matches.
- **AD 70, "Musonius prosecutes Egnatius Celer and wins".** Text: "In 70 he prosecuted Publius Egnatius Celer ... and won (Histories 4.10, 4.40)". Caption: 4.10, 4.40 (70). Matches.
- **Vespasian's expulsion.** The text gives it no date (Dio 65(66).13.2), and the caption says correctly that it is not drawn. The caption's range of 60–70 matches the dates drawn.

**Kind and legibility.** All the events are single points, so a timeline is the right kind. The years are spaced in proportion (60, 62, 65, 67, 69, 70). No label is clipped or overlapping on desktop, in either theme. The AD 67 label sits under the AD 65 label, but there is a clear gap between them, and its own date heading and leader line tie it to 67. The phone frames show the whole figure across two frames, with nothing lost at the joins.

**Optional points (not faults):**

- (a) "after his fall" in the caption could be read as Musonius' fall. "Musonius' public career under Nero and after Nero's fall, 60–70" would remove the doubt.
- (b) The December 69 point sits at the start of the 69 tick, so the lecture looks about a year before the 70 prosecution. In fact the text puts them only weeks apart. If the data accepts fractional years, placing the point at about 69.9 would show this. The year-level spacing does not contradict the text, so this is not a fault.

## Figure 2 (tree): PASS

Every node traced to "What is up to us":

- **"Things".** Text: "Some things are up to us, some are not."
- **"Up to us: free, unhindered, unimpeded".** Text: "The first are by nature free, unhindered and unimpeded ... (1.2)".
- **"Not up to us: weak, slavish, hindered, another's".** Text: "the second weak, slavish, hindered and another's (1.2)".
- **"judgement, impulse, desire, aversion".** Text: "Up to us are judgement, impulse, desire and aversion", inside the division the text cites as 1.1.
- **"body, property, reputation, office".** Text: "Not up to us are body, property, reputation and office" (1.1).

**Caption.** It attributes the examples to Handbook 1.1 and the qualities to 1.2, as the text cites them. It claims only what the figure shows.

**Kind and legibility.** The figure is a binary division with each class above its members, so a tree fits. The text is readable in both themes and nothing is clipped. On a phone the two branches fall in separate frames, which is the house rule.

**Optional point (not a fault):** the text says "by nature free". If the box should carry the full wording, it would read "Up to us: by nature free, unhindered, unimpeded".

## Figure 3 (flow): PASS

Every box traced to "Impressions, assent and the three fields":

- **"Desire and aversion: desire only what is up to us; the most urgent, because it governs the passions".** Text: "The first, the most urgent because it governs the passions, trains desire and aversion: desire only what is up to us."
- **"Impulse to action: the appropriate acts (kathēkonta) owed to others".** Text: "The second trains impulse to action, the appropriate acts (kathēkonta) owed to others."
- **"Assent: secures assent against error, even in sleep, drink or depression; for those already advanced".** Text: "The third secures assent against error even in sleep, drink or depression, and belongs to those already advanced."

**Caption.** "in the order he sets them ... after Discourses 3.2.1–5". Text: "Epictetus ordered the training in three fields (topoi, Discourses 3.2.1–5)". The text cites this one passage for all three fields, so naming one passage for all three is correct. The text's other citation here, 3.2.6–8 (the mockery of students who polish syllogisms), is not drawn and is rightly left out.

**Kind and legibility.** The arrows match the text's order: the first field is the most urgent and the third belongs to the advanced, so a flow fits. The grey monospace body text on the tinted boxes is low in contrast in dark mode but still readable. Nothing is clipped. On a phone the third box falls in the second frame, which is the house rule.
