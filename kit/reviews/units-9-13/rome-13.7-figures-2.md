# Rome 13.7, "Marcus Aurelius: a life": figure check 2

Material: `text.txt`, `captions.md`, and every desktop and phone image of figures 1–3 in dark and light. House rules (natural-size sideways scroll below 768 px, wide-figure scroll on desktop, short span bars above the axis, range events on the first year) were not counted as faults.

## Figure 1 (tree): FIX

**Trace**
- Root "Marcus' teachers"; branches "Rhetoric" and "Philosophy". Text: Dio names Rusticus and Apollonius as Marcus' Stoic teachers, and Fronto and Herodes as his rhetors (72(71).35). Matches.
- "Fronto (Latin)": "the leading Latin orator of the age ... He taught Marcus rhetoric". Matches.
- "Herodes Atticus (Greek)": "His Greek rhetoric teacher, Herodes Atticus". Matches.
- "Diognetus" under Philosophy: "Diognetus, who first acquainted him with philosophy ... (Meditations 1.6)". Matches.
- "Junius Rusticus": "Quintus Junius Rusticus", Stoic teacher per Dio 72(71).35. Matches.
- "Sextus of Chaeronea": "Sextus, the Boeotian philosopher (71.1.2), whom the Historia Augusta calls Plutarch's nephew, from Chaeronea (3.2)". Matches. The sources do not conflict here.
- "Apollonius of Chalcedon": the text writes "Apollonius of Nicomedia (the Historia Augusta's Apollonius of Chalcedon, 2.7)". The text gives two cities. It names him first by Dio's Nicomedia and does not choose between them. The figure takes the person from Dio 72(71).35 but prints only the Historia Augusta's city, so the box states more than the text does and drops the text's "or". The caption mentions the variant, but the drawing does not.
- Caption: each passage is attached correctly (Dio 72(71).35 for the four, Marcus 2.7 for Chalcedon, Dio 71.1.2 and Marcus 3.2 for Sextus, Meditations 1.6 for Diognetus, and the HA list 2.2–3.6 not drawn). These are all the teachers the report names. Nothing in it goes beyond what is drawn. There are no dates.
- Kind and readability: a two-level grouping, which suits a tree. No clipping or overlap, and both themes are legible. The third phone frame holds only the tail of the last box, which is allowed by the house scroll rule.

**Fix**
- Box: "Apollonius of Chalcedon" → **"Apollonius (Nicomedia or Chalcedon)"**.
- Caption, first sentence after "by subject.": **"Fronto, Herodes, Rusticus and Apollonius from Dio 72(71).35, which places Apollonius at Nicomedia; the Historia Augusta, Marcus 2.7, has Chalcedon."** The rest of the caption stays as it is.

## Figure 2 (flow): FIX

**Trace** (the boxes follow the order of the section "175: Avidius Cassius and Faustina")
1. "False report of Marcus' death / spring 175": "That spring, on a false report that Marcus had died ... (Dio 72(71).22–23)". Matches. The passage appears in the caption.
2. "Cassius proclaimed emperor / Egypt and most of Syria accept him": same sentence, same passage. Matches.
3. "Senate declares him a public enemy / Historia Augusta, Marcus 24.9". Matches.
4. "Cassius killed by the centurion Antonius / after three months and six days (Dio 72(71).27)": "a centurion named Antonius cut Cassius down; his dream of empire had lasted three months and six days (72(71).27)". Matches.
5. "Papers destroyed unread, most supporters pardoned / Dio 72(71).28–30; Marcus 25.6". Matches.
6. "Faustina dies at Halala / winter 175 (Marcus 26.4)". Matches. In the text this comes after the Faustina-complicity paragraphs, so the order is right.
7. "Athens: Eleusis and the chairs / 176 (Marcus 27.1)": the text reads "in 176 he was initiated at Eleusis (Marcus 27.1) and endowed the chairs of philosophy and rhetoric (Greek module 7.4; ...)". The text attaches Marcus 27.1 to the initiation only. For the chairs it gives no passage, only a cross-reference to a module. **The box attaches Marcus 27.1 to the chairs as well.**
- Omissions (the Iazyges terms 72(71).17, the speech 72(71).24–26, the head 72(71).28 / Marcus 25.3) do not make the sequence false, and the caption does not claim it is complete.
- Caption: it says "with the passage the text cites for each" and names only the Dio 72(71).22–23 attachment. It does not name the passage for each item one by one. Through box 7 it repeats the error that ties Marcus 27.1 to the chairs.
- Kind and readability: a told sequence suits a flow. The wrap-around arrow is clear. No clipping or overlap, and both themes are legible.

**Fix**
- Box 7: **"Athens: initiated at Eleusis / 176 (Marcus 27.1)"**. If the chairs are kept, use **"Athens: Eleusis and the chairs / 176 (Marcus 27.1, for Eleusis)"**.
- Caption: **"The revolt of 175 and its aftermath in the order the section "175: Avidius Cassius and Faustina" tells them. The text cites Dio 72(71).22–23 for the false report, the proclamation, Egypt and Syria's acceptance and its date in the spring of 175; Historia Augusta, Marcus 24.9, for the Senate's declaration; Dio 72(71).27 for Cassius' death after three months and six days; Dio 72(71).28–30 and Marcus 25.6 for the papers and the pardons; Marcus 26.4 for Faustina's death in the winter of 175; and Marcus 27.1 for the initiation at Eleusis in 176."** If the chairs stay in box 7, add: **"It gives the chairs at Athens no passage of their own."**

## Renderer note (all three figures; not a verdict)

In the phone frames the caption sits inside the scrolling figure at about 540 px wide. Each caption line therefore runs past the 390 px frame and is cut mid-word (for example "Ru…", "Augu…", "Chroni…"). To read the caption, the reader has to scroll back and forth on every line. I counted this under the house rule that a figure keeps its natural size below 768 px, so it does not change any verdict. If it is to be met, it needs a renderer or theme change (wrap the figcaption to the frame width outside the scroll container), not a change to the figure data.
