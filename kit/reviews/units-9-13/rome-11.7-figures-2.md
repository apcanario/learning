# Rome 11.7, The Ides of March: figure check 2

Checked against `text.txt` and `captions.md` only. I looked at every image: the desktop and phone frames of both figures, in dark and light. Sideways scrolling on phone and the missing cue line are house rules, so neither counts against a figure.

## Figure 1 (flow): FIX

**The drawing, box by box.** Each box is traced to the text below.

- **Calpurnia's dream, "the night before".** Omens and warnings says "That night Caesar dreamed … Calpurnia dreamed …" (81.3). It follows "The day before…", so "the night before" is right, and the label carries the qualifier that this falls before the morning. Plutarch's two forms are cited as Caesar 63. Both of the caption's passages trace.
- **Decimus fetches Caesar, "takes him by the hand".** The text: "he took Caesar by the hand and led him out (Caesar 64; Suetonius, Julius 81.4)". Traces. Nicolaus's bare "Brutus" is not cited for this box, which is correct.
- **Artemidorus' note, "carried unread".** The text: "On the way … pressed on him a note … which Caesar carried unread into the meeting (Plutarch, Caesar 65; Suetonius, Julius 81.4, without the name)". Traces, and the caption keeps the qualifier "without the name".
- **Popilius Laenas, "a private word at the litter".** The text: he "spoke privately to Caesar at length as he left his litter" (Appian 2.116; Plutarch, Brutus 16). Traces. "A private word" is looser than "at length" but claims nothing beyond the text. If the writer wants it exact: "speaks privately at length at the litter". Optional, not a fault.
- **Order of Artemidorus and Popilius.** The text puts the note "on the way" and Popilius "as he left his litter", that is, at the end of the journey. The order holds.
- **Trebonius holds Antony, "outside".** The text: "Trebonius kept Antony talking outside" (Cicero, Philippics 2.34; Plutarch, Brutus 17; Appian 2.117; Dio 44.19). Traces. The text then turns to "Inside, Tillius Cimber…", so Trebonius before Cimber matches the text's order. The caption's closing sentence on Plutarch, Caesar 66 (Decimus) matches the text's "a slip within Plutarch's own work".
- **Cimber's petition, "seizes the toga".** The text: Cimber "approached as if to plead for his exiled brother … seized his toga at both shoulders", and Caesar's cry "(Julius 82.1)". Traces. "Petition" is fair for the pretended plea.
- **Casca strikes first.** The murder section: "One of the Cascas struck from behind … (82.2)", with Plutarch's Latin and Greek cries "(Caesar 66)". The conspirator list adds Casca "who struck first". Traces.
- **Caesar falls, "at the base of Pompey's statue".** The text: "He fell at the base of Pompey's statue", with no passage. The sentences before it cite Caesar 66, Appian 2.117 and Julius 82.2 for the collapse. The caption names those three and says the place has no passage. Correct.
- **The rest of the caption.** The three section names are right: Calpurnia and Artemidorus are in Omens and warnings, Decimus and Popilius in The morning and the hall of Pompey, the rest in The murder. Spurinna's answer is indeed dated only "On the morning" (81.4), and the caption says it is left out of the chain. That is correct.
- **Kind and legibility.** A left-to-right chain that wraps once, with an elbow connector from Popilius to Trebonius, is the right kind for a sequence. Boxes, labels and arrows are readable in both themes, with nothing clipped or overlapping. The phone frames split the figure cleanly.

**Fault.** The caption cites Cimber and Casca together: "Cimber and Casca (Suetonius, *Julius* 82.1–2; Plutarch, *Caesar* 66)". They are two separate boxes, and the text cites them differently. For Cimber it cites only Julius 82.1. Plutarch, Caesar 66 is cited only for Casca's cries, and Julius 82.2 only for Casca's blow. So the caption does not give each item its passage, and it attaches Caesar 66 to Cimber, which the text does not do.

**Fix.** In the caption, replace "Cimber and Casca (Suetonius, *Julius* 82.1–2; Plutarch, *Caesar* 66)" with:

> Cimber (Suetonius, *Julius* 82.1); Casca (Suetonius, *Julius* 82.2; Plutarch, *Caesar* 66)

No change to the drawing.

## Figure 2 (matrix): PASS

**The drawing.** Each row is traced to the text below. The columns are Nicolaus, Plutarch, Suetonius, Appian and Dio: the five continuous narratives of the opening section. Cicero is rightly left out, since he wrote "never a narrative". Every number sits under the author whose number it is, and Plutarch's two lives are told apart in the cells.

- **Who led Caesar out.**
  - Nicolaus 'Brutus': the text has "Nicolaus names only 'Brutus'" (23–24). The quotation marks keep the bare name.
  - Plutarch Decimus (Caesar 64) and Suetonius Decimus (Julius 81.4): both are cited to the Decimus sentence.
  - Appian and Dio blank: the text cites nothing for them.
- **Who kept Antony outside.**
  - Plutarch "Decimus (Caesar 66); Trebonius (Brutus 17)": matches "Plutarch's Caesar gives the task to Decimus (66)" and the Trebonius citation of Brutus 17.
  - Appian Trebonius (2.117) and Dio Trebonius (44.19): both trace.
  - Nicolaus and Suetonius blank: correct, as the text cites neither here.
- **Wounds.**
  - Nicolaus 35 (24) traces.
  - Plutarch "23 (Caesar 66); many (Brutus 17)" traces.
  - Suetonius 23 (Julius 82.3) and Appian 23 (2.117) trace.
  - Dio "many" (44.19) traces: the text says "say only many".
- **Last words.**
  - Nicolaus "none recorded": the text says "Nicolaus records none". There is no passage, and the caption says so.
  - Plutarch "Latin cry to Casca; no words to Brutus, head covered": matches "accursed Casca, what are you doing?" and "Plutarch has no words for Brutus, only the covered head" (Caesar 66). The Latin cry is the last speech the text gives in Plutarch, so placing it in this row does not overstate.
  - Suetonius "a groan; some say kai su, teknon": matches "uttered only a groan at the first blow, without a word, 'though some have written' … kai su, teknon" (Julius 82.2). The qualifier "some" is kept.
  - Dio "nothing (truest); some add kai su, teknon": matches "the truest account is that he could say nothing, but some add the Greek words" (44.19).
  - Appian blank: the text cites nothing for him.
- **Caption.** The three sections named are the right sources: The morning and the hall of Pompey, The murder (for Antony, the wounds and Plutarch's cry) and The last words. Every passage the caption lists, row by row, matches the text's citation for that cell. The caption claims nothing the figure does not show, and it says what a blank cell means.
- **Kind and legibility.** Five sources against four points suits a matrix. Headers and cells read correctly in both themes, and blank cells stand apart from filled ones in dark and light. Nothing is clipped or overlapping. The phone frames split between the Plutarch and Suetonius columns, which the house rule allows.

**Optional, not a fault.** The Suetonius cell could read "some have written kai su, teknon" to match the text's wording. "Some say" carries the same qualifier.
