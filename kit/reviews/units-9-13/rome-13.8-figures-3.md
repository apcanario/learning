Material: `text.txt`, `captions.md`, and the desktop and phone renders of figures 02 and 03 in both themes. Every image was viewed. Figure 1 has already passed and was not checked.

## Figure 2 (tree): PASS

**Labels traced to the text**
- "Marcus' notes, bulk usually dated to the 170s": The text, para. 3: "Most scholars put the bulk of the work in the 170s, which is as far as the evidence goes." "Usually dated" keeps the "most scholars" qualifier. The sentence has no citation.
- "Old copy, falling to pieces": The transmission, para. 1: Arethas "had long owned an old copy of the emperor's most profitable book, so worn it was falling to pieces".
- "Arethas' copy, before 907 (lost)": same paragraph. He wrote "before 907" that he "had had it copied", so the copy existed before 907. "His copy is lost." The sentence has no citation.
- "Palatine manuscript (lost), printed as the first edition, Zurich 1558–59": The transmission, para. 2. The first edition came from the Palatine manuscript, printed at Zurich, preface October 1558 and title page 1559. "The manuscript was then lost" (Sellars, "The Early Modern Reception of the Meditations", 2025).
- "Vaticanus Graecus 1950, fourteenth century": The text, para. 1, and The transmission, para. 2. That sentence carries no citation. The Sellars citation in the next sentence covers the title and the book division, not the manuscript.

**Descent and kind.** The chain runs Marcus → old copy → Arethas' copy, then splits to the Palatine manuscript and Vaticanus Graecus 1950. That follows the text's order. The split matches "usually thought to be the ancestor of the manuscripts we know". The caption's "as it is usually supposed, not proven" carries the text's qualifier. A tree is the right kind for one parent with two surviving lines.

**Caption.** Each claim matches what is drawn:
- It names both sections correctly.
- It marks the 170s dating and Vaticanus Graecus 1950 as given without a cited source.
- It gives the old copy and Arethas' copy from his letter to Demetrius of Heraclea, dated before 907 without a passage.
- It attaches the Palatine manuscript and the first edition to Sellars, "Early Modern Reception" (2025). That is the paragraph's only citation, and it closes the account of the manuscript's loss at the printer's.
- It says the Suda's and Tzetzes' quotations are not drawn. That is true: the text does not say which copy they came from.

**Rendering.** All five boxes are fully legible in both themes, desktop and phone, with nothing clipped or overlapping. On phone the tree scrolls sideways across two frames at natural size, which is a house rule. The connector lines are faint (about 1.1:1 against the background in dark, 1.2:1 in light). They are still visible in both themes, and the vertical stacking carries the descent on its own. This is a theme setting, not a fault in this figure (RENDERER note, not blocking).

**Fault: Lipsius has a cited source, but the caption lists him as uncited.** The caption puts "Lipsius (1584)" among "dates the text gives without a cited source". The self-test answer reads: "Lipsius built neo-Stoicism in On Constancy (1584) chiefly on Seneca and quoted Marcus only occasionally … (Sellars 2025)". So the text cites Sellars 2025 for exactly what the label says: the work, the date and "built chiefly on Seneca". In the body, the same claim runs straight into "(Sellars 2025)" at the end of the next sentence. The text gives this citation only in short form, and two 2025 works by Sellars are cited in the report. The caption therefore cannot name the specific work.

**Fix.** In the caption:
1. Delete "Lipsius (1584), " from the uncited list, so that it reads "Arethas' letter (before 907), Tzetzes (c. 1150), Casaubon (1634), Gataker (1652), the Exeter workshop (2012) and Holiday and Hanselman (2016), dates the text gives without a cited source."
2. After the Zurich clause, add: "Lipsius' On Constancy (1584), built chiefly on Seneca, after Sellars (2025), whom the text cites here in short form without saying which of his two 2025 works;"
