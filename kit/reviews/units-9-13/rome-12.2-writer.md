# Rome 12.2 writer's unsure list (Opus 5.5, 1 November 2026)

1. Austin's commentary on book 1 (1971) as the source for Cato the Younger as the statesman of 1.148–153: known only through a 1998 *Greece & Rome* note citing Austin for Cato; the commentary itself not checked. "Others have seen Augustus" is stated unnamed.
2. Pöschl, *Die Dichtkunst Virgils* (1950), reading the storm and Neptune's calm as the key to the poem: from memory; publisher (Rohrer, Innsbruck and Vienna) not checked.
3. Duckworth (1962): the sea/land alternation and the thirds are given unnamed; only the mathematical proportions are credited to him. The 1962 date and title are checked.
4. Pisander (Macrobius, *Saturnalia* 5.2.4–5): "usually taken for a writer of the third century AD" rests on Ceccarelli's *Brill's New Jacoby* summary (Pisander of Laranda favoured, the question open).
5. Servius on 2.557 (Pompey, *ingens* for *Magnus*, and Pacuvius): checked only in a teaching handout quoting the note, not in Thilo–Hagen.
6. Aeneid line numbers are from memory of the OCT. Most likely to be off: 1.402–409, 1.520–558, 1.586–593, 2.354, 2.428, 2.707–720, 3.325–332, 3.435–440, 5.159–182, 5.232–243, 5.719–740, 5.746–761, 6.136–235, 6.268–416.
7. Homeric parallels from memory: *Odyssey* 5.297 and 5.306–312 (the storm speech), 7.19–36 (Athena as a girl), 10.156–184 (the stag), 12.208–212 (Odysseus before Scylla), 13.96–112 (Phorcys); *Iliad* 16.482–484 (the felled tree), 22.93–95 (the snake), 23.262–652, 23.653–699, 23.773–777, 23.850–883.
8. Suetonius, *Augustus* 18.2 for Nicopolis and its games: from memory, not checked; the text no longer says they were held every four years.
9. Pausanias 10.26.1 (Creusa and Eurydice) and Dionysius 1.49–51, 1.64, 1.72.2–4 were checked against online Loeb translations. The section number 10.26.1 is inferred from the page range, since the page printed no section numbers.
10. Beroe's "seventh summer" (5.626) set beside 1.755–756 as a seam: the inconsistency is a commonplace; the line number 5.626 is from memory.
