# Rome 13.3, The rival schools at Rome: figure check

Checked against `text.txt` (figures removed), `captions.md` and the desktop and phone renders of both figures in dark and light. The checker did not write the report or its figures. Sideways scrolling in phone frames and short span bars are house rules and are not counted as faults.

## Figure 1 (matrix): FIX

**Trace**

- Rows follow the module's section order: Philodemus and Epicureans in public life, Cicero the sceptic, Antiochus, Peripatetics, Nigidius and the Sextii, Cynics. The Stoics come last as the comparison row. This is correct.
- Epicureans, "Piso, Cassius, Atticus". Piso: "Cicero hails Piso as an Epicurean" (Against Piso 37) and "as Piso and Cassius were". Cassius: "became one in the 40s" (Letters to Friends 15.16, 15.19). Atticus: "Cicero's letters treat him as an Epicurean" (On the Laws 1.21; On Ends 5.3). All traced. Chair: Philostratus and Lucian both list Epicureans. Traced.
- Academy, "Cicero, Lucullus, Varro, Brutus". All four are in the text, but the text puts them in two different schools. Cicero is the New Academy ("a follower of the New Academy to the end"). Lucullus, Varro and Brutus belong to Antiochus' Old Academy. The text also lists the New Academy and the Old Academy as separate rivals in "Why the Stoa won". Brutus's school is disputed in the text: Moles finds Stoic traits, Sedley reads him as an Antiochean. The figure marks Sextius "(disputed)" but gives Brutus no such mark. Chair: "chair (Platonic)". The text says "a Platonist chair, not Cicero's scepticism" and names "Platonists" (Philostratus 566; Eunuch 3). Putting "Platonic chair" against a row headed by Cicero suggests his Academy held the chair, which is the opposite of what the text says. The text also says "Platonist", not "Platonic".
- Peripatetics, "Cratippus, Cicero's son". Plutarch, Cicero 24; On Duties 1.1, 3.5–6; Letters to Friends 16.21.3. Traced. Chair: Philostratus, Lucian. Traced.
- Pythagoreans, "Nigidius, Quintus Sextius (disputed)". Timaeus 1; Seneca, Letters 64.2 against Claudianus Mamertus 2.8 ("Where sources disagree"). Traced, with the qualifier carried. "None": "there was no chair for Cynics or Pythagoreans". Traced.
- Cynics, "Demetrius, Peregrinus". Seneca, On Benefits 7.8, Letters 62.3; Lucian, Peregrinus 17 (trained under the Cynic Agathobulus). Traced. "None". Traced.
- Stoics, "Cato, Seneca". Seneca "wrote as a Stoic" (Natural Questions 7.32.2, in "Why the Stoa won"). Cato: "from Cato on, an example of how to die" (same section). Traced. Chair: Philostratus, Lucian. Traced. The caption, however, does not name the section the Stoics row comes from.
- Headers "ADHERENTS NAMED HERE" and "CHAIR AT ATHENS, 176" read correctly. "176": "the endowment of 176" (Dio 72(71).31.3). Traced.
- The caption says "with the adherents it names". The figure shows a selection. It leaves out Philodemus, the main Epicurean of the first section, as well as Siro, Torquatus, Velleius, Trebatius, Philo, Antiochus, Pupius Piso and Anaxilaus.
- Rendering: legible in both themes, no clipping or overlap. On a phone the chair column scrolls into frame 2 (house rule).

**Fixes**

1. Split the Academy row so it does not merge the two Academies or attach the Platonist chair to Cicero:
   - Row "New Academy": "Philo, Cicero". Chair: "none (the chair was Platonist, not sceptical)".
   - Row "Old Academy (Antiochus)": "Lucullus, Varro, Brutus (disputed)". Chair: "Platonist chair (its ethics passed into Middle Platonism)".

   If the matrix must stay at six rows, use one row headed "Academy: New (Cicero) and Old (Lucullus, Varro, Brutus, disputed)" with the chair cell "Platonist chair, not Cicero's scepticism". Either way, the chair cell says "Platonist", not "Platonic".
2. Add Philodemus to the Epicureans cell: "Philodemus, Piso, Cassius, Atticus". He is the school's central figure in the text.
3. Rewrite the caption so it names a source for every row and claims only a selection:

   > Some of the adherents this module names for each school, and whether each school held one of Marcus Aurelius' salaried chairs at Athens in 176. Epicureans: the sections Philodemus, Piso and the villa and Epicureans in public life. New and Old Academy: Cicero the sceptic and Antiochus and the Old Academy. Peripatetics: The Peripatetics and Cratippus. Pythagoreans: Nigidius, the Sextii and the Pythagoreans. Cynics: The Cynics under the empire. Stoics: Why the Stoa won, and the chairs of 176. The chairs: Dio 72(71).31.3, Philostratus, Lives of the Sophists 566, and Lucian, Eunuch 3 for the four salaried schools, as recorded in Greek module 7.4. Brutus and Sextius are disputed, as the text's "Where sources disagree" passages show.

## Figure 2 (timeline): FIX (plus one RENDERER item)

**Trace**

- 88 BC, "Philo flees to Rome": "fled to Rome in 88" (Cicero the sceptic). The text gives no passage for the year. Its parenthesis cites Greek module 12.8 for Philo's doctrine and module 13.1 for Cicero's student years. "Brutus 306" appears nowhere in the text, so the caption's "citing Greek module 12.8 (Cicero, Brutus 306)" is a citation the text does not make.
- 79 BC, "Cicero hears Antiochus at Athens": "at Athens for six months in 79 (Brutus 315; module 13.1)". Traced, and the caption is correct.
- 55 BC, "Cicero's Against Piso": "Attacking Piso in 55". No passage for the year. The caption says so correctly.
- 45 BC, "Cassius' Epicurean letter; Nigidius dies in exile". "In January 45 Cicero teased him (15.16.1), and Cassius replied (15.19.2–3)"; the self-test says "his letter of 45". "Jerome's Chronicle records his death there in 45". Traced. "January" belongs to Cicero's letter, not Cassius', so leaving it off is correct.
- 28 BC, "Anaxilaus expelled": "the expulsion from Rome in 28 of Anaxilaus" (Jerome's Chronicle, after Eusebius). Traced.
- AD 66, "Demetrius at Thrasea's death": "with Thrasea Paetus at his death in 66 (Tacitus, Annals 16.34)". Traced.
- AD 121, "Plotina's letter for the Garden": "In 121 ... Plotina asked Hadrian ... (IG II² 1099), and Plotina wrote to the 'friends'". Traced.
- AD 165, "Peregrinus burns himself at Olympia": "At the games of 165 ... leapt into a pyre at Harpina, twenty stades from Olympia" (35–36). The year is traced. "At Olympia" loosens the text's place, although the self-test uses the same loose wording.
- AD 176, "Marcus endows the chairs at Athens": "the endowment of 176 ... (Dio 72(71).31.3)". Traced.
- The events are in chronological order, and the caption's range, "88 BC to AD 176", matches the first and last events.
- Coverage: the caption calls this "The rival schools at Rome", but no Peripatetic event is drawn. The text gives a dated Peripatetic event: "Sulla, taking Athens in 86, seized the library of Apellicon ... and shipped it to Rome" (Plutarch, Sulla 26; Strabo 13.1.54). Every other rival school has at least one event.
- Rendering: in both themes, the tick labels "AD 150" and "AD 175" run together as "AD 150AD 175". The last label is pulled left, apparently to stay inside the frame, and collides with its neighbour. No other overlap or clipping. Leader lines and labels are clear in dark and light, and the phone frames split the axis cleanly (house rule).

**Fixes**

1. Caption, Philo: replace "Philo: the year as the section Cicero the sceptic gives it, citing Greek module 12.8 (Cicero, Brutus 306)" with "Philo: the year as the section Cicero the sceptic gives it, without a passage". If Brutus 306 is meant to be the source, the text needs it first. In Cicero the sceptic, the sentence would become: "Philo of Larissa, head of the Academy, fled to Rome in 88, and the young Cicero heard him (Brutus 306; Greek module 12.8 has his doctrine; module 13.1 the student years)." Only after that change can the caption cite it.
2. Add the Peripatetic event: "86 BC: Sulla ships Aristotle's books to Rome". Add "Sulla: Plutarch, Sulla 26; Strabo 13.1.54" to the caption between Philo and Antiochus.
3. Label, AD 165: change "Peregrinus burns himself at Olympia" to "Peregrinus burns himself at the Olympic games", or "near Olympia", to match "at Harpina, twenty stades from Olympia".
4. RENDERER: the collision of "AD 150" and "AD 175" comes from how the renderer places the last tick label at the frame edge. No change to the figure's data or caption can fix it. The renderer should drop or thin a tick label that would overlap its neighbour, or pad the axis domain past the last event.
