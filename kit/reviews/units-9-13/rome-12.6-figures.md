# Rome 12.6, The gods of the Aeneid: figure check

Checked against `text.txt` and `captions.md` only, with every desktop and phone image looked at in both themes. Neither figure is wider than its frame in a way the house rules count as a fault: Figure 2 is about 1,210 px wide, so it scrolls in the 760 px column and across four phone frames. That is a house rule, not a fault.

## Figure 1 (flow): FIX

**Kind and order.** A flow fits: it is a chain of eight events in two rows of four, joined by a return arrow. The order matches the text's event order. First the causes (1.12–28), then the storm, the pact, the ships and Allecto with the gates of war, in the order of the list at 7.312 ff. Then the reconciliation (12.791–842), Hannibal's war (10.11–13; Ennius, as Servius reports on 1.281), and Servius on 12.841 on the third war.

**Trace of the labels.**
- "Causes: Carthage; Paris; Ganymede (1.12–28)". Text: the list at 1.12–28, with Carthage at 1.19–22 and Paris and Ganymede at 1.26–28. Correct.
- "The storm 1.50–123". Text: "the storm of book 1 (1.50–123)". Correct.
- "The pact with Venus 4.90–128". Text: "the pact with Venus that brings Aeneas and Dido to the cave (4.90–128)". Correct.
- "Iris burns the ships 5.604–663". **Says more than the text.** The text says "Iris sent to burn the ships (5.604–663)", and the table says she "as Beroe, persuades the Trojan women to burn the ships". The text never says Iris burns them herself.
- "Allecto; the gates of war 7.323–571, 7.620–622". Text: "Allecto and the war in Latium (7.323–571), even the gates of war, which Juno pushes open with her own hand … (7.620–622)". Correct.
- "Reconciliation 12.791–842". Text: "The reconciliation of book 12 (12.791–842)". Correct.
- "Hannibal: forecast 10.11–13; Ennius in Servius on 1.281". Both citations are in the text: 10.11–13 for Jupiter's forecast, and Ennius as Servius reports on 1.281. **Misleading as drawn.** The node pairs the forecast of battle with Ennius but does not say what Ennius says. The text sets the two against each other: Ennius "put Juno's peace with Rome in the second Punic war: then, appeased, Juno began to favour the Romans", while Virgil's Jupiter "forecasts a lawful time for battle under Hannibal". A reader of the node could take Ennius as a second source for the forecast.
- "Scipio: Servius on 12.841: Juno brought to Rome in the third war; whether she was is uncertain". Text: Servius says Juno "in the third was brought to Rome by Scipio with certain rites", and whether she "was ever formally summoned to Rome in 146 … is uncertain". The qualifier is carried. Correct.

**Caption.** The section names are right: 10.11–13, 12.791–842 and Ennius are in the Jupiter section, and the rest is in the Juno section. The fault is that the caption gives the poem's passages as one block list and attaches only 10.11–13 to its item. The storm, the pact, Iris, Allecto and the reconciliation are never matched to their passages, and the caption does not say what "Ennius' date" is.

**Rendering.** Both themes are readable, and no box is clipped or overlaps another, on desktop or in the two phone frames. The return arrow is clear.

**Fixes.**
1. Change the label "Iris burns the ships" to **"Iris sent to burn the ships"**.
2. Change the Hannibal node's body to **"Jupiter's forecast 10.11–13; Ennius (Servius on 1.281): Juno appeased in the second Punic war"**. The text never says outright that Hannibal's war is the second Punic war, so naming the war keeps the label to what the text says.. Keep the title "Hannibal".
3. Replace the caption with:

> Juno's anger from its causes to the Punic wars, in the order of events, from the sections “Juno's anger and its causes” and “Jupiter: the prophecy, the council and the reconciliation”: her causes, Carthage, Paris' judgement and Ganymede (Aeneid 1.12–28); the storm (1.50–123); the pact with Venus (4.90–128); Iris sent to burn the ships (5.604–663); Allecto and the gates of war (7.323–571, 7.620–622); the reconciliation (12.791–842); the battle Jupiter forecasts when Carthage sends ruin through the Alps (10.11–13), and Ennius' date for Juno's peace with Rome, the second Punic war, as Servius reports it on 1.281; and Servius' note on 12.841 that Scipio brought Juno to Rome in the third Punic war, which the text calls uncertain.

## Figure 2 (tree): FIX

**Kind.** A tree fits. The root is "Divine agents and who sends them", with three senders under it (Juno, Jupiter, Venus) and their agents as leaves. "The three great gods" is the text's own phrase ("Below the three great gods …").

**Trace of the labels.**
- Juno → "Aeolus (1.65–75)". Text: "she bribes Aeolus with a nymph to loose the storm (1.65–75)". Correct.
- Juno → "Iris (4.693–705; 5.604–663; 9.1–24)". The table row gives Iris, acts for Juno, with 4.693–705, 5.604–663 and 9.1–24. Correct.
- Juno → "Allecto (7.323–571)". Text: "Allecto and the war in Latium (7.323–571)", and the table row gives acts for Juno. Correct.
- Juno → "Juturna (12.134–160)". Text: Juno "urges Juturna, Turnus' sister … (12.134–160)". Correct. The node carries no "urged", but the caption supplies it.
- Jupiter → "Mercury (1.297–304; 4.219–278)". The table row gives acts for Jupiter, 1.297–304 and 4.219–278. Correct. Leaving out 4.554–570 says less than the text, not more.
- Jupiter → "A Dira (12.843–886)". Text: "He sends one of the two Dirae … (12.843–886)". Correct.
- Venus → "Cupid (1.657–722)". Text: "sending Cupid in the shape of Ascanius … (1.657–722)". Correct.
- Venus → "Neptune, asked (5.779–826)". The table row has Neptune "grants Venus the passage (5.779–826)", and the Venus section has her negotiating "with Neptune for a safe passage". Correct, with its qualifier.
- Venus → "Vulcan, asked (8.370–406)". Text: "with Vulcan, her husband, for arms (8.370–406)". Correct, with its qualifier.
- Apollo is left out, rightly. The text names no god who sends him; he acts for "the Trojan future".

**Caption.** The four sections named are the ones the leaves' passages come from:
- Allecto 7.323–571: Juno section.
- The Dira 12.843–886: Jupiter section.
- Cupid 1.657–722 and Vulcan 8.370–406: Venus section.
- Aeolus, Juturna, Iris, Mercury and Neptune 5.779–826: the agents section.

The fault is that the caption attaches passages only to Aeolus and Juturna. The seven other leaves (Iris, Allecto, Mercury, the Dira, Cupid, Neptune, Vulcan) are never matched to their passages.

**Rendering.** All the text in the boxes is legible in both themes. The connectors are thin and pale in the light theme but visible. Desktop shows only the Juno and Jupiter branches, and Venus is reached by scrolling; that falls under the house rule for wide figures. In phone frame 3 Venus and its three leaves sit whole, and frame 4 holds only the right edge of the Vulcan box and the frame's end. Nothing is clipped beyond the scroll edge, and nothing overlaps.

**Fix.** Replace the caption with:

> The agents the three great gods send or ask, with the passages of the Aeneid the text cites for each, from the sections “Juno's anger and its causes”, “Jupiter: the prophecy, the council and the reconciliation”, “Venus as mother” and “The agents”: Juno bribes Aeolus (1.65–75), sends Iris (4.693–705, 5.604–663, 9.1–24) and Allecto (7.323–571), and urges Juturna (12.134–160); Jupiter sends Mercury (1.297–304, 4.219–278) and one of the Dirae (12.843–886); Venus sends Cupid (1.657–722) and asks Neptune (5.779–826) and Vulcan (8.370–406) rather than sending them.

The text grounds "sends" for Iris and Mercury in two places: "Each is sent by someone", and the table's "Acts for" column naming Juno and Jupiter.

An optional change, not required: the root label could read "Divine agents and who sends or asks them", to match the caption and the two "asked" leaves.
