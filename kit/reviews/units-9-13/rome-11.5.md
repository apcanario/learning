## Findings
1. [error] [figure] Section "How politics was done".
   Current: "would pay each consul 400,000 sesterces"
   Problem: Cicero's figure is HS quadragena, forty hundred-thousands, that is 4,000,000 sesterces to each consul, not 400,000. The writer flagged the figure as from memory.
   Fix: "would pay each consul 4,000,000 sesterces"
   Basis: Latin text of Letters to Atticus 4.17.2, "uti ambo HS quadragena consulibus darent", https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/cicero/att4.shtml; Winstedt's translation, "they were both to give the consuls 4,000,000 sesterces each", https://www.attalus.org/cicero/atticus4.html (letter 17, section 2).

3. [error] [accuracy] Section "Exile, return and the change of course, 58–54".
   Current: "a statesman must steer with the weather, as Plato advised, and constancy means"
   Problem: The letter does not give the steering image to Plato. Plato is cited for a different rule: press a political case only so far as you can persuade your fellow citizens, and use no force against parent or country (1.9.18). The sailing image, yielding to the storm even if you cannot make harbour, and the same end not the same words, are Cicero's own (1.9.21).
   Fix: "Plato taught that a man should press his case in politics only so far as he could persuade his fellow citizens; a statesman must steer with the weather, and constancy means"
   Basis: Latin text of Letters to Friends 1.9, "id enim iubet idem ille Plato ... tantum contendere in re publica, quantum probare tuis civibus posses" and, separately, "ut in navigando tempestati obsequi artis est ... non idem semper dicere, sed idem semper spectare debemus", https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/cicero/fam1.shtml; section 21 for the sailing passage, https://sententiaeantiquae.com/tag/ship-of-state/. Section 18 for the Plato sentence not checked.

4. [should-fix] [accuracy] Section "A reluctant governor: Cilicia, 51–50".
   Current: "Cato, asked for support, wrote a courteous refusal and spoke against a thanksgiving (<em>Letters to Friends</em> 15.5, answering 15.4)"
   Problem: Caelius' report of the debate says Cato spoke of Cicero with honour but did not vote for the thanksgiving; Hirrus and Favonius sided with him, and the thanksgiving passed. "Spoke against" misstates it; the writer flagged the vote as uncertain.
   Fix: "Cato, asked for support (<em>Letters to Friends</em> 15.4), praised Cicero in the Senate but voted against a thanksgiving, one of only three who did (8.11.2), and wrote to explain why (15.5)". In the bibliography add 8.11.2 to the Letters to Friends line.
   Basis: Latin text of Letters to Friends 8.11, "tantum Catoni assensus est, qui de te locutus honorifice non decrerat supplicationes; tertius ad hos Favonius accessit", https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/cicero/fam8.shtml.

5. [should-fix] [accuracy] Section "The civil war: knowing whom to flee, 49–47".
   Current: "When Pompey abandoned Rome in January 49 Cicero wrote that he knew whom to flee but not whom to follow"
   Problem: Letters to Atticus 8.7 dates to about 23 February 49, a month after Pompey left Rome; the sentence implies January.
   Fix: "Pompey abandoned Rome in January 49; a month later Cicero wrote that he knew whom to flee but not whom to follow"
   Basis: Letters to Atticus 8.7, dated "23? February 49", https://www.attalus.org/cicero/atticus8.html; Latin "quem fugiam habeo, quem sequar non habeo" at 8.7.2, https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/cicero/att8.shtml.

6. [should-fix] [register] Section "Four collections and how they survived", first dispute box.
   Current: "reviewers admired the ingenuity and rejected the thesis, and most scholars now put publication in the first century AD, before Seneca."
   Problem: Carcopino is named on one side; the other side is "reviewers" and "most scholars", with nobody named. The house rule wants a source or scholar on each side. The fix names the ancient source the report already cites as the latest possible date.
   Fix: "reviewers admired the ingenuity and rejected the thesis, and most scholars now put publication in the first century AD, no later than the early 60s, when Seneca could assume Lucilius knew the letters (<em>Letters</em> 21.4)."
   Basis: Wikipedia, Epistulae ad Atticum ("may not have been published until the middle of the first century CE"), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistulae_ad_Atticum. The reception of Carcopino's thesis was not checked: the JRS review of the English translation (Cambridge Core) timed out on permission and is not cited here. A named reviewer would be better if the maintainer can confirm one.

   Basis: Latin text of Letters to Friends 14.4, "fidissima atque optima uxor", from Brundisium, 29 April 58, and 14.20, Venusia, Kalends of October 47, "labrum si in balineo non est, ut sit", https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/cicero/fam14.shtml; rome/r-11.4.html, section "Terentia and Tullia" (divorce in 46, Plutarch, Cicero 41).
