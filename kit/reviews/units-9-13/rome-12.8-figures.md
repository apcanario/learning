# Rome 12.8, "The Aeneid after Rome": figure check

Checked against `text.txt` and `captions.md` only, with every image looked at (desktop 760 px and phone frames, dark and light). Desktop timeline dots measured against the axis (1920 at x≈57, 2020 at x≈703, about 6.46 px a year).

## Figure 1 (flow): FIX

Trace, box by box (section "The Aeneid in English: Douglas, Surrey, Dryden"):
- **Caxton, Eneydos**: "1490, from a French prose romance". Text: "William Caxton's Eneydos of 1490, a version of a French prose romance". Date right. "from" is not the text's word; the text says "a version of".
- **Douglas, Eneados**: "finished 1513, printed 1553; Scots, with Vegio's book 13". Text: "finished his Eneados in 1513 ... printed in London in 1553"; "He calls his tongue 'Scottis', adds the thirteenth book that Maffeo Vegio wrote". Correct.
- **Surrey, book 4**: "1554, 'a straunge metre': blank verse". Text: "Book 4 was printed in 1554 as 'drawne into a straunge metre' ... the strange metre was English blank verse". Correct.
- **Surrey, books 2 and 4**: "1557". Text: "and both books in 1557". Correct.
- **Dryden, Works of Virgil**: "1697, rhymed, by subscription". Text: "Dryden's Works of Virgil (1697)"; "Dryden supplies the 'godlike' and the rhyme"; "he paid for it by subscription". Correct.

Kind: a flow fits; the text links each step to the one before (Douglas's prologue attacks Caxton; Ridley shows Surrey borrowing Douglas's words; Surrey is set beside Dryden). Boxes run in date order, which is the right order for a flow.

Caption: says the boxes are "in the order ... the section ... gives them". The section names Douglas first and brings in Caxton through Douglas's attack, so the boxes follow the dates, not the section's order. "The Aeneid into English ... from Caxton" also presents Caxton's Eneydos as a translation of the poem, though the text calls it a version of a French prose romance, "nothing like Virgil", and makes Douglas's the first British translation of the whole poem. "The text cites no passage for these dates" is right.

Rendering: "Surrey, books 2 and 4" wraps so that "4" sits alone on the second line. It can still be read. Nothing is clipped or overlapping in either theme. The phone frames scroll sideways as the house rule allows.

Fixes:
1. Caxton box detail: "1490, from a French prose romance" → "1490, a version of a French prose romance".
2. Replace the caption:

> The Aeneid in English and Scots, from Caxton's Eneydos, a version of a French prose romance, to Dryden, in order of the dates the section "The Aeneid in English: Douglas, Surrey, Dryden" gives. The text cites no passage for these dates.

## Figure 3 (matrix): FIX

Trace, row by row:
- **Proba's Cento**: "lines and half-lines, rearranged" / "all of Virgil". Text: "694 hexameters built entirely of lines and half-lines from Virgil"; "a cento on Christ made of Virgil's verses". The first cell is supported. "All of Virgil" says more than the text: the cento is made entirely of Virgil, but the text never says it draws on the whole of Virgil. **Fault.**
- **Sortes Vergilianae**: "one line as an answer" / "any page". Text: "opened the poem at random and read the first line the eye fell on as an answer". Supported, but "any page" is not the text's wording.
- **Roman d'Eneas**: "the story, as a love romance" / "the whole, weighted to Lavine". Text: "It keeps Virgil's story and moves its weight ... the second half becomes a love story Virgil never wrote." The first cell turns the whole story into a love romance, but the text says only the second half becomes one. **Fault.**
- **Dante**: "the poet as guide" / "book 6; the Eclogue". Text: "What belongs here is the choice of Virgil himself"; reasons: "the one classical descent into the underworld" (book 6, with deep dive 12.5) and "through the fourth Eclogue, a prophet" (Purgatorio 22.67–73). "The Eclogue" leaves out the text's qualifier "fourth". It is also not part of the Aeneid, although the column header says "from the poem". **Fault.**
- **Purcell and Tate**: "Dido's tragedy" / "book 4". Text: "Tate's libretto takes book 4 alone and ends with the queen". Supported.
- **Berlioz**: "Troy and Carthage" / "books 1, 2 and 4". Text: "the fall of Troy from book 2 ... and Carthage from books 1 and 4". Correct.
- **Mantua monument**: "the charge to rule" / "6.851–853". Text: "Anchises' charge to Rome, to rule the peoples, spare the conquered and war down the proud (6.851–853)". Correct.
- **Broch**: "the order to burn it" / "Donatus' life". Text: "Its subject is Donatus' story of the order to burn the poem (module 12.1)". The cells are correct, but Donatus' Life is not part of the poem, so the header "FROM THE POEM" is wrong for this row. **Fault (header).**
- **Le Guin**: "the silent bride's voice" / "Lavinia's silence". Text: "gives a voice to the woman Virgil left silent"; "the bride with no lines"; Lavinia "never speaks" in the Aeneid. Supported.

Order: the rows do not follow the text. The Mantua monument comes from the last section ("Virgil in Fascist Italy and after"), after Broch and Le Guin ("Eliot, Broch, Le Guin"), but the figure and caption put it before them. As drawn, the rows run by date, but the caption claims the order of the sections. **Fault.**

Header: "FROM THE POEM", and the caption's "from the Aeneid and from which part", are wrong for three rows: Proba (Virgil's verses in general), Dante (the fourth Eclogue) and Broch (Donatus' Life). **Fault.**

Caption: "Dante (Inferno 1, Purgatorio 22)" leaves out the line numbers the text gives and does not attach them to items. The text cites Inferno 1.85–87 for Virgil as master and guide (1.70–72 for his self-naming) and Purgatorio 22.67–73 for the fourth Eclogue. It gives no passage for book 6. "The sortes (Historia Augusta, Aubrey)" names the sources of the examples. The row shows the practice in general, which the text describes without a passage. Mantua (6.851–853) and "Broch, after Donatus' Life" are right.

Colour: the rows come in coloured pairs (Proba and sortes; Eneas and Dante; Purcell and Berlioz; Broch and Le Guin; Mantua alone). Nothing explains them, and they do not match the text's sections: Proba and the sortes are in different sections, and so are the Eneas and Dante. A reader may take the pairs as categories. If the figure source sets the colours, make them one colour or match them to the sections. If the renderer cycles colours on its own, this is RENDERER and not part of this FIX.

Rendering: the cells are readable in both themes. "lines and half-lines, rearranged" and "the whole, weighted to Lavine" fill their cells to the edge but are not clipped. The phone frames scroll sideways as the house rule allows.

Fixes:
1. Column header "FROM THE POEM" → "WHERE FROM".
2. Proba, second cell: "all of Virgil" → "Virgil's verses".
3. Sortes, second cell: "any page" → "opened at random".
4. Roman d'Eneas: first cell → "the story, its weight moved"; second cell → "all; second half a love story".
5. Dante, second cell: "book 6; the Eclogue" → "book 6; the fourth Eclogue".
6. Move the Mantua monument row to the end, after Le Guin. Final order: Proba's Cento, Sortes Vergilianae, Roman d'Eneas, Dante, Purcell and Tate, Berlioz, Broch, Le Guin, Mantua monument.
7. Replace the caption:

> What each later work in this module takes and where it comes from, in the order of the sections from "The Christian Virgil" to "Virgil in Fascist Italy and after": Proba's *Cento*, made of Virgil's verses; the *sortes*, described without a passage (the examples come from the *Historia Augusta* and Aubrey); the *Roman d'Eneas*, without a passage; Dante's choice of Virgil as guide (*Inferno* 1.85–87) and the fourth Eclogue through Statius (*Purgatorio* 22.67–73), with book 6 given without a passage; Tate and Purcell, and Berlioz, without passages (deep dive module 12.4 has more); Broch, after Donatus' *Life* (module 12.1); Le Guin, without a passage; the Mantua monument, Anchises' charge to Rome (6.851–853).
