# Rome 12.1 — figure check 2 (fresh checker)

Material: `text.txt`, `captions.md` and the desktop and phone renders of figures 1–3 in both themes. All twelve phone frames and six desktop renders were looked at. Not counted as faults: the sideways scroll in the phone frames, and span bars drawn above the axis.

## Figure 1 (timeline): FIX

Every item in the drawing traces to the text:

- **15 October 70 BC, Born at Andes near Mantua.** The text says "born on 15 October 70 BC … at Andes near Mantua (Life 2)". Matches.
- **55 BC, Toga of manhood at Cremona.** The text says "spent his boyhood at Cremona, took the toga of manhood on his fifteenth birthday, in 55 … (Life 6)". The text states the date flatly and explains why it is fifteen rather than the transmitted seventeen, so no "c." is owed. "At Cremona" comes from the order of the text's sentence, which puts the toga between the boyhood at Cremona and the move to Milan. Acceptable.
- **40 BC, Fourth Eclogue, Pollio consul.** The text says "It begins in Pollio's consulship: te consule (4.11), so in 40." Matches.
- **29 BC, Georgics read at Atella.** The text says "the reading to Octavian at Atella in 29 (Life 27)", in the Georgics section. Matches.
- **after 23 BC, Books 2, 4 and 6 read to Augustus.** The text cites Life 32, then says "since Marcellus died in 23, the reading came after that". The figure keeps the qualifier "after". Matches.
- **21 September 19 BC, Dies at Brundisium.** The text says "died at Brundisium on 21 September 19 BC … (Life 35–36)". Matches.
- **ECLOGUES, C. 42–38 BC.** The text says "usually dated about 42–38". "C." carries the "about". Matches.
- **GEORGICS, C. 36–29 BC.** The text says "seven years in the writing (Life 25) and finished in 29, so begun about 36". Matches.
- **AENEID, 29–19 BC.** The text says "begun in 29 after the Georgics and unfinished at his death in 19". Matches.

**Positions.** Measured on the axis at about 11.8 px a year, every dot and every bar end sits on its year: 70, 55, 40, 29, 23, 19, and the bars at 42–38, 36–29 and 29–19.

**Labels.** Each span label carries its years. The labels are staggered on three tiers and none overlaps. Nothing is clipped at 760 px: "Dies at Brundisium" ends inside the frame. Contrast is fine in dark and light.

**The fault is in the caption, which leaves out two dates the text gives without a passage.**

1. **The end of the Georgics in 29.** The text's "finished in 29" carries no citation. Life 25 supports only the seven years. The caption lists "the start of the Aeneid in 29" as having no passage, but not the end of the Georgics, which is the same year in the same unsourced way. The words "the seven years of Life 25 ending in 29" also suggest that Life 25 gives the 29.
2. **Marcellus' death in 23.** The text gives this year without a passage. The caption also leaves out the line the text uses to date the reading: tu Marcellus eris (6.883).

**Fix.** Replace the caption with:

> Virgil's life and the composition of the three works. Birth and toga from Donatus, Life 2 and 6; Atella from Life 27; the reading of books 2, 4 and 6 from Life 32, dated after Marcellus' death in 23 by the lines on him (6.883); death from Life 35–36. The fourth Eclogue is dated by Pollio's consulship (4.11). The spans are those the text gives: the Eclogues about 42–38, the Georgics seven years (Life 25) ending in 29, the Aeneid from 29 to the poet's death. The text gives the dates of the Eclogues, the end of the Georgics in 29 (and so their start about 36), the start of the Aeneid in 29 and Marcellus' death in 23 without a passage; the Eclogues and the start of the Georgics are approximate.

The drawing itself needs no change.

## Figure 2 (matrix): FIX

Every row traces to the text, which says: "Modern readers map the ten as pairs around a centre: 1 and 9 on the confiscations, 2 and 8 on love's laments, 3 and 7 on singing contests, 4 and 6 on songs that rise above pastoral to prophecy and the making of the world. Poem 5, Daphnis' death and deification, stands at the middle; poem 10 gives the elegist Gallus, lovesick in Arcadia, a farewell that closes the book."

| Row | Cells | In the text |
|---|---|---|
| The confiscations | 1, 9 | yes |
| Love's laments | 2, 8 | yes |
| Singing contests | 3, 7 | yes |
| Prophecy and the making of the world | 4, 6 | yes, a fair shortening |
| Daphnis' death and deification, the centre | 5 | yes |
| Gallus in Arcadia, the close | 10 | yes |

**What is correct.**

- The numbers and themes are right.
- The rows run from the outer pair inward to the centre, then to the close, which follows the text's order.
- The caption is accurate. The text gives no passage for the pairing: Clausen is cited for the planning of the book, not for the pairs. "Not an ancient one" follows from the text's "Modern readers map".
- A grid of theme × pair suits the shape of the content.
- Both themes are legible, and nothing is clipped.

**The fault is in the column headers.** They read "FIRST POEM / SECOND POEM". That puts poems 5 and 10 under "first poem", next to an empty "second poem" cell. A reader can take 10 as the first half of a pair whose partner is missing. The text says the opposite: 5 stands alone at the centre, and 10 sits outside the pairs as the close.

**Fix.** Change the column headers to "POEM" and "PAIRED WITH". Better still, also put "—" in the two empty cells. The empty cells then read as "unpaired" rather than "missing". The caption needs no change.

## Figure 3 (flow): PASS

Every step traces to the text:

Notes on three steps:

- **Journey to Greece.** The text gives the Megara fever without a citation, after Life 35. The caption says so.
- **Dies at Brundisium.** The caption names the section this date comes from.
- **Varius and Tucca edit.** The self-test also says "Varius and Tucca published it at Augustus' wish … (Life 41)", and Servius has both men removing the Helen lines. The pair is supported.

**Order.** The order is that of events. The text tells the death first and then goes back to the book-boxes, but it places the request "in his last illness", so the request comes before the death, as drawn. The reading after 23 comes before the journey of 19, also as drawn.

**Caption.** It claims only what is drawn, names the sections, and discloses both items that have no citation of their own.

**Drawing.** The kind fits a sequence. The wrap from step 4 back to step 5 is clear. No box text overflows: step 5 wraps to four lines but stays inside its box. Both themes are legible.
