## Findings
1. [error] [accuracy] Section "Dido after Virgil".
   Current: "As a boy at Carthage, Augustine was made to learn"
   Problem: The schooling of *Confessions* 1.13 is Augustine's boyhood at Thagaste (then Madauros); he went to Carthage only at about seventeen, to study rhetoric. Book 1 covers his life only to about fourteen.
   Fix: "As a schoolboy in Africa, Augustine was made to learn"
   Basis: James J. O'Donnell, *Augustine* (Twayne), ch. 1, https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/aug1.html (Thagaste, then Madauros, Carthage at about seventeen); Wikipedia, "Confessions (Augustine)" (book 1 runs to age 14).

2. [error] [accuracy] Section "The questions" (end of "How far is Dido Cleopatra, and Carthage Egypt?").
   Current: "<p>Dido returns once in the Iliadic half: Aeneas dresses the dead Pallas in one of two robes that Sidonian Dido once wove for him with her own hands (11.72–75).</p>"
   Problem: She appears twice: in book 9 Ascanius promises Nisus "an ancient mixing bowl that Sidonian Dido gave" (*cratera antiquum quem dat Sidonia Dido*, 9.266).
   Fix: "<p>Dido returns twice in the Iliadic half: Ascanius promises Nisus an ancient mixing bowl that Sidonian Dido gave (9.266), and Aeneas dresses the dead Pallas in one of two robes that Sidonian Dido once wove for him with her own hands (11.72–75).</p>"
   Basis: Dickinson College Commentaries, *Aeneid* 9.246–313, line 266, https://dcc.dickinson.edu/node/5061.

3. [should-fix] [attribution] Section "The questions", dispute box on Cleopatra.
   Current: "A. S. Pease's commentary on book 4 (1935) and Janice Benario (\"Dido and Cleopatra\", <em>Vergilius</em>, 1970) collected the parallels; Karl Galinsky (2003) argued that it is Horace's Cleopatra of <em>Odes</em> 1.37, more than the historical queen, that shapes Dido."
   Problem: Benario did more than collect parallels: like Galinsky, she built on the affinities between Dido and Horace's Cleopatra of *Odes* 1.37 and concluded that Virgil knew Horace's portrait as well as the real queen. The writer's own source (Astorino) groups Benario with Galinsky, not with Pease.
   Fix: "A. S. Pease's commentary on book 4 (1935) detailed the parallels; Janice Benario (\"Dido and Cleopatra\", <em>Vergilius</em>, 1970) traced them to Horace's Cleopatra of <em>Odes</em> 1.37 as well as the historical queen, and Karl Galinsky (2003) argued that Horace's Cleopatra shapes Dido more than the historical one does."
   Basis: P. M. Astorino, "Dido como alusión a Cleopatra en la Eneida", *Myrtia* 35 (2020) 275–292, https://revistas.um.es/myrtia/article/download/455231/293041/1547981 (the survey the writer used).

4. [minor] [attribution] Section "The questions", dispute box on Cleopatra.

5. [should-fix] [register / accuracy] Section "Dido after Virgil", Purcell paragraph and dispute box.
   Current (box): "<aside class=\"dispute\"><strong>Where sources disagree.</strong> The traditional account makes the opera a school piece of 1689, a loyal entertainment for the new reign of William and Mary."

   Fix (box end): add after "with the Chelsea performance a revival.": " A letter of 1688 has since linked the opera to the school by that summer, before the change of reign, which undoes the loyal occasion on either view."

   Basis: Wikipedia, "Dido and Aeneas" (composed no later than July 1688; a letter links it to the school by summer 1688); Pinnock and Wood, *Early Music* 20.3 (1992) 373–390, abstract at https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/67266 (the traditional account "founded on sand").
