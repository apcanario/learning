## Findings
1. [error] [accuracy] Section "The afterlife".
   Current: "The book became a classic in the seventeenth century, through Casaubon's English, Gataker's edition and readers from Shaftesbury to Adam Smith."
   Problem: Shaftesbury's Marcus notebooks date from 1698–1712 and Smith brings Marcus in only in the sixth edition of *The Theory of Moral Sentiments* (1790), so the readers named are eighteenth-century. Sellars puts the book's standing as a classic in the eighteenth century (Brucker's twenty pages in the 1740s, translations into every major vernacular by mid-century).
   Fix: "The book became a classic over the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, through Casaubon's English, Gataker's edition and readers from Shaftesbury to Adam Smith, who brought Marcus into the sixth edition of <em>The Theory of Moral Sentiments</em> (1790) (Sellars 2025)."
   Basis: Sellars, "The Early Modern Reception of the Meditations", author's copy, https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/files/58868701/Sellars_-_Early_Modern_Meditations.pdf (Shaftesbury's *Askēmata* 1698–1712; Smith absent from TMS 1759, present in the 1790 edition; Brucker 1740s).

2. [should-fix] [register] Section "The text: twelve books, a borrowed title and two place notes" (first dispute box).
   Current: "C. R. Haines' Loeb edition (1916) prints "at Carnuntum" at the end of book 2 and notes that it may be meant as the heading of book 3; Sellars (2025) gives both notes as the titles of books 2 and 3. If they are endings, book 1 was written among the Quadi;"
   Problem: Both named authorities stand on the "headings" side; nobody is named for the "endings" side, which the box then argues from. Haines' own introduction also reads the notes as headings (book 2 written among the Quadi, book 3 at Carnuntum), which is firmer than "notes that it may be meant". George Long prints the Quadi note as the close of book 1 and suggests book 1 was written in that war.
   Fix: "George Long's translation (1862) prints "among the Quadi at the Granua" as the close of book 1 and suggests that book 1 was written during that war; C. R. Haines, introducing his Loeb edition (1916), reads the notes as showing that book 2 was written among the Quadi and book 3 at Carnuntum, and Sellars (2025) gives both as the titles of books 2 and 3. If they are endings, book 1 was written among the Quadi;"
   Basis: Long's note at the end of 1.17, https://lexundria.com/m_aur_med/1.17/lg ("the editor suggests Book 1 may have been written during that war"); Haines' introduction, https://ia800304.us.archive.org/1/items/thecommuningswit00marcuoft/thecommuningswit00marcuoft.pdf (notes "between Books I and II and II and III" show Book II written "among the Quadi on the Gran", Book III "at Carnuntum"). Haines' placement of the Carnuntum note on the page not seen (the scan excerpt stops at 1.17).

3. [should-fix] [attribution] Section "The afterlife".
   Current: "It answered a passage of John Stuart Mill's <em>On Liberty</em> (1859), which set Marcus, the best of pagan rulers, beside his persecution of the Christians (Lee Behlman, "The Victorian Marcus Aurelius", <em>Journal of Victorian Culture</em>, 2011)."
   Problem: Behlman's abstract says only that Mill's comparison of Stoic and Christian ethics in *On Liberty* prompted Arnold's reply; it does not say which passage. *On Liberty* ch. 2 has two candidates in separate paragraphs: the Marcus paragraph (writings "the highest ethical product of the ancient mind", yet he "persecuted Christianity") and, later, Christian morality as "in great part, a protest against Paganism", "negative rather than positive", which (from memory, not verified) is the passage Arnold's essay opens by quoting. The report picks one without a source for the choice.
   Fix: "It answered John Stuart Mill's comparison of Stoic and Christian ethics in <em>On Liberty</em> (1859), the book in which Mill called Marcus' writings the highest ethical product of the ancient mind and set beside them his persecution of Christianity (chapter 2; Lee Behlman, "The Victorian Marcus Aurelius", <em>Journal of Victorian Culture</em>, 2011)."
   Basis: Behlman abstract, https://digitalcommons.montclair.edu/english-facpubs/27 (JVC 16.1, 2011, 1–24); Mill, *On Liberty* ch. 2, https://www.utilitarianism.com/ol/two.html and https://gutenberg.org/files/34901/34901-h/34901-h.htm (both passages confirmed, in separate paragraphs). Arnold's own text not reached (searches returned no copy of the essay).

5. [minor] [accuracy] Section "Notebook or exercises: date and purpose".
   Current: "Epictetus had told his pupils to write such things down daily and keep them at hand (<em>Discourses</em> 1.1.21–25)."
   Problem: 1.1.25 says philosophers should practise these things, write them daily and exercise themselves in them; "keep them at hand" (*procheiron*) is Epictetus' wording elsewhere, not in this passage.
   Fix: "Epictetus had told his pupils that these were the things to write down daily and to exercise themselves in (<em>Discourses</em> 1.1.21–25)."
   Basis: Epictetus, *Discourses* 1.1.25 (Oldfather, Loeb; Long's translation, classics.mit.edu, returned by search); from knowledge of the text, page not opened.
