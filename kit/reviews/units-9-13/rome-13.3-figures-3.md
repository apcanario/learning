# Rome 13.3, The rival schools at Rome: figure check 3

Material checked: `text.txt`, `captions.md`, and all twelve images (desktop and phone frames, dark and light) for figures 1 and 2. House rules applied: on a phone the figure keeps its natural width and scrolls sideways, and a timeline span is a short bar above the axis. Neither is counted as a fault.

## Figure 1 (matrix): FIX

**Kind and drawing.** Seven schools (rows) against two attributes (columns: "Some adherents named here", "Chair at Athens, 176"). A matrix fits that shape. Headers and cells are readable in both themes, nothing is clipped or overlapping, and the widest cell ("Nigidius, Quintus Sextius (disputed)") fits. Phone frames 1 and 2 together show every cell. In both themes the "chair" and "none" fills are easy to tell apart.

**Trace, row by row.**
- Epicureans: Philodemus (the trained Epicurean, section "Philodemus, Piso and the villa"); Piso (Cicero's "Epicurean led out of the pigsty", *Against Piso* 37; "a senator could be one, as Piso and Cassius were", in "Why the Stoa won"); Cassius ("became one in the 40s"); Atticus ("Cicero's letters treat him as an Epicurean"). Chair: Epicureans are named by Philostratus 566 and Lucian, *Eunuch* 3. All supported.
- New Academy: Philo (fled to Rome in 88, head of the Academy) and Cicero ("a follower of the New Academy to the end"). The cell "none (the chair was Platonist)" matches "The Academy now meant Plato taught as doctrine, a Platonist chair, not Cicero's scepticism." Supported.
- Old Academy (Antiochus): Lucullus (Plutarch, *Lucullus* 42), Varro ("a declared follower"; *City of God* 19.1–3), and Brutus marked "(disputed)", which matches the Brutus dispute box (Moles against Sedley). Supported. **Chair cell: "Platonist chair (Plato as doctrine)", in the "chair" fill. This is not supported.** The text says only that "the Academy now meant Plato taught as doctrine, a Platonist chair". It never says that Antiochus' Old Academy held that chair or turned into it. What the text does say of the Old Academy points the other way: "After Antiochus the school had no head of his stature, and its ethics passed into Middle Platonism", and Seneca finds "the Old and New Academy without a head". The cell therefore tells the reader that one school held the chair and the other did not, which goes beyond the text.
- Peripatetics: Cratippus (taught at Mytilene and Athens) and "Cicero's son (pupil)", from "Cicero's son Marcus studied with him". The qualifier is kept. Chair: the Peripatetics are in Philostratus and Lucian. Supported.
- Pythagoreans: Nigidius ("revived the Pythagoreans' teaching", *Timaeus* 1) and "Quintus Sextius (disputed)". The disputed mark matches the Sextius dispute box (Seneca's near-Stoic against Claudianus Mamertus' Platonist-Pythagorean). The cell "none" matches "there was no chair for Cynics or Pythagoreans". Supported.
- Cynics: Demetrius and Peregrinus, both in "The Cynics under the empire". The cell "none" is supported in the same way.
- Stoics: Cato ("from Cato on, an example of how to die", in "Why the Stoa won") and Seneca ("He wrote as a Stoic", in the same section). Chair: Stoics are in Philostratus and Lucian. Supported.
- Column year 176: "the endowment of 176, recorded in Greek module 7.4". Supported.

**Caption.** Each school's sections are named correctly: the heading titles are shortened, but each one can be found. The sources for the chairs (Dio 72(71).31.3; Philostratus, *Lives of the Sophists* 566; Lucian, *Eunuch* 3) match the text. "Brutus and Sextius are disputed, as the text's dispute boxes show" is correct. Two faults:
1. "as cited in Greek module 7.4". The text says the endowment is "recorded in Greek module 7.4". It does not say that Dio or Philostratus is cited there.
2. The text gives the year 176 only as "the endowment of 176, recorded in Greek module 7.4". The cited sentence (Dio 72(71).31.3) carries no year. The caption cites Dio for the chairs but does not say that the year is given without a passage.

**Fixes.**
- Old Academy chair cell. Replace "Platonist chair (Plato as doctrine)" with **"none as such (the chair was Platonist)"**, drawn in the "none" fill, so that it matches the New Academy row. The other way to fix it is to keep the cell and add a sourced sentence to the text tying the Platonist chair to Antiochus' dogmatic Academy. As the text stands, no such sentence can be written without a new source, so changing the cell is the fix.
- Caption, chairs sentence. Change it to: "The chairs: Dio 72(71).31.3 and Philostratus, *Lives of the Sophists* 566, with Lucian, *Eunuch* 3, for the four salaried schools (Platonists, Stoics, Peripatetics, Epicureans); the year 176 as the text gives it from Greek module 7.4, without a passage. The Academy's chair taught Plato as doctrine, not Cicero's scepticism, and the text assigns it to neither Academy."

## Figure 2 (timeline): FIX

**Kind and drawing.** Eleven dated points on one axis from 88 BC to AD 176, with no spans. A timeline fits. I measured the dot positions against the tick scale (about 2.35 px a year at desktop width), and every dot sits at its stated year. Labels are stacked on leader lines and none overlap, in either theme. The two phone frames together show every label.

**Trace, point by point.**
- 88 BC, Philo flees to Rome: "fled to Rome in 88 (Cicero, *Brutus* 306)". Supported.
- 86 BC, Sulla takes Athens, seizes Apellicon's library: "Sulla, taking Athens in 86, seized the library of Apellicon … (Plutarch, *Sulla* 26; Strabo 13.1.54)". Supported.
- 79 BC, Cicero hears Antiochus at Athens: "at Athens for six months in 79 (*Brutus* 315)". Supported.
- 55 BC, Cicero's Against Piso: "Attacking Piso in 55". The year has no passage, and the caption says so. Supported.
- 45 BC, Cassius' Epicurean letter; Nigidius dies in exile: Cicero's letter of January 45 (15.16.1) and Cassius' reply (15.19.2–3); the self-test calls the reply "his letter of 45". The label correctly leaves out "January", which the text gives only for Cicero's letter. Nigidius: "Jerome's Chronicle records his death there in 45". Supported.
- 28 BC, Anaxilaus expelled: "the expulsion from Rome in 28 of Anaxilaus of Larissa" (same chronicle). Supported.
- AD 66, Demetrius at Thrasea's death: "with Thrasea Paetus at his death in 66 (Tacitus, *Annals* 16.34)". Supported.
- AD 121, Plotina asks Hadrian's favour for the Garden: "In 121 Trajan's widow Plotina asked Hadrian … (IG II² 1099)". Supported.
- AD 165, Peregrinus burns himself near Olympia: "At the games of 165 … a pyre at Harpina, twenty stades from Olympia (35–36)". Supported.
- AD 176, Marcus endows the chairs at Athens: the event is supported by Dio 72(71).31.3. The year is not in Dio's sentence; it comes only from "the endowment of 176, recorded in Greek module 7.4".

**Caption.** Each passage named matches the text: *Brutus* 306; *Sulla* 26 and Strabo 13.1.54; *Brutus* 315; *Letters to Friends* 15.19 and 15.16; Jerome, *Chronicle*; *Annals* 16.34; IG II² 1099; *On the Death of Peregrinus* 35–36. The caption says correctly that the year of *Against Piso* has no passage. But it presents the chairs as dated by Dio 72(71).31.3, when the text gives that year without a passage, through Greek module 7.4. The Cassius references are also less precise than the text's (15.19.2–3, 15.16.1).

**Fix.** Caption, last item. Change "the chairs: Dio 72(71).31.3." to **"the chairs: Dio 72(71).31.3 for the endowment, the year 176 as the text gives it from Greek module 7.4, without a passage."** Optional tightening: "Cassius: *Letters to Friends* 15.19.2–3, his reply to Cicero's letter of January 45 (15.16.1)".
