Checked against `text.txt` and `captions.md` only; every desktop and phone image looked at in both themes. House rules applied: sideways scroll below 768 px and on the wide timeline, spans as short bars, range-dated events on the first year.

Trace:
- "The family / 1.1–4: grandfather, father, mother, great-grandfather". Text, Book 1 §2: grandfather, father's memory, mother, great-grandfather, "(1.1–4)". The order matches the text.
- "Teachers and friends / 1.5–15: from the tutor to Severus and others". Text: "Then teachers and friends (1.5–15)". The tutor is 1.5 and Severus is 1.14. "and others" covers the entries the text names in between and 1.15, which it does not name. The label says nothing the text does not.

- "The gods / 1.17". Text: "last the gods ... (1.17)".
- The four stages run in the text's order ("It moves in four stages"), and a flow is the right kind for that sequence.
- Caption: names the section and gives the passage for each stage (1.1–4, 1.5–15, 1.16, 1.17), all as in the text. There are no dates, and it claims nothing the figure does not show.
- Legibility: the drawing fits the 760 px column. Phone frames 1–2 show the four boxes and arrows cleanly in dark and light, with no clipping or overlap.

Optional, not a fault: "the tutor (1.5) to Severus (1.14) and others" would stop the label reading as though Severus closes the stage.

## Figure 2 (tree): FIX

Trace:
- "Marcus' notes, bulk usually dated to the 170s". Text, §The text: "Most scholars put the bulk of the work in the 170s". The qualifiers are kept ("bulk", "usually" ≈ "most scholars"). No source is cited, and the caption says so.
- "Old copy, falling to pieces". Text, §Transmission: "an old copy ... so worn it was falling to pieces".
- "Arethas' copy, before 907 (lost)". Text: he wrote "before 907" that he "had had it copied"; "His copy is lost but is usually thought to be the ancestor of the manuscripts we know".
- "Palatine manuscript (lost): edition of 1558–59". Text: the first edition came from the Palatine manuscript; preface October 1558, title page 1559; "The manuscript was then lost" (Sellars, "The Early Modern Reception", 2025).
- "Vaticanus Graecus 1950, fourteenth century". Text, §The text, uncited.
- Shape: a single line of descent that splits into two witnesses under Arethas' copy, so a tree fits. The caption's "as it is usually supposed, not proven" carries the text's "usually thought". Leaving out the Suda and Tzetzes is correct, since the text does not place them.
- Legibility: all nodes and edges are readable in both themes. The light-theme edges are faint but visible. The phone frames split the two leaves across frames 1–2 with nothing clipped inside a node.

Faults:
1. **The caption drops the text's qualifier on the dating.** "the dating of the notes to the 170s" states as fact what the text gives as the view of most scholars about the bulk of the work. It also uses "the notes", the text's word for the two place notes, which the text does tie to the 170s, so the reader can confuse the two. Fix: "the dating of the bulk of the work to the 170s by most scholars and Vaticanus Graecus 1950 (fourteenth century), which the text gives without a cited source".
2. **The caption makes the date before 907 look sourced.** "the old copy and Arethas' copy (before 907) from his letter to Demetrius of Heraclea" presents the letter as the source of the date. The text gives "before 907" with no reference, and Figure 3's caption correctly lists Arethas' letter (before 907) among the uncited dates. Fix: "the old copy and Arethas' copy, which the text knows from his letter to Demetrius of Heraclea, dated before 907 without a cited source".
3. **The Palatine node reads as if the manuscript dates from 1558–59.** Its sibling's date ("Vaticanus Graecus 1950, fourteenth century") and Arethas' copy ("before 907") are dates of the manuscripts themselves, so "Palatine manuscript (lost): edition of 1558–59" invites the same reading. The text gives no date for the Palatine manuscript, only for the edition printed from it. Fix the label: "Palatine manuscript (lost), printed as the first edition, Zurich 1558–59".

## Figure 3 (timeline): FIX

Trace (scale measured on the images, about 1.14 px per year):
- "before 907 / Arethas' letter to Demetrius", drawn at about 907. Text: "wrote to Demetrius ... before 907", uncited. The label keeps "before".
- "late tenth century / Suda: twelve books", drawn at about 990, which the caption discloses. Text, §The text: the twelve-book division is first attested in the Suda, late tenth century (Sellars, introduction, 2025). §Transmission: "in twelve books".
- "c. 1150 / Tzetzes quotes books 4 and 5". Text: "quoted books 4 and 5 about 1150", uncited.
- "1558–59 / First edition, Zurich", on 1558, the first year of the range. Text: printed at Zurich, preface October 1558, title page 1559.
- "1584 / Lipsius, On Constancy". Text: "On Constancy (1584)". The Sellars 2025 citation that follows belongs to the sentence on the handbooks of 1604, so 1584 is rightly listed as uncited.
- "1634 / Casaubon's English translation". Text, §The text: "Meric Casaubon's translation of 1634", uncited.
- "1652 / Gataker's edition". Text: "Thomas Gataker's edition (Cambridge, 1652)", uncited.
- "1863 / Arnold's essay". Text: "Matthew Arnold's essay 'Marcus Aurelius' (1863 ...)".
- "2012 / Exeter workshop, origin of Stoicism Today". Text: "The Stoicism Today project grew from a workshop at the University of Exeter in 2012", uncited.
- "2016 / The Daily Stoic". Text: "Holiday and Stephen Hanselman's The Daily Stoic (2016)", uncited.
- Order: chronological and consistent with the text. A timeline is the right kind.
- Caption: lists all ten drawn items and no others.
- Legibility: in both themes every label is readable and none is clipped. The 2012 and 2016 dots touch at this scale, but their labels are staggered with leader lines and stay distinct. Phone frame 5 is an empty slice past the figure's end, a by-product of the cut. The desktop image shows only 900 to about 1350, which is the house scroll rule.

Faults:
1. **The Lipsius label implies reception the text denies.** The figure is captioned as "the book's survival and reception", but the text cites On Constancy as neo-Stoicism "built ... chiefly on Seneca" and uses Lipsius to show how far the book's fame lagged. A bare "Lipsius, On Constancy" on this axis reads as a milestone in the reception of the Meditations. Fix the label: "Lipsius, On Constancy, built chiefly on Seneca".
2. **The caption attributes the Zurich edition and Arnold's essay on different rules.** It credits the Zurich edition (1558–59) to Sellars, "The Early Modern Reception", whose citation sits in the next sentence, about the manuscript's loss at the printer's. It lists Arnold (1863) as uncited, although the text's next sentence, "It [the essay] answered John Stuart Mill's comparison ... (chapter 2; Lee Behlman, 'The Victorian Marcus Aurelius', Journal of Victorian Culture, 2011)", is about the essay and carries a citation in the same way. Fix: move Arnold to the cited items, "... the Zurich edition of 1558–59 after Sellars, 'The Early Modern Reception of the Meditations' (2025); Arnold's essay (1863) after Behlman, 'The Victorian Marcus Aurelius' (2011); Arethas' letter (before 907), Tzetzes (c. 1150), Lipsius (1584), Casaubon (1634), Gataker (1652), the Exeter workshop (2012) and Holiday and Hanselman (2016), dates the text gives without a cited source."
3. **Arethas' placement is not disclosed, unlike the Suda's.** The caption discloses the Suda's placement at 990 but not Arethas' letter, drawn at 907, its latest possible date. Fix: end the caption "The text dates the Suda only to the late tenth century; it is drawn at 990. Arethas' letter, dated only before 907, is drawn at 907."
