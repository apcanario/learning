## Findings
1. [error] [attribution] Section "Arria and the women of the house".
   Current: "When he hesitated to die, she stabbed herself"
   Problem: Pliny, *Letters* 3.16, the only source cited for the scene, does not say Paetus hesitated; that detail is Dio's (60.16: when her husband showed cowardice, she steadied him). The reporter and the claim are crossed.
   Fix: "When he hesitated to die, Dio says (60.16), she stabbed herself"
   Basis: Pliny 3.16 in the Attalus translation at https://scholarly.romanletters.org/letters/pliny_younger/3016 (no hesitation; Arria is the only actor); Dio 60.16 at https://lexundria.com/dio/60.16/cy ("when her husband displayed cowardice, she strengthened his resolution").

2. [error] [accuracy] Section "Thrasea Paetus: the life of Cato and the death in 66".
   Current: "Capito's speech is the earliest statement of the "Stoic opposition" as an idea."
   Problem: The report itself, in "Was there a Stoic opposition?", cites Tigellinus in 62 calling the Stoics a sect that makes men turbulent and hungry for affairs (*Annals* 14.57), four years before Capito's speech of 66. "Earliest" is contradicted by the report.
   Fix: "Capito's speech is the fullest ancient statement of the "Stoic opposition" as an idea; Tigellinus had sketched it against Rubellius Plautus in 62 (<em>Annals</em> 14.57)."
   Basis: internal; *Annals* 14.57 is dated to 62 (as the report says in its last section).

3. [error] [pointer] Section "Thrasea Paetus: the life of Cato and the death in 66".
   Current: "Lucan had died reciting his own verse (15.70; module 10.2)."
   Problem: Module 10.2 does not tell Lucan's death; it says only that he "was dead at twenty-five". Module 7.1 gives the order to die with *Annals* 15.70.
   Fix: "Lucan had died reciting his own verse (15.70; module 7.1)."
   Basis: rome/r-10.2.html, section "Lucan: the anti-epic of civil war"; rome/r-7.1.html ("Seneca was ordered to die (15.60–64; deep dive module 13.5), and the poet Lucan too (15.70; module 10.2)").

4. [should-fix] [register] Section "Self-test", question 6.
   Current: "He dies in a garden among friends, listening to the Cynic Demetrius on the soul's parting from the body,"
   Problem: Tacitus has the quaestor find Thrasea in his garden (16.34), but he dies in a chamber to which he takes Helvidius and Demetrius (16.35). The body says only that he was in the garden when the quaestor came; the answer goes beyond the body and against Tacitus.
   Fix: "He receives the sentence in his garden among friends, listening to the Cynic Demetrius on the soul's parting from the body,"
   Basis: *Annals* 16.34–35, Church and Brodribb translation at https://sacred-texts.com/cla/tac/a16030.htm ("he led Helvidius and Demetrius into a chamber").

5. [should-fix] [accuracy] Section "Domitian: the praise of Thrasea and Helvidius as a capital charge".
   Current: "Epictetus left Rome for Nicopolis in the same expulsion (Gellius 15.11); module 13.6 follows him."
   Problem: The report's own dispute box says it is uncertain whether one measure lies behind Tacitus', Suetonius', Gellius' and Pliny's accounts, so "the same expulsion" asserts what the box calls uncertain. Gellius' decree is dated by some editors to 89 (the Perseus translation of Gellius inserts "A.D. 89"). Module 13.6 hands "the decree and its date" to this module.
   Fix: "Epictetus left Rome for Nicopolis because of a senatorial decree under Domitian (Gellius 15.11), which some editors date to 89 rather than 93; module 13.6 follows him."
   Basis: Gellius 15.11 at https://fgh.perseids.org/read/latinLit/phi1254/phi001/perseus-eng1/15.11 (editorial note "(A.D. 89.)", not in the Latin); rome/r-13.6.html ("the decree and its date are module 13.4's").

6. [should-fix] [lane] Section "Helvidius Priscus against Vespasian".
   Current: "Suetonius has Demetrius, already condemned, insult Vespasian on the road and the emperor content to call him a dog (<em>Vespasian</em> 13). "
   Problem: Module 13.3, the home of Demetrius, tells the same Suetonius anecdote ("met the emperor on the road, would not rise and barked at him, and Vespasian thought it enough to call him a dog (Suetonius, Vespasian 13)"). Dio's barking-dog message belongs with the expulsion here; Suetonius' version should have one home.
   Fix: "Module 13.3 gives Suetonius' version (<em>Vespasian</em> 13). "
   Basis: rome/r-13.3.html, the paragraph on Demetrius in the Cynics section.

7. [should-fix] [register] Section "Carry forward", item 1.
   Current: "<li>Cato became a text before he became a model: the pamphlet war of 46–45 fixed two Catos, Cicero's and Seneca's sage of constancy and the republican partisan whom Caesar attacked and Lucan exalted, and every later use chose between them.</li>"
   Problem: It disagrees with the body. The body says Cicero's *Cato* was dangerous because praising the man meant praising his politics (the Archimedean problem), that Caesar's attack was personal (avarice, Marcia, drink), and that Seneca drained off the politics while Lucan restored them. Pairing Cicero's Cato with Seneca's apolitical sage, and having Caesar attack a "republican partisan", reverses those points; the pamphlet war of 46–45 also cannot have fixed Seneca's Cato.
   Fix: "<li>Cato became a text before he became a model: in the pamphlet war of 46–45 Cicero praised the man and with him his cause, and Caesar attacked his private life; later Seneca drained off the politics to leave a sage of constancy, Lucan restored them, and every later use chose between those Catos.</li>"
   Basis: internal (sections "The pamphlet war" and "Seneca's Cato and Lucan's").

8. [minor] [accuracy] Section "The pamphlet war: Cicero's Cato and Caesar's Anticato".
   Current: "each found eager readers, the one for its author's eloquence, the other for its author's rank (<em>Caesar</em> 54)"
   Problem: Plutarch says Cicero's work was eagerly read as the ablest orator's on the noblest theme, and that both treatises have eager readers "on account of Caesar as of Cato". "Its author's rank" is the report's gloss, not Plutarch's.
   Fix: "Cicero's was eagerly read as the ablest orator's work on the noblest theme, and both found eager readers, for Caesar's sake and for Cato's (<em>Caesar</em> 54)"
   Basis: Plutarch, *Caesar* 54, Perrin translation at https://cts.perseids.org/read/greekLit/tlg0007/tlg048/perseus-eng2/54.1-54.3.

9. [minor] [attribution] Section "Cato as Plutarch draws him".
   Current: "and Cato argued it against the Peripatetic Demetrius"
   Problem: At 67 Plutarch says only "the Peripatetic"; Demetrius is named as the Peripatetic at 65 (with "Apollonides the Stoic"), and the two withdraw together at 70. The next sentence also names the Stoic but leaves the Peripatetic unnamed.
   Fix: "and Cato argued it against the Peripatetic, Demetrius (65)"; and replace "And he died with a Peripatetic and a Stoic, Apollonides, in the house (65–69)" with "And he kept the Stoic Apollonides and the Peripatetic Demetrius with him to the last night (65, 70)"
   Basis: *Cato Minor* 65 at https://cts.perseids.org/read/greekLit/tlg0007/tlg050/perseus-eng2/65.2-66.2 ("Apollonides the Stoic and Demetrius the Peripatetic"); 67 at https://lexundria.com/plut_cat_mi/67/prr (unnamed); 70 at https://lexundria.com/plut_cat_mi/70/prr ("Demetrius and Apollonides slowly withdrew").

10. [minor] [accuracy] Section "The pamphlet war: Cicero's Cato and Caesar's Anticato".
    Current: "while the <em>Anticato</em> survived only in Plutarch's refutations."
    Problem: The paragraph before cites Pliny, *Letters* 3.12.3, who preserves Caesar's drunkenness story (and names Gaius Caesar as its teller), so Plutarch is not the only witness.
    Fix: "while the <em>Anticato</em> survived only in Plutarch's refutations and Pliny's anecdote."
    Basis: Pliny 3.12 at https://scholarly.romanletters.org/letters/pliny_younger/3012/ ("Caius Caesar, in telling the story, blames him").

11. [minor] [accuracy] Section "Helvidius Priscus against Vespasian".
    Current: "The year of his death is not given; it fell in the mid-70s."
    Problem: The mid-70s date is a modern inference (a McMaster thesis gives "perhaps 75", a CAMWS abstract 74, disputed), stated here as fact.
    Fix: "The year of his death is not given; modern accounts place it in the mid-70s."
    Basis: McMaster thesis at https://prod-ms-be.lib.mcmaster.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/3f999adb-9036-4e11-9a59-03a7b10a0e13/content; writer's notes.

12. [minor] [accuracy] Section "Arria and the women of the house".
    Current: "The younger Arria, forbidden to die with Thrasea in 66,"
    Problem: Tacitus has Thrasea urge her (counsel) to live for their daughter, not forbid her; the body's own earlier sentence ("told his wife Arria not to follow him") is closer.
    Fix: "The younger Arria, urged by Thrasea not to die with him in 66,"
    Basis: *Annals* 16.34 at https://sacred-texts.com/cla/tac/a16030.htm ("he counselled to preserve her life").

13. [minor] [style] Section "Domitian: the praise of Thrasea and Helvidius as a capital charge".
    Current: "for a farce thought to glance at the emperor's divorce (<em>Domitian</em> 10.3)."
    Problem: The younger Helvidius is in *Domitian* 10.4; 10.3 is Rusticus and the expulsion.
    Fix: "for a farce thought to glance at the emperor's divorce (<em>Domitian</em> 10.4)."
    Basis: Suetonius, *Domitian* 10 at https://lexundria.com/suet_dom/10/r.

14. [minor] [style] Section "Was there a Stoic opposition?".
