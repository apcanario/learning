## Findings
1. [error] [accuracy] Section "Tacitus and Suetonius as writers".
   Current: "What survives rests on two manuscripts, one for <em>Annals</em> 1–6 and one from Monte Cassino for the rest."
   Problem: The sentence follows the list of all five works, so "what survives" takes in the <em>Agricola</em>, <em>Germania</em> and <em>Dialogue</em>. They come down through a different line, the lost Hersfeld codex and its fifteenth-century copies. Only the two histories depend on the two Medicean manuscripts.
   Fix: "What survives of the two histories rests on two manuscripts, one for <em>Annals</em> 1–6 and one from Monte Cassino for the rest."
   Basis: standard transmission (First Medicean, Laur. 68.1, for Annals 1–6; Second Medicean, Laur. 68.2, Beneventan script from Monte Cassino, for Annals 11–16 and Histories 1–5; the minor works from the Hersfeld codex). Reynolds (ed.), Texts and Transmission (1983), "Tacitus".
2. [should-fix] [accuracy] Section "Tacitus and Suetonius as writers".
   Current: "Plutarch wrote moral portraits in Greek; Suetonius gave biography its Latin form,"
   Problem: Nepos wrote Latin lives (<em>On Famous Men</em>) a century and a half earlier, and module 5.6 tells this. "Gave biography its Latin form" overclaims and contradicts that module. What Suetonius fixed was the form of imperial biography.
   Fix: "Nepos had written Latin lives (module 5.6), Plutarch moral portraits in Greek; Suetonius set the form of imperial biography,"
   Basis: rome/r-5.6.html, section "Varro and Nepos: putting the past in order" (Nepos' <em>On Famous Men</em>, surviving <em>On Foreign Generals</em>).
3. [minor] [accuracy] Section "Petronius' Satyricon and Trimalchio's dinner".
   Current: "until the watch breaks in to put out the fire (71–78)"
   Problem: There is no fire. The horn-players' din makes the watch think the house is burning, and they break in with water and axes.
   Fix: "until the watch, thinking the house on fire, breaks in (71–78)"
   Basis: Petronius, Satyricon 78.7 ("rati ardere Trimalchionis domum").
4. [minor] [attribution] Section "Seneca's tragedies".
   Current: "Thomas Newton's <em>Seneca His Tenne Tragedies</em> (1581) put all ten into English"
   Problem: Newton edited the collection and translated only the <em>Thebais</em>. Heywood, Studley, Neville and Nuce translated the other plays.
   Fix: "Thomas Newton's collection, <em>Seneca His Tenne Tragedies</em> (1581), put all ten into English"
   Basis: title page of the 1581 edition ("translated into Englysh" by several hands, ed. Newton). Standard reference knowledge; not searched.
5. [minor] [style] Section "Seneca's tragedies".
   Current: "Kyd's <em>Spanish Tragedy</em> and <em>Hamlet</em> owe their ghosts"
   Problem: This reads as Kyd's <em>Hamlet</em>, which suggests the lost Ur-Hamlet sometimes given to Kyd.
   Fix: "Kyd's <em>Spanish Tragedy</em> and Shakespeare's <em>Hamlet</em> owe their ghosts"
   Basis: not a fact check; the wording is ambiguous.
6. [minor] [pointer] Section "Coda: late Latin and the bridge to the Middle Ages".
   Current: "Ammianus Marcellinus began his history in 96, where Tacitus' <em>Histories</em> ended (module 8.4);"
   Problem: Module 8.4 uses Ammianus as its main source but never says he began in 96 or continued Tacitus. Where it stands, the pointer appears to vouch for that claim.
   Fix: "Ammianus Marcellinus, module 8.4's main source, began his history in 96, where Tacitus' <em>Histories</em> ended;"
   Basis: rome/r-8.4.html (Ammianus cited throughout, ending on 31.16.9; no mention of the start in 96). The start is in Ammianus 31.16.9 ("a principatu Caesaris Nervae exorsus").
7. [minor] [style] Section "The Plinys and Quintilian".
   Current: "published nine books of letters, each on one subject,"
   Problem: "Each" could mean each book. The single-subject rule applies to each letter.
   Fix: "published nine books of letters, each letter on one subject,"
   Basis: Pliny's single-topic convention (Sherwin-White, The Letters of Pliny, 1966).
