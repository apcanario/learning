# Rome 12.4 writer notes (unsure or unverified)

1. Line numbers of Aeneid 4 and 6 are from memory of the OCT (Mynors); check especially 4.141–150 hunt start (given 4.136–150), 4.395, 4.448–449, 4.474–498, 4.534–552, 4.569–570, 4.596–602, 4.646–647, 4.669–671, 4.696–699, 4.702–705, 6.453–454, 6.469–476; 5.1–7; 11.72–75; 12.819–840; 1.482 (the line reused at 6.469); 2.557–558.
2. Curse: the clause-by-clause split of 4.612–620 and the reading of 4.629 as hypermetric (nepotesque elided into the next line) (standard, but check the claim and the line count).
3. Feeney, "The Taciturnity of Aeneas", CQ 33 (1983) 204–219: verified by search that it treats the characters' disagreement over the relationship; Monti 1981 (Brill, Mnemosyne Suppl. 66) verified by catalogue (chapters on fides and the right hand, dynastic marriage); what Monti argues beyond that is stated only generally.
4. Dido and Cleopatra: Pease 1935, Benario 1970 (Vergilius 16), Galinsky 2003 (Horace's Cleopatra), La Penna 1985 (Enc. Virg., "Didone") taken from a 2020s Myrtia article's survey (revistas.um.es), not read directly; the full title and publisher of the Galinsky volume are from memory.
5. Augustine, City of God 9.4 quoting 4.449 of Aeneas: verified in translation; Confessions 1.13.20–22 Latin verified (bkv.unifr.ch). Confessions 5.8.15 (flight from Monica at Cyprian's shrine) from memory.
6. Dante, Monarchia 2.3 (Dido as second wife, quoting 4.171–172): from memory; book.chapter numbering varies (2.3 in most editions). Inferno 5.61–63, 5.85 from memory.
7. Ovid, Tristia 2.533–536 ("nullo legitimo foedere iunctus amor") and Heroides 7.195–196 epitaph: from memory; the epitaph's couplet number varies by edition.
8. Purcell: Chelsea libretto "usually dated 1689"; Pinnock and Wood, Early Music 20 (1992) verified by eprints abstract; "perhaps as early as 1684" from a Wikipedia summary of Wood and Pinnock; the two quoted Tate lines from memory. Earliest score "eighteenth century" (Wikipedia: copied no earlier than 1750).
9. Marlowe: title page, Children of the Chapel, 1587–93, Ganymede opening, Anna loves Iarbas, triple suicide verified (Wikipedia); that Dido speaks "sic, sic iuvat ire sub umbras" is from memory, only partly confirmed (a gloss "I rejoice at passing into the darkness" at 5.1.313).
10. Berlioz: dates, Karlsruhe 1890/Mottl, Théâtre-Lyrique 1863, "Adieu, fière cité", the Hannibal and Rome visions verified (Wikipedia). Eliot's "most telling snub" verified via a secondary blog quoting What is a Classic?; the 1944 date (16 October, in the bibliography) and the Faber 1945 edition from memory. Appian, Punica 136 for the refounding of Carthage under Caesar and Augustus from memory.
