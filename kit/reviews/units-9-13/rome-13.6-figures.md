- **Caption citations.** Each item's passage is named correctly. The caption says correctly that the canal's 67 is given without a passage. It also says correctly that Vespasian's expulsion is undated in the text (Dio 65(66).13.2 has no year) and is not drawn.
- **Drawing.** There are point events only and no spans, so the span rule does not apply. A timeline suits the content. Nothing is clipped or overlapping in either theme. The AD 65 and December 69 labels are staggered lower with leader lines and stay clear of their neighbours. AD 69 and AD 70 on the axis are close but separate.

**Fault 1 (caption).** "under Nero and in the civil war, 60–70" makes a claim the text does not. The text never says "civil war". The 70 item, the Celer prosecution, falls after the war, so the phrase does not cover the whole range drawn. The text's own framing is "He was back in Rome after Nero's fall."

Fix: open the caption with "Musonius' public career under Nero and after his fall, 60–70." Leave the rest of the caption as it is.

**Fault 2 (label; minor, fix alongside fault 1).** "Egnatius Celer condemned" is the only label in a figure of Musonius' career that does not name him. It also states the outcome in a word the text does not use: the text says "he prosecuted … and won".

Fix: relabel AD 70 as "Musonius prosecutes Egnatius Celer and wins".

## Figure 2 (tree): FIX

**How each label traces to the text**

- Root "Things": "Some things are up to us, some are not" (1.1). ok
- "Up to us: free, unhindered, unimpeded": "The first are by nature free, unhindered and unimpeded." ok
- "Not up to us: weak, slavish, hindered, another's": "the second weak, slavish, hindered and another's." ok
- Leaf "judgement, impulse, desire, aversion": "Up to us are judgement, impulse, desire and aversion." ok
- Leaf "body, property, reputation, office": "Not up to us are body, property, reputation and office." ok

**Drawing.** A two-way division with examples suits a tree. Nothing is clipped or overlapping in either theme. On a phone the two branches fall into separate frames, which is the house scroll rule.

**Fault (caption citation).** The caption assigns "Handbook 1.1 (the examples) and 1.2 (the qualities)". The text never cites 1.2 on its own. It cites "(1.1)" after "The Handbook opens with a division". The qualities sentence then carries no citation of its own, and the paragraph closes with "(1.2–3)" on the sentence about consequences ("Mistake the second … no enemy"). As written, a reader checking citations cannot find the caption's "1.2 (the qualities)" in the text.

Fix (preferred: one change to the text, caption kept as it is). Cite the qualities sentence and the consequences sentence separately:

> "The first are by nature free, unhindered and unimpeded; the second weak, slavish, hindered and another's (1.2). Mistake the second for your own and you will be thwarted, grieve and blame gods and men; keep to the first and no one will compel or hinder you, and you will have no enemy (1.3)."

Alternative (change the caption only): "… after *Handbook* 1.1 (the division and its examples) and 1.2–3 (the qualities, under the passage the text cites for them and their consequences)."

**Fault.** The words under each title paraphrase Epictetus beyond what the report says. Every phrase in a figure must trace to the text.

Fix: replace the box contents so that they keep to the text's wording.

- Box 1: title "Desire and aversion"; body "desire only what is up to us; the most urgent, because it governs the passions".
- Box 2: title "Impulse to action"; body "the appropriate acts (kathēkonta) owed to others".
- Box 3: title "Assent"; body "secures assent against error, even in sleep, drink or depression; for those already advanced".

The other way is to keep the current wording and add it to the text with its passage (3.2.1–5). That would be a change to the report, not to the figure.
