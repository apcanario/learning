## Findings
1. [error] [accuracy] Section "Protagoras: the measure tested in the Theaetetus".
   Current: "perceiver and perceived are twin offspring of meeting motions"
   Problem: In the secret doctrine the twins are the perception and the perceived quality (aisthēsis and aisthēton, 156a–b); the eye and the object are the parents, which become a seeing eye and a white thing (156d–e). The perceiver is not one of the twins.
   Fix: "perception and its object are twin offspring of meeting motions"
   Basis: Plato, Theaetetus 156a–b and 156d–e, Fowler's translation at https://cts.perseids.org/read/greekLit/tlg0059/tlg006/perseus-eng2/154-159 ("offspring, infinite in number, but always twins, the object of sense and the sense").

2. [error] [attribution] Section "The trial continued: Polycrates, Xenophon and the two Apologies".
   Current: "Hermippus, quoted by Diogenes Laertius (2.38), says Polycrates wrote the speech Anytus delivered at the trial."
   Problem: Hermippus says only that Polycrates wrote the speech; Diogenes adds that some gave it to Anytus as author, and Favorinus (in the same section) names Polyeuctus as the one who delivered it. "Anytus delivered" comes from later sources, not from Hermippus.
   Fix: "Hermippus, quoted by Diogenes Laertius (2.38), says Polycrates wrote the prosecution speech for the trial; others, Diogenes adds, gave it to Anytus."
   Basis: Diogenes Laertius 2.38–39, Hicks' translation at https://cts.perseids.org/read/greekLit/tlg0004/tlg001/perseus-eng2/2.5.38-2.5.47 ("The speech was written by Polycrates the sophist, according to Hermippus; but some say that it was by Anytus").

3. [should-fix] [pointer] Section "Which Socrates? The four witnesses weighed".
   Current: "adds to module 6.5's definitions and induction the intellectualism"
   Problem: Module 6.5 gives Aristotle's testimony that Socrates dealt with ethics, sought universal definitions and did not separate them (Metaphysics 987b1–6, 1078b17–32), but never mentions Socratic induction; its only "induction" is Aristotle's own epistemology (Posterior Analytics 2.19).
   Fix: "adds to module 6.5's universal definitions the inductive arguments (<em>Metaphysics</em> 13.4, 1078b27–29), the intellectualism"
   Basis: greece/r-6.5.html, sections "Socrates: sources and method" and "Aristotle"; Aristotle, Metaphysics 13.4, 1078b27–29 ("two things may fairly be ascribed to Socrates, inductive arguments and universal definition").

4. [should-fix] [register] Section "Self-test", question 2.
   Current: "The reversal works only if Socrates may drop the qualifier \"true for him\" at 171a–c, which a relativist can restore."
   Problem: The answer settles a question the body's first dispute box leaves open: Burnyeat argued that the reversal still catches a relativist, and Fine reads the dropped qualifiers as showing that Plato's Protagoras is an infallibilist, against whom the reversal works. As written the answer takes one side against Burnyeat.
   Fix: "Against a relativist the reversal seems to need the qualifier \"true for him\" dropped at 171a–c, a move Burnyeat defends and Fine takes as a sign that Plato's Protagoras is an infallibilist."
   Basis: MacFarlane's survey (https://www.johnmacfarlane.net/measure.pdf), summarising Burnyeat, Philosophical Review 85 (1976) 44–69 and 172–195, and Fine, "Plato's Refutation of Protagoras in the Theaetetus", Apeiron (1998).

6. [minor] [accuracy] Section "Gorgias: On Not Being and the Helen".
   Current: "What is, if anything, is either what-is or what-is-not. What-is-not is not."
   Problem: Sextus gives three alternatives, not two (what-is, what-is-not, or both), and refutes the third at 7.75–76; the two-way version undercuts the next paragraph's point that Gorgias "exhausts" the alternatives.
   Fix: "If anything is, it is what-is, or what-is-not, or both. What-is-not is not, and nothing can be both (7.75–76)."
   Basis: Sextus, Against the Mathematicians 7.66 and 7.75–76 (DK 82 B 3).

7. [minor] [accuracy] Section "Which Socrates? The four witnesses weighed".
   Current: "<strong>Aristophanes</strong> alone wrote in Socrates' lifetime."
   Problem: Other comic poets also put Socrates on stage in his lifetime: Ameipsias' Connus (423, quoted by Diogenes Laertius 2.28) and Eupolis survive in fragments. True only of the four witnesses.
   Fix: "Of the four, <strong>Aristophanes</strong> alone wrote in Socrates' lifetime."
   Basis: Diogenes Laertius 2.28 (Ameipsias on Socrates); Ameipsias' Connus competed against the Clouds in 423.
