# Rome 13.4, Cato and the Stoic opposition: figure check 2

Material: `text.txt`, `captions.md` and the twelve PNGs (desktop and phone frames, dark and light) for figures 1 and 2. Every image was looked at. Positions on the timeline were measured from the light desktop image.

## Figure 1 (tree): PASS

**Boxes, top to bottom**
- "Arria the Elder, wife of Caecina Paetus; stabbed herself, 42". The text gives "In 42 the elder Arria's husband, Caecina Paetus, was condemned … she stabbed herself" (Dio 60.16; Letters 3.16.6). The self-test ties 42 to 3.16.6 ("handed Paetus the dagger in 42 (3.16.6)").
- "Arria the Younger, wife of Thrasea Paetus; exiled in 93". The text gives "Thrasea's wife Arria was the daughter of a more famous Arria" and "The younger Arria … was exiled in 93 (Letters 3.11.3)".
- "Fannia, wife of Helvidius Priscus; exiled in 93". The text gives "her daughter Fannia, Helvidius' wife … (7.19.4)", "Charged in 93 … (Letters 7.19.4–6)", and Pliny's list "Mauricus, Gratilla, Arria and Fannia exiled (Letters 3.11.2–3)". The text names Gaius Helvidius Priscus as Thrasea's son-in-law.
- No box says more than the text says about that woman.

**Order of descent.** Elder Arria, then her daughter (Thrasea's wife), then that daughter's daughter Fannia. This matches the text: "daughter of a more famous Arria", "her granddaughter Fannia (Letters 3.16.2)", "her daughter Fannia".

**Caption**
- It claims only what the figure shows: three generations, mother to daughter, with each woman's husband.
- Each passage is attached to the item the text attaches it to:
  - 3.16.2: the line from grandmother to granddaughter.
  - 3.16.6 with Dio 60.16: the dagger in 42.
  - 3.16.10: Thrasea as son-in-law, through the "her daughter … with him" exchange.
  - 3.11.3: the younger Arria exiled in 93.
  - 7.19.4–6 with 3.11.2–3: Fannia charged and exiled in 93.
- Every date in the figure has a passage, so the caption has no undated items to declare.

**Kind and legibility**
- A tree is the right kind for a line of descent.
- Boxes and connectors are readable in both themes, with no overlap.
- In the phone frames the column of boxes sits at its natural position and is cut at the frame edges. This falls under the scrolling house rule and is not a fault.
- Renderer note, not counted against the figure: in the standalone drawing the caption sits inside the sideways-scrolling frame, so on a phone its lines run past the 390 px frame. If the site places the caption inside the scroller the same way, only a renderer change can fix that.

**Spans**
- "VESPASIAN, 69–79" matches "Vespasian's reign (69–79)".
- "DOMITIAN, 81–96" matches "under Domitian (81–96)".
- Both labels carry their years, so they read as periods. Both bars sit on the right years: 69 to 79 and 81 to 96.

**Axis.** Ticks at 40 BC, AD 1, AD 40 and AD 80 sit correctly against one another, within about one year. The renderer appears to count a year zero between BC and AD, which puts 46 BC about one year (about 4 px) too far left. That is not visible at this scale and is not counted.

**Legibility.** Everything is readable in both themes with no clipping inside the drawing. The phone-frame cuts through "Arria: Paete, non dolet" fall under the scrolling house rule.

**Faults**

1. **Caption: a date presented as cited when the text gives it without a passage.** The caption puts "93, after Agricola's death in August" inside the parenthesis that follows Agricola 2 and 45, Suetonius, Dio and Pliny. That makes it read as if those passages give the date.
   - The text states "The year was 93, after Agricola's death in August" with no passage. The next sentence cites Agricola 45.1 for the Senate's guilt, not for the date or the month.
   - Pliny 3.11.3 carries 93 only for the younger Arria's exile.
   - The house rule requires the caption to say which dates the text gives without a passage. This one is not declared.

2. **Caption: Suetonius reference.** The text cites this item to Domitian 10.4 in its narrative ("Domitian killed Rusticus … removed all philosophers from Rome and Italy … (Domitian 10.4)"). It cites 10.3 in "Where sources disagree" and in the self-test. The caption gives only 10.3. Fix: cite "Domitian 10.3–4", which covers both places where the text attaches Suetonius to this item.

3. **Label: "Arria" is ambiguous.** The text has two Arrias. The younger one, Thrasea's wife, is central to the scene in 66 and was exiled in 93, both of which are on this axis. A bare "Arria" at 42 can be misread as her. The text and Figure 1's caption say "the elder Arria".
   - Corrected label: **"Arria the Elder: Paete, non dolet"**. It wraps to three lines, and there is room above the "Thrasea walks out" label.
   - The caption item becomes "the elder Arria's dagger, 42".
