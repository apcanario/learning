## Findings
1. [error] [attribution] Section "The Aeneid in English: Douglas, Surrey, Dryden".
   Current: "Ezra Pound thought Douglas better than the original, because Douglas had heard the sea (<em>ABC of Reading</em>, 1934)."
   Problem: The "better than the original, as Douglas had heard the sea" remark is in Pound's essay "How to Read" (1929), cited from *Literary Essays* (1954), p. 35. *ABC of Reading* says something different about Douglas: a "robuster versification" than Chaucer's.
   Fix: "Ezra Pound thought Douglas better than the original, because Douglas had heard the sea (\"How to Read\", 1929)." Change the bibliography entry to match.
   Basis: UEA eprint, "Dreamscape into Landscape in Gavin Douglas", n. 4 (Pound, *Literary Essays*, 1954, p. 35), https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/71565/1/Dreamscape_into_Landscape_in_Gavin_Douglas.pdf; Heavy Feather Review on what *ABC of Reading* says, https://heavyfeatherreview.org/2022/09/01/aeneid/; Wikipedia "Eneados" (*ABC of Reading* p. 115 compares Douglas with Chaucer).

2. [error] [accuracy] Section "The Christian Virgil: the fourth Eclogue and Proba" (dispute box).
   Current: "a correspondent of Augustine and Jerome"
   Problem: Augustine wrote to Anicia Faltonia Proba (*Letters* 130, 131, 150). Jerome's letter 130 is to her granddaughter Demetrias. No letter between Jerome and Proba is known.
   Fix: "a correspondent of Augustine"
   Basis: Wikipedia "Anicia Faltonia Proba", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anicia_Faltonia_Proba (Augustine's letters 130, 131, 150; Jerome named only through Demetrias).

3. [error] [accuracy] Section "Oracle and magician" (dispute box).
   Current: "The humanist's Virgil was the poet of the text, and the magician did not survive the printed edition."
   Problem: The magician did survive into print. The romance of Virgil the magician was printed in French (*Les faictz merveilleux de Virgille*, Paris, Nyverd and Trepperel, about 1500–1515; Lyon, about 1535), in Dutch (Vorsterman, Antwerp, about 1518–1525) and in English (*Virgilius*, Doesborch, Antwerp, about 1518).
   Fix: "The humanist's Virgil was the poet of the text, though the magician lived on in print, in the French, Dutch and English chapbooks of the early sixteenth century."
   Basis: DBNL, *De Nederlandse volksboeken*, https://dbnl.org/tekst/deba001nede01_01/deba001nede01_01_0032.php; Morgan Library record of *La Vie, les ditz, et merveilles de Vergille* (Lyon, about 1535), https://themorgan.org/node/1770.

4. [should-fix] [accuracy] Section "A school text and its commentary".
   Current: "A papyrus from Masada carries <em>Aeneid</em> 4.9, written by a soldier of the garrison after the siege of 73 or 74."
   Problem: The line (Mas. 721, *Anna soror*) is confirmed. Who wrote it, and when, is not settled. The editors attribute the Latin papyri of locus 1039 to legionaries of X Fretensis, "perhaps with the exception of no. 721", and date the group to the years or months before the fall. The archive was assembled after the fall. The writer flags this claim as written from memory.
   Fix: "A papyrus found at Masada among the Roman garrison's documents carries <em>Aeneid</em> 4.9 (<em>Masada</em> II, no. 721); who wrote it, and when, is uncertain."
   Basis: P. Berdowski, "Papirusy i inskrypcje z Masady", *Nowy Filomata* 11 (2007) 25–40, summarising Cotton and Geiger, *Masada* II (1989), https://open.icm.edu.pl/bitstreams/b591b0fe-d5af-4db4-a2d4-b956fb29642a/download.

5. [should-fix] [attribution] Section "The Aeneid in English: Douglas, Surrey, Dryden" (dispute box).
   Current: "and John Barnard's study of the subscription (1963) found its subscribers drawn from every party, a national monument rather than a partisan one."
   Problem: Hammond (2014), whom the box cites, gives the finding that the subscribers "were drawn from diverse political groupings" to Barnard's essay in *John Dryden: Tercentenary Essays* (2000), not to the 1963 article in *PBSA*. "Every party" also goes beyond "diverse".
   Fix: "and John Barnard (2000) found its subscribers drawn from diverse political groupings, a national monument rather than a partisan one." Add Barnard, "Dryden, Tonson, and the Patrons of *The Works of Virgil* (1697)", in *John Dryden: Tercentenary Essays* (Oxford, 2000), to the bibliography, unless the 1963 article is checked and makes the same point.
   Basis: Hammond, "Dryden's Virgilian Kings" (2014), White Rose eprint, https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/78778/3/hammond1.pdf.

6. [should-fix] [attribution] Section "The Aeneid in English: Douglas, Surrey, Dryden".
   Current: "and had Aeneas' face recut, in the 1697 printing, to resemble William III."
   Problem: That the face was recut for 1697 is documented: a different nose and no moustache. That it was recut to resemble William is an inference (the Folger's "See the resemblance?"). Hammond says only that Aeneas "might suggest a compliment to William III". The dispute box then states it as fact: "Tonson's Aeneas wore William's face".
   Fix: "and had Aeneas' face recut for the 1697 printing, it seems to resemble William III." In the dispute box, change "Tonson's Aeneas wore William's face" to "Tonson's Aeneas seemed to wear William's face".
   Basis: Folger Collation, "Dryden's Virgil, Ogilby's Virgil: Aeneas' nose job", https://www.folger.edu/blogs/collation/drydens-virgil-ogilbys-virgil-aeneas-nose-job; Hammond 2014 (above).

7. [minor] [attribution] Section "The Aeneid in English: Douglas, Surrey, Dryden" (dispute box).
   Current: "and drafts borrowed from the Jacobite Earl of Lauderdale"
   Problem: Hammond says Dryden "appears to have drawn extensively on Lauderdale's manuscript translation". He does not speak of drafts.
   Fix: "and wide borrowing from the manuscript translation of the Jacobite Earl of Lauderdale"
   Basis: Hammond 2014 (above), footnote on Lauderdale.

8. [minor] [accuracy] Section "A school text and its commentary".
   Current: "Juvenal pitied the grammar-master who earned less than a charioteer for a year in a room where every Horace was discoloured and soot clung to the blackened Virgil (7.226–227)."
   Problem: The pay comparison is a separate passage, 7.242–243. There, at the year's end, the teacher is paid what the crowd demands for one winning charioteer. As written, the sentence blurs the comparison and cites only the soot lines.
   Fix: "Juvenal pitied the grammar-master who was paid for a year what a charioteer won in one race (7.242–243), after a year in a room where every Horace was discoloured and soot clung to the blackened Virgil (7.226–227)."
   Basis: Juvenal 7.225–227 and 7.241–243, Evans translation at https://pages.pomona.edu/~cmc24747/sources/juvenal/juv_7.htm.

9. [minor] [figure] Section "The Aeneid in English: Douglas, Surrey, Dryden" (flow figure).
   Current: "The text cites no passage for these dates; Florence Ridley's edition (1963) is its authority for Surrey's debt to Douglas."
   Problem: The caption claims something the figure does not show: no step shows Surrey's debt to Douglas. The figure also says "heroic couplets" for Dryden, words the text does not use (the text says "the rhyme").
   Fix: "The text cites no passage for these dates." In the JSON, change "1697, heroic couplets, by subscription" to "1697, rhymed, by subscription".
   Basis: BACKLOG.md, figure lessons R-1 (caption claims only what the figure shows; every name in a figure is in the text).

10. [minor] [accuracy] Section "Oracle and magician".
    Current: "Severus Alexander drew the charge to rule the peoples (6.851; <em>Severus Alexander</em> 14.5)."
    Problem: The *Historia Augusta* says that verses from the "Vergil-oracle" seemed to point to Alexander, and it quotes the whole of 6.847–853, the arts left to others, the charge to rule and *parcere subiectis*. It does not say that he drew them himself.
    Fix: "Severus Alexander was seen in the lines on the arts left to others and the charge to rule the peoples (6.847–853; <em>Severus Alexander</em> 14.5)."
    Basis: *Historia Augusta*, *Severus Alexander* 14.5, Loeb (Magie) on LacusCurtius, https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Historia_Augusta/Severus_Alexander/1*.html.

11. [minor] [register] Section "Self-test", question 8.
    Current: "vanishes when Beatrice comes (30.49–51), and sees the just Ripheus saved in his place (<em>Paradiso</em> 20.67–69)."
    Problem: Virgil has gone before the *Paradiso*, so he sees nothing there. The body has the contrast right: Ripheus in heaven, the poet in Limbo.
    Fix: "vanishes when Beatrice comes (30.49–51), and stays in Limbo while the just Ripheus is saved (<em>Paradiso</em> 20.67–69)."
    Basis: the report's own dispute box in "Dante's guide".
