## Findings
1. [error | accuracy] Section "Lucretius: physics against fear".
   Current: "The one contemporary notice is Cicero's remark to his brother in February 54"
   Problem: Nepos, Lucretius' contemporary, also names him: he ranks Julius Calidus the most elegant poet of the age "after the deaths of Lucretius and Catullus" (Atticus 12.4). Cicero's letter is the earliest notice and the only contemporary verdict on the poem, not the only notice.
   Fix: "The earliest notice is Cicero's remark to his brother in February 54"
   Basis: Nepos, Atticus 12.4 ("post Lucretii Catullique mortem"); not checked online: the passage is the standard evidence for both poets' deaths.

2. [should-fix | register] Section "Self-test", question 1.

   Problem: The model answer repeats the error in finding 1.
   Fix: "is the earliest witness and the only contemporary verdict on the poem, and it says only that the poems combine genius and art."
   Basis: as finding 1.

3. [should-fix | attribution] Section "Catullus and the new poets" (dispute box).
   Current: "The old picture of the grammarian Valerius Cato as the group's leader has been given up (T. P. Wiseman was among its critics), and many now prefer a loose network of friends to a movement with a programme."
   Problem: The Wiseman attribution is on the writers' unverified list, was softened once when written, and I could not confirm it. The sceptical side needs a name that can be checked.
   Fix: "The old picture of the grammarian Valerius Cato as the group's leader has been given up, and sceptics such as Edward Courtney (<em>The Fragmentary Latin Poets</em>, 1993) doubt that the category existed at all; many prefer a loose network of friends to a movement with a programme."
   Basis: SCS abstract "Neoteric questions", which classes Lyne 1978 as treating the neoterics as a school and Courtney 1993 (with Lightfoot 1999 and Stroup 2010) among sceptics "who doubt the category existed at all": https://classicalstudies.org/neoteric-questions . Wiseman not confirmed: searches found nothing tying him to the Valerius Cato question. About +7 words.

4. [should-fix | pointer] Section "Caesar: the general as stylist".
   Current: "and an <em>Anticato</em> answering Cicero's eulogy of Cato after Utica (Suetonius, <em>Julius</em> 56.5), both lost."
   Problem: Module 5.4 ("Cicero's Cato and Caesar's Anticato fought over him (module 5.6)") and the new syllabus prompt for deep dive 13.4 ("Cicero's Cato and Caesar's Anticato (module 5.6)") both send readers here, but 5.6 gives the pair one clause: Cicero's work is not named, dated or described, nor is the reply. Suetonius 56.5 also lists the Anticato without saying that it answered Cicero; Plutarch says so.
   Fix: "and an <em>Anticato</em> (Suetonius, <em>Julius</em> 56.5), both lost. Cicero's <em>Cato</em> of 46 praised the dead man; Caesar's reply praised Cicero's eloquence and life, likening him to Pericles and Theramenes, while it blackened Cato (Plutarch, <em>Cicero</em> 39; <em>Caesar</em> 54)."
   Basis: Plutarch, Cicero 39 (Perrin: "he praised Cicero's eloquence and his life, as most resembling that of Pericles and Theramenes"): https://lexundria.com/plut_cic/39/prr ; Plutarch, Caesar 54 (the Anticato written in reply to Cicero's encomium Cato). About +23 words; with findings 3, 6 and 7 the body stays near 2,820, inside the band (2,250–2,875).

   Problem: The box runs to six sentences against the house limit of two to four.
