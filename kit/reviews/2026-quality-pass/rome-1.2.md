## Findings
1. [error] [accuracy] Section "The language: read aloud, barely understood".
   Current: "a ritual calendar written about 250 BC, to judge by its letters, on linen radiocarbon-dated to about 390 BC,"
   Problem: The published radiocarbon study of the linen book gives about 360 to 210 cal BC, which agrees with the third-century letter forms. "About 390 BC" is the figure Wikipedia repeats ("390 BC +/- 45"), probably an uncalibrated age, and it makes up a century's gap between linen and writing. This is the date changed in the first-pass fix, so that fix needs redoing.
   Fix: "a ritual calendar written about 250 BC, to judge by its letters, on linen radiocarbon-dated to about 360–210 BC,"
   Basis: R. A. Housley, D. Srdoč and N. Horvatinčić, "AMS and radiometric dating of an Etruscan linen book and associated mummy", Radiocarbon 31.3 (1989) 970–975, abstract: linen book "ca 360–210 cal BC" (https://repository.arizona.edu/handle/10150/652924; https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/6E6A6D2D3820FCC6253A82DD90CFA74D).
2. [minor] [style] Section "What Rome took from Etruria".
   Current: "Larissa Bonfante Warren (1970)"
   Problem: The report also cites "Larissa Bonfante" (in "Tombs, banquets and women" and in a language dispute box). Readers will take these for two scholars, but they are one person writing under her married name in 1970.
   Fix: "Larissa Bonfante (as Bonfante Warren, 1970)"
   Basis: L. Bonfante Warren, "Roman triumphs and Etruscan kings", JRS 60 (1970); the same scholar as Larissa Bonfante.
