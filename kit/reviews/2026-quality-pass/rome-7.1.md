## Findings
1. [error] [accuracy] Section "Tiberius: accession, mutiny and Germanicus" (dispute box).
   Current: "names poison only in Germanicus' words"
   Problem: The decree never names poison. It records only Germanicus' own testimony that Piso was the cause of his death (line 28); no murder or poisoning charge appears, and Piso is condemned for maiestas.
   Fix: "names Piso as the cause of the death only in Germanicus' own testimony"
   Basis: BMCR 1997.07.22 (review of Eck, Caballos and Fernández 1996): "this accusation only appears in the mouth of Germanicus (l. 28)" and "it does not appear that Piso was charged with murder (poisoning)" (https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/1997/1997.07.22/); the translation at https://ancientromanhistory31-14.com/home/tiberius/germanicus/the-trial-of-piso/the-senatus-consultum-de-cn-pisone-patre/ contains no mention of poison.

2. [error] [accuracy] Section "Self-test" (question 2; same fact as finding 1).
   Current: "mentions poison only in Germanicus' words"
   Problem: As finding 1: the decree does not mention poison.
   Fix: "names Piso as the cause of the death only in Germanicus' own testimony"
   Basis: as finding 1.

3. [error] [accuracy] Section "Nero: conspiracy, the Greek tour and the end".
   Current: "and recognised Galba (Suetonius, <em>Galba</em> 11)"
   Problem: Galba 11 reports only that Nero was dead and that the citizens had sworn allegiance to Galba; it does not mention the Senate. The Senate's recognition is in Plutarch, where Icelus reports that the army, then the Senate and people, had proclaimed Galba.
   Fix: "and recognised Galba (Plutarch, <em>Galba</em> 7.1)"
   Basis: Suetonius, Galba 11 (https://lexundria.com/suet_gal/11/r); Plutarch, Galba 7.1 (https://cts.perseids.org/read/greekLit/tlg0007/tlg065/perseus-eng2/7.1-7.3). LacusCurtius' Suetonius page was blocked.

4. [minor] [style] Section "Gaius Caligula: the first years and the turn" (dispute box).
   Current: "(<em>Caligula: The Corruption of Power</em>, 1989; revised 2015)"
   Problem: The 2015 second edition appeared under a changed subtitle, so a reader looking for it under the 1989 title will not find it.
   Fix: "(<em>Caligula: The Corruption of Power</em>, 1989; revised as <em>Caligula: The Abuse of Power</em>, 2015)"
   Basis: Classics for All review of the 2015 edition: "a second edition, published with a slightly revised subtitle" (https://classicsforall.org.uk/reading-room/book-reviews/caligula-abuse-power).

## Confirmed
- Dio 58.28.3: Gaius smothered Tiberius in heavy clothes, "aided to a certain extent by Macro" (LacusCurtius, Dio book 58, https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cassius_Dio/58*.html). Dio 58.10.1 and 58.11.4–5: letter read, Sejanus condemned and executed the same day (same page). Whether 58.9–11 is Dio's text or epitome is not marked in the Loeb; the report claims nothing that depends on it.
- Dio 61.3.1: Nero read to the soldiers, and then to the Senate, speeches written by Seneca (LacusCurtius, Dio book 61).
- Barrett, Agrippina (1996): the Poppaea motive weak because Nero married her only three years after the murder; Agrippina "remained an important force in political affairs right up to the end"; the quinquennium coincides with her lifetime and her moderating influence (BMCR 1997.03.11, Susan Wood, https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/?p=20989).
- Barrett, Rome Is Burning (2020): origins "remain undetermined" (Kirkus); Nero "largely, and surely rightly, exonerated" (James Romm, LRB 43.12, https://lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v43/n12/james-romm/who-started-it).
- Champlin, Nero (2003): "the most prominent recent adherent of the Nero-as-arsonist theory" (Romm, LRB); publisher's description: Nero "probably instigated the burning of Rome"; chapter 1, "The Once and Future King", starts from the afterlife; chapter 7, "One House", on the Golden House (Perlego listing).
- Barrett, Caligula (1989), pp. 240–241: "not clinically mad" but "so obsessed with a sense of his own importance as to be practically devoid of any sense of moral responsibility" (quoted in a Goodreads review); the 2015 edition holds insanity unproven (Classics for All).
