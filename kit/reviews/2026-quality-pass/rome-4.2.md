## Findings
1. [should-fix] [figure] Section "The cost in Polybius' figures".
   Current: "On Polybius' complement of 300 rowers and 120 marines (1.26.7), the Camarina wreck alone could have drowned around 100,000 men if the ships were fully manned,"
   Problem: The report's own arithmetic does not hold: 284 wrecked ships at 420 men is about 119,000, not 100,000; and some of the 364 ships were prizes taken off the Hermaeum (1.36.11), which would not have carried full crews. The number is neither Polybius' nor a named modern estimate.
   Fix: "At Polybius' complement of 300 rowers and 120 marines (1.26.7), the 284 ships wrecked off Camarina would have carried nearly 120,000 men; some were lightly crewed prizes from the Hermaeum, so the toll is unknown, but it ran to tens of thousands,"
   Basis: Polybius 1.26.7, 1.36.11, 1.37.2 (364 ships, 80 saved); 284 × 420 = 119,280; review_notes list the figure among the writer's unverified items. The sentence continues unchanged with "mostly allied rowers and the poorest citizens".

2. [minor] [attribution] Section "Hamilcar Barca and the Aegates Islands" (dispute box).
   Current: "Tusa and Jeffrey Royal (2012) and William Murray think they come from ships no larger than triremes, not Polybius' quinqueremes"
   Problem: Murray's view is confirmed: the rams come from classes "smaller than 'fours'", and Polybius' picture of fleets of fives is "oversimplified and misleading". The 2012 article by Tusa and Royal, as far as its opening pages show, fixes the battle site and defers ram classes to later publications. Attribute the size argument to Murray unless the 2012 text is checked.
   Fix: "William Murray judges from their size that they come from ships smaller than "fours", not Polybius' quinqueremes"
   Basis: W. M. Murray, "The Ship Classes of the Egadi Rams and Polybius' Account of the First Punic War", SCS 2014 abstract (https://classicalstudies.org/annual-meeting/145/abstracts/william-m-murray); Tusa and Royal, JRA 25 (2012) (https://journalofromanarchaeology.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/v25.7-1.pdf).

3. [minor] [attribution] Section "A fleet from a wreck: Mylae and the corvus" (dispute box), and self-test answer 4.
   Current: "H. T. Wallinga (<em>The Boarding-Bridge of the Romans</em>, 1956) argued that its weight in the bow made ships unstable in heavy seas."
   Problem: Not confirmed as Wallinga's own conclusion: his book prints the naval architect J. W. Bonebakker's calculation that a bridge of about a ton was "most improbable" to upset a quinquereme's stability seriously, and the accounts found give the instability thesis to unnamed historians. The maintainer should check Wallinga before keeping the attribution here and in answer 4 ("Wallinga argued that its weight made ships unstable in heavy seas.").
   Fix: if unconfirmed: "Its weight in the bow has been blamed for losses in heavy seas, though a calculation in H. T. Wallinga's <em>The Boarding-Bridge of the Romans</em> (1956) found it unlikely to upset a quinquereme seriously." and in answer 4: "Some blame its weight for losses in heavy seas, though Wallinga's book doubts it."
   Basis: Wikipedia, "Corvus (boarding device)", citing Wallinga 1956 for Bonebakker's calculation (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvus_(boarding_device)); https://www.liquisearch.com/corvus_boarding_device; Wallinga's text not seen.

## Confirmed
- Appian, Punica 4: Carthage ordered the captains to throw Xanthippus overboard on the voyage home (https://www.livius.org/sources/content/appian/appian-the-punic-wars/appian-the-punic-wars-1/); Diodorus 23.16 has him sunk in a leaky ship as well. Valerius Maximus 9.6 ext. 1 not checked online.
