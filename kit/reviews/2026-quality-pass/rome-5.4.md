## Findings
1. [should-fix | register] Section "The consulship of 59: Bibulus watches the sky" (dispute box).
   Current: "Many modern scholars doubt that a notice sent from home, rather than announced in person, counted as <em>obnuntiatio</em> at all. The doubt is one reason Caesar would never face Rome as a private citizen."
   Problem: The modern side of the box is unnamed (the writers' own unverified item), and "The doubt" reads as if the scholars' doubt drove Caesar; the reason was the unsettled validity of his acts.
   Fix: "Many modern scholars doubt that a notice sent from home, rather than announced in person, counted as <em>obnuntiatio</em> at all, and Robert Morstein-Marx (<em>Julius Caesar and the Roman People</em>, 2021) argues that Bibulus stretched obstruction past its customary limits and that the acts of 59 stayed formally valid. The open question is one reason Caesar would never face Rome as a private citizen."
   Basis: chapter abstract, "Caesar's first consulship", Morstein-Marx 2021 (Bibulus and allies "pushed the obstructive devices available to them far beyond their customary limits"; "Caesar's acta of 59 remained formally valid"): https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/julius-caesar-and-the-roman-people/caesars-first-consulship/68AE8915AC666B9517761CC222162555. The box stays at four sentences; about +27 words, well inside the band.

2. [should-fix | figure] Section "The breakdown with Pompey and the Rubicon".
   Current: "with one legion a few days later."
   Problem: A troop figure with no source; every other number in the report is attributed.
   Fix: "with one legion (<em>Civil War</em> 1.7.8) a few days later."
   Basis: Caesar, Civil War 1.7.8 (the soldiers of the Thirteenth, "quae aderat", and Caesar sets out for Ariminum "cum ea legione"); Plutarch, Caesar 32.1, gives 300 horse and 5,000 foot.

3. [minor | accuracy] Section "The dictator's reforms".
   Current: "The Senate grew to about 900 (Dio 43.47), the quaestors to forty and the praetors to sixteen"
   Problem: Dio 43.47 gives fourteen praetors (for 45); the sixteen are in 43.51 (for 44).
   Fix: "The Senate grew to about 900 (Dio 43.47), the quaestors to forty and the praetors to sixteen (43.51.3–4)"
   Basis: Dio 43.47 ("fourteen praetors and forty quaestors"; senate of 900) and 43.51.3–4 (forty quaestors, sixteen praetors), Cary translation: https://lexundria.com/dio/43.47/cy and https://lexundria.com/dio/43.51/cy

   Fix: "Pliny admired a mind that could dictate four letters at once (7.91);"
   Basis: internal repetition (section "Gaul in outline, 58–50", paragraph on later totals).

5. [minor | lane] Section "The breakdown with Pompey and the Rubicon".
   Current: "His case was his <em>dignitas</em>, which he says was always dearer to him than life (1.9), the rights of the tribunes, and freedom from the faction of a few (1.22)."
   Problem: Module 5.6 ("Caesar: the general as stylist") has the same sentence with the same citations (1.9.2, 1.22.5) and reads it as propaganda. One sentence each is within the allowance, so this is optional; a pointer would make 5.6 the single home.

   Basis: rome/r-5.6.html, section "Caesar: the general as stylist".
