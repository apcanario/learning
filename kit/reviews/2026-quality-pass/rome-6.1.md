## Findings
1. [minor] [accuracy] Section "The inner circle and the problem of succession".
   Current: "made him adopt Germanicus, who was married to Augustus' granddaughter Agrippina, to keep Augustus' blood in the succession."
   Problem: At the adoptions of 26 June AD 4 Germanicus was not yet married; the marriage to Agrippina is usually put in the following year, AD 5.
   Fix: "made him adopt Germanicus, soon to marry Augustus' granddaughter Agrippina, to keep Augustus' blood in the succession."
   Basis: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanicus ("married ... Agrippina the Elder, probably the next year"); the first child, Nero Caesar, was born about AD 6.

2. [minor] [accuracy] Section "The inner circle and the problem of succession" (table, Tiberius row).
   Current: "powers equal to Augustus' in the provinces, AD 13 (Velleius 2.121.1)"
   Problem: Velleius sets the grant before the Pannonian triumph, which the Fasti Praenestini date to 23 October AD 12; the date AD 13 rests on Suetonius' consular law. Cite both, since the year comes from the second.
   Fix: "powers equal to Augustus' in the provinces, AD 13 (Velleius 2.121.1; Suetonius, <em>Tiberius</em> 21.1)"
   Basis: Velleius 2.121.1–2, Shipley's translation at LacusCurtius (https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Velleius_Paterculus/2D%2A.html): the grant, then the triumph; https://biblearchaeology.org/?p=73018 on Velleius' order implying AD 12 and the triumph of 23 October AD 12.

3. [minor] [pointer] Section "Refusals and the father of his country".
   Current: "Cicero had been so hailed, and Caesar had borne the title (module 5.4);"
   Problem: Module 5.4 lists Father of his Country among Caesar's honours, but neither 5.3 nor 5.4 records Cicero's acclamation, so his half of the sentence has no source.
   Fix: "Cicero had been so hailed after 63 (<em>Against Piso</em> 6), and Caesar had borne the title (module 5.4);"
   Basis: rome/r-5.3.html and rome/r-5.4.html (no mention of Cicero as parens patriae); Cicero, Against Piso 6 (Catulus named him parens patriae in a full Senate); not checked online.
