## Findings
1. [should-fix] [register] Section "Hadrian, 117–138" (Bar Kokhba dispute box).
   Current: "against accounts that shrink it."
   Problem: Only one side of the modern dispute is named. Eck's article was written against the view that the threat to Roman power had been grossly exaggerated, and his note on that view cites Bowersock among others.
   Fix: "against those, G. W. Bowersock (1980) among them, who held the threat grossly exaggerated."
   Basis: Eck, JRS 89 (1999), abstract ("it has been maintained that the threat to Roman power constituted by the revolt has been grossly exaggerated") and n. 4 citing Bowersock (https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-roman-studies/article/abs/bar-kokhba-revolt-the-roman-point-of-view/27E95F52A627562F93178F17A51D5FD4). Bowersock, "A Roman Perspective on the Bar Kochba War" (1980), not read; if the maintainer cannot confirm his position, name no one and write "against those who held the threat grossly exaggerated".

2. [minor] [lane] Section "Hadrian, 117–138" ("The travelling emperor").
   Current: "he built the Wall that the <em>Historia Augusta</em> says ran eighty miles "to divide barbarians from Romans" (<em>Hadrian</em> 11.2); module 7.5 treats it"
   Problem: Module 7.5 quotes the same sentence of the Historia Augusta (Hadrian 11.2, eighty miles, barbarians from Romans) in its own account of the Wall. The pointer is enough here.
   Fix: "he built the Wall; module 7.5 treats it"
   Basis: rome/r-7.5.html, section "The frontiers and the army in peacetime".

3. [minor] [accuracy] Section "Gibbon's verdict and what it leaves out".
   Current: "Gibbon closed his third chapter with the sentence that named the age"
   Problem: The sentence comes near the end of chapter 3, but the chapter goes on after it: the passage on the instability of a happiness resting on one man's character (which this report cites two sentences later) and the closing picture of the empire as a prison ("To resist was fatal, and it was impossible to fly") both follow it.
   Fix: "Near the end of his third chapter Gibbon wrote the sentence that named the age"
   Basis: Gibbon, Decline and Fall, chapter 3 (text as known; the Gutenberg page was blocked); the "impossible to fly" passage at https://oll.libertyfund.org/quotes/175.
