## Findings
1. [should-fix] [lane] Section "Veii: the ten-year siege".
   Current: "Young men in white then asked Juno whether she wished to go to Rome, and cried that she had nodded; later tellers made her speak (5.22.3–7). Camillus dedicated her temple on the Aventine in 392 (5.31.3). Evocatio as a mechanism is module 2.5."
   Problem: Module 2.5 tells the same sequence with the same citations: the prayer at 5.21.3, the young men in white, the nod, the Aventine temple of 392 (5.22.3–7, 5.31.3), and Livy's stage remark at 5.21.8–9. It also states "Module 3.3 narrates the siege", which leaves the rite to 2.5. The prayer is needed here for the narrative; the consent scene and the temple belong to 2.5.
   Fix: "Her consent, a nod in Livy and speech in later tellers (5.22.3–7), and her Aventine temple of 392 belong with the rite in module 2.5."
   Basis: rome/r-2.5.html, section "Evocatio: calling a god out of an enemy city".

2. [should-fix] [lane] Section "Self-test", question 4.
   Current: "<li>How did Camillus bring Juno Regina to Rome, as Livy tells it?"
   Problem: The question tests 2.5's material rather than the siege, and once finding 1 is applied its answer no longer appears in this report.
   Fix: replace the whole item with: "<li>Which details of the siege of Veii are probably sound, and what shaped the rest?<details><summary>Answer</summary><p>Pay for citizen soldiers from 406, funded by a war tax (Livy 4.59–60), and winter campaigning (5.2) are institutional details. The ten years and the Trojan comparison drawn by Livy's own speaker (5.4.11) are literary shaping; Cornell accepts the fall in 396 but doubts the ten years.</p></details></li>"
   Basis: the report's own section "Veii: the ten-year siege" and its dispute box.

3. [should-fix] [lane] Section "Rebuilding: the wall and the second founder".
   Current: "Arnaldo Momigliano ("Camillus and Concord", 1942) argued that the temple of Concord Camillus supposedly vowed in 367 is a later invention."
   Problem: Module 3.1 makes the same point from the same article ("Momigliano ... judged the earlier one invented"). There it is evidence for the module's question about the reliability of Livy; here it is a second telling.
   Fix: "His temple of Concord of 367 is probably invented (module 3.1)."
   Basis: rome/r-3.1.html, "Livy books 2 to 10 and the Gracchan lens", second paragraph.

4. [should-fix] [figure] Section "Rebuilding: the wall and the second founder".
   Current: "about 11 km long and enclosing some 426 hectares,"
   Problem: The length and area do not say whose measurement they are. They are the usual figures, but at least one current reference prints 246 hectares, so the source matters.
   Fix: name the measurement used, for example "about 11 km long and enclosing some 426 hectares on the usual modern reckoning," or a named author if the maintainer has the page.
   Basis: not checked against Cornell or Cifani. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servian_Wall gives 11 km (citing Fields 2008) and 246 hectares, apparently a transposition.

5. [minor] [accuracy] Section "Veii: the ten-year siege".
   Current: "Veii, 16 km up the Tiber,"
   Problem: Veii stands on a plateau over the Cremera valley, not on the Tiber. Module 1.2 has "16 km from Rome".
   Fix: "Veii, 16 km north of Rome,"
   Basis: rome/r-1.2.html; rome/r-0.2.html (Veii "held the right bank").

6. [minor] [style] Section "Veii in the ground".
   Current: "given there by Avile Vipiennas,"
   Problem: The inscription reads "mini muluva[an]ece avile vipiienas", and module 1.5 spells it Vipiienas.
   Fix: "given there by Avile Vipiienas,"
   Basis: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aulus_Vibenna; rome/r-1.5.html.

7. [minor] [register] Section "Self-test", question 5.
   Current: "It was the largest single gain of land in Roman history:"
   Problem: This states as fact a judgement that the body rightly attributes to Oakley. Unqualified, it is open to dispute given later annexations such as the Sabine land in 290.
   Fix: "In Oakley's judgement it was the largest single increase in Roman territory, and on Taylor's reckoning it added about half:"
   Basis: Oakley ("the largest increase to Roman territory in the history of the state") and L. R. Taylor (1960, "increased the Roman territory by some 50%"), both quoted at https://keytoumbria.com/ROMAN_REPUBLIC/Political_Settlements_in_Etruria_%28396_-_265_BC%29.html.
