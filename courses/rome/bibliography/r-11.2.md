# Rome 11.2: Generals and their armies: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Modern works
- Brunt, P. A., "The Army and the Land in the Roman Revolution", *Journal of Roman Studies* 52 (1962) 69–86; the report also says it was revised in *The Fall of the Roman Republic and Related Essays* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1988). The quoted "with arms in their hands" was checked by the writer against the JSTOR first-page extract; the other positions attributed to Brunt come from a secondary summary.
- Brunt, P. A., *Italian Manpower, 225 B.C.–A.D. 14* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1971) (title and publisher from memory; not verified). The writer's account of its argument that the levy remained the normal method is second-hand.
- Chrissanthos, Stefan G., "Caesar and the Mutiny of 47 B.C.", *Journal of Roman Studies* 91 (2001) 63–75. The writer's account of the argument comes from a thesis summary, not the article.
- de Blois, Lukas, *The Roman Army and Politics in the First Century before Christ* (Amsterdam: Gieben, 1987).
- Gabba, Emilio, *Republican Rome, the Army and the Allies*, trans. P. J. Cuff (Berkeley: University of California Press; Oxford: Blackwell, 1976). The report's "articles of 1949 and 1951" are "Le origini dell'esercito professionale in Roma: i proletari e la riforma di Mario", *Athenaeum* 27 (1949) 173–209, and "Ricerche sull'esercito professionale romano da Mario ad Augusto", *Athenaeum* 29 (1951) 171–272 (identified from context, the titles confirmed in a bibliography listing them). The writer drew on a secondary summary of Gabba's *Esercito e società nella tarda repubblica romana* (Florence: La Nuova Italia, 1973), not the book itself; the report's "Gabba's (1976)" is the English collection.
- Gruen, Erich S., *The Last Generation of the Roman Republic* (Berkeley: University of California Press, 1974). The writer used a secondary summary citing the 1995 reissue.
- Keaveney, Arthur, *The Army in the Roman Revolution* (London: Routledge, 2007) (publisher not verified). The writer worked from *BMCR* 2008.07.52, not the book.
- Premerstein, Anton von, *Vom Werden und Wesen des Prinzipats*, Abhandlungen der Bayerischen Akademie der Wissenschaften, phil.-hist. Abteilung, N.F. 15 (Munich: Beck, 1937) (series and publisher not verified).
- Syme, Ronald, "Imperator Caesar: A Study in Nomenclature", *Historia* 7 (1958) 172–188 (not verified; volume and pages from memory, since a bibliography that cites it printed neither).

## Corpora and inscriptions
- Crawford, Michael H., *Roman Republican Coinage* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1974) (*RRC*), no. 359 (Sulla's coins of 84–83 with two trophies and IMPER ITERVM). The writer gave the number from memory (not verified).

## Not resolved
- "an essay of 2000 on the military 'middle cadre'" (Lukas de Blois, 2000): the report gives no title. A volume in the *Histos* Supplements (no. 15, 2023) cites it as "de Blois (2000)" for the emergence of a middle cadre of career soldiers, but I could not find the full entry. The writer's notes say the title was verified, but it is not recorded in the report. The 2007 chapter "Army and General in the Late Roman Republic", in P. Erdkamp (ed.), *A Companion to the Roman Army* (Oxford: Blackwell, 2007), is a related work, but the report does not cite it.
- "Brunt (1988)" and "Brunt (1962, revised in *The Fall of the Roman Republic and Related Essays*, 1988)": the page range of the revised essay in the 1988 volume is not confirmed.
- Claims taken by the writer from secondary summaries or reviews and not checked in the works themselves: Gabba's "military clientele bound by economic interest alone", Keaveney's views on Sulla and the commander as magistrate, Brunt on conditional loyalty, and de Blois on officers.
