# Rome 13.2: Stoicism made Roman: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Augustine, *City of God* 9.4 (retelling Gellius' pale Stoic).
- Cicero, *Letters to Atticus* 16.11.4; 16.14.3. The writer gave the letters from memory and left the sections out of the argument on purpose.
- Cicero, *On Duties* 1.8; 1.46; 1.53–55; 1.57–58; 1.107–121; 1.107; 1.108–113; 1.110; 1.112; 1.114; 1.115; 1.117–118; 1.124; 3.13–16. The report writes the main range as 1.107–121; the writer's notes give 1.107–124.
- Cicero, *On Ends* 3.16; 3.22; 3.48; 3.51–52; 3.52; 3.57; 3.75; 4 (general, for the charge that the Stoics differed from the Peripatetics only in words); 4.74. The writer gave 3.16, 3.48, 3.52, 3.57, 3.75 and 4.74 from memory.
- Cicero, *On Fate* 1–2; 7–8; 10–11; 23–25; 42–43. The text is incomplete at both ends. The writer gave the section numbers from memory.
- Cicero, *On the Laws* 2.5.
- Cicero, *Paradoxa Stoicorum* (*Stoic Paradoxes*), preface 4.
- Cicero, *Pro Murena* 61–66, especially 61, 62 and 63–64.
- Cicero, *Tusculan Disputations* 3.6; 3.7; 3.76; 3.76–77; 4.11–14; 4.12–14; also 3–4 as a pair. The writer gave the section numbers from memory and thought 3.7 might be 4.10.
- Epictetus, *Handbook* 53 (Cleanthes' hymn, four lines in the Greek).
- Gellius, *Attic Nights* 7.2 (including 7.2.15); 9.5.8 (Hierocles); 19.1 (the pale Stoic, citing Epictetus' lost fifth book of *Discourses*). The writer gave 7.2.15 as preserving Cicero's lost verdict on Chrysippus.
- Hierocles, *Elements of Ethics*, from a papyrus published in 1906; and the circles of oikeiosis excerpted in Stobaeus, *Anthology* 4.27.23 (= LS 57G). Stobaeus' edition is Wachsmuth and Hense. The 1906 first edition is, I believe, H. von Arnim, *Hierokles, Ethische Elementarlehre (Papyrus 9780)*, Berliner Klassikertexte 4 (Berlin, 1906) (not verified).
- Hippolytus, *Refutation of All Heresies* 1.21 (= SVF 2.975; the dog tied to a cart).
- Horace, *Epistles* 1.1.106–108.
- Horace, *Satires* 1.3 (lines 96–98 and 124–139); 2.3 (line 326). Line numbers given from memory by the writer.
- Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 6.44.
- Plutarch, *Cato Minor* 21.
- Plutarch, *On Stoic Self-Contradictions* (*De Stoicorum repugnantiis*, *Moralia*) 1033b–c.
- Seneca the Younger, *Letters* 42.1; 75; 94.1; 94.2; 95.52–53; 95.65–66; 107.11; 116.5. The claim that the fifth line of 107.11 has no counterpart in Epictetus' Greek rests on *Handbook* 53; the writer gave the letter numbers from memory.
- Seneca the Younger, *On Anger* 1.9; 1.17; 2.1–4 (including 2.2 and 2.4.1); 3.36.
- Seneca the Younger, *On Leisure* 3.2; 4.1; 6.4; 8.
- Seneca the Younger, *On Providence* 1.1; 2.1; 2.6; 2.9–12; 3; 4.6; 4.7; 6.7.
- Seneca the Younger, *On the Happy Life* 17.3; 26.1.
- Stobaeus, *Anthology* 4.27.23 (see Hierocles above).
- Tacitus, *Annals* 6.22. The report treats the "unnamed school" as usually read as Stoic; the label is a modern reading, since Tacitus gives the view to "some" (*alii*), as the checker noted.

## Modern works
- Gill, Christopher, "Personhood and Personality: The Four-Personae Theory in Cicero, *De officiis* I", *Oxford Studies in Ancient Philosophy* 6 (1988) 169–199. The writer's account of his reconstruction of a Panaetian original comes through a summary by Matthew Roller.
- Inwood, Brad, "Seneca and Psychological Dualism", in Jacques Brunschwig and Martha C. Nussbaum (eds), *Passions and Perceptions: Studies in Hellenistic Philosophy of Mind. Proceedings of the Fifth Symposium Hellenisticum* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1993) (book confirmed; Inwood's chapter and its pages, which I recall as 150–183, not verified). The writer took his position from *BMCR* and *NDPR* reviews.
- Nussbaum, Martha C., "Patriotism and Cosmopolitanism", *Boston Review* 19.5 (October/November 1994) 3–6 (the essay and its Boston Review forum are confirmed; issue and pages from memory, not verified). It was reprinted in Joshua Cohen (ed.), *For Love of Country* (Boston: Beacon Press, 1996). The use of Hierocles' circles is from the writer's memory.
- Sorabji, Richard, *Emotion and Peace of Mind: From Stoic Agitation to Christian Temptation* (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2000) (subtitle and publisher not verified). The writer took the position from reviews.

## Corpora and inscriptions

## Not resolved
- "Zopyrus read in Socrates' face": the report cites *On Fate* 10–11 only; section numbers not checked against the text.
- "Gill 1988 as reconstructing a Panaetian original", "Sorabji 2000" and "Inwood 1993": positions taken from secondary summaries and reviews, not the works.
- Hierocles' papyrus of 1906: the edition is identified from memory, and the report names no editor.
- "Seneca's dialogues" and "letters" section numbers: as listed above, given by the writer from memory and not checked.
