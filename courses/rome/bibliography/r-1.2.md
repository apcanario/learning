# Rome 1.2: The Etruscans: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Aristotle, *Politics* 1280a (Etruscan–Carthaginian treaties on imports and mutual protection).
- Aristotle, *Customs of the Tyrrhenians*, quoted in Athenaeus 1.23d ("under the same cloak").
- Athenaeus, *Deipnosophistae* 1.23d; 4.153f (Nicolaus of Damascus on gladiators); 12.517d–518b (Theopompus, *FGrHist* 115 F 204). Loeb (Olson).
- Censorinus, *On the Birthday* 17.5–6 (citing Varro; the ten *saecula*).
- Cicero, *On Divination* 1.72 (*libri haruspicini*, *fulgurales*, *rituales*); 1.92 (sons of leading men sent to learn the discipline; haruspices at Rome); 2.50 (Tages).
- Diodorus Siculus 5.13 (Elban iron); 5.40 (Posidonius' luxurious Etruscans); 11.51 (Cumae, 474); 15.14 (Pyrgi sacked, 384).
- Dionysius of Halicarnassus, *Roman Antiquities* 1.28.2 (Xanthus); 1.28.3 (Hellanicus, Nanas and Cortona); 1.28.4 (Myrsilus of Lesbos); 1.30 and 1.30.3 (the Etruscans native; *Rasenna*); 1.89–90 (Romans as Greeks); 3.61 (regalia sent to Tarquinius Priscus). Loeb (Cary).
- Festus, s.v. *rituales* (no locator beyond the lemma in the report).
- Herodotus 1.94 (Lydian origin); 1.166–167 (Alalia); 4.152 (Sostratos).
- Livy 1.8.3 (curule chair, *praetexta*, twelve lictors); 4.23, 4.25 and 5.17 (Fanum Voltumnae); 4.37 (Capua); 5.1 and 5.1.6 (the league and Veii in 403; Etruscan religiosity); 5.4.11 (Veii and Troy); 5.21–22 (fall of Veii); 5.33 (Etruscans beyond the Apennines); 9.40.17 (Campanian gladiators); *Periochae* 16 (first gladiatorial show, 264).
- Pindar, *Pythian* 1.71–75.
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* 2.138 (lightning); 2.143 (regions of the sky); 34.34 (Volsinii's statues, citing Metrodorus of Scepsis); 35.157 (Vulca of Veii).
- Posidonius, as reported in Diodorus 5.40 (see above).
- Seneca, *Natural Questions* 2.32.2; 2.41.
- Servius on Virgil, *Eclogues* 9.46 (the comet of 44 BC).
- Strabo 5.2.3 (Caere's treasury at Delphi).
- Suetonius, *Claudius* 42.2 (the *Tyrrhenika*).
- Tacitus, *Annals* 4.55 (Sardis, AD 26); 11.15 (college of haruspices).
- Theopompus, *FGrHist* 115 F 204 (in Athenaeus 12.517d–518b). Jacoby, *Die Fragmente der griechischen Historiker*, part IIB.
- Virgil, *Aeneid* 8.479 ("Lydian people").
- Vitruvius, *On Architecture* 4.7 (Tuscan temple form).
- Zosimus 5.41 (haruspices and Alaric, AD 408).

- Larissa Bonfante Warren, "Roman Triumphs and Etruscan Kings: The Changing Face of the Triumph", *Journal of Roman Studies* 60 (1970) 49–66 (not verified). The report cites her as "Bonfante (as Bonfante Warren, 1970)".

- Georges Ville, *La gladiature en Occident des origines à la mort de Domitien* (Rome: École française de Rome, 1981) (identified from context; not verified).

## Corpora and inscriptions
- *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum* XI 5265 (the Constantinian rescript to Hispellum; cited once).
- Etruscan texts named without numbers: the Pyrgi tablets, the *Liber Linteus* (Zagreb), the Tabula Capuana, the Cippus Perusinus, the Marsiliana d'Albegna alphabet tablet, the Lemnos stele (Kaminia), the Regolini-Galassi silver vessels (*mi larthia*). The standard collection is H. Rix and others, *Etruskische Texte* (Tübingen: Narr, 1991), but the report cites no numbers (not verified).

## Not resolved
- "John Boardman" (against Vickers and Gill), "Nigel Spivey; Christoph Reusser" (Etruscan buyers and scenes), "Frederik Woudhuizen" (Etruscan as Luwian), "Helmut Rix", "Carlo de Simone", "Larissa Bonfante" (women's standing, no title or year), "Simonetta Stopponi" (excavations since 2000), "John Ward-Perkins' South Etruria survey": named without any work or year.
- "Hellanicus", "Myrsilus of Lesbos", "Xanthus", "Metrodorus of Scepsis", "Nicolaus of Damascus": known only through the intermediaries cited above.
