# Rome 12.6: The gods of the Aeneid: sources

Written 1 November 2026 (Opus 5.5) with the report. Line numbers of the *Aeneid* follow the standard Oxford text (Mynors); "(not verified)" marks a reference given from memory that could not be confirmed this session.

## Ancient sources
- Virgil, *Aeneid* (OCT, ed. R. A. B. Mynors, 1969): 1.10; 1.11; 1.12–28 (1.19–22, 1.26–28); 1.33; 1.37–49; 1.39; 1.50–123; 1.65–75; 1.124–156; 1.229–253; 1.254; 1.257–296 (1.262, 1.279–282, 1.294–296); 1.297–304; 1.314–405; 1.378; 1.407–408; 1.657–722; 1.740–746; 2.293–297; 2.314–317; 2.594–595; 2.602–603; 2.610–612; 2.707–720; 2.717; 3.84–98 (3.97–98); 3.147–171; 3.403–409; 3.435–440; 4.68–69; 4.90–128; 4.206–218; 4.219–278; 4.259–267; 4.361; 4.379–380; 4.393–396; 4.449; 4.554–570; 4.614; 4.693–705 (4.696); 5.604–663; 5.709–710; 5.779–826 (5.803–811, 5.815); 6.69–74; 7.293–294; 7.312; 7.313–316; 7.323–571 (7.327, 7.341–405, 7.406–474, 7.475–510, 7.552–560); 7.620–622; 8.268–272; 8.370–406; 8.398; 8.608–625; 8.679; 8.698–706 (8.704–706); 8.720; 9.1–24; 9.638–658 (9.641); 10.11–13; 10.18–95; 10.112–113; 10.464–473 (10.467, 10.470–471); 10.517–520; 10.622–627; 12.134–160; 12.311–317; 12.411–424; 12.725–727; 12.791–842 (12.793, 12.819–828, 12.829, 12.833, 12.834–840, 12.841); 12.843–886; 12.946–949.
- Augustine, *City of God* 9.4 (the Stoic sage and *Aeneid* 4.449).
- Cicero, *On the Nature of the Gods* 1.116 (pietas as justice towards the gods); 2.66 (air consecrated under Juno's name).
- Euripides, *Heracles* (Hera sends Iris and Lyssa; general reference, no line given).
- Homer, *Iliad* 16 (Sarpedon; general); 20.293–308; 20.307–308; 22.209–213.
- Horace, *Odes* 3.3 (Juno's speech), 3.3.30–33.
- Lucretius, *On the Nature of Things* 2.651 (the gods untouched by anger; numbered 2.651 in Bailey's OCT; some editions differ by a line).
- Macrobius, *Saturnalia* 3.9 (the formula of evocation used at Carthage).
- *Res Gestae Divi Augusti* 34.2 (the golden shield).
- Seneca, *Letters* 107.11 (Cleanthes' prayer in Latin).
- Servius, *Commentary on the Aeneid*, on 1.47 (Juno as air) (not verified); on 1.281 (Ennius: "bello Punico secundo, ut ait Ennius, placata Iuno coepit favere Romanis", as quoted by Feeney 1984, n. 51); on 12.841 ("sed constat bello Punico secundo exoratam Iunonem, tertio vero bello a Scipione sacris quibusdam etiam Romam esse translatam", as quoted in a secondary article found by search).
- Ennius, *Annales*, known here only through Servius on *Aeneid* 1.281.
- Suetonius, *Augustus* 31.1 (the Sibylline Books in gilded cases under Palatine Apollo).

## Modern works
- Armstrong, David, Jeffrey Fish, Patricia A. Johnston and Marilyn B. Skinner (eds), *Vergil, Philodemus, and the Augustans* (Austin: University of Texas Press, 2004); Fish's chapter on anger, known from the review in *Bryn Mawr Classical Review* 2005.04.64.
- Bailey, Cyril, *Religion in Virgil* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1935); his identification of fate with Jupiter's will as reported by Casali (2024).
- Bowra, C. M., "Aeneas and the Stoic Ideal", *Greece & Rome* 3.7 (1933) 8–21.
- Casali, Sergio, review of Zanker, *Bryn Mawr Classical Review* 2024.10.47.
- Feeney, D. C., "The Reconciliations of Juno", *Classical Quarterly* 34.1 (1984) 179–194 (reprinted in S. J. Harrison (ed.), *Oxford Readings in Vergil's Aeneid*, Oxford, 1990, not verified).
- Feeney, D. C., *The Gods in Epic: Poets and Critics of the Classical Tradition* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1991); the Virgil chapter as summarised in *Bryn Mawr Classical Review* 1992.02.08.
- Heinze, Richard, *Virgils epische Technik* (Leipzig: Teubner, 1903); his view of fate as reported by Casali (2024).
- Zanker, Graham, *Fate and the Hero in Virgil's Aeneid: Stoic World Fate and Human Responsibility* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2023).
- Diogenes Laertius, *Lives of the Philosophers* 7.135 (god, mind, fate and Zeus one).
