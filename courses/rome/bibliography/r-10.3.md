# Rome 10.3: Reading Roman art and architecture: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Cassius Dio, *Roman History* 53.27.2 (why the Pantheon is so called); 69.7.1 (Hadrian holding court there).
- Cicero, *Letters to Atticus* 1.8–1.10 (statues for Tusculum). Standard edition: D. R. Shackleton Bailey, *Cicero's Letters to Atticus* (Cambridge, 1965–70).
- Cicero, *Letters to Friends* (*Ad familiares*) 7.23 (to Fadius Gallus; older editions read Fabius).
- Eusebius, *Life of Constantine* 4.15.
- Herodian, *History of the Empire* 3.9.12 (battle paintings sent to Rome by Severus).
- Historia Augusta, *Hadrian* 26.1.
- Livy 39.44.7 (Cato's Basilica Porcia).
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* 35.22–23 (Messalla's and Mancinus' pictures); 35.110 (Philoxenus of Eretria for Cassander); 35.120 (Famulus or Fabullus); 35.154 (Varro on Damophilus and Gorgasus); 36.34 (the group by Apollonius and Tauriscus in Pollio's collection); 36.39–40 (Pasiteles); 36.184 (Sosus' unswept room); 36.189 (figured pavements; glass mosaic).
- Pliny the Younger, *Panegyricus* 52.4–5.
- Suetonius, *Domitian* 23.1.
- Velleius Paterculus, *Roman History* 1.11.5 (first marble temple at Rome); 1.13.4 (Mummius and the contractors).
- Vitruvius, *On Architecture* 2.6.1 (Puteolan dust); 4.7 (the Tuscan temple); 7.5.1–4 (with 7.5.3–4, reeds for columns and monsters for real things).
