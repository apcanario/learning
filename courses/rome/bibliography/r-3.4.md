# Rome 3.4: The Samnite Wars and the Roman system in Italy: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Appian, *Samnite History*: fragments, cited generally as a source for the conquest (no passage).
- Aristotle, *Politics* 1270a; 1326b.
- Aulus Gellius, *Attic Nights* 16.13.9 ("little images and likenesses" of the Roman people).
- Cicero, *De lege agraria* 2.73 ("bulwarks of empire").
- Cicero, *Pro Balbo* 35–36.
- Diodorus Siculus, *Library of History* 16.45.8; 19.72; 20.36; 21.6.1 (Duris of Samos on Sentinum). Books 19 and 20 are also relied on generally.
- Dionysius of Halicarnassus, *Roman Antiquities* 2.16–17; the fragments of his later books are cited generally.
- Duris of Samos: lost; known here only through Diodorus 21.6.1.
- Frontinus, *On Aqueducts* 5 (the Aqua Appia, dated to "the thirtieth year of the Samnite war"). Standard edition: Loeb (Bennett).
- Herodotus, *Histories* 7.234.
- *Ineditum Vaticanum* (anonymous Greek text; the exchange between a Roman and a Carthaginian in 264, no passage number given). Edition: Norsa and Vitelli, Studi e Testi 53 (Vatican City, 1939) (from memory, not verified).
- Livy, *History of Rome* 7.19.4; 7.29–31; 8.8; 8.8.3; 8.16; 8.21.11; 8.22–23; 8.23.12; 9.2–6; 9.3; 9.5–11; 9.5.2; 9.8–11; 9.12–15; 9.23; 9.29–34; 9.29.9–11; 9.44–45; 9.45; 9.46; 9.46.10–15; 10.1; 10.3; 10.3–5; 10.7–8; 10.11–12; 10.16–18; 10.27; 10.27–29; 10.28–29; 10.29; 10.30; 10.38–42; 27.9–10; 27.38; 29.15. Books 7–10 are also relied on generally. *Periochae* 11–15 (the report cites "11" for the peace of 290 and the conquest of the Sabines). The *Periochae* are in vol. 14 of the Loeb Livy (Schlesinger and Geer).
- Polybius, *Histories* 1.20.14; 2.18–23 (the Gallic digression); 2.19–20; 2.19.5–6; 2.23–24; 2.24; 3.107.12; 6.19–42; 6.39.15; 18.28–32. Standard edition: Loeb (Paton, rev. Walbank and Habicht).
- Sallust, *Catiline* 51.38 (Caesar's speech).
- Tacitus, *Annals* 11.24 (Claudius' speech).
- Fabius Pictor and Valerius Antias: lost annalists named as Livy's sources (no fragments cited).
