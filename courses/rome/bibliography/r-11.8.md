# Rome 11.8: Why the Republic fell: sources

Curated 1 November 2026 by the writer (Opus 5.5). "(not verified)" marks a reference given from memory and not checked during writing.

## Ancient sources
- Appian, *Civil Wars* 1.1–5 (the preface: quarrels within the law until Tiberius Gracchus, escalation through Sulla, Caesar, the triumvirs, to Augustus), 1.6 (from civil commotion to concord and monarchy), checked against H. White's Loeb translation (1913) on Bill Thayer's LacusCurtius.
- Augustine, *City of God* 2.21 (preserving Cicero, *On the Republic* 5.2) (not verified; via module 5.6).
- Cassius Dio 44.2 (democracy's fair name; monarchy practical; an empire too large for democratic moderation; Brutus and Cassius should have considered this), checked against Cary's Loeb translation via Lexundria; book 52 (the debate of Agrippa and Maecenas, set in 29 BC) (not verified).
- Cicero, *On the Republic* 5.2 (the republic lost in fact through vice) (not verified; via module 5.6).
- Livy, *Periochae* 60 (census of 125/124: 394,736) and 98 (census of 70: 900,000) (via deep dive module 11.3).
- Lucan, *Civil War* 1.67–69 (the question of causes), 1.70–82 (envious fate; great things collapse upon themselves), 1.84–93 (Rome the common property of three masters; shared power never lasting), 1.128 (the winning cause pleased the gods, the losing one Cato), 1.158–182 (wealth, luxury, estates, bought offices, debt, war profitable to many), line numbers checked against H. T. Riley's translation (Pomona College web edition).
- Plutarch, *Cato Maior* 27 (Scipio Nasica: Carthage to be kept so that fear would discipline Rome) (via module 4.4).
- Polybius 6.57 (prosperity, rivalry for office, the people's anger; the fairest names, freedom and democracy, over mob rule) (not verified against a text; content from memory and module 4.5).
- Sallust, *Catiline* 10 (after Carthage, the desire for money and then power); *Jugurtha* 41.2–5 (*metus hostilis*; licence of nobles and people) (via module 5.6); *Histories* (discord pushed back to the Republic's beginning) (via module 5.6).
- Suetonius, *Julius* 54.2 (Gallic gold sold at 3,000 sesterces a pound) (via deep dive module 11.6).
- Tacitus, *Histories* 2.38 (the old lust for power grew with the empire; tribunes, consuls, Marius, Sulla, Pompey) (not verified against a text).
- Velleius Paterculus 2.1 (decline from 146) (via module 0.3).
