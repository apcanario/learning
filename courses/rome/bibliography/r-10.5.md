# Rome 10.5: Capstone: the whole thing in one sitting: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed. This report is an examination built from the earlier modules; its model answers cite ancient passages and a few modern scholars, and most of the underlying references are curated in the reports it points to.

## Ancient sources
- Appian, *Punic Wars* (*Libyke*) 76–81 (Carthage's surrender and the demands of 149; the report cites it as "*Punica*"). *Civil Wars* 1.7 (the rich take the public land).
- Augustine, *City of God* 6.5 (Varro on myth and the poets).
- Aurelius Victor, *Caesares* (Diocletian greeted as lord and god; the report gives no passage).
- Cassius Dio 51.20.6–7 (temples with Roma at Pergamum and Nicomedia); 53.12.3 (Augustus leaves the senators unarmed); 68.3 (Nerva adopts Trajan); 69.1 (Plotina and the adoption); 69.20 (adoption of Antoninus); 79(78).36.3 (the cost of Caracalla's pay rise).
- Cicero, *Against Catiline* 4.10 (and the four speeches generally).
- Cicero, *Brutus* 62 (false triumphs, consulships and genealogies).
- Cicero, *De domo sua* 39–40.
- Cicero, *On Divination* 1.72; 2.50 (Tages and the Etruscan discipline).
- Cicero, *On the Laws* 2.58–59; 2.64 (funeral rules taken from Solon); 3.22 (Sulla's tribunate).

- Cicero, *Pro Sestio* 106.
- Collatio legum Mosaicarum et Romanarum 6.4; 15.3 (Diocletian's edict on the greatest crime).
- Diodorus Siculus 14.116–117 (the gold paid and recovered at Veascium); 20.14.4–6 (the sacrifice of 310 BC).
- Dionysius of Halicarnassus, *Roman Antiquities* 1.5.1; 1.11.1 (Cato's Achaean Aborigines); 1.28–30; 1.61–62; 1.72.2 (Hellanicus on Aeneas); 1.74 (rival foundation dates, citing Timaeus, Cincius and Ennius); 1.89–90; 2.18–19; 12.4.2–5 (Servilius Ahala and Maelius, citing Piso and Cincius). Standard text: Jacoby (Teubner, 1885–1925) and Cary (Loeb); the report names no edition.
- Eusebius, *Life of Constantine* 1.27–32 (with 1.28.1; the vision of the cross); 4.61–62 (baptism near Nicomedia). *Church History* is mentioned without a passage.
- Festus, *On the Meaning of Words*, cited for Romulus' tomb guesses and the order of priests; no passage given.
- Gaius, *Institutes* 1.8 (persons, things, actions); 1.55 (patria potestas). The same writer is cited in *Digest* 10.1.13 (a boundary rule matched to Solon's).
- Gellius, *Attic Nights* 6.3.37 (Cato in 167 and a 500-iugera penalty).
- Homer, *Iliad* 20.307–308; *Odyssey* 8.
- Horace, *Epistles* 2.1.156; *Satires* 1.6.45–48.
- Jerome, *Chronicle* (Constantine's baptism by Eusebius of Nicomedia); the report cites this through report 10.4 and gives no year.
- Justinian, *Digest* 1.2.2.4 (Pomponius on Hermodorus of Ephesus); 10.1.13 (Gaius); 48.9.5 (Marcian on Hadrian and the father who killed his son); the constitution *Deo auctore* (533) by name.
- Juvenal, *Satires* 10.77–81 (bread and circuses).
- Lactantius, *On the Deaths of the Persecutors* 44.5–6 (dream before the Milvian Bridge).
- Livy 1.5.1; 1.59; 2.1.7; 2.2.1–2; 2.5; 2.41; 3.31.8; 5.49.1–7; 8.7; 8.14; 8.40.4–5; 22.10.9; 27.9–10; 30.37; 31.6–8; 39.8–19; 40.42. Standard text: Oxford Classical Texts (several editors by book).
- Panegyrici Latini VI(7).21.3–5 (Constantine and Apollo at the Gallic temple, 310).
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* 3.30 (Vespasian's Latin right to Spain); 3.57 (cited beside Plutarch for the early Greek notices of the sack); 34.21 (Hermodorus).
- Pliny the Younger, *Letters* 10.96; 10.96–97. *Panegyricus* 7.4–6.
- Plutarch, *Camillus* 22; *Cato Maior* 26–27; *On Superstition* 13; *Roman Questions* 63; *Romulus* 12; *Tiberius Gracchus* 8 (with 8.7, Gaius Gracchus' pamphlet).

- Sallust, *Catiline* (the report gives 31.6 for the First Catilinarian).
- Seneca the Younger, *On Mercy* 1.15.
- Suetonius, *Augustus* 7.2; 31.5; 50. *Julius* 20.1; 30.3; 76–79.
- Symmachus, *Relatio* 3.10.
