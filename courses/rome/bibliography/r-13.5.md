# Rome 13.5: Seneca: sources

Written 1 November 2026 by the report's writer (Opus 5.5). Passages are those cited in the report. "(checked)" marks a reference confirmed against an online text during writing; the rest are from the writer's knowledge of the standard texts (Loeb editions unless stated).

## Ancient sources
- Acts of the Apostles 18.12–17 (Gallio, proconsul of Achaea, dismisses the case against Paul at Corinth).
- Augustine, *Letters* 153.14 (mentions the Seneca–Paul letters).
- Cassius Dio, *Roman History* (Loeb book numbers, with the traditional number in brackets where the two differ; from 61(60).29 the books cited here survive only in Byzantine epitome): 59.19.7–8 (Gaius orders Seneca's death; spared as consumptive) (checked, Cary's Loeb translation); 60.8.5 (Julia Livilla exiled, Seneca exiled with her) (checked); 61(60).31.2 (Messalina has Polybius killed) (citation confirmed at second hand); 61.3.1 (Seneca writes Nero's first speeches) (checked); 61.4.1 (Seneca and Burrus govern) (checked); 61.10 (the charge of hypocrisy: Agrippina, tyrant's teacher, the book from exile, 300 million sesterces, five hundred citrus-wood tables) (checked); 62(61).12.1 (Seneca said to have urged Agrippina's murder); 62.2.1 (recall of forty million sesterces in loans, via module 7.1); 62.25 (the death, in epitome).
- Dante, *Inferno* 4.141 ("Seneca morale" in Limbo).
- Gellius, *Attic Nights* 12.2 (critics of Seneca's style); 12.2.3 (a twenty-second book of the *Letters*).
- Jerome, *On Illustrious Men* 12 (Seneca in the catalogue on the strength of the letters to Paul).
- Lactantius, *Divine Institutes* 6.24 (Seneca could have been a true worshipper of God had someone shown him).
- Plato, *Phaedo* 117b (Socrates asks about a libation from the cup).
- Pseudo-Seneca and Pseudo-Paul, *Correspondence of Paul and Seneca* (fourteen letters, fourth century).
- Quintilian, *Institutio oratoria* 10.1.125–131 (the verdict on Seneca's style), especially 125–127, 128, 129, 130, 131.
- Seneca the Elder, *Controversiae* 1 preface 11 (kept from hearing Cicero by the civil wars); *Suasoriae* (named only).
- Seneca the Younger, *Consolation to Helvia* 6–9; 17.3–4; 19.2; 19.4–7.
- Seneca the Younger, *Consolation to Polybius* 12.3–13.4; 13.2.
- Seneca the Younger, *Letters* 2.5; 12.11; 33.11; 54.1–2; 78.1–2; 104.1–2; 108 (Attalus); 108.17–22 (Sotion and the vegetarian year; 108.22 cited in the Self-test).
- Seneca the Younger, *Natural Questions* 1 preface; 4a preface 1; 6.1.2.
- Seneca the Younger, *On Anger* 3.36.1, 3.36.3, 3.36.4 (and the work in general).
- Seneca the Younger, *On Benefits* 1.3.8–1.4.6; 3.6–17.
- Seneca the Younger, *On Clemency* 1.1.1; 1.1.2; 1.3.1; 1.5.1; 1.9.1; 1.11.2; 1.11.3; 2.3.1; 2.4–6.
- Seneca the Younger, *On Providence* 6.7.
- Seneca the Younger, *On the Happy Life* 3.2; 13.1–2; 17.1–2; 17.3–18.1; 23.1; 26.1.
- Seneca the Younger, *On the Shortness of Life*; *On Tranquillity of Mind* 1 (named in the table).
- Suetonius, *Caligula* 53.2 (sand without lime).
- Tacitus, *Annals* 12.8.2; 12.42; 13.2; 13.3; 13.15–17; 13.18.1; 13.42 (13.42.3); 13.43; 13.42–43; 14.7; 14.11.3; 14.52; 14.53–54; 14.55–56; 15.22.2; 15.45.3; 15.60–64 (15.60, 15.60–61, 15.62, 15.63, 15.64, 15.64.2); 15.65; 16.17. The sequence of the death in 15.60–64 checked against Church and Brodribb's translation (Fordham Ancient History Sourcebook).
- Tertullian, *On the Soul* 20.1 ("Seneca saepe noster").
- *Golden Verses* (Pythagorean), the three evening questions (named, not cited by line).

## Modern works

- Griffin, Miriam T., *Seneca: A Philosopher in Politics* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1976).
- Hadot, Pierre, *Philosophy as a Way of Life: Spiritual Exercises from Socrates to Foucault*, ed. Arnold I. Davidson, trans. Michael Chase (Oxford: Blackwell, 1995).
- Ker, James, *The Deaths of Seneca* (New York: Oxford University Press, 2009).
- Romm, James, *Dying Every Day: Seneca at the Court of Nero* (New York: Alfred A. Knopf, 2014).
