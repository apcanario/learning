# Rome 11.7: The Ides of March: sources

Curated 1 November 2026 by the writer (Opus 5.5). "(not verified)" marks a reference given from memory and not checked during writing.

## Ancient sources
- Appian, *Civil Wars* 2.107–154 (the narrative); 2.114 (choice of the Senate) (not verified); 2.116 (Popilius Laenas; heartless victim); 2.117 (Trebonius and Antony; Casca; twenty-three wounds; Brutus' thigh wound; fall at Pompey's statue); 2.119–120 (the Capitol; analysis of the plebs); 2.143–147 (the funeral), checked against the Perseus translation (H. White) and the Livius translation of 2.143–147.
- Augustus, *Res Gestae* 2 (the killers exiled by courts and twice defeated).
- Caesar, *Gallic War* 3.11 (Decimus in command of the fleet).
- Cassius Dio 44.1 (motives), 44.2 (monarchy best for Rome), 44.8 (the Senate received seated), 44.19 (Trebonius and Antony; the murder; "some add" the Greek words), 44.36–49 (Antony's oration), 47.25.3 (Brutus' coins), Cary's Loeb translation via Lexundria.
- Cicero, *Letters to Atticus* 14.1.1 (Matius), 14.4.2, 14.9.2, 14.10.1, 14.21.3, checked against the Shuckburgh translation (attalus.org).
- Cicero, *Letters to Friends* 10.28.1 (to Trebonius: the feast of the Ides), checked against an English translation; 12.4.1 (to Cassius, the same joke).
- Cicero, *Philippics* 2.28 (Brutus calls Cicero's name), 2.34 (Trebonius and Antony; Narbo), 2.85 (the golden chair, via the Dickinson commentary), 2.90–91 (the funeral eulogy and the torches).
- Cicero, *On Divination* 1.119 (heartless victim on the day of the golden chair), checked via the Dickinson commentary; 2.36–37 (Marcus' reply) (not verified).
- Cicero, *On Duties* 3.19, 3.32 (tyrannicide), 3.83 (Caesar desired to be king and achieved it).
- Nicolaus of Damascus, *Life of Augustus* 19 (more than eighty conspirators; motives), 21 (Lupercalia), 23–24 (omens; "Brutus" leads Caesar in; the murder; thirty-five wounds), 26 (Capitol; the body carried home), in C. M. Hall's translation (1923), CSUN web edition.
- Plutarch, *Antony* 11 (fat and long-haired men), 13 (Trebonius at Narbo), 14 (the funeral eulogy).
- Plutarch, *Brutus* 2 (Empylus), 7 (urban praetorship), 8 (Cassius), 9 (slogans), 12 (recruitment; Cicero excluded; Decimus' gladiators), 13 (Portia), 16 (Popilius Laenas), 17 (Trebonius; Brutus' hand wounded), 18 (Brutus addresses the people; Antony spared), 20 (the funeral; seventy-five drachmas; Cinna), 29 (Antony's verdict), 36 (the apparition), 37 (Cassius' Epicureanism); Perrin's Loeb translation via Perseus and Lexundria for 12 and 17.
- Plutarch, *Caesar* 56, 60, 61, 62, 63 (omens; Calpurnia; Livy on the pediment), 64 (Decimus), 65 (Artemidorus), 66 (the murder; Decimus with Antony), 68 (Cinna); Perrin's Loeb translation via Perseus for 64–66.
- Suetonius, *Galba* 4.1 (Augustus' Greek words to Galba).
- Suetonius, *Julius* 76.1, 78.1, 79.3, 80.1, 80.3–4, 81.1–4, 82.1–4, 83.1–2, 84.2–5 (Pacuvius' *Judgement of Arms*), 85, 87, 88, 89; Rolfe's Loeb translation via Lexundria for 88.
- Valerius Maximus 8.11.2 (Spurinna).
