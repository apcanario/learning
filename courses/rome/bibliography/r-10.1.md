# Rome 10.1: Ovid and the Roman remaking of Greek myth: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Antoninus Liberalis, *Metamorphoses* 9 (Pierides and Muses); 10 (daughters of Minyas, citing Nicander book 4 and Corinna); 17 (Leucippus); 23 (Battus); 30 (Byblis). Summaries of Nicander's *Heteroioumena*, which is lost.
- Callimachus, *Aetia* fr. 1.3 Pfeiffer (the refusal of one continuous song). Standard edition: R. Pfeiffer, *Callimachus* I (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1949).
- Nicolaus, *Progymnasmata* (a version of Pyramus and Thisbe with Pyramus as a Cilician river and Thisbe as a spring). The report gives no passage. Standard edition: J. Felten, *Nicolai Progymnasmata* (Leipzig: Teubner, 1913) (not verified).
- Ovid, *Amores* 2.18.
- Ovid, *Ars Amatoria* 3.345–346.
- Ovid, *Fasti* 1.3; 1.461–586; 1.536; 2.3; 2.193–242; 2.475–512; 2.685–852; 3.167–258; 3.259–392; 3.523–696; 4.417–620; 5.1–110; 5.183–378; 6.1–100; 6.797–812; and the Callisto story in book 2 (no line numbers given). Standard text: E. H. Alton, D. E. W. Wormell and E. Courtney (Leipzig: Teubner, 1978).
- Ovid, *Heroides* (letters 7, 15, and 16–21 by name; Sappho's letter is the disputed 15; no line numbers cited).
- Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 1.3–4; 1.168–176; 1.199–205; 1.548–552; 2.401–530; 3.339–510; 4.53; 4.55–166 (with 4.73; 4.121–124; 4.166); 4.389–415; 4.790–803; 5.341–661; 6.1–145 (with 6.73; 6.103–128; 6.129–130); 10.243–297; 13.623–14.608 (with 14.78–81; 14.581–608); 14.805–851 (and 14.805–828); 15.165; 15.420–435; 15.622–744; 15.745–870 (with 15.746–761; 15.761–850; 15.843–850; 15.858–860); 15.871–879 (with 15.871–872; 15.875–876; 15.877–879). The report also notes the minority reading *illa* at 1.2. Standard text: R. J. Tarrant, *P. Ovidi Nasonis Metamorphoses* (Oxford Classical Texts, 2004).
- Ovid, *Tristia* 1.7; 2.549–552; 4.10.43–44.
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* 2.93–94 (Augustus' memoirs on the comet of 44 BC).
- Propertius 4.3 (Arethusa's letter to her husband).
- Virgil, *Aeneid* book 4 (Dido, as reread in *Heroides* 7) and the whole poem as the model compressed in Ovid's "little Aeneid"; no line numbers cited.
- Virgil, *Eclogue* 6.5.

## Modern works
- E. J. Kenney (ed.), *Ovid: Heroides XVI–XXI* (Cambridge Greek and Latin Classics; Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1996).
- Richard Heinze, "Ovids elegische Erzählung", *Berichte über die Verhandlungen der Sächsischen Akademie der Wissenschaften zu Leipzig, philologisch-historische Klasse* 71.7 (1919) (series, volume and number from memory; pages not verified; reprinted in Heinze, *Vom Geist des Römertums*, not verified).
- Stephen Hinds, *The Metamorphosis of Persephone: Ovid and the Self-Conscious Muse* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1987). (The report gives "Stephen Hinds, *The Metamorphosis of Persephone*, 1987"; subtitle from memory.)
- Brooks Otis, *Ovid as an Epic Poet* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1966; 2nd edn 1970).
- R. J. Tarrant, "The Authenticity of the Letter of Sappho to Phaon (*Heroides* XV)", *Harvard Studies in Classical Philology* 85 (1981) 133–153 (volume checked against a catalogue listing of HSCP 85; pages not verified).
- Thea S. Thorsen, *Ovid's Early Poetry: From His Single Heroides to His Remedia Amoris* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2014). (Title and publisher confirmed by catalogue listings; the report gives only "*Ovid's Early Poetry*, 2014".)
- Stephen M. Wheeler, *Narrative Dynamics in Ovid's Metamorphoses* (Classica Monacensia 20; Tübingen: Narr, 2000) (series number from memory, not verified).

Medieval and early modern works the report names as part of Ovid's afterlife (dates as the report gives them):
- *Ovide moralisé*, early fourteenth century. Standard edition: C. de Boer (ed.), *Ovide moralisé: poème du commencement du quatorzième siècle*, 5 vols (Amsterdam: Koninklijke Akademie van Wetenschappen, 1915–1938) (not verified).
- Arthur Golding, *The XV Bookes of P. Ouidius Naso, entytuled Metamorphosis* (London, 1567).
- William Shakespeare, *A Midsummer Night's Dream*; *Titus Andronicus* 4.1; *The Tempest* 5.1.
- Geoffrey Chaucer, *The Book of the Duchess*; *The Legend of Good Women*; the Wife of Bath's Tale (Midas).
- Francis Meres, *Palladis Tamia* (1598) (the date is the report's; title from memory, not verified).
- Thomas Bulfinch's handbook of 1855 with Latin-named gods, cited by pointer to Greek module 9.3 (title and publication not given; see Not resolved).

## Not resolved
- "Karl Lachmann (1848)": no title or place given. Lachmann's name is attached to the view that *Heroides* 15 is spurious, but the work is not identified here and no reference was found.
- "Edward Courtney (1997)": no title given, and the work defending the Sappho letter could not be confirmed by search. Not listed above.
- "Ludwig Traube's *aetas Ovidiana*": no work or year given. Presumably Traube's lectures on medieval Latin philology (not verified).
- "Arnulf of Orléans in the twelfth century and John of Garland in the thirteenth": no works named.
- "the *Philomena* attributed to Chrétien de Troyes": no edition named.
- "Aemilius Macer ... his poem on birds ... and his poem on snakes and healing herbs (*Tristia* 4.10.43–44)": the poems are lost; only the Ovid passage survives.
- "Boios' *Ornithogonia*, Parthenius, Nicander's *Heteroioumena*": cited by cross-reference to Greek module 3.7, without passages.
- "a Roman-period mosaic in the House of Dionysus at Nea Paphos": archaeological find; no publication cited.
- "Thomas Bulfinch's handbook of 1855": no title given (presumably *The Age of Fable*, Boston, 1855; not verified).
