# Rome 3.1: The Struggle of the Orders: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Aulus Gellius, *Attic Nights* 6.3.37 (Cato's speech for the Rhodians, 167 BC); 15.27.4 (Laelius Felix on plebiscites); 20.1.12; 20.1.48–49 (the Twelve Tables on judgment debtors). Oxford Classical Texts (ed. P. K. Marshall) is the standard edition.
- Cicero, *On the Laws* 2.59; 2.59–64; 3.44.
- Cicero, *On the Republic* 2.2; 2.63.
- Diodorus Siculus, *Library of History* 12.24 (Verginia, told briefly).
- Dionysius of Halicarnassus, *Roman Antiquities* 2.9–10; 6.86; 6.89; 6.90; 6.94. The report also relies on the fifth-century books (5–11) generally. Standard edition: Loeb (Cary).
- Festus, *On the Meaning of Words*, s.v. *praeteriti senatores* (no passage number given). Macrobius is named only as a source of Twelve Tables quotations (no passage).
- Gaius, *Institutes* 1.3; 1.132. Gaius' commentary on the Twelve Tables, as excerpted in the *Digest* (see below), is also relied on.
- Justinian, *Digest* 1.2.2.4 (Pomponius, on Hermodorus of Ephesus); 10.1.13 (Gaius, on the boundary rule matched to Solon's).
- Livy, *History of Rome* 1.8.7; 1.33.2; 2.16.4–5; 2.18; 2.23.3–7; 2.31–32; 2.32.3; 2.32.8; 2.32.9–12; 2.33.1–3; 2.33.2–3; 2.41; 2.56–58; 2.58.1; 3.9; 3.30.7; 3.31.8; 3.34.6; 3.36–38; 3.37.4; 3.44–48; 3.44–52; 3.50–52; 3.55; 3.55.10; 3.55.13; 3.57.10; 4.1–6; 4.3–5; 4.6.8; 4.7.2; 4.8; 4.43; 4.54.2–3; 5.12.9; 6.1.2–3; 6.34–42; 6.34.5–10; 6.35.4–5; 6.35.10; 6.42; 6.42.11–14; 7.9.5; 7.16.1; 7.16.9; 7.17.6; 7.22.10; 7.42.1–2; 8.12.14–16; 8.15.9; 8.28; 8.28.1; 9.46.5; 10.6–9; 10.7–8; 10.9.3; 10.9.3–6; 40.44.1. Also *Periocha* 11 (the secession of 287). The *Periochae* are in vol. 14 of the Loeb Livy (Schlesinger and Geer).
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* 16.37; 28.17–18; 34.21.
- Plutarch, *Camillus* 42.
- Sallust, *Histories* 3 (Licinius Macer's speech of 73 BC; fragment, no further locator given).
- Servius, commentary on Virgil, *Aeneid* 6.609.
- Tacitus, *Annals* 6.16.
- Varro, *On the Latin Language* 7.105. Standard edition: Loeb (Kent).
- Xenophon, *Memorabilia* 2.3.18.

## Corpora and inscriptions
- The Twelve Tables: no single corpus is cited; the report refers to Godefroy (1616) for the numbering and Crawford, *Roman Statutes* (1996), for the modern reordering (see Modern works). In Crawford the text is no. 40.
- The Fasti (consular lists, "the Fasti's firsts"): cited by name only; standard edition A. Degrassi, *Inscriptiones Italiae* XIII.1 (Rome, 1947) (not verified as the report's source).

## Not resolved
- "Ettore Pais and Édouard Lambert (1902) dismissed the Tables as a later compilation": no work given for Pais (probably his *Storia di Roma*, but the report does not say); for Lambert the year only. The article is, I believe, "La question de l'authenticité des XII Tables et les annales maximi" (1902/3), but I could not confirm title, journal or pages.
- "Wieacker, Watson and Crawford accept a fifth-century code": no works given for Wieacker or Watson.
- "Niebuhr made the plebs a conquered people"; "Mommsen ... derived it from clients and outsiders": no works or years given.
- "Momigliano and Cornell ... see in the patriciate a religious and political elite": Cornell is *The Beginnings of Rome* (1995); no work given for Momigliano on this point (the Momigliano article listed above is the separate "Camillus and Concord").
- "Beloch, Ogilvie and Forsythe treat the first two as anticipations": no works given for Beloch or Ogilvie.
- Licinius Macer, Valerius Antias, Calpurnius Piso, Claudius Quadrigarius, Aelius Tubero, Fabius Pictor: lost annalists named as Livy's sources; fragments in T. J. Cornell (ed.), *The Fragments of the Roman Historians* (Oxford, 2013), but the report cites that edition nowhere, so it is not listed above.
