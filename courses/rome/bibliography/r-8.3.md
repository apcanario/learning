# Rome 8.3: Constantine: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Ammianus Marcellinus, *Res gestae* 14.11.20 (Crispus at Pola). Section from memory, per the writer's note.
- Athanasius, *On the Decrees of the Council of Nicaea* 3 (the report cites it as *On the Decrees of Nicaea*).
- Aurelius Victor, *Caesares* 40.25.
- *Epitome de Caesaribus* 41.3; 41.11–12.
- Eusebius of Caesarea, *Church History* 9.9.5–8; 9.9.10–11; 10.5 (as a whole); 10.5.2–14; 10.5.15–17; 10.5.18–20; 10.5.21–24; 10.6; 10.7. Standard edition: Loeb (Lake and Oulton).
- Eusebius of Caesarea, *Life of Constantine* 1.27–32 (and 1.28.1); 1.51–54; 2.26–29; 2.45; 2.64–72; 3.8; 3.10–13; 3.15; 3.18–20; 3.48; 3.54; 4.26; 4.56–57; 4.58–60; 4.61–62; 4.62–64; 4.73. Translation and commentary: Averil Cameron and Stuart G. Hall (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1999).
- Eutropius, *Breviarium* 10.6; 10.8. Section 10.6 from memory, per the writer's note.
- Jerome, *Chronicle* under the year 330 (the Latin quoted, "dedicatur Constantinopolis omnium paene urbium nuditate"); also named for the baptiser at Nicomedia and for Crispus's tutor, Lactantius, in *On Illustrious Men* 80. The writer's note says the Latin of the Chronicle was quoted from memory.
- Julian, *Caesars* 336a–b.
- Lactantius, *On the Deaths of the Persecutors* 18–19; 24; 24.9; 25; 29–30; 32; 44.5–6; 44.8; 46; 48. Edition with translation and commentary: J. L. Creed (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1984). The writer's note records that the Latin "transversa X littera, summo capite circumflexo" (44.5) was quoted from memory.
- Libanius, *Oration* 30.6.
- Optatus of Milevis, *Against the Donatists*, appendix 3 (Constantine's letter, on fearing the anger of the highest divinity). The writer's note says the appendix number was not checked.
- *Origo Constantini imperatoris* (the Anonymus Valesianus, part 1) 2.2–3; 2.4.
- *Panegyrici Latini*: VII(6) of 307; VI(7) of 310 (VI(7).2; VI(7).21.3–5); V(8) of 311; XII(9) of 313 (XII(9).2.4–5; XII(9).3.3); IV(10), the speech of Nazarius at Rome in 321. Section numbers in XII(9) from memory, per the writer's note.
- Philostorgius, *Church History* 1.7; 2.4; 2.9. Chapters from memory, per the writer's note.
- Socrates, *Church History* 1.7; 1.8.
- Sozomen, *Church History* 1.5.
- Zosimus, *New History* 2.15.1–2; 2.29.2; 2.29.3–4; 2.30; 2.31.2–3. Standard edition: F. Paschoud (Budé).
- Eunapius's lost history (named as Zosimus's source, no passage).
- *Theodosian Code* 1.27.1; 3.16.1; 4.7.1; 5.17.1; 8.16.1; 9.16.1; 9.24.1; 9.40.2; 15.12.1; 16.2.2; 16.2.4; 16.10.1; 16.10.2. Standard edition: Mommsen and Meyer; translation by C. Pharr.
- *Code of Justinian* 3.12.2.

## Corpora and inscriptions
- CIL VI 1139 (*Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum*, vol. 6): dedicatory inscription of the Arch of Constantine, "instinctu divinitatis".
- CIL XI 5265: the Hispellum rescript (333–337).
- *P.Lond.* 878 (papyrus of the letter to the provincials of Palestine, quoted at *Life of Constantine* 2.26–29), as identified by Jones and Skeat (1954).
- The Constantinian coinage cited (the Ticinum medallion of 315, Sol Invictus bronze into the 320s, consecration coins): no catalogue references in the report.

## Not resolved
- "Zosimus 2.30" for the new city wall "some fifteen stades beyond the old": the report cites the chapter only; the writer's note says the chapter number was from memory.
- "Barnes (2011) adopted the thesis [of Weiss] as explaining all the early evidence": the writer's note says this could not be verified against the book.
