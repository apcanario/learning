## Modern works
- Dillon, John M., and A. A. Long (eds), *The Question of "Eclecticism": Studies in Later Greek Philosophy* (Berkeley: University of California Press, 1988).
- Göransson, Tryggve, *Albinus, Alcinous, Arius Didymus*, Studia Graeca et Latina Gothoburgensia 61 (Göteborg: Acta Universitatis Gothoburgensis, 1995). The writer confirmed from *BMCR* 1995.12.08 that it challenges the traditional picture of Arius Didymus as the doxographer, but did not check its position on whether Augustus' Arius is the same man.
- Gruen, Erich S., *Studies in Greek Culture and Roman Policy* (Leiden: Brill, 1990). The writer confirmed the chapter on the expulsions; the one-line summary of his reading is from memory. A later reissue is Berkeley: University of California Press, 1996 (not verified).
- Marx, Friedrich (ed.), *Incerti auctoris de ratione dicendi ad C. Herennium libri IV* (Leipzig: Teubner, 1894). The Teubner edition is confirmed (a corrected 1964 reprint is recorded in the Masaryk University catalogue); the 1894 date is the report's, taken by the writer from memory. The political reading of Plotius Gallus is in his introduction.
- Rawson, Elizabeth, *Intellectual Life in the Late Roman Republic* (London: Duckworth, 1985) (publisher not verified).
- Ungern-Sternberg, Jürgen von, "Die populären Beispiele in der Schrift des Auctors ad Herennium", *Chiron* 3 (1973) 143–162. The page gives only the issue number "3"; the pages and title are confirmed, and the article does say, following Gelzer, that nothing certain can be said of Plotius' politics.
- Volk, Katharina, *The Roman Republic of Letters: Scholarship, Philosophy, and Politics in the Age of Cicero and Caesar* (Princeton: Princeton University Press, 2021) (subtitle and publisher not verified).

## Not resolved
- "Matthias Gelzer rejected the thesis" (Marx's political reading of Plotius Gallus): no work is named in the report, and the Chiron article by von Ungern-Sternberg that mentions it also gives none. The writer could not open Gelzer.
- "Eduard Zeller's history of Greek philosophy" (the "eclecticism" label): no title, volume or edition is given; the work is probably *Die Philosophie der Griechen in ihrer geschichtlichen Entwicklung*, but the report does not say so and I did not confirm it.
- "Athenaeus ... Rutilius, Tubero and Mucius Scaevola ... (6.274c–e)": the passage, letter range and third name were given from memory and not checked.
- "Strabo 11.1.6": see above.
- "Gruen (1990) reads the series as gestures by which the governing class displayed its authority": summarised from memory.
- Section numbers flagged by the writer as from memory, not checked: Cicero, *On Ends* 2.24 and 4.23; Plutarch, *Cicero* 3–4; *Letters to Atticus* 2.20 (no section given in the report).
