# Rome 4.1: Carthage and Dido: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Appian, *Punica* 1.
- Aristotle, *Politics* 2.11 (1272b24–1273b26). Standard edition: Ross, Oxford Classical Texts.
- Arrian, *Anabasis* 2.24.5.
- Ausonius, Latin version of the Greek epigram on Dido's statue (cited without a locator).
- Cicero, *De lege agraria* 2.95.
- Columella, *On Agriculture* 1.1.13.
- Diodorus Siculus, *Library of History* 5.16; 14.47–53; 16.69.1; 20.14 (20.14.4–6).
- Dionysius of Halicarnassus, *Roman Antiquities* 1.74.1 (Timaeus' foundation date of 814/813).
- Eratosthenes, date of the fall of Troy (1184/3), cited without a locator; a lost work known through later chronographers.
- Hanno, *Periplus* (Greek version surviving in one ninth-century manuscript at Heidelberg; cited without section numbers).
- Herodotus, *Histories* 1.166; 7.165–167 (7.166).
- Hebrew Bible, 1 Kings 5; Jeremiah 7.31.
- Homer, *Odyssey* 14.288–289.
- Jerome, *Against Jovinian* 1.43.
- Josephus, *Against Apion* 1.125–126 (quoting Menander of Ephesus on the Tyrian annals).
- Justin, *Epitome of Pompeius Trogus* 18.4–6 (18.6.9 cited for the date, 18.6 for her cult); 18.7; 19.1; 19.1–2; 19.2. Standard Latin text: Seel, Teubner (1972). Trogus' own *Philippic Histories* is lost.
- Livy, *History of Rome* 7.27.2; 9.43.26; 21.4.9; 33.46–47.
- Macrobius, *Saturnalia* 5.17.5–6.
- Mago, Carthaginian treatise on agriculture in twenty-eight books (lost; known through Pliny, *Natural History* 18.22, 18.35, Varro, *On Agriculture* 1.1.10, and Columella 1.1.13).
- Naevius, *Punic War* (lost; the report says only that he "may already have brought Aeneas to Carthage"; no fragment cited).
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* 2.169; 6.200; 16.216; 18.22; 18.35.
- Plautus, *Poenulus* 113; 930–949.
- *Planudean Anthology* 16.151 (the epigram on Dido's statue).
- Plutarch, *On Superstition* 13.
- Polybius, *Histories* 3.22–26 (3.22.3; 3.24; 3.25; 3.25.6–9; 3.26; 3.26.1); 3.28.1–2; 6.51; 6.56; 7.9. Standard edition: Loeb (Paton, rev. Walbank and Habicht).
- Sallust, *Jugurtha* 108.3.
- Servius, commentary on Virgil, *Aeneid* 4.682 (Varro on Anna); also cited without a locator for the meaning of "Dido".
- Strabo, *Geography* 3.5.5.
- Tertullian, *Apology* 9.2.
- Thucydides 6.2.6.
- Timaeus of Tauromenium, on Elissa and the foundation of Carthage (FGrHist 566 F 82, preserved in an anonymous Greek treatise *On Women*); also his foundation date via Dionysius of Halicarnassus 1.74.1. Fragments: Jacoby, *Die Fragmente der griechischen Historiker* (FGrH) 566.
- Varro, *On Agriculture* 1.1.10 (Cassius Dionysius of Utica's Greek version of Mago).
- Velleius Paterculus 1.2.3.
- Virgil, *Aeneid* 1.340–368; 4.196–218; 4.622–629.
