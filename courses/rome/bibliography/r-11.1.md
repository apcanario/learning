# Rome 11.1: How the Republic worked at its height: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Appian, *Punica* 112 (the people's insistence on Scipio Aemilianus as consul for 147).
- Cicero, *Letters to Friends* 8.8 (Caelius' letter; vetoed decrees recorded as *auctoritates*). The writer gave the reference from memory (not verified).
- Cicero, *On Divination* 1.103.
- Cicero, *On Friendship* 96.
- Cicero, *On the Laws* 3.35–37.
- Cicero, *On the Republic* 2.39.
- Cicero, *Philippics* 2.82; 5.47. The writer checked 5.47 against Yonge's translation only.
- Cicero, *Pro Murena* 38; also cited without section for the speech's picture of bribery at its height.
- Cicero, *Pro Plancio* 49.
- Dionysius of Halicarnassus, *Roman Antiquities* 2.9–10 (Romulus assigns plebeians to patricians as clients).
- Gellius, *Attic Nights* 13.16 (Messalla on *contionem habere* against *cum populo agere*); 14.7 (Varro's handbook of Senate procedure for Pompey). Both from memory in the writer's notes (not verified).
- Livy 1.43.11–12; 4.25.13; 7.15.12–13; 27.11.9–12; 31.6–8; 32.27.6; 40.19.11; 40.44.1; 40.44.2. The writer gave 4.25.13, 7.15.12–13, 40.19.11 and the Baebian law of 40.44.2 from memory; Wikipedia, as the writer notes, disagrees about how far that law was observed.
- Livy, *Periochae* 15; 46; 47; 48; 50. The writer checked 46–48 and 50 on Livius.org and 15 via Smith's *Dictionary*.
- Macrobius, *Saturnalia* 3.17 (Orchian law of 182, Fannian law of 161). From memory in the writer's notes.
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* 7.139–140 (Metellus' eulogy for his father).
- Plutarch, *Cato Maior* 1.
- Plutarch, *Flamininus* 2.
- Plutarch, *Gaius Gracchus* 5.
- Plutarch, *Marius* 5.
- Polybius 6.14; 6.19.4; 6.56.4. The writer checked 6.56.4 at chapter level only.
- [Quintus Cicero], *Commentariolum Petitionis* (the canvassing handbook of 64 BC; the report gives no section).
- Sallust, *Jugurtha* 41.7; 63.6–7.
- *Twelve Tables* (the curse on a patron who cheated his client; the report gives no table).
- Valerius Maximus 7.5 (the chapter on electoral defeats).
- Varro, *On Agriculture* 1.2.9 (Crassus turning to face the Forum).

## Modern works
- Astin, A. E., *The Lex Annalis before Sulla*, Collection Latomus 32 (Brussels: Latomus, 1958) (not verified; the report gives title and year only).
- Brunt, P. A., "Clientela", in *The Fall of the Roman Republic and Related Essays* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1988) (Clarendon imprint and 1988 date confirmed; the essay's page range, which I recall as 382–442, is not verified).
- Gelzer, Matthias, *Die Nobilität der römischen Republik* (Leipzig: Teubner, 1912) (not verified).
- Hölkeskamp, Karl-Joachim, *Reconstructing the Roman Republic: An Ancient Political Culture and Modern Research*, trans. Henry Heitmann-Gordon (Princeton: Princeton University Press, 2010). The report's "German 2004" is *Rekonstruktionen einer Republik: die politische Kultur des antiken Rom und die Forschung der letzten Jahrzehnte*, Historische Zeitschrift, Beihefte, N.F. 38 (Munich: Oldenbourg, 2004). Where the report gives "Hölkeskamp (2004)" the German book is meant; the report's "(2010)" is the English edition.
- Hopkins, Keith, with Graham Burton, *Death and Renewal: Sociological Studies in Roman History 2* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1983). The Hopkins and Burton figures come from the joint chapter on political succession in the late Republic; the writer took them from Finley's review (*London Review of Books*, 1983) and not from the book.
- Millar, Fergus, "The Political Character of the Classical Roman Republic, 200–151 B.C.", *Journal of Roman Studies* 74 (1984) 1–19.
- Millar, Fergus, "Politics, Persuasion, and the People before the Social War (150–90 B.C.)", *Journal of Roman Studies* 76 (1986) 1–11 (the "sequel on 150–90"; identified from context).
- Millar, Fergus, *The Crowd in Rome in the Late Republic* (Ann Arbor: University of Michigan Press, 1998) (publisher not verified).
- Morstein-Marx, Robert, *Mass Oratory and Political Power in the Late Roman Republic* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2004).
- Mouritsen, Henrik, *Plebs and Politics in the Late Roman Republic* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2001). The writer took the capacity figures from the *BMCR* review (2001.12.12), not the book.

## Not resolved
- "Livy 40.44.2 ... Baebian law ... four and six in alternate years": the writer gave the content from memory and noted that Wikipedia says the law was not observed; not checked against the text.
- "Lex Villia ... a two-year interval between curule offices and minimum ages of about 39 for the praetorship and 42 for the consulship ... most reconstructions": the figures are attributed to Astin's method but Astin's own figures were not checked.
- "Hopkins and Burton ... about 40 per cent of consuls with consular fathers, a third with consular sons": taken from a review, not the book (see above).
- "Yakobson (1999) ... the first class splitting and the count reaching the middle classes": the argument is Yakobson's 1992 article as much as the 1999 book; the writer summarised it from abstracts.
