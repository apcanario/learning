<h2 class="hsec">Frame</h2>
<section class="howto edge" style="--hc:var(--red)"><h2>Pick</h2>
  <p><strong>A course for the coach who helps an athlete they coach on the business side, as sponsor through a business of their own, as unpaid representative to other sponsors and funders, or both, with the conflict between those roles declared and managed in writing from the second unit.</strong></p>
  <ul><li>It is a common position in small sports, and nothing found teaches it.</li><li>Sponsor work gets six units: both seats, athlete's and sponsor's.</li><li>Rules are taught as questions, with Portugal worked as the example.</li></ul>
  <p class="over"><strong>Override if:</strong> you want the course to serve coaches with no business of their own first; then AC9 becomes optional reading and AC2 shrinks to coach and representative.</p>
</section>
<div class="report"><ul>
  <li><strong>What the course is for.</strong> Helping an athlete you coach on the business side without harming the athlete or the coaching: the rules that bind what the athlete may sell, the funding and the season's money, what the athlete has to sell, sponsorship from the athlete's seat and from your own as a sponsor, contracts and agents as questions, and the career alongside and after sport. It is the second course of the Sports track.</li>
  <li><strong>Three hats.</strong> Coach, sponsor through a business of your own, unpaid representative: any two or all three at once. Working unpaid removes the commission conflict and none of the others; AC2 is the unit on holding the roles together, AC9 is the reader as sponsor, AC10 the reader finding other sponsors while their own business is one.</li>
  <li><strong>Builds on, by pointer.</strong> Sport Psychology for Coaches holds the coach's line with minors and the athlete's wellbeing; the public personal-brand course holds how an audience is built. Neither is re-taught. The case is Sport Psychology's three athletes, Sara (400 m), Dani (judo) and Milan (football, the contrast), with a commercial layer.</li>
  <li><strong>Out of scope.</strong> Team-sport transfers and employment contracts beyond the contrast case; investing; coaching and psychology method; audience-building method; US college recruiting.</li>
  <li><strong>Rules as questions.</strong> Rules differ by sport and country and they move: the course gives each rule-maker's own dated text as one example and the question to put to your own federation, event or authority, and never states your rule. Ten units carry an "In Portugal" section, one country's worked example, and the last deep dive assembles them. Nothing is legal, tax, financial or medical advice.</li>
  <li><strong>Where the evidence pushes back.</strong> Nothing was found on coaches who sponsor their own athletes; AC2 builds on the general literature on conflicts of interest and on sport bodies' own conflict rules, and grades its own reasoning as practice. Research on athlete brands, endorsers, sponsorship, athletes' finances and career transitions studied famous athletes, large sponsorships and consumers; for an unknown athlete and a small sponsor almost nothing was measured, so a transfer is labelled as the unit's inference. Success stories are the visible minority: the comparables deep dive needs an independent record for each case and at least one athlete who stopped.</li>
</ul></div>
