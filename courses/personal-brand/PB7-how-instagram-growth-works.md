# How Instagram growth works

Everything below about Instagram's rules, features and metrics was checked on 12 November 2026 unless another date is given. Instagram's Help Centre articles load their text in the browser and could not be read by the tools used for this unit; where a Help Centre page would have settled a point, the unit says so and uses the next-best source, labelled. Statements by Instagram's head, Adam Mosseri, are cited through the press reports that quote them, with the date of the report; the original videos and posts were not opened.

## 1 · How Instagram says each surface ranks content

**One ranking system per surface.** Instagram's own explanation, published by its head, Adam Mosseri, on 31 May 2023 and the most complete statement of its ranking found, says each part of the app has its own ranking ([Instagram, Instagram Ranking Explained, 31 May 2023](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained); platform-official). What it says each surface shows and the signal groups it names, in its order:

| Surface | Whose posts it shows (Instagram, 2023) | Signal groups Instagram names, in its order |
|---|---|---|
| Feed | "a mix of content from the accounts you've chosen to follow", recommended posts from accounts you do not follow, and ads | your activity; information about the post; information about the person who posted; your history of interacting with them |
| Stories | "The stories you see are from people you've chosen to follow, as well as ads" | viewing history; engagement history; closeness |
| Explore | "photos and videos that we go out and find for you from accounts you don't yet follow" | information about the post; your activity in Explore; your history with the person who posted; information about them |
| Reels | mostly accounts you do not follow, with an emphasis on entertainment | your activity; your history with the person who posted; information about the reel; information about the person who posted |
| Search | named as having its own algorithm; no detail on the 2023 page | see below |

The same page says Instagram aims to show fewer reels that are "low-resolution or watermarked", muted, bordered, mostly text, or that have "already been posted on Instagram"; that recommendations follow Recommendation Guidelines covering Explore, Reels, Search, suggested posts in Feed and suggested accounts; that an account that repeatedly breaks them will find "none of their posts will be eligible for recommendation for a period of time"; and that Account Status, a menu in the Instagram profile, shows whether an account and its content are eligible to be recommended, with an appeal (same source).

**Search.** Instagram's search post of 25 August 2021 says "The text you enter in the search bar is by far the most important signal for Search", followed by the searcher's own activity and then, among many matching results, popularity signals such as clicks, likes, shares and follows ([Instagram, Breaking Down How Instagram Search Works, 25 Aug 2021](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/break-down-how-instagram-search-works/); platform-official, five years old, and no later Instagram statement on search ranking was found).

**How a new post is tested.** On 30 April 2024 Instagram said each eligible piece of content "is shown to a small audience that we think will enjoy it", whether or not they follow the account, and that "the top performing set of reels are shown to a slightly wider audience"; it said it was adding "A new input to ranking that will give smaller creators more distribution", and that before the change "accounts with the largest followings often saw the most reach" ([Instagram for Creators, Helping Creators Find New Audiences, 30 Apr 2024](https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality); platform-official). The page gives no size for the first audience, no time window and no effect size for the new input.

**What the head of Instagram has said since.** In January 2025 Mosseri said "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends", and "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content"; for Insights he pointed creators to "average watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach" ([Social Media Today, 22 Jan 2025](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/), quoting his video; executive statement via the press). He did not define "connected"; Social Media Today reads it as content from accounts the viewer follows, which is the reading used here. In a June 2024 interview he said "I would look at sends per reach. So of the people who saw it, how many of them sent it to a friend?", and "There's way more unconnected content than there is connected content" ([Social Media Today, 10 Jun 2024](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-shares-insights-into-its-algorithms-creator-monetization/718540/); executive statement via the press).

**The viewer can now steer Reels.** On 10 December 2025 Instagram launched Your Algorithm in the US: an icon in Reels shows a viewer the topics shaping their recommendations, summarised by AI from their activity, and lets them ask for more or less of a topic or add one; a global English rollout was announced for "soon", and Explore was named as a later surface ([TechCrunch, 10 Dec 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/10/instagrams-new-your-algorithm-tool-gives-you-more-control-over-the-reels-you-see); announcement via the press). For a creator it means one more reason a Reel reaches people who already chose its topic, and one more reason a post outside an account's topic can be filtered out by the viewer (this unit's inference).

**What Instagram says it does not do.** The 2023 page says that when people use "shadowban" to mean content "limited or hidden without a clear explanation or justification", "that's not our intention", and that Instagram does not "suppress content to encourage people to buy ads" (Instagram Ranking Explained). That is a statement of intent; nothing published lets an outsider test it.

## 2 · What reaches non-followers and what reaches followers

Set side by side, Instagram's statements give each format a primary audience.

**Reaches non-followers.** Reels and Explore, by Instagram's own description, show mostly accounts the viewer does not follow (§1). Feed carries some recommended posts. Search reaches anyone who types words that match the account's name, bio or caption (§7). Every eligible post, in Instagram's 2024 account, starts with a small test audience that need not follow the account (§1). Reposts by other people, collaborations and remixes carry a post to someone else's followers (§6).

**Reaches followers.** Stories, which show only accounts a viewer follows (§1). Feed, which is built first from followed accounts. Mosseri's statement that likes weigh slightly more for connected content puts likes on the follower side of the ledger, and sends on the stranger side (§1).

**How much of each.** Instagram publishes no share of views from non-followers. The vendor figures closest to a small account:

- Fanpage Karma, from more than 3 million Reels, carousels and image posts published January to April 2026, reports for accounts under 10,000 followers a median reach per post of 4.9% of followers for Reels, 3.5% for image posts and 2.6% for carousels; "Reels reach nearly three times as many non-followers as carousels"; and "reach within the existing community" of 2.1% for image posts and 2.0% for Reels ([Fanpage Karma, Reel, carousel, or image post, 7 Aug 2026, modified 28 Aug 2026](https://www.fanpagekarma.com/insights/instagram-format-reach/)). Vendor data; the number of accounts is not stated, and the community measure is not defined further.
- Metricool and HypeAuditor's Instagram Content Playbook (700 million posts from 28 million influencer accounts, January to June 2025, medians), as reported by Net Influencer: in the under-1,000 tier, median reach of 134 for Reels, 56 for carousels and 23 for static images, with average likes of 7 for carousels and 6 for Reels ([Net Influencer, 14 Oct 2025](https://www.netinfluencer.com/instagram-carousels-outperform-reels-for-larger-accounts-content-performance-benchmarks-show/); [Metricool, press release, 14 Oct 2025](https://metricool.com/press-release-instagram-content-playbook/)). Vendor data via the press; the full study is behind an email form and was not read.

At 140 followers, Fanpage Karma's 4.9% is about 7 accounts a Reel and its 2.6% about 4 a carousel (derived; PB3 §3 made the same calculation). HypeAuditor's tier is the one Mara (Mara, fictional) starts in, and puts a median Reel at about 134 accounts. The two cannot both describe her: one is a share of followers in a band that runs to 10,000, the other a count in a band that runs to 1,000, and neither says how many accounts sit at the bottom of its band. Her own Insights split of views by followers and non-followers (§4) is the number that settles it for her.

**The role this sets.** PB3 gave Instagram the shop-window role: mostly Reels for reach to non-followers, carousels or images for followers. Instagram's own statements hold that split on the surface side (Reels and Explore for strangers, Stories and Feed for followers). The format data in §3 supports Reels as the stranger-reaching format at small sizes in three datasets out of five, and disputes it in two. The role stands, with a test in the deliverable.

**Where they agree.** Single images trail on reach in every dataset that reports it. Carousels lead or tie on engagement in every dataset that compares them; in Fanpage Karma's small band, which gives no carousel figure, images lead Reels, and Metricool 2026 does not compare formats on engagement.

**Where they disagree, and why it may be.** Reach: three datasets put Reels first (HypeAuditor, Fanpage Karma, Buffer); two put carousels first or level (Socialinsider, Metricool 2026). The two that favour carousels measure views, which count repeats (§4), and one of them is brand accounts only; the three that favour Reels measure reach or reach-like counts on influencer, mixed or Buffer-user accounts. Which of those differences drives the disagreement is not tested in any of them. For a small personal account, the influencer dataset (whose smallest tier is under 1,000) and the under-10,000 band of Fanpage Karma are the nearest populations, and both put Reels first.

**Stories.** Metricool's 2026 data shows Stories reach down 1.77% and replies up 88% year on year (Metricool, Instagram Trends 2026). Stories are not in the reach comparisons above; Buffer's frequency study left them out: "Since stories are not typically shown to non-followers" ([Buffer, How often should you post on Instagram, 13 Aug 2025](https://buffer.com/library/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)).

**How long a post works.** Metricool's 2026 data puts 65% of a Reel's views and 76% of a carousel's in days 1 to 3, and 44% of a single image's lifetime views on day 1 (Metricool, Instagram Trends 2026; vendor). That is why the metrics sheet reads a post's numbers only once it is 7 days old: by then most of what it will get, in this vendor's data, has arrived.

## 4 · The signals Instagram names, and where Insights shows them

**The signals, in Instagram's words.** Watch time, likes and sends are the "top three signals" (Mosseri, January 2025, §1). The 2023 page names the groups each surface draws on (§1). Instagram's metric changes since 2024 are its own account of what it thinks creators should read:

- **Views.** In August 2024 Instagram said "We're introducing 'Views' as the primary metric across all formats including reels, live, photos, carousels, and stories", and "repeat views of content will be counted" ([Social Media Today, 7 Aug 2024](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-updates-metrics-to-focus-creators-on-views/723645/), quoting Instagram). Metricool reported that impressions would leave Insights and that Instagram "will use the same calculations to display 'Followers' and 'Non-followers' views by format", and quoted Mosseri: "Sends, reach, and views are the most important metrics for anybody trying to understand how their content is doing" ([Metricool, 14 Aug 2024](https://metricool.com/instagram-insights/); executive statement via a vendor). Meta's developer reference now defines views as "The number of times your content was played or displayed", reach as "The number of unique accounts that have seen your content, at least once" (estimated), and lists FOLLOWER and NON_FOLLOWER as a breakdown ([Meta for Developers, Instagram account insights](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/api-reference/instagram-user/insights); platform-official).
- **Skip rate.** "skip rate is the percentage of views from people who decided to skip your reel during those first 3 seconds", replacing view rate, with a retention chart for each Reel where "the flatter the line, the more engaged your audience is" ([Social Media Today, 24 Aug 2025](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/), quoting Instagram). Meta's reference defines reels_skip_rate in the same terms and ig_reels_avg_watch_time as "The average amount of time spent playing the reel" ([Meta for Developers, Instagram media insights](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/reference/instagram-media/insights/); platform-official).
- **Shares, saves, likes, comments, follows, profile visits.** All defined per post in the same reference. Its account-level page says follows and unfollows, and follower demographics, are "Not returned if the IG User has less than 100 followers" in the API (Meta for Developers, account insights). Whether the app hides them on the same threshold was not checked.

**Who can see Insights.** Social Media Today reported on 1 March 2026 that Instagram had made the insights dashboard, content scheduling and trending audio "available to all public accounts", previously limited to Professional Mode, with other tools still tied to Professional Mode or follower counts ([Social Media Today, 1 Mar 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-opens-up-content-scheduling-to-all-users/813448/); platform change via the press; the article does not list the thresholds).

**The Instagram equivalents of YouTube's two numbers.** PB1's verdict 3 reads YouTube's impressions click-through rate and percentage viewed. A Reel plays as soon as it reaches the screen, so Instagram has no click on an impression to count. The closest pair:

| YouTube (PB6) | Nearest Instagram measure | What differs |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions click-through rate: did the package make a viewer choose the video? | Skip rate: the share of views skipped in the first 3 seconds; one minus it is the share that stayed past 3 seconds | The viewer did not choose to start; skip rate measures the first frames, so it judges the opening, where CTR judges the thumbnail and title |
| Average percentage viewed | Average watch time divided by the Reel's length (her calculation), with the retention chart | Instagram reports watch time in seconds; the share is arithmetic, and replays count in views |

The only published skip-rate figure found is Socialinsider's: an average of 65.50% for brand accounts of 1,000–5,000 followers and 60.50% at 100,000–1 million, from 9,771,822 organic Reels, January to June 2026 ([Socialinsider, Instagram Reels statistics, 24 Jun 2026, updated 6 Nov 2026](https://socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-statistics/); vendor, brand accounts; PB5 §3 used it). No watch-time benchmark was found. So the Instagram format read in the deliverable sets her Reels against 65.5% for skip rate and against her own earlier quarters for watch-time share.

**Carousels.** The developer reference says "Insights data is not available for any media within an Instagram Media album" (media insights page): a carousel is read as one post, with no per-slide figures. The unit uses no per-slide measure.

## 5 · Originality and reposted content: Instagram's stated rules

**The rule.** Instagram's original content guidelines define original content as "work that you wholly created or reflects your unique perspective". Unoriginal content includes work "you had no role in creating", content "you didn't produce or film and doesn't contain any material edits", screenshots of others' work even with credit, and "low-effort edits (e.g. borders, watermarks, speed changes, just crediting the original creator)". Unoriginal content "may be ineligible to appear in recommendations to people who don't already follow you"; an account that mostly posts it "may not be seen in recommendations to new audiences"; eligibility returns when "a significant percentage of your recently posted reels, photos, and carousels are considered original" over a rolling 30 days, checked in Account Status; and "Our original content guidelines do not impact how we show people content from accounts they already follow" ([Instagram Creators, Original content guidelines](https://creators.instagram.com/original-content-guidelines/), undated, read 12 Nov 2026; platform-official). "Significant percentage" is not defined.

**The 2024 and 2026 changes.** On 30 April 2024 Instagram said copies would be replaced in recommendations by the original when the original is relatively new, with a label linking to the original creator, and aggregators that repeatedly repost without material changes would be excluded from recommendations (Helping Creators Find New Audiences, 30 Apr 2024). On 30 April 2026 it extended the protections from Reels to photos and carousels; accounts that mainly post unoriginal photos, carousels or reels "will no longer be shown in places where we recommend content"; the update "does not impact how we show people content from accounts they follow"; edits that add new information, context or a point of view can count as original; and 75% of recommendations in the US now come from original posts. It points creators who want to include others' work to collabs, remix, the paid-partnership label, sharing to Stories and reposts, which credit the original ([Instagram Creators, Rewarding original creators on Instagram, 30 Apr 2026](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram); platform-official).

**What this means for an account built on its own footage.** The rule removes reach to strangers; reach to followers is untouched by it. An account that films its own work is original by the definition. Two of its habits can still fall under the 2023 visibility list: a Reel exported from another app with that app's watermark, and a Reel already posted on Instagram and posted again (§1). Neither page says whether a creator's own clip carrying her own watermark counts against her; the 2023 page lists watermarked reels among those made less visible without that distinction. PB5 already exports every Instagram file from her own edit (PB5 §5).

**For an account under 1,000 followers.** Trial reels, on the only eligibility statement found, open at 1,000 followers; below that a test opening goes out as an ordinary Reel, which is also shown to a small test audience first (Instagram, 30 Apr 2024, §1). Reposts, collabs and remix need no follower count on the sources found. A collab or repost carries a post to another account's followers; a collab needs a partner who made the post with you, or the 2026 originality rule applies to whoever posts someone else's work without material change.

## 7 · Hashtags and search, as currently documented

**Hashtags.** On 18 December 2025 Instagram said in a Threads post that it "will gradually update the number of hashtags that you can include in a caption for a reel or post to five", and that "using fewer (up to 5) more targeted hashtags, rather than many generic ones" "can improve both your content's performance and people's experience on Instagram" ([Social Media Today, 18 Dec 2025](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/), quoting Instagram; platform statement via the press). The same article reports Mosseri saying in 2025 that hashtags "don't improve visibility on Instagram", though they are "a great way to let people know what a post is about and connect posts" (same source). No Instagram Help Centre page stating the limit was read.

**Search inside Instagram.** The 2021 post's advice: "Use a fitting handle and profile name"; "Include relevant keywords and locations in your bio"; "Use relevant keywords and hashtags in captions", in the caption and not the comments; accounts that post spam or break the guidelines "may appear lower in search results" (Instagram, 25 Aug 2021). In August 2026 Instagram published guidance that "Captions add relevant context, spark conversation, and tell search engines what your content is about" ([Social Media Today, 23 Aug 2026](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-stresses-the-importance-of-captions/828554/), quoting an Instagram post; platform statement via the press). Mosseri has said of long captions, "It is not going to affect your reach much one way or the other" ([TechJuice, 25 Aug 2025](https://www.techjuice.pk/instagram-chief-finally-puts-rest-to-a-big-caption-myth/), reporting a Stories question-and-answer; executive statement via the press, date of the answer not given).

**Search outside Instagram.** From 10 July 2025, public posts of professional accounts whose owners are over 18 can be indexed by Google and other search engines unless the owner turns the setting off (§6). No source found measures how many views this brings a small account.

**What follows for a small account.** The words a stranger would type for the subject belong in the name field, the bio and the first line of each caption; hashtags are at most five, chosen for topic, and are labels more than reach (Instagram 2021, 2025, 2026 statements above). PB2 already put the subject words in Mara's name field and bio (PB2 D3); no change is made to them here.

## 8 · Cadence

**What Instagram says.** No current Instagram page recommending a posting frequency was found. The figure widely repeated in creator blogs, "two feed posts a week and two stories a day", was found only in vendor blogs that do not link to Instagram, and is not used.

**What the vendor data says.**

- Buffer's fixed-effects analysis of about 2.1 million feed posts (photos, Reels and carousels; Stories excluded) from 102,000 accounts, comparing each account with itself: against weeks with no posts, weekly follower growth was higher by 0.12% at 1–2 posts a week, 0.26% at 3–5, 0.44% at 6–9 and 0.66% at 10 or more; reach per post was about 12% higher at 3–5 posts than at 1–2, 18% at 6–9 and 24% at 10 or more; Buffer calls 3–5 a week "likely the sweet spot" and labels 6–9 or more "treat with caution"; results are not split by account size or format ([Buffer, 13 Aug 2025](https://buffer.com/library/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)). Vendor data; within-account association, the strongest design found for Instagram. PB1 row I4.
- Metricool's 2026 data: accounts of 2,000–10,000 followers did best per post at 7–14 posts a week, and accounts of 100,000 to 1 million did better per post when they posted less, while accounts of 10,000 to 100,000 did best at 14 to 30 posts a week (Metricool, Instagram Trends 2026). Vendor data; between-account comparison; no band under 2,000.
- Metricool and HypeAuditor: 2–3 posts a week associated with about 19% average growth and 10 or more with 79% ("10+ posts per week will most likely boost it by 79%"), the baseline and window undefined (Metricool press release, 14 Oct 2025). Not used: the measure is undefined.

**For a small account.** At 140 followers Buffer's 0.26% is about 0.36 followers a week (PB1, derived). The reasons to post three times a week are the 12% higher reach per post that Buffer found at 3–5 against 1–2, and the test audience every eligible post gets (§1); the follower increment at her size is a fraction of a follower. Mara's three posts a week (PB1, PB3, PB4) sit in Buffer's 3–5 band, the one Buffer recommends. This unit keeps it.

## 9 · Sending an Instagram viewer to YouTube

**The mechanics, as documented.**

- **Profile links.** Since 18 April 2023 a profile can carry up to five links, open inside the Instagram app, with an option to open them in the phone's browser ([TechCrunch, 18 Apr 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/18/instagram-takes-on-linktree-and-others-with-support-for-up-to-5-links-in-bio/); announcement by Meta's chief executive via the press).
- **Story link sticker.** Since 27 October 2021, "we're expanding the ability to add links in Stories to all accounts"; new accounts and accounts that repeatedly break the Community Guidelines "will not have access to the Link sticker" ([Instagram, Expanding Sharing Links in Stories to Everyone, 27 Oct 2021](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/expanding-sharing-links-in-stories-to-everyone); platform-official; "new" is not defined). Stories reach followers only (§1), so a link sticker reaches followers only, which Social Media Today also states, adding that Story links are "at least in theory, not reach restricted" ([Social Media Today, 25 Nov 2024](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/heres-each-big-social-platform-stand-external-links/733946/)).
- **Captions.** Social Media Today: "Instagram doesn't let you post links in captions" (same source). Meta confirmed in March 2026 that it was testing clickable caption links for subscribers to Meta Verified, its paid service, with no word on wider availability ([Engadget, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/meta-is-testing-clickable-links-in-instagram-captions-for-verified-subscribers-184555406.html)).
- **Links inside Reels.** Links added to a Reel in Instagram's Edits app, reported in January 2026, point to an Instagram account or another public Reel, so they keep the viewer inside Instagram ([Blog du Modérateur, 20 Jan 2026](https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/instagram-integre-enfin-liens-cliquables-reels/), citing Instagram's Creators account; via the press).

**The path a stranger takes.** A non-follower who meets a Reel can reach YouTube only through the profile: tap the name, tap a link, and the video opens in the in-app browser or the phone's browser (this unit's reading of the sources above; no source lays the path out). A follower can also tap a Story link sticker.

**How the move is measured, and its limits.** Instagram counts taps on profile links in Insights under profile activity, by the description of third-party guides; no Instagram page stating the metric was read, and Meta's developer reference lists only taps on contact buttons (call, email, directions and the like) under profile_links_taps (Meta for Developers, account insights). On the YouTube side, "External" is "Traffic from websites and apps that have your YouTube video embedded or that link to it", with a card showing the sites and apps; apps YouTube cannot identify are counted under "Direct or unknown sources", which covers "direct URL entry, bookmarks, signed-out viewers and unidentified apps" ([YouTube Help, Traffic sources for views, answer 1714921](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1714921?hl=en-GB); platform-official). So views arriving from Instagram may sit in either row, and the External count is a floor.

**What the move is worth.** No source found measures how many people who see a creator's Reel go on to watch the same creator's YouTube video (PB3 §4, unchanged by this unit's searches). A plan that depends on that rate rests on no number. Instagram's role in this course is the reach to strangers and the contact with followers it gives in its own right; any YouTube views it sends are read as direction, from the two counts above.

## 10 · Common claims for which no source was found

Each was searched for on 12 November 2026. None has a source that meets this course's standard; several have a platform statement against them.

1. **Hashtags raise reach, and more is better.** Mosseri said in 2025 that hashtags "don't improve visibility"; Instagram limits them to five (§7).
2. **Instagram hides accounts to sell ads ("shadowban").** Instagram says it does not "suppress content to encourage people to buy ads" (§1). Recommendation limits it does apply are shown in Account Status.
3. **A link in a Story or in the profile cuts reach.** No Instagram statement on the links themselves was found; the only press reading is that Story links are "at least in theory, not reach restricted" (§9). On the related claim that writing "link in bio" in a post cuts reach, Mosseri said "You're more than welcome to say 'link in bio,' it will not affect your reach one way or another" ([Social Media Today, 23 Jul 2025](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-doesnt-penalize-posts-that-include-link-in-bio/753899/); executive statement via the press).
4. **Instagram recommends a set number of posts a week or Stories a day.** No current Instagram page found (§8).
5. **There is a best hour or day to post.** Only vendor correlations (Metricool and HypeAuditor's 8 p.m. local time, Wednesday and Friday, Metricool press release, 14 Oct 2025); no Instagram statement found.
6. **The first hour decides a post's fate.** Instagram describes a small test audience followed by wider ones (§1) and gives no time window.
7. **Instagram shows an unengaged carousel again from its second slide.** No Instagram statement was found. Lia Haberman summarises a 2024 Mosseri post as saying the algorithm gives a carousel "a second chance after it's posted, resurfacing it to followers who previously swiped by" ([ICYMI, 14 Oct 2025](https://liahaberman.substack.com/p/the-best-instagram-content-formats); her words, not his), and a music-licensing company's blog paraphrases a recent Mosseri interview with Colin and Samir (the article does not date it) to the same effect without quoting him on it ([Uppbeat, 3 Nov 2026](https://uppbeat.io/blog/creator-news/whats-working-on-instagram-right-now-carousels-and-sends-says-adam-mosseri)). No rule here depends on it.
8. **Posts published through a scheduler get less reach.** The only tests found were run by companies that sell scheduling; no Instagram statement found.
9. **Editing a caption after posting resets a post's reach.** No Instagram statement found; vendor blogs disagree.
10. **Instagram converts Reel viewers into YouTube viewers at a known rate.** No source (§9).
