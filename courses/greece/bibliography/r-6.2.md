# Greece 6.2: The Athenian empire and the radical democracy: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Aeschylus, *Eumenides* (458): cited as a whole, with no line numbers.
- Aristotle (attributed), *Athenaion Politeia* (*Constitution of the Athenians*) 23–28 (constitutional change); 23.1 (guardianship of the laws); 23.5 (the oaths and the iron ingots); 25 (Ephialtes' reform); 26.2 (the archonship opened to the zeugitai); 26.4 (the citizenship law); 27.3–4 (jury pay).
- Aristotle, *Politics* 1292a (extreme democracy); 1281a–b (the judgement of the many).
- Diodorus Siculus 11–12 (cited for archon-year dating); 12.4 (the Peace of Callias).
- Plato, *Gorgias* 515e (Pericles and pay). Plato, *Protagoras* 319. Plato, *Republic* 557a–564a (democracy); 488a–489a (the ship of state).
- Plutarch, *Aristides* 25; *Cimon* 10; 15; *Pericles* 11–14; 12 (transfer of the treasury, Samian proposal); 12–13 (the building programme); 22–23 (Pleistoanax); 37 (the citizenship law).
- Pseudo-Xenophon (the "Old Oligarch"), *Constitution of the Athenians* 1.1–9; 3.1–9. The report notes that the work is preserved among Xenophon's but not his.
- Theopompus: named for calling the Peace of Callias a forgery and the Oath of Plataea a fourth-century fabrication; no work or locator given.
- Thucydides 1.40; 1.89–118 (the Pentecontaetia); 1.95; 1.96 (first assessment of 460 talents); 1.98.4 (Naxos); 1.99; 1.100–101 (Thasos); 1.102 (Cimon at Ithome); 1.102–103; 1.113–114; 1.115–117 (Samos); 1.115.1 (Thirty Years' Peace); 2.35–46 (Funeral Oration); 2.65 and 2.65.9; 3.38; 8.48; 8.97.
