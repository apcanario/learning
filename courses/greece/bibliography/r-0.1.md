# Greece 0.1: How we know anything: the sources: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Aeschylus, *Eumenides* (458 BC), cited for the move of Orestes' trial to the Areopagus; no passage given.
- Apollodorus (Pseudo-), *Library* 1.1.5–7 (three accounts of Cronus' children); the report also relies on the *Epitome*, found 1885–87. Frazer's Loeb (1921) is the standard English-facing edition.
- Aristotle, *Poetics* 1453b22–26.
- Bacchylides, ode 17 (Theseus' dive to Poseidon's palace).
- Cicero, *De oratore* 3.137 (Pisistratus and the recension of Homer).
- Diodorus Siculus, *Library of History* books 4–5 (no passages given).
- Euripides, *Helen*; *Medea* (with the scholion on line 264); *Phoenician Women* (no passages given beyond *Medea* 264).
- Herodotus, *Histories* 1.1–5; 2.43–45; 2.49; 2.53; 2.113–120; 7.152; 7.204.
- Hesiod, *Theogony* 27–28; 154–210 (castration of Uranus); 188–206 (birth of Aphrodite); 278–279 (Poseidon and Medusa); 453–506 (Cronus and the birth of Zeus); 507–616 (Prometheus and the first woman).
- Hesiod, *Works and Days* 42–105 (Pandora); 109–201 (Ages of Man).
- Hesiod (attrib.), *Catalogue of Women*, cited as surviving in papyrus fragments; no fragment numbers given.
- Homer, *Iliad* 5.370 (Aphrodite daughter of Zeus and Dione); 8.362–369 (Heracles' labours); 24.25–30 (Judgement of Paris).
- Homer, *Odyssey* 11.271–280 (Oedipus); 12.69–72 (the Argonauts).
- Homeric Hymns to Demeter, Apollo, Hermes and Aphrodite (the four long hymns); no line numbers given. Thucydides 3.104 quotes the *Hymn to Apollo* as Homer's.
- Hyginus, *Fabulae* (no passages given).
- Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 3.339–510 (Echo and Narcissus); 4.790–803 (Medusa); 6.1–145 (Arachne); 10.243–297 (Pygmalion).
- Palaephatus, *On Unbelievable Things*; Parthenius; Antoninus Liberalis, *Metamorphoses*; [Eratosthenes], *Catasterisms*: named as handbooks, no passages given.
- Pausanias, *Description of Greece* 2.3.6–11 (Medea's children, citing Eumelus); 5.17–19 (Chest of Cypselus); 8.8.3; 10.25–31 (Polygnotus at Delphi).
- Pindar, *Olympian* 1.28–29; 1.36–53; 9.35–41.
- Plato, *Phaedrus* 243a (quoting Stesichorus' Palinode).
- Plutarch, *Theseus* 1; also *Moralia* (*Greek Questions*, *On Isis and Osiris*), named without passages.
- Proclus, *Chrestomathy*, known through Photius and the Venetus A manuscript of the *Iliad*; the lost Epic Cycle (*Cypria*, *Aethiopis*, *Little Iliad*, *Sack of Ilion*, *Returns*, *Telegony*, the Theban poems, *Titanomachy*) is cited only through these summaries and West's collection of fragments (see Modern works).
- Sappho, fr. 16 (numbering of Voigt).
- Sophocles, *Oedipus at Colonus* and *Electra* (named for variant versions; no passages given).
- Stesichorus, *Palinode*, *Geryoneis*, *Oresteia* (fragments; no numbers given).
- Thucydides, *Histories* 1.2–19 (the "Archaeology"); 1.9–11; 1.21–22; 3.104.

## Modern works
- Marcel Detienne, *L'invention de la mythologie* (Paris: Gallimard, 1981); English translation by Margaret Cook, *The Creation of Mythology* (Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1986). The report gives the 1981 date.
- Robert L. Fowler, *Early Greek Mythography*, vol. 1: *Text and Introduction* (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2000); vol. 2: *Commentary* (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2013). The machine list's "Hellanicus (2013)" is vol. 2 (identified from context).
- Timothy Gantz, *Early Greek Myth: A Guide to Literary and Artistic Sources*, 2 vols (Baltimore: Johns Hopkins University Press, 1993).
- Paul Veyne, *Les Grecs ont-ils cru à leurs mythes? Essai sur l'imagination constituante* (Paris: Seuil, 1983); English translation by Paula Wissing, *Did the Greeks Believe in Their Myths? An Essay on the Constitutive Imagination* (Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1988).
- M. L. West (ed. and trans.), *Greek Epic Fragments from the Seventh to the Fifth Centuries BC*, Loeb Classical Library 497 (Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 2003).
- M. L. West, *Indo-European Poetry and Myth* (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2007). The report cites "West, 2007" for Indo-European parallels (identified from context).

## Corpora and inscriptions
- *Die Fragmente der griechischen Historiker* (FGrH), ed. F. Jacoby (Berlin and Leiden, 1923–): cited generally for the local historians; no numbers given.
- Parian Marble (*Marmor Parium*; the report dates it 264/3 BC and gives Troy's fall as 1209/8): FGrH 239 (standard number, not given in the report).
- Lindian Chronicle (99 BC): FGrH 532 (standard number, not given in the report).
- Linear B tablets from Knossos, Pylos, Thebes and Mycenae, and the Khania tablet: cited for the divine names only, with no tablet numbers; see 1.2 for numbered tablets.
