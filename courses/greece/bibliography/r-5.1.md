# Greece 5.1: The eighth-century renaissance: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Aelian, *Varia Historia* 13.14 (Peisistratus and the Homeric poems).
- Cicero, *De oratore* 3.137 (Peisistratus arranging the books of Homer).
- *Contest of Homer and Hesiod* (named in the discussion of Homer's date; no passage given).
- Diogenes Laertius, *Lives of the Philosophers* 1.57 (Solon and the relay rule).
- Eusebius, *Chronicle* (named as the channel preserving Hippias of Elis's Olympic victor list; no passage given).
- Herodotus, *Histories* 2.53; 4.150–159; 4.151; 5.58; 5.99; 8.144.
- Hesiod, *Theogony* 22–34.
- Hesiod, *Works and Days* 27–41; 38–39; 109–201; 156–173; 225–247; 263–264; 376–380; 383–384; 441; 528; 633–640; 650–662; 663–694.
- Hippias of Elis, Olympic victor list (known only through Eusebius, as the report says; no fragment number given).
- Homer, *Iliad* 2.684; 7.86–91; 9.404–405; 11.632–637; 11.709.
- Homer, *Odyssey* 6.162; 9.116–141.
- *Homeric Hymn to Apollo* 146–164; 281–374.
- Josephus, *Against Apion* 1.12.
- Livy, *History of Rome* 8.22.5–6.
- Pausanias, *Description of Greece* 2.1.6–7; 2.15.2–3; 5.8.5; 5.8.5–6; 5.20.1; 7.26.13; 9.31.4.
- Pindar, *Nemean* 2.1 (cited for the scholiast's note on Cynaethus of Chios); *Olympian* 10.43–59; "Pindar passim" for Isthmia.
- [Plato], *Hipparchus* 228b (pseudo-Platonic, as the report says).
- Plutarch, *Life of Lycurgus* 1.1 (citing Aristotle, as the report says).
- Plutarch, *Life of Numa* 1.4.
- Plutarch, *Moralia* 153f (the Amphidamas story; the report gives only "Moralia 153f". Identified from context as the *Banquet of the Seven Sages*, but not verified).
- Strabo, *Geography* 5.4.9; 8.3.30.
- Thucydides, *History* 1.3; 1.15.3; 3.104; 6.3–5.

## Modern works
- Carla M. Antonaccio, *An Archaeology of Ancestors: Tomb Cult and Hero Cult in Early Greece* (Lanham, MD: Rowman & Littlefield, 1995).
- John McK. Camp, "A Drought in the Late Eighth Century B.C.", *Hesperia* 48 (1979) 397–411. Identified from the report's "John Camp (1979)", which describes the abandoned Agora wells around 700 read as a drought.
- Paul Christesen, *Olympic Victor Lists and Ancient Greek History* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2007).
- J. N. Coldstream, "Hero-cults in the Age of Homer", *Journal of Hellenic Studies* 96 (1976) 8–17.
- J. N. Coldstream, *Geometric Greece* (London: Methuen, 1977).
- Robin Hägg (ed.), *The Greek Renaissance of the Eighth Century B.C.: Tradition and Innovation* (Stockholm: Swedish Institute at Athens, 1983; Acta Instituti Atheniensis Regni Sueciae, 4°, 30). The report cites this as the source of the label "renaissance" (symposium of 1981).
- Richard Janko, *Homer, Hesiod and the Hymns: Diachronic Development in Epic Diction* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1982).
- Minna Skafte Jensen, *The Homeric Question and the Oral-Formulaic Theory* (Copenhagen: Museum Tusculanum Press, 1980). Identified from context: the report credits "Minna Skafte Jensen (1980)" with reviving a strong Peisistratid dating.
- Alfred Mallwitz, "Cult and Competition Locations at Olympia", in Wendy J. Raschke (ed.), *The Archaeology of the Olympics: The Olympics and Other Festivals in Antiquity* (Madison: University of Wisconsin Press, 1988) 79–109. Identified from context: the report's "Alfred Mallwitz (1988)" argues from the stratigraphy of stadium and wells that organised games cannot predate c. 700.
- Ian Morris, *Burial and Ancient Society: The Rise of the Greek City-State* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1987).
- Catherine Morgan, *Athletes and Oracles: The Transformation of Olympia and Delphi in the Eighth Century BC* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1990).
- Joseph Naveh, "Some Semitic Epigraphical Considerations on the Antiquity of the Greek Alphabet", *American Journal of Archaeology* 77 (1973) 1–8 (pages not verified). Identified from context: the report's "Joseph Naveh (1973)" dates the borrowing of the alphabet to c. 1100.
- François de Polignac, *La naissance de la cité grecque: cultes, espace et société, VIIIe–VIIe siècles avant J.-C.* (Paris: La Découverte, 1984) (not verified). The report gives only "François de Polignac (1984)", on extra-urban sanctuaries and territory. English translation: *Cults, Territory, and the Origins of the Greek City-State* (Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1995).
- Barry B. Powell, *Homer and the Origin of the Greek Alphabet* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1991).
- Anthony M. Snodgrass, *Archaic Greece: The Age of Experiment* (London: Dent, 1980).
- Anthony M. Snodgrass, "Les origines du culte des héros dans la Grèce antique", in G. Gnoli and J.-P. Vernant (eds.), *La mort, les morts dans les sociétés anciennes* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press; Paris: Maison des sciences de l'homme, 1982) 107–119 (not verified). Identified from context: the report cites "Snodgrass (1982, 1988)" for the view that farmers claimed old tombs as ancestors.
- Anthony M. Snodgrass, "The Archaeology of the Hero", *Annali di Archeologia e Storia Antica* (Naples; cited in bibliographies as *AION(archeol)*) 10 (1988) 19–26.
- Anthony M. Snodgrass, *Homer and the Artists: Text and Picture in Early Greek Art* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1998).
- Martin L. West, *Hesiod: Theogony* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1966) (identified from context and not verified: the report gives "West (1966)" for dating the *Theogony* c. 730–700).
- Martin L. West, *The Making of the Iliad: Disquisition and Analytical Commentary* (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2011).
- James Whitley, "Early States and Hero Cults: A Re-appraisal", *Journal of Hellenic Studies* 108 (1988) 173–182.
- Friedrich August Wolf, *Prolegomena ad Homerum* (Halle, 1795) (not verified). The report gives only "Friedrich Wolf (1795)".
