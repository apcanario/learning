# Greece 6.4: Tragedy and comedy: sources

Curated 27 October 2026 (Sonnet 5.5) from the report's inline citations. Edition notes are given where the report or the standard edition makes them clear; "(not verified)" marks a reference completed from memory that could not be confirmed.

## Ancient sources
- Aeschines, *Against Ctesiphon* 41–8 (honours announced at the Dionysia).
- Aeschylus, plays discussed as a set, without line numbers except where stated: *Persians*; *Seven against Thebes*; *Suppliants*; *Agamemnon* (with 1372ff, the ekkyklema); *Libation Bearers*; *Eumenides*; *Prometheus Bound* (authorship disputed). Also the lost satyr play *Proteus* (with the *Oresteia*) and the satyr play *Net-Haulers*, known from papyri. The ancient *Life of Aeschylus* (the epitaph naming Marathon) is cited without a section number.
- Aristophanes: *Acharnians* 502–8 (no strangers present); 628–64 (parabasis, Cleon's prosecution); *Knights* 507–50 (parabasis); *Clouds* (with 518–62, the parabasis and the third place); *Wasps*; *Peace* 174–6 (the dung beetle and the machinist); *Birds* (with the scholion on 1297, the alleged decree of Syracosius); *Lysistrata*; *Women at the Thesmophoria* (*Thesmophoriazusae*); *Frogs* 954–8; 1019–27; *Assemblywomen*; *Wealth*. The lost *Babylonians* is named. Plays are listed with dates in the report's tables.
- Aristotle, *Poetics* 1448a29–b2; 1449a9–21 (origins); 1449a15–18 (the actors); 1449b12 (one day); 1449b24–8 (definition of tragedy); 1450a38; 1452a22–33; 1453a7–17 (hamartia); 1453a29; 1456a25. Aristotle, *Politics* 8.1341b–1342a (katharsis). Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* 1135b (hamartema). Aristotle, lost *Didascaliae*: named, not cited by locator.
- Euripides: *Medea*; *Hippolytus*; *Alcestis*; *Children of Heracles*; *Andromache*; *Hecuba*; *Suppliant Women*; *Electra*; *Heracles*; *Trojan Women*; *Iphigenia among the Taurians*; *Ion*; *Helen*; *Phoenician Women*; *Orestes*; *Bacchae*; *Iphigenia at Aulis*; *Rhesus* (authorship disputed); *Cyclops*. The lost *Alexandros* and *Palamedes* are named. The report cites the plays as wholes, without line numbers.
- Herodotus 1.23 (Arion and the dithyramb); 5.67 (tragic choruses at Sicyon); 6.21 (Phrynichus' *Capture of Miletus*).
- Homer, *Iliad* 10 (source of *Rhesus*); *Odyssey* 9 (source of *Cyclops*).
- Horace, *Ars Poetica* 220–50 (satyr play); 275–7 (Thespis' wagon).
- Isocrates, *On the Peace* 82.
- *Life of Sophocles* 4 (the chorus of fifteen).
- Lysias 4.3 (judging); 21.1–5 (cost of a tragic chorus).
- Menander: *Dyskolos* (316); *Samia*; *Epitrepontes*; *Aspis*; *Perikeiromene*, from the Bodmer codex and other papyri. Aristophanes of Byzantium's remark on Menander: no locator given.
- Pausanias 1.14.5 (Aeschylus' epitaph).
- Plato, *Apology* 18b–19c; *Symposium* 175e.
- Plutarch, *Cimon* 8 (Cimon's generals as judges in 468); *Solon* 29.6 (Solon and Thespis).
- Pollux, *Onomasticon* 4.133–54 (tragic masks).
- Sophocles: *Antigone*; *Oedipus Tyrannus*; *Oedipus at Colonus*; *Ajax*; *Philoctetes*; *Electra*; *Women of Trachis*; and the satyr play *Trackers* (papyri). The treatise *On the Chorus* is known through the Suda.
- Suda: entries cited for Sophocles' *On the Chorus*, for Arion's "tragic mode" and for comedy at the Dionysia in 486; no entry numbers given.

## Corpora and inscriptions
- Marmor Parium (the Parian Marble), *FGrH* 239 (F. Jacoby, *Die Fragmente der griechischen Historiker*): cited for Thespis' first tragic victory between 538 and 528.
- Papyri: Aeschylus' *Net-Haulers* and Sophocles' *Trackers* (satyr plays, "papyri preserve much"); Menander's Bodmer codex (published 1958). No papyrus numbers given.
- Hypotheses prefixed to the plays and the inscribed festival records (didascalic inscriptions): cited generally, with no inscription numbers.
