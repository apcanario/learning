# Audience Retention and Audience-to-Revenue Conversion for Small Solo Creators (as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: figures are recorded with source and date. "Measured" = platform data or a dataset with a stated sample; "survey" = self-reported; "aggregator" = secondary stats sites with weak or absent primary sourcing (flagged as such; use with caution); "anecdote" = a named creator's own reported numbers.

## Key Question 1: Subscriber/follower churn and inactivity — what share of an audience actually shows up?

### Takeaway
No platform publishes a true "share of subscribers who watch each video" figure; the best available proxies suggest that only a small minority of a follower base sees any given piece (Patreon's 2025 survey: only 24% of Instagram users' time, 32% of YouTube's and 38% of TikTok's is spent on creators they follow; a 2026 scrape of 87 YouTube uploads found a median first-day views-to-subscriber ratio of ~12–14%). Instagram per-follower engagement across business accounts sits at a median of 0.30% per post (2026) and falls as follower counts rise; email lists are claimed to lose ~20–30% of addresses a year but every churn figure I found is unsourced aggregator material.

### Cited Findings
**YouTube**
- Measured (public views, not unique subscriber watches): across 87 long-form uploads from 56 channels published 21 Aug–3 Sep 2026, the median ratio of first-24-hour views to subscriber count was 13.6% pooled / 12.0% with equal channel weighting; 31.1% median for 100K–999K-sub channels; 2.0% for 1M+ channels; 10th percentile 0.4%, 25th 2.4%, 75th 69.3%. Under-100K channels (only 13 videos) showed a median of 373.9% because views come mostly from non-subscribers. The authors explicitly reject any universal "10% of subscribers watch every video" rule and note public views include non-subscribers and are not unique viewers — [OverseerOS, 5 Sep 2026](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/what-percentage-of-subscribers-watch-youtube-video)
- Older methodological note (2014): YouTube Analytics' "subscriber views" filter can be divided by subscriber count to estimate views per subscriber per period; the Wizdeo page gives only a hypothetical example (30K subs, 60K subscriber views in a month = 2 videos per subscriber), not measured data — [Wizdeo, 30 Jan 2014](https://blog.wizdeo.com/en/63-stats-analysis/583-are-your-customers-loyal)
- YouTube replaced its new/returning viewer split with New / Casual (returned 1–5 times in past year) / Regular (6+ times in past year) tiers in mid-July 2025; the Vidpros article (citing undated third parties) says most channels sit "well below double-digit" Regular share, >10% is "excellent" for new channels, 20–40% is the recommended range for established channels, and return-rate figures become meaningful only after ~20 uploads — [Vidpros, 25 Jul 2026](https://vidpros.com/youtube-returning-viewers/) (third-party claims; not YouTube-published benchmarks)
- Metricool study cited via Vidpros: 82,000+ accounts, 7.3M videos, average views per video up 76% YoY and engagement down 37% (date not given) — [Vidpros, 25 Jul 2026](https://vidpros.com/youtube-returning-viewers/)

**Follow-feed decay / algorithmic reach (survey + platform estimates)**
- Patreon's State of Create (1,000 US creators + 2,000 fans, via NewtonX, published Feb 2025): "only 24% of users' time is spent viewing content from creators they follow" on Instagram; YouTube 32%, TikTok 38%. 53% of creators say it is harder to reach their followers than five years ago; 60% say Instagram does not always show fans their most important work; 75% say platforms punish creators who are not constantly publishing; 70% report unpredictable monthly incomes — [Net Influencer summary of Patreon State of Create 2025](https://www.netinfluencer.com/patreon-2025-state-of-create-report-how-creators-are-breaking-free-from-platform-control/); primary: [Patreon, State of Create 2025](https://news.patreon.com/articles/state-of-create) (company-sponsored research)
- Creator confidence that fans will see their work: 72% on Patreon, 56% YouTube, 53% TikTok, 48% Instagram. Fan confidence they will see creators they follow: 95% Patreon, 78% YouTube, 69% TikTok, 67% Instagram — [Net Influencer / Patreon, Feb 2025](https://www.netinfluencer.com/patreon-2025-state-of-create-report-how-creators-are-breaking-free-from-platform-control/)

**Instagram engagement per follower**
- Measured, business accounts: median Instagram engagement rate per post by follower was 0.30% in the 2026 report, down from 0.36% (~17% drop); TikTok 2.01%; X 0.03% (doubled from 0.015%). Method: total interactions / followers, medians across 150 companies per industry, 18 industries, from a 200K+ company database — [Quid (Rival IQ) 2026 Social Media Industry Benchmark Report, 5 Mar 2026](https://www.quid.com/knowledge-hub/resource-library/blog/2026-social-media-industry-benchmark-report). Note: Rival IQ's own page was not fetchable in this session; Quid is Rival IQ's parent.
- Measured, influencer accounts (Captiv8 data, 5,000+ organic posts, 14M+ engagements; engagement/impressions on IG, engagement/video views on TikTok): Instagram nano-influencers (<10K followers) had the platform's highest engagement at 6.23%, with engagement falling as follower count rises; TikTok 1.5M+ "All Star" creators averaged 4.95% — [EMARKETER, 28 Mar 2024](https://www.emarketer.com/content/exclusive-instagram-s-nano-influencers-outperform-as-do-tiktok-s-upper-echelon-of-creators) (older data)
- EMARKETER chart "Engagement Rates on Instagram Decline Steadily as Influencer Follower Count Grows" (worldwide, 2025), published 16 Feb 2026; values paywalled — [EMARKETER](https://www.emarketer.com/chart/c/360157/engagement-rates-on-instagram-decline-steadily-influencer-follower-count-grows-360157)
- Aggregator (unsourced "industry analyses"): Instagram nano ~1.7%, micro ~0.7%, macro ~0.6%, mega ~0.7%; TikTok nano ~10%, micro ~8%, macro ~7%; page contradicts itself (3.86% IG micro elsewhere) — [Stack Influence, 2025/26](https://stackinfluence.com/the-average-influencer-engagement-rate-in-2025/) (low reliability)

**Newsletter / email list decay**
- Aggregator, unsourced: "the average email list loses 22% of its members each year"; typical ranges 20–30% annually; media & publishing 20–30%; e-commerce 25–35%. No primary study is cited for any churn figure; the only dated citation (Return Path 2022) concerns spam filtering, not churn — [EmailListValidation, 2026](https://emaillistvalidation.com/blog/average-email-list-churn-rate-per-year-by-industry/) (low reliability)
- Measured platform-wide (beehiiv, 65,000+ newsletters, 28B emails in 2025): open rate 41.24% (up from ~37.7% in 2024), CTR 3.23% (down from 4.74%), spam complaint 0.02%, bounce 1.48%. Unsubscribe rate and free-to-paid conversion are not reported. Median subscribers for 2025 launches: 482 in month one, 8,314 at end of year one — [beehiiv, State of Newsletters 2026](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/beehiiv-the-state-of-newsletters-2026) (platform-published; some internal inconsistencies noted)
- Measured benchmark via aggregator: GetResponse 2024 unsubscribe benchmark 0.15% per send, spam complaints <0.01% — cited in [EmailListValidation](https://emaillistvalidation.com/blog/average-email-list-churn-rate-per-year-by-industry/)

### Inferences
- For a small channel (<100K), the subscriber base is a poor predictor of views; most views on any upload come from non-subscribers via recommendation, so "subscribers" measures past reach, not present attention. The actionable retention metric is YouTube's Regular-viewer share, and for a new channel a Regular share above ~10% is a good sign (third-party claim).
- A 0.15%/send unsubscribe rate at weekly cadence compounds to roughly 7–8% a year from unsubscribes alone; the oft-quoted 20–30% annual list decay must therefore include disengagement and dead addresses, not just opt-outs. Treat it as an order-of-magnitude guide only.
- Instagram per-follower engagement for a solo creator under 10K followers is plausibly 10–20× the business-account median (0.30%), but that advantage shrinks sharply above ~10K.

### Gaps
- No platform-published figure for the share of YouTube subscribers who watch a given video; YouTube Analytics exposes it per channel but no aggregate exists. The Hank Green "subscribers don't see videos" talk could not be located in search results.
- No measured newsletter annual churn figure from a primary source (beehiiv, Kit, Substack do not publish unsubscribe or churn rates). Kit/ConvertKit State of the Creator Economy 2024 page shows only sample size (1,000 creators); the full report is gated.
- Rival IQ's own 2025/2026 report page could not be fetched (not on allowlist); Quid's republication was used instead.

## Key Question 2: Retention levers with evidence

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence points to consistency of cadence and format (YouTube's own guidance plus an 18,000-channel correlational study), with named communities (+4–8% monthly retention on Patreon) and members-only content (20%+ lower cancellation) as the measured community levers; evidence that a "method/format" channel retains better than a "topic" channel is directional (YouTube's own advice) rather than quantified.

### Cited Findings
**Platform guidance (YouTube official)**
- YouTube's help page on growing Regular viewers: "Try creating content that's more consistent and similar in topic or format, or work with the same host"; "Create a series of videos from topics that have already proven popular with your viewers"; "publish content regularly and maintain a strong connection with your audience"; "engage more with your community, like responding to comments"; also lists community posts, live premieres/chats, brand consistency, and reminding viewers to turn on notifications. No quantified effect sizes — [YouTube Help, "New, casual, & regular viewers tips"](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13615784?hl=en)

**Posting cadence and consistency (measured, correlational)**
- AIR Media-Tech study of 18,000 English-language channels, 11 niches, 12-month snapshot, primary segment 100K–1M monthly views: across every niche, channels on a predictable schedule earned more views per subscriber than channels at the same monthly volume posting on random days (effect size not reported); the advantage is largest for channels posting 5–20 videos/month; channels going dark 14+ days three or more times a year got fewer views per video; channels posting under 2 videos/month sit below the niche median; over-posting correlates with lower subscriber counts. Health & Fitness: 2–5 videos/month median 172,000 subs vs 77,700 at 10–20/month; Education: 2–5/month peaks at 116,000. Sensitivity to breaks is "moderate" for Health & Fitness, "low" for Education/Science. Authors state correlation not causation; vendor publishes to sell audits — [AIR Media-Tech, 8 Jun 2026](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/does-posting-more-on-youtube-help-we-studied-18000-channels-to-find-out)

**Community / membership mechanics (measured, Patreon)**
- Patreon 2022 Creator Census (13,000+ creators, 113+ countries): creators with custom names for their patron community had 4–8% higher monthly retention; ~25% of creators had such names (33% of podcasters). Creators promoting their Patreon daily earned 75% more than monthly promoters; weekly promoters 31% more. 79% promote at least monthly, 49% weekly. Most common benefits: "ability to support the creator" (70%), exclusive content (69%), early access (57%) — [Net Influencer, 2022 Patreon Creator Census](https://www.netinfluencer.com/2022-patreon-creator-census/) (older data)
- Patreon State of Create 2025: 94% of fans feel a deep sense of community with other fans on Patreon vs 75% YouTube, 74% TikTok, 68% Instagram; 81% of creators want direct communication channels with fans — [Net Influencer / Patreon, Feb 2025](https://www.netinfluencer.com/patreon-2025-state-of-create-report-how-creators-are-breaking-free-from-platform-control/) (company-sponsored)
- YouTube channel memberships (attributed to "YouTube's own creator data" but unlinked): creators mentioning memberships in videos see ~3× more members; posting 2+ members-only videos per month ~15× more new members; ~20% of new members joined after seeing a members-only promotion; members who watch members-only content in their first month cancel at a rate 20%+ lower — [YTGrowth, updated 1 Sep 2026](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/youtube-channel-memberships) (unverified attribution)

**Newsletter as retention layer (measured, beehiiv platform)**
- beehiiv: newsletters using Recommendations grew 2.75× faster on average; publishers that recommend others are 32× more likely to be recommended back; weekly is the dominant cadence (≈46.7% of newsletters), monthly 27%, daily 1.4% — [beehiiv, State of Newsletters 2026](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/beehiiv-the-state-of-newsletters-2026)
- Kajabi 2025 State of Creator Commerce (sponsored press release, sample not stated): creators with community offerings report making twice as much as those without; 55% of creators cite audience ownership as the most important success factor; 57% of "entrepreneurial" creators say niche focus is key to growth vs 22% of "social-first" creators — [Kajabi via Silicon UK, 17 Apr 2025](https://www.silicon.co.uk/press-release/kajabis-2025-state-of-creator-commerce-report-unveils-major-shift-in-the-creator-economy-as-creators-embrace-an-entrepreneurial-mindset) (vendor-sponsored)

**Apparel/DTC community tactics (anecdotes)**
- SET Active (Lindsey Carter, launched 2018, ~500K Instagram followers by 2023): asked followers to vote on colours, styles and drops; posted behind-the-scenes development ("The consumer lives and breathes for teasers"); no revenue figures disclosed — [Shopify blog, 16 Mar 2023](https://www.shopify.com/blog/set-active-building-community)
- Alpha Clothing / AC1 (Ryan Tanner Mueller, ~125K Instagram followers): reports a ~67% year-over-year returning-customer rate; included a message card in every order; paid creators to wear the product; no revenue or follower-to-buyer figures — [Startland News, 27 Dec 2022](https://startlandnews.com/2022/12/alpha-ac1-2/)

### Inferences
- For a solo creator, "consistent format + predictable cadence at 2–5 long-form uploads a month" is the best-supported retention recipe for health/fitness and education-type niches; more frequent posting is associated with worse outcomes there.
- Named community identity and small recurring members-only touchpoints are the only community mechanics with measured retention deltas (4–8% monthly on Patreon; 20%+ lower cancellation on YouTube memberships, unverified). Discord/Circle spaces have no published retention data for solo creators.
- The evidence that format-based channels beat topic-based channels is YouTube's own stated recommendation (consistency of "topic or format, or same host"), not a quantified study. Treat as directional.

### Gaps
- No controlled or large-sample study isolating series/season formats versus one-off videos on returning-viewer share.
- No published Discord retention data for creator communities (search returned only gaming-studio and enterprise material).
- No measured data on reply-to-comments effects on retention; only YouTube's qualitative recommendation.
- Rosanna Pansino Think with Google case study appeared in search but was not fetched (older, 2010s).

## Key Question 3: Conversion benchmarks — what fraction of an audience buys?

### Takeaway
Measured and well-documented conversion rates cluster at low single digits: Substack says 5–10% of free subscribers go paid but independent aggregations of hundreds of publications put the median at ~3% with only 20% above 5%; Patreon's 60M free members convert at ~400K/month (~0.7%/month); YouTube memberships are claimed at 0.5–3% of subscribers; courses sold to an engaged email list at ~2–3%; merch purchase conversion is claimed at 0.4–1.2% of followers with ~$48 AOV. Patreon reports $52 annual income per fan and $110 per paying member.

### Cited Findings
**Paid newsletters / Substack**
- Substack's official guidance: "We tend to see 5-10% of free subscribers convert to paying subscriptions, with 10% being a rate to aim for" — [Substack, Going Paid Guide](https://substack.com/going-paid-guide) (undated)
- Independent aggregation (hundreds of publication reports over five years): median paid conversion 3%; only 20% of publications above 5%; journalists report 2–5%; two cases of 10% both small publications — [Really Good Business Ideas, 4 Sep 2026](https://www.reallygoodbusinessideas.com/p/substack-average-paid-subscriber-conversion-rate)
- Crowd-sourced ranges by list size (methodology not stated): 100 free subs 0.5–2%; 1,000 subs 1–3%; 10,000 subs 2–5%; 100,000 subs 3–7%; "real average is about 3%"; consistently above 7–10% is rare — [Steven Scesa, 23 Aug 2025](https://stevenscesaon.substack.com/p/substack-paidfree-ratio-analyses)
- Creator anecdote: Casey Newton (Platformer) reported ~5% free-to-paid in 2021 versus Substack's 10% guidance; Simon Owens called 5% "about right" for professional newsletters. Reader comment (unverified) claims tech newsletters are under 5%, closer to 2%, and political newsletters 4–7 points higher — [Simon Owens, 21 Sep 2021](https://simonowens.substack.com/p/whats-a-realistic-conversion-rate) (older data)
- Creator anecdote (AI newsletter): 13,413 free / 121 paid on 1 Jan 2025 = ~0.9%, described by the author as a "consistent ~1%"; paid grew 65→121 during 2024 — [Artificial Ignorance, 11 Feb 2025](https://www.ignorance.ai/p/two-years-of-artificial-ignorance)
- Secondary aggregator: average 3% conversion; niche/specialised 4–10%; tech category 8%; ~100,000 Substack publications earning money as of Apr 2026 (up from 50,000 in May 2025) against 2M+ active publications (2024) — [Best Writing, updated 24 Oct 2026](https://bestwriting.com/substack-statistics) (secondary; sources are other aggregators)
- beehiiv: paid subscriptions generated $19M for creators in 2025 (vs $8M in 2024); median time to first dollar for 2025 launches 66 days; example: Generalist World 43,000 subscribers and 700+ paying members (~1.6%) — [beehiiv, State of Newsletters 2026](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/beehiiv-the-state-of-newsletters-2026)

**Patreon**
- Sacra estimate: free membership tiers have grown to 60 million users; about 400,000 free members convert to paid each month (≈0.67%/month, my arithmetic); 2025 revenue est. $179M, blended take ~8.5% — [Sacra, 2025](https://sacra.com/c/patreon/) (analyst estimates)
- Patreon State of Create 2025: "average annual income per fan on Patreon is $52, while income per paying member reaches $110"; creators report earning 40× more annual revenue per fan on Patreon than on TikTok — [Net Influencer / Patreon, Feb 2025](https://www.netinfluencer.com/patreon-2025-state-of-create-report-how-creators-are-breaking-free-from-platform-control/) (company-sponsored)
- Graphtreon via IMH: 273,000+ creators with at least one patron (Jul 2024); 13.9M paid memberships monthly average (Jun 2023–Jun 2024), peak 14.54M; monthly creator payouts $23.78M (Jun 2024). Implied ~51–53 memberships per creator and ~$1.64 per membership per month are derived, not reported, and mix measurement periods — [Influencer Marketing Hub, 2024](https://influencermarketinghub.com/patreon-stats-revenue-users/)
- Graphtreon category totals (undated, via Blogging Wizard, updated Feb 2026): Video 54,000+ creators, $5.9M/month, 3.8M+ pledges (≈$109/month and ≈70 pledges per creator, derived); Podcasts 15,800+ creators, $3.6M/month (≈$228/month per creator, derived) — [Blogging Wizard](https://bloggingwizard.com/patreon-statistics/)
- Patreon crossed $10B cumulative creator payments (Aug 2025) — [Axios, 5 Aug 2025](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/05/patreon-10-billion-creator-economy-ai) (headline only; not fetched)

**YouTube channel memberships**
- Typical conversion "0.5% to 3% of subscribers" (unsourced); most channels price the entry tier at $2.99–$4.99; YouTube takes 30%; unnamed creator examples: memberships ≈26% of revenue at 130K subs, ≈50% (with Super Thanks) at 489K, ≈10% at 859K, ≈30% at 928K — [YTGrowth, 1 Sep 2026](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/youtube-channel-memberships) (unverified)

**Courses / digital products**
- Teachery "Make $500" course guidance: expect about 2–3% of an engaged email audience (regular contact for the past few months) to purchase a mini-course; e.g. 300 subscribers → 6–9 sales, 500 → 10–15, 800 → 16–24 — [Teachery, Part 2: Managing Expectations](https://make500.teachery.co/lessons/part-2-managing-expectations) (practitioner guidance, undated)
- Li Jin (2020, older): Teachable: nearly 500 creators earned over $100K in 2019, 25 averaged over $1,000 per sale; an art-advice creator earned $110,000 from 76 students (avg $1,437); a physiotherapy instructor $141,000 from 61 students (avg $2,314); Patreon share of new patrons paying over $100/month grew 21% since 2017 — [a16z, 6 Feb 2020](https://a16z.com/1000-true-fans-try-100/)

**Merch / apparel**
- Aggregator (attributions to "Spring Benchmark Studies / Linktree" and "Fourthwall" but links point to homepages): follower purchase conversion 0.40–1.20%; average order value $48.50 (typical $38–65); print-on-demand gross margin 42% vs 12% dropshipping; 78% of creator stores use POD. Implied revenue per 1,000 followers ≈$194–582 per campaign (my arithmetic, one order per buyer) — [VoxBooster, 2026](https://voxbooster.com/blog/merch-sales-creator-statistics-2026) (low reliability; unverifiable attributions)
- Fourthwall case studies give almost no numbers: Santagato Studios "passing six-figures in less than six months"; Snazzy Seagull made more sales in her first weekend on Fourthwall than in a month elsewhere; no audience sizes or conversion rates; no sports/athlete apparel brands listed — [Fourthwall case studies](https://fourthwall.com/case-studies)
- Linktree Creator Commerce Report (Nov 2024, ~2,000 creators): about 20% of Linktree's 1.3B monthly clicks go to retail/e-commerce sites; clicks rose 150% around Black Friday/Cyber Monday; 70% of creators earned under $49,000 in the past year — [Passionfruit summary, Nov 2024](https://passionfru.it/linktree-creators-109807/)

### Inferences
- A working planning range for a solo creator: 1–3% of an engaged, owned list (email/free tier) converts to a paid product; 0.5–1% of a social follower base buys merch per drop; fewer than 1% of free Patreon followers convert per month. Substack's 5–10% is the upper bound and skews to political/news writers.
- Revenue per 1,000 engaged subscribers per year, order of magnitude: paid newsletter at 3% × ~$50–100/yr ≈ $1,500–3,000; Patreon at $52 per fan/year implies ~$52,000 per 1,000 *fans* but that is per Patreon follower (a self-selected subset), not per social follower; merch at 0.4–1.2% × $48 ≈ $200–600 per drop. All derived from the figures above.
- The platform-published figure (Substack 5–10%) and the measured median (~3%) differ by 2–3×; the report should quote the measured figure and label Substack's as aspirational.

### Gaps
- No primary Patreon-published free-to-paid conversion rate; only Sacra's estimate.
- No YouTube-published membership conversion figures; the 0.5–3% range is unsourced.
- No measured own-brand apparel conversion data for athlete/creator brands; Fourthwall, Shopify and Startland pieces are qualitative. The Chicknlegs (running apparel) podcast and Roots of Fight podcast surfaced in search but were not fetched.
- Kit/ConvertKit full report and Kajabi methodology are gated; Kit's product-type revenue breakdown could not be obtained.

## Key Question 4: The "1,000 true fans" thesis and the income distribution in 2025–2026

### Takeaway
Across every dataset found, a small minority of creators earn a living: Goldman Sachs (2023) puts professionals at ~4% of 50M creators, more than half of surveyed creators earn under $15,000 a year (2025), 70% earn under $49,000 (Linktree 2024), and only 28% of European brand-collaborating creators work full-time (2025). Li Jin's "100 true fans" reframing (2020) holds up in the sense that higher-priced, outcome-based products (courses at $1,000+, coaching tiers) let very small paying audiences generate full-time income, but no 2025–26 source quantifies how many creators reach 1,000 paying fans.

### Cited Findings
- Goldman Sachs: ~50 million global creators; "Only about 4% of global creators are deemed professionals," defined as earning more than $100,000 a year; creator economy $250B (2023) → $480B by 2027 — [Goldman Sachs, 19 Apr 2023](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/the-creator-economy-could-approach-half-a-trillion-dollars-by-2027) (older)
- SignalFire (2021, older): ~50M creators, of which ~2M professional/full-time and ~46.7M amateur (≈4%) — [Hypebot, 14 Jun 2021](https://www.hypebot.com/how-many-creators-are-there-and-how-do-they-make-money-signalfire-study)
- Influencer Marketing Hub / NeoReach Creator Earnings 2025 (3,000+ creators): "more than half still earn under $15,000 a year" (48% in 2023); ~57% of full-time creators earn less than the $44,000 US living wage; 43.8% have under 100K followers; 49%+ earn most revenue from brand deals (down ~10 points); full-time creators who own a brand average close to $100K; no median reported; self-selected sample — [Influencer Marketing Hub, 4 Aug 2025](https://influencermarketinghub.com/creator-earnings-report-2025/)
- Linktree Creator Commerce Report (~2,000 creators, Nov 2024): 70% earned under $49,000 in the past year; 59% struggle to produce consistently; 55% cite unpredictable algorithms — [Passionfruit, Nov 2024](https://passionfru.it/linktree-creators-109807/)
- Kolsquare × NewtonX (783 European creators with 5K+ followers and a brand deal, fieldwork May–Jun 2025): only 28% work full-time; 38% of women and 23% of men earn under €500/month; 67% earn most from paid collaborations; 65% feel stressed by the work — [Kolsquare, 7 Jul 2025](https://www.kolsquare.com/en-gb/free-resources/voices-of-the-creator-economy-by-kolsquare-newtonx)
- Patreon State of Create 2025 sample: 35.4% full-time, 43.4% part-time, 21.2% hobby; "67% more creators report earning from direct fan revenue streams compared to five years ago" (ambiguous phrasing); direct-to-fan market estimated $194B (2025) → $237B (2027) — [Net Influencer / Patreon, Feb 2025](https://www.netinfluencer.com/patreon-2025-state-of-create-report-how-creators-are-breaking-free-from-platform-control/)
- Simon Owens citing Goldman/Citi: the "average YouTube creator" earns ~$150 a year; a 2021 YouTube-commissioned study estimated 400,000 US people work 40+ hours/week producing for the platform — [Simon Owens, 26 Apr 2023](https://simonowens.substack.com/p/the-creator-economy-middle-class) (older)
- Li Jin, "1,000 True Fans? Try 100": argues 100 fans at ~$1,000/year replaces Kelly's 1,000 at $100; evidence is 2019–20 Patreon/Podia/Teachable examples (above) — [a16z, 6 Feb 2020](https://a16z.com/1000-true-fans-try-100/) (older)
- Secondary aggregator (low reliability): median annual creator earnings ≈$4,000; top-quartile above $16,000 (source key-g.com, international creator base, not Substack-specific) — [Best Writing, Oct 2026](https://bestwriting.com/substack-statistics)
- Patreon 2022 Creator Census: Patreon made up over 40% of surveyed creators' income on average — [Net Influencer, 2022](https://www.netinfluencer.com/2022-patreon-creator-census/)

### Inferences
- Combining Patreon's $110 per paying member per year with Kelly's 1,000-fan model gives ~$110,000/year gross — i.e. 1,000 *paying* Patreon members is roughly a six-figure business before the ~8–10% platform fee and taxes; but at ~0.7%/month free-to-paid conversion and 1–3% list conversion, reaching 1,000 paying fans implies an engaged free base of tens of thousands.
- No dataset gives the share of creators with ≥1,000 paying fans; the ~4% "professional" share (over $100K) is the closest proxy and has been stable in 2021 and 2023 estimates.

### Gaps
- No 2025–26 primary data on median creator earnings from Kit, Patreon or Substack; medians are either absent or from unreliable aggregators.
- Graphtreon's earnings-bracket distribution (share of Patreon creators above $100/$1,000 per month) was not retrievable in this session.

## Key Question 5: Trust and parasocial dynamics — why honesty works and the documented risks

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed work finds trustworthiness and expertise (not attractiveness) build parasocial relationships that in turn predict purchase intention, while qualitative studies of successful creators (Administrative Science Quarterly 2026; New Media & Society 2026) document "audience entanglement," unspeakable burnout and algorithm-driven fear of taking breaks; Patreon's survey finds 78% of creators say burnout affects their motivation.

### Cited Findings
**Why trust converts**
- Frontiers in Communication 2026 (407 Indonesian beauty-influencer followers, PLS-SEM): expertise and trustworthiness positively predict parasocial relationship (PSR); attractiveness does not predict PSR; PSR positively predicts purchase intention; persuasion knowledge has a small direct association with purchase intention; no effect sizes in abstract — [Hidayat et al., Frontiers in Communication, 2026](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2026.1723759/text)
- Patreon State of Create 2025 (fan side): 95% of fans confident they will see creators they follow on Patreon; 94% feel community with other fans there — [Net Influencer / Patreon, Feb 2025](https://www.netinfluencer.com/patreon-2025-state-of-create-report-how-creators-are-breaking-free-from-platform-control/)
- Linktree 2024: 63% of creators refuse brand deals for companies they don't believe in — framed as "persuasion currency" — [Passionfruit, Nov 2024](https://passionfru.it/linktree-creators-109807/)

**Audience capture / entanglement (academic)**
- Pillemer, Harrison, Murphy & Park, "Audience Entanglement: How Independent Creative Workers Experience the Pressures of Widespread Appeal on Digital Platforms," Administrative Science Quarterly (2026): inductive qualitative study, 54 successful creators (Instagram visual artists averaging 500K+ followers; YouTube musicians averaging ~280K subscribers), 74 interviews with follow-ups. Most creators initially experience "dysfunctional entanglement": hypersensitivity to comments/likes/views, metrics as main validation, distress from algorithm changes, doubts and desire to quit ("chamber of despair"). Three strategies move creators toward "functional entanglement": distancing from audience input (delaying reading comments, having someone filter messages), depersonalising critique, and distilling personal standards (anchoring in own creative goals). Relapse possible after platform changes or harassment. Limitations: self-report; two creator types only — [PsyPost, 20 Feb 2026](https://www.psypost.org/new-study-sheds-light-on-the-psychological-burden-of-having-a-massive-social-media-audience); [NYU Stern summary](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/experience-stern/faculty-research/research-highlights/when-your-audience-becomes-your-boss-invisible-management-creator-economy.md)
- Popular essay definition: audience capture "works in both directions simultaneously" — creators notice what earns a response and repeat it; audiences reward consistency and punish surprise; capture is complete when maintaining approval matters more than creative vision; money and status amplify but the root engine is the need for social validation — [Tom Asacker, The Age of Unknowing, 23 Feb 2026](https://ageofunknowing.substack.com/p/audience-capture) (essay, not research)

**Burnout (academic + survey)**
- Nguyen & Duffy, "'Creator Burnout is Real': Risk, Responsibility, and Un/speakability in Platform-Dependent Creative Labor," New Media & Society (7 Jul 2026): 58 creator-authored TikTok/YouTube pieces, 62 news accounts, 78 in-depth interviews. Burnout is normalised and often "unspeakable" because of the dream-job frame, gratitude toward audiences/platforms, fear of backlash, and the fear "If I take a break, I'm going to get punished by the algorithm." Men tended toward a "grinding" ethos; women toward self-care. Authors argue individualised responsibility obscures structural fixes — [Cornell Chronicle, 8 Jul 2026](https://news.cornell.edu/node/333256)
- Maddox, "Comfort Creators: Gender, Parasocial Relationships, and Emotional Labor" (PCA conference, Apr 2026): comfort creators perform emotional labour for audiences who feel closer than they are; the constant demand for comfort leads to burnout; sample size not given — [UGA Grady College, Jan 2026](https://grady.uga.edu/research/comfort-creators-gender-parasocial-relationships-and-emotional-labor/)
- Patreon State of Create 2025: 78% of creators say burnout impacts their motivation to create; 78% say algorithms impact what they create; 56% say algorithms prevent them exploring their creative interests; yet "almost 90%" would still recommend being a creator — [Net Influencer / Patreon](https://www.netinfluencer.com/patreon-2025-state-of-create-report-how-creators-are-breaking-free-from-platform-control/); [Patreon](https://news.patreon.com/articles/state-of-create)
- Kolsquare 2025: 65% of European creators feel stressed by their work (deadlines, content pressure) — [Kolsquare, Jul 2025](https://www.kolsquare.com/en-gb/free-resources/voices-of-the-creator-economy-by-kolsquare-newtonx)

### Inferences
- The retention value of honesty rests on trustworthiness → parasocial bond → purchase intention, which the 2026 SEM study supports; the same bond is the mechanism that produces "dysfunctional entanglement." The protective practices identified in the ASQ study (time-boxed comment reading, a filter person, anchoring in self-set standards) are the evidence-based guardrails to name in a report.
- No study found quantifies over-disclosure risk specifically; the comfort-creator and ASQ work treat it as emotional labour and boundary erosion rather than a measured variable.

### Gaps
- No quantitative burnout prevalence from a peer-reviewed sample; the 78% figure is a company-sponsored survey.
- No academic study found on vulnerability/self-disclosure as a driver of conversion specifically (only trustworthiness/expertise as credibility dimensions).
- No research found on audience capture in the athlete/fitness creator segment specifically.

## Key Question 6: Small apparel/sports brands run by athletes or creators — documented audience-driven sales

### Takeaway
Documented evidence is thin and qualitative: the two athlete/creator-run athleisure cases found (SET Active, Alpha/AC1) describe community co-design, teasers, order inserts and a ~67% returning-customer rate, but neither discloses follower-to-buyer conversion or revenue; Fourthwall's creator case studies are similarly non-quantitative.

### Cited Findings
- Alpha Clothing / AC1 (Kansas City, founder Ryan Tanner Mueller): ~125K Instagram followers; started with ~$3,600; returning-customer rate ~67% year over year; tactics: paying Instagram/YouTube creators to wear product, message card in every order, "taking care of customers" as core value; community grew from "having fun with the work," not a plan; revenue undisclosed — [Startland News, 27 Dec 2022](https://startlandnews.com/2022/12/alpha-ac1-2/)
- SET Active (Lindsey Carter, 2018 launch, ~500K Instagram followers by 2023, started with $20K credit-card debt): followers polled on colours, cuts and drop themes; behind-the-scenes development content; posted customer reviews during development; first pop-up in 2022; celebrity wearers; revenue undisclosed — [Shopify, 16 Mar 2023](https://www.shopify.com/blog/set-active-building-community)
- Fourthwall creator case studies: only "six figures in under six months" (Santagato Studios) and a qualitative first-weekend-beat-a-month claim (Snazzy Seagull); no sports/athlete apparel brand among listed cases — [Fourthwall case studies](https://fourthwall.com/case-studies)
- Aggregate merch benchmarks (low-reliability aggregator): 0.4–1.2% follower purchase conversion, $48.50 AOV, 42% POD gross margin — [VoxBooster, 2026](https://voxbooster.com/blog/merch-sales-creator-statistics-2026)
- Linktree 2024: ~20% of 1.3B monthly Linktree clicks go to retail/e-commerce — [Passionfruit, Nov 2024](https://passionfru.it/linktree-creators-109807/)

### Inferences
- The only hard number from an athlete-apparel brand is a repeat-purchase rate (~67%), which suggests that for a small apparel brand retention of *buyers* (not followers) is where the measurable leverage is; co-design polls and drop teasers are the recurring community tactics across both cases.
- Applying the aggregator merch benchmark to a small following (e.g. 5,000 engaged followers × 0.4–1.2% × $48) yields roughly $100–290 per drop — a sanity-check figure for expectation-setting, but it rests on unverifiable sourcing.

### Gaps
- No case study with disclosed follower count, buyer count and revenue for an athlete- or creator-run sports apparel brand. Candidate sources not fetched: Chicknlegs running-apparel founder podcast (Pocket Casts), Roots of Fight founder podcast, Business of Fashion "How athletes went from selling merch to building fashion brands" (likely large-athlete focused).
- No data specific to martial-arts or karate apparel brands.
