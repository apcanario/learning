# YouTube audience growth for a solo long-form (10–40 min) channel publishing ~every two weeks, as of 2026

Research date: 27 Oct 2026. Sources older than 2024 are flagged "(pre-2024)". Most quantitative material comes from three third-party data publishers (vidIQ Research, AIR Media-Tech, Metricool); all three are correlational, draw on their own tool users / managed channels rather than random samples, and say so. Official YouTube statements come from YouTube Help pages and interviews with Todd Beaupré (Senior Director of Growth & Discovery).

## Q1. How YouTube's recommendation surfaces work in 2025–2026 and which signals matter

### Takeaway
YouTube describes recommendation as a per-viewer "pull," ranked per session from watch history, context, time of day and device, with the stated objective being long-term viewer value measured by return visits and satisfaction surveys; no single metric (CTR, watch time) is treated as the goal, and signal weights vary by device and context. Subscriptions are a weak signal: Beaupré says subscribers skip ~90% of uploads, and suggested videos (not the subscriptions feed) are the biggest source of views from subscribed channels.

### Cited Findings
- Official (Creator Insider interview published 1 Sep 2026, reported 2 Sep 2026): Beaupré said "each viewer gets a different ranking"; rankings are assembled at session start from watch history, context, time of day and device; the system does not judge a video "just based on an overall aggregate opinion" but on how it does across audiences — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- Official: "probably all your videos have about maybe a 10% or less click-through rate" in the subscribers traffic source; "even your best videos, 90% of the time, your subscribed audience isn't deciding to watch"; subscribing "isn't as strong as a lot of people think" — [PPC Land, Sep 2026](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- Official: suggested videos are the largest source of views from subscribed channels; the subscriptions feed is "quite small by comparison" — [PPC Land, Sep 2026](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- Official: objective is long-term user value measured by return visits and whether viewers felt the platform delivered; "no metric on its own is a good indicator of value"; watch time is not assumed valuable in itself, and surveys separate time spent from satisfaction; signal weighting varies by person, over time and by device (time spent may matter more on TV, likes/dislikes more on phones) — [PPC Land, Sep 2026](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- Official: YouTube tested a cap of eight recommendations per video and found engagement sometimes came on later attempts; one viewer had missed recent uploads recommended 19 times — [PPC Land, Sep 2026](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- Official: longer videos grow because TV viewers want them, not because YouTube pushes them ("puddles don't cause rain"); the desktop homepage redesign ~2 years earlier increased long-form attention (no figures) — [PPC Land, Sep 2026](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- Official (Beaupré, interview reported Mar 2025): "We've enabled the system to learn that different factors can have different importance in different contexts"; watch time may weigh more on TV than mobile and in podcasts more than music; on CTR vs reach: "Would you as a Creator rather have a video that has a 20% CTR and 10,000 views or a 5% CTR and 100,000 views?" — [AdOutreach / Aleric Heck, 14 Mar 2025](https://adoutreach.beehiiv.com/p/how-youtube-s-algorithm-really-works-in-2025-straight-from-youtube-s-director-of-growth)
- Official (Beaupré with Rene Ritchie, Mar 2024): the discovery algorithm "is focused more on individual videos" not channel averages; "a lot of creators think of YouTube as pushing videos out to a bunch of people, but it's actually more the reverse"; no "penalty box" for breaks ("we aim to not overemphasize historical data if that data isn't particularly predictive"); one weak video does not permanently harm a channel; recommendations are not limited to recent videos and older videos can regain traction — [Search Engine Journal, 4 Mar 2024](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-myths-debunked-insights-from-the-growth-team/510091/)
- Official (YouTube Help): "YouTube recommends a video when it's relevant to a viewer and its average view duration suggests viewers find it interesting"; clickbait = high CTR + low AVD + lower-than-expected impressions, and is less likely to be recommended — [YouTube Help, Impressions and CTR FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en-GB)
- Third-party (vidIQ, updated 5 Jun 2026): describes Home (broad interests + recent viewing), Suggested (relation to current video), Search (relevance first, then satisfaction; small channels can outrank large), and Shorts (separate ranking model weighing swipe-vs-watch, replays, shares more than CTR); claims a layered rollout (core audience → recent viewers → topic matches → adjacent) with initial test audiences of "roughly 10–100 viewers" and expansion "7–14 days" after strong early signals — the article cites no YouTube source for the layer model, the 10–100 figure, or its "30% of trending topics come from small channels" claim — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-algorithm)
- Third-party (Metricool, 799,718 videos / 71,177 accounts, Jul 2026): share of organic views by discovery source across its sample — Shorts feed >61%, subscribers 11%, search 9%, related videos 5.77%, playlists 5.05%, direct channel visits ~3% (sample is brand/business-heavy and dominated by Shorts volume) — [Metricool YouTube Trends 2026](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)

### Inferences
- For a solo long-form creator, the practical signals to monitor are per-video: impressions CTR by traffic source and the retention curve, read against YouTube's stated relevance + AVD rule; subscriber count is not a distribution lever.
- Because ranking is per-viewer and per-video, a bi-weekly cadence is not penalised by a "channel average" mechanism; each upload is evaluated on its own.
- Everything about "test audiences of N viewers" and "layered rollouts" is creator folklore; YouTube has only confirmed that early impressions go to diverse audiences including non-viewers of the channel.

### Gaps
- No official document quantifies the relative weight of CTR vs AVD vs survey responses; YouTube explicitly says weights vary by context.
- Could not access YouTube's official "How YouTube recommends" help page directly (fetch blocked); the Beaupré interviews are the primary official source used.

## Q2. Realistic benchmarks for a new channel (time/video count to 1K and 10K subs, views per video, CTR and retention, first 24–48 h)

### Takeaway
Median long-form uploads from brand-new channels get ~180–270 views in 30 days; 1,000 subscribers is reached by ~41% of all channels with any subscribers but by only 0.2–1.9% of sub-1K channels that add fewer than 12 videos a year, versus ~31% of those adding 52+; and ~68% of channels that did hit 1K had uploaded more than 40 videos. YouTube's own CTR range is 2–10% for half of all channels; third-party retention guidance for a 15–30 min video is 30–45% average percentage viewed. Only anecdotal data exists on calendar time to 1K (5–26 months in a seven-channel sample).

### Cited Findings
**Views per video for a new channel**
- vidIQ First 10 Videos study (448,413 channels created Jan–Jun 2025 with ≥10 same-format uploads in first 90 days; views at 30 days; published 28 Jul 2026): median long-form views — 1st upload 180, 5th 211, 10th 272, best of first ten 1,948 (~10.8x the first); Shorts — 1,362 / 1,275 / 1,344 / best 8,832. Long-form median rose ~51% from 1st to 10th upload. 57.8% of long-form channels had at least one of their first ten videos reach 1,000+ views; 30.8% had one reach 10,000+. Per individual long-form video: 30.2% reached 1,000+, 10.0% reached 10,000+. Channels that quit before 10 uploads are excluded, so these are survivor numbers — [vidIQ Research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study/)
- Metricool (Jul 2026): global average 5,985 views per long-form video across its sample; channels under 2,000 subs saw long-form views +267% YoY but AVD down >58%; 2K–10K channels had the highest tier-up rate (16.01%) vs 9.59% for <2K, 7.01% for 10K–100K, 2.11% for 100K–1M; overall 10.08% of accounts moved up a tier in a year — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)

**How rare milestones are / growth rates**
- vidIQ Subscriber Benchmarks (61,186,246 channels with ≥1 subscriber, snapshot 20 Jul 2026): ≥1,000 subs 40.6%; ≥10,000 7.90%; ≥100,000 1.30%; ≥1M 0.13%. Median monthly growth over the 90 days to 20 Jul 2026 among 11.75M channels that added videos: 1–99 subs +0.5 subs/mo (1.26%/mo); 100–499 +1.4 (0.59%); 500–999 +3.7 (0.53%); 1K–9,999 +13 (0.51%); 10K–99,999 +109 (0.48%) — [vidIQ Research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-subscriber-benchmarks-2026/)
- vidIQ uploads-to-1K study (32,926,088 channels under 1,000 subs with ≥1 video, 360-day window to 10 Sep 2026): share reaching 1,000 subs by increase in video count — 0 videos added: 0.20%; 1–11: 1.86%; 12–51: 8.65%; 52+: 30.77%. Total 500,409 of 32.9M (1.5%) crossed 1K in the year. Formats not separated — [vidIQ Research, 11 Sep 2026](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-uploads-1000-subscribers/)
- vidIQ (5,330,249 vidIQ-user channels created within 3 years that reached 1K; 9 Jun 2025): videos uploaded at the moment of reaching 1K — 0–5: 5.77%; 6–9: 4.17%; 10–19: 8.69%; 20–39: 13.36%; 40–69: 14.54%; 70–149: 22.58%; 150+: 30.89%. ~10% did it with <10 videos; ~68% had 40+ — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/how-many-videos-to-get-1000-subscribers/)
- Anecdotal calendar time to 1K (7 channels, author's own and clients, undated): 5, 7, 18, 18, 22, 26 months (and one 9-vs-18 inconsistency); time to 10K where reached: 29, 31, 32, 36 months; to 20K: 35–48 months — [Social Video Playza / Dexxter Clark](https://www.socialvideoplaza.com/en/articles/how-long-does-it-take-to-get-1000-subscribers-on-youtube) (small, self-reported, undated; treat as illustrative only)

**CTR**
- Official: "Half of all channels and videos on YouTube have an impressions CTR that can range between 2% and 10%"; CTR varies by content type, audience and surface; high-impression placements (homepage) usually have lower CTR; videos/channels under a week old or with <100 views show a wider range; low-impression videos often have higher CTR because they reach a narrower, loyal audience; compare over the long term and wait for substantial impression volume — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en-GB)
- Third-party tiers (unsourced beyond the YouTube 2–10% figure; updated 8 Aug 2026): <2% needs work; 2–4% below average; 4–6% good; 6–10% very good; >10% exceptional. Claimed by traffic source: search 8–15%, suggested 5–10%, browse/home 2–6%; by channel size: <1K subs 6–12%, 1K–10K 5–10%; first 48 h often 8–12% falling to 3–4% on back catalogue; wait for ≥1,000–2,000 impressions before judging — [ytgrowth.io](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/youtube-ctr) (no named dataset; treat as practitioner heuristics)
- vidIQ (Jun 2026): calls CTR below 4% a sign the packaging needs work — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-algorithm)

**Retention / average percentage viewed (APV)**
- vidIQ guidance (21 Jul 2026), not YouTube-official: target APV under 5 min 50–70%; 5–15 min 40–55%; 15–30 min 30–45%; >30 min 25–35%. vidIQ's own channel: AVD 3:11 and 30.3% APV across 16.5M long-form views, 26 Jun 2025–25 Jun 2026 — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/average-view-duration/)
- AIR Media-Tech (22 Sep 2026): APV under 5 min commonly 50–70%, over 40 min tapers to 25–35%; YouTube "doesn't publish official retention benchmarks"; the "typical retention" line in Studio is built from the creator's own 10 most recent similar-length videos; a steep drop in the first 30 s suggests the intro does not deliver on the title/thumbnail — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-glossary/what-is-youtube-audience-retention-and-average-view-duration)
- AIR APV study (21,755 videos, linked): median APV 26% for 15–60 minute videos in the kids niche — [AIR Media-Tech video-length study](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/ideal-youtube-video-length-by-niche-data-from-18000-channels)
- Metricool (Jul 2026): average long-form AVD in its sample fell from 3.98 to 2.51 min YoY (−37%) — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)
- Pre-2024, business sample: Databox median AVD 1:31 across 84 companies (2023) — [Databox](https://databox.com/youtube-average-view-duration)

**First 24–48 hours**
- Official: early impressions reach diverse audiences including people who never watched the channel; some videos peak on day one, others gain most of their audience a month or more later; YouTube does not decide "based on those initial moments" alone — [PPC Land, Sep 2026](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/); older videos can regain traction — [SEJ, Mar 2024](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-myths-debunked-insights-from-the-growth-team/510091/)
- Metricool (Jul 2026): videos peak in the first three days and ~83% of interactions arrive within the first ten days (sample-wide average, Shorts-heavy) — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)

### Inferences
- A bi-weekly cadence (~26 long-form videos/year) sits in the "12–51 videos added" band where 8.65% of sub-1K channels crossed 1K in a year, versus 30.77% for 52+; a beginner should plan on 2–3 years and 40–70+ videos to reach 1K, with 10K typically another 1–2 years away in the anecdotal data.
- Views per video in the low hundreds for the first ten uploads is the median outcome, not a failure signal; one in three new long-form channels gets a 10K+ video among its first ten.
- For 10–40 min videos, 30–45% APV and 4–6% CTR are reasonable "healthy" targets; above 50% APV on a 20+ min video is strong.

### Gaps
- No large-sample study reports calendar time to 1,000 or 10,000 subscribers; vidIQ's studies give uploads-to-1K and monthly growth rates but not durations.
- No official YouTube retention benchmarks exist; APV ranges are tool-vendor heuristics.
- No dataset splits new-channel views by long-form video length.

## Q3. Does publishing cadence matter, and does video length matter for a small channel?

### Takeaway
Across 10.2M channels, median growth rises monotonically with uploads per month (12+/month ≈ 4.1x the view growth of <1/month, 2.45x the 1–3/month tier), and sub-1K channels that added 52+ videos a year crossed 1K at 3.6x the rate of those adding 12–51; Metricool finds 2–4 long-form posts a week most efficient per post. All of it is correlational. On length, YouTube says long-form growth is viewer-driven (TV), and AIR's engagement data shows 10–30 min videos performing well in fitness and business at small-channel scale.

### Cited Findings
**Cadence**
- vidIQ Upload Frequency Study (10,210,278 channels with ≥1K subs; Apr 2025–Mar 2026; published 8 Jul 2026): median monthly view growth / subscriber growth by uploads per month (Shorts + long-form combined) — <1: 0.53% / 0.00%; 1–3: 0.89% / 0.10%; 4–7: 1.32% / 0.39%; 8–11: 1.70% / 0.63%; 12+: 2.18% / 0.91%. Largest step is from occasional to repeatable publishing. Authors note correlation only and that larger channels may have more resources — [vidIQ Research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-upload-frequency-study/)
- vidIQ uploads-to-1K (32.9M sub-1K channels, 360 days to Sep 2026): share crossing 1K — 1–11 videos added 1.86%; 12–51 added 8.65%; 52+ added 30.77% — [vidIQ Research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-uploads-1000-subscribers/)
- Metricool (799,718 videos, Jul 2026): long-form views per post highest at 2–4 posts/week (10,552 views/post average); 4–7/week adds only ~6% monthly views; >7/week lowers monthly views; less than weekly gives the weakest monthly result — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)
- Official: no "penalty box" for taking breaks; the system avoids over-weighting historical data that isn't predictive — [SEJ, Mar 2024](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-myths-debunked-insights-from-the-growth-team/510091/)
- Paddy Galloway (via Passionfruit, Aug 2024): recommends two videos per week for the first ~16 weeks while experimenting, then one per week with equal effort; ~100 ideas brainstormed per week; 4 h/week studying YouTube — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/paddy-galloways-how-to-grow-youtube-71562/) (opinion, not data)
- vidIQ (Jun 2026) lists "post 1–2 times per week" under myths and says consistency in topic matters more than frequency — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-algorithm)

**Video length**
- Official: "puddles don't cause rain" — longer videos grow because TV viewers want them, not because YouTube favours length — [PPC Land, Sep 2026](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- AIR video-length study (18,080 channels, 4,538,463 videos ≥180 s, 2025–Q1 2026; published 8 Jun 2026): engagement rate ((likes+comments)/views) at the 100K–1M monthly-views tier — Fitness 10–20 min 7.50%, 20–30 min 7.32%; Business/Finance 10–20 min 7.40%, 1–2 h 10.79%; Tech 10–20 min 4.00%, 20–30 min 4.18%; Entertainment 10–20 4.37%, 20–30 4.63%, 30–40 4.75%; Beauty 10–20 7.87% falling to 4.90% at 30–40 min. No education niche in the study. DIY case: lengthening from 20–25 to 25–30+ min cut views 9% but raised revenue 20% and watch hours 7.6% — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/ideal-youtube-video-length-by-niche-data-from-18000-channels)
- Official: watch time is weighted more heavily on TV than mobile — [AdOutreach, Mar 2025](https://adoutreach.beehiiv.com/p/how-youtube-s-algorithm-really-works-in-2025-straight-from-youtube-s-director-of-growth)

### Inferences
- The data consistently favours more uploads, but the marginal gain flattens above ~weekly for long-form; bi-weekly is viable but should be expected to roughly halve the growth rate versus weekly (1–3/month tier vs 4–7/month tier in vidIQ data).
- Nothing in the data penalises 10–40 min videos; engagement rates at small-channel scale are flat-to-higher from 10 to 30 min in fitness, tech and business. The real constraint is retention: a 30-min video needs ~30–45% APV to be healthy.
- The "fewer, better" strategy (Galloway's weekly-with-full-effort) and the raw-volume data are not contradictory: the volume studies cannot separate quality from quantity.

### Gaps
- No study isolates weekly vs bi-weekly long-form cadence for channels under 1,000 subscribers; vidIQ's frequency study starts at 1K subs and mixes Shorts.
- No causal (experimental) evidence on cadence exists; all sources are correlational.

## Q4. Titles/thumbnails vs content, with data

### Takeaway
YouTube's own rule is that recommendation needs both a click (CTR) and satisfying watch (AVD): clickbait with high CTR and low AVD is demoted. Quantitative evidence on thumbnails is thin: vidIQ's 500-breakout-thumbnail study finds faces in 69% of breakouts rising to 80% among top overperformers; A/B case studies report CTR lifts of ~2 percentage points; YouTube's Test & Compare picks winners on watch-time share, not CTR.

### Cited Findings
- Official: a video is recommended when relevant and its AVD shows interest; clickbait = high CTR, low AVD, lower-than-expected impressions → less likely to be recommended — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en-GB)
- Official: Beaupré's framing that 5% CTR at 100,000 views beats 20% CTR at 10,000 — [AdOutreach, Mar 2025](https://adoutreach.beehiiv.com/p/how-youtube-s-algorithm-really-works-in-2025-straight-from-youtube-s-director-of-growth)
- vidIQ Thumbnail Study (500 breakout thumbnails, Jun 2026): face usage 69% overall, 80% among top overperformers; high contrast flat at ~56% — [vidIQ Research index](https://www.vidiq.com/research)
- vidIQ Title Study (>4M 2026 uploads, Jul 2026): "caught-on-camera" titles averaged >4x and "transformation" titles >3x (baseline multiple not defined on index page) — [vidIQ Research index](https://www.vidiq.com/research)
- Test & Compare (YouTube's native thumbnail A/B tool, launched 2023–24, expanding 2024–25): declares a winner only with "strong statistical evidence," otherwise "inconclusive"; case studies — Nick Nimmin +2 pp CTR from a simpler high-contrast thumbnail on an older video; JackSucksAtLife "978% more views" after a thumbnail swap (TikTok anecdote, not a CTR figure); MrBeast makes 20–30 variations per video; a 0.5 pp CTR difference can be significant at millions of impressions — [Influencer Marketing Hub, updated 19 Aug 2026](https://influencermarketinghub.com/youtube-test-compare/)
- Galloway recommends two thumbnails per video and A/B testing (TubeBuddy) — [Passionfruit, Aug 2024](https://passionfru.it/paddy-galloways-how-to-grow-youtube-71562/)
- vidIQ heuristics (Jun 2026): keyword within first 40 title characters, 2–4 words of thumbnail text, intro under 10 s, pattern breaks every 20–30 s; revisit packaging if CTR <3% after 48 h — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-algorithm) (unsourced)

### Inferences
- Packaging decides whether a video gets tested; content decides whether the test expands. For a small channel the measurable lever is CTR on browse/suggested impressions, but any CTR gain that lowers AVD is net negative per YouTube's own rule.
- Expected gains from thumbnail iteration are on the order of 1–3 percentage points of CTR, which at small impression volumes is hard to detect — hence the 1,000–2,000-impression minimum before judging.

### Gaps
- No large-sample controlled study quantifies the share of view variance explained by thumbnails versus content.
- Test & Compare eligibility criteria and aggregate lift statistics are not published by YouTube.

## Q5. Do Shorts grow long-form subscribers or create a separate audience?

### Takeaway
Evidence is mixed and niche-dependent: AIR's 18,000-channel analysis finds a 25–40% Shorts mix correlates with more subscribers in fitness (+41%), business (+39%) and education (+37% at small scale) but fewer in gaming, beauty and entertainment; Metricool finds ~5 Shorts/month correlates with 46%-above-average long-form views while 35+/month erases the benefit. Claims that Shorts and long-form audiences overlap only ~10% are unsourced.

### Cited Findings
- AIR Media-Tech (18,000 English channels with ≥40 videos, Mar 2025–Mar 2026; updated 28 May 2026): median subscribers, Long-Only vs Long+Short Mix at 100K–1M monthly views — Fitness 84,900 vs 120,000; Education 82,200 vs 113,000; Business 76,350 vs 106,000; Gaming 70,100 vs 37,850; Beauty 251,500 vs 173,000; Entertainment 73,600 vs 72,400. Optimal Shorts share 25–40% of uploads (fitness ~37%, education ~38%); long-form performance drops above ~55% Shorts. Channels that shifted strategy >30 points mid-year had lower medians. Shorts-to-long-form conversion cited as moving "from 0.04% to 0.5%" without methodology — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)
- Metricool (Jul 2026): channels posting no Shorts averaged 4,364 views per long-form video (below the 5,985 average); ~5 Shorts/month → 8,789 (+46%); 35+ Shorts/month → back to roughly the no-Shorts level; correlation only — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)
- Metricool (Jul 2026): Shorts feed accounts for >61% of organic views in its sample; Shorts AVD ~16 s — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)
- vidIQ First 10 Videos (Jul 2026): new-channel Shorts get ~7x the median 30-day views of new-channel long-form (1,362 vs 180) but views, not subscribers, were measured — [vidIQ Research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study/)
- vidIQ (Jun 2026) states Shorts uses a separate ranking model from long-form (attributes a 2025 YouTube confirmation but gives no link) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-algorithm)
- Unsourced practitioner claims: Shorts/long-form viewer overlap "as low as around 10%"; Shorts "related video" link converts "sometimes under 1%"; pinned-comment links 1–3% — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-hurting-long-form-views) (no sources, no dates; unverified)
- Official (Mar 2024): "YouTube is focused on Shorts because audiences have let us there" — Beaupré — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-myths-debunked-insights-from-the-growth-team/510091/)
- Shorts views count only toward the Shorts YPP path (3M/90 d early access; 10M/90 d full), not toward long-form watch hours — [AIR Media-Tech YPP guide, 18 Sep 2026](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide)

### Inferences
- For a fitness/sport or education/self-improvement channel the correlational data leans toward a modest Shorts mix (roughly 1 Short per long-form video, i.e. ~25–40% of uploads) as a net positive for subscriber count, with diminishing or negative returns past ~55% Shorts or ~35 Shorts/month.
- No source demonstrates that Shorts subscribers watch long-form; the subscriber gains may reflect a parallel audience. Treat Shorts as a top-of-funnel bet, not a substitute for long-form distribution.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube statement or dataset on the share of Shorts-acquired subscribers who later watch long-form from the same channel.
- No small-channel (sub-100K monthly views) data on the Shorts/long-form relationship; AIR's smallest tier starts at 100K monthly views.

## Q6. YouTube Partner Program requirements in 2026 and realistic ad revenue for a small channel

### Takeaway
Two tiers: 500 subs + 3 uploads/90 d + (3,000 watch hours/12 mo or 3M Shorts views/90 d) unlocks fan funding only; 1,000 subs + (4,000 watch hours/12 mo or 10M Shorts views/90 d) unlocks ads, rising on 1 Feb 2027 to 8,000 hours / 20M Shorts views. Median RPM from 300 managed channels (May 2025–May 2026) is $1.23 in Health & Sport and $10.22 in Education & Science; a small fitness channel needs ~800K views for $1,000.

### Cited Findings
**YPP (AIR guide citing YouTube Help pages, 18 Sep 2026)**
- Tier 1 (early access / fan funding): 500 subscribers; 3 valid public uploads in 90 days; 3,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months OR 3 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days; unlocks memberships, Super Chat/Stickers/Thanks, Jewels and gifts, Shopping, Creator Partnerships; no ad revenue; 99 countries — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide)
- Tier 2 (full): 1,000 subscribers; 4,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months OR 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days; adds ad revenue and YouTube Premium share; 138 countries — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide)
- Both tiers: monetization-policy compliance, eligible country, no active Community Guidelines strikes (expire after 90 days), 2-Step Verification, advanced features access in Studio, linked AdSense for YouTube account — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide)
- From 1 Feb 2027: Tier 2 long-form threshold rises to 8,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days and Shorts to 20 million views in 90 days; Tier 1 unchanged; existing partners keep status but must accept updated terms by 31 Jan 2027 — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide) (could not fetch the YouTube Help page directly to confirm; AIR cites it)

## Q7. Common documented reasons small channels stall

### Takeaway
The data points to three documented patterns: too few uploads (0.2–1.9% of low-volume channels cross 1K in a year), packaging that fails YouTube's CTR-plus-AVD test, and topic/format drift (channels that shifted strategy sharply mid-year had lower medians). Official statements rule out "penalty boxes" and single-bad-video damage as causes.

### Cited Findings
- Volume: 83% of sub-1K channels in vidIQ's index added zero videos in 360 days (27.36M of 32.93M) and 0.20% of them reached 1K; 1–11 added → 1.86%; 12–51 → 8.65%; 52+ → 30.77% — [vidIQ Research, Sep 2026](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-uploads-1000-subscribers/)
- Survivorship: vidIQ's First 10 Videos study excludes channels that did not post 10 same-format videos in 90 days; those that did had a median 30-day long-form view count of 180–272 — [vidIQ Research, Jul 2026](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study/)
- Clickbait/mismatch: high CTR + low AVD + lower-than-expected impressions is YouTube's stated clickbait signature and reduces recommendation — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en-GB)
- Strategy drift: channels that shifted Shorts/long-form mix by >30 points mid-year had lower median subscribers across niches (e.g. education 1M–10M tier 56,350 vs 354,000–395,000 for stable channels) — [AIR Media-Tech, May 2026](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)
- Subscriber dependence: "90% of the time, your subscribed audience isn't deciding to watch" — relying on subscribers as distribution is a stated misconception — [PPC Land, Sep 2026](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- Not causes (official): no penalty for breaks; no channel-average effect; a bad video does not permanently harm the channel; "it's sometimes hard to understand why some things are successful when similar things are not" — [SEJ, Mar 2024](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-myths-debunked-insights-from-the-growth-team/510091/)
- Retention collapse in the smallest tier: channels under 2,000 subs saw AVD drop >58% YoY with the steepest engagement fall of any tier (Metricool, Jul 2026) — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)
- Galloway (Aug 2024): "Don't distract your audience by trying too many different angles"; review top-5 and bottom-5 videos weekly; most important thing is "starting and staying with it" — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/paddy-galloways-how-to-grow-youtube-71562/)
- Social Video Playza anecdotes: milestones took 5–26 months to 1K; the author attributes variance to ideas, thumbnails, upload frequency, niche and evergreen content without quantifying — [Social Video Playza](https://www.socialvideoplaza.com/en/articles/how-long-does-it-take-to-get-1000-subscribers-on-youtube)

### Inferences
- The dominant documented stall cause is simply stopping: the overwhelming majority of sub-1K channels publish nothing in a given year. A bi-weekly cadence sustained for 2–3 years already puts a channel in the top ~17% of sub-1K channels by volume.
- The second is misreading early numbers: low-hundreds views per video and single-digit subscriber gains per month are the median for the first year, and YouTube says videos can grow months later, so quitting on first-week data is premature.

### Gaps
- No survey-based study of why creators quit (motivational, financial, burnout) with quantified reasons was found in the 2024–2026 window.
- No data on how long after launch channels typically go dormant.
