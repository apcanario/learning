# Platform strategy for a one-person creator with 5–7 h/week (as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: I prioritised primary data (platform reports, large-sample studies, first-person creator write-ups with numbers). Pre-2024 items are flagged **[pre-2024]**. Several vendor sources (Ahrefs, beehiiv, Buffer, Metricool, AIR Media-Tech, Dataslayer) have commercial interest; flagged where relevant. The creator-economy "analyst" sources named in the brief (Colin & Samir, Jay Clouse, Nathan Barry, Dan Koe, Kit's State of the Creator Economy) mostly publish their platform advice in podcasts/paywalled or promo pages; the fetchable pages contained little quotable, data-backed advice — see Gaps.

## KQ1. One platform first vs omnichannel — what do analysts and data say, and what does cross-posting really cost/return?

### Takeaway
No rigorous study directly compares "one platform first" against "omnichannel" for solo creators; the best large-sample data (Buffer 52M posts, Metricool 674k LinkedIn posts, AIR 18k YouTube channels) all point the same way: consistency on a platform beats breadth, format performance is platform-specific, and adding a secondary *format* on the *same* platform (YouTube long + Shorts at a 25–40% mix) correlates with more subscribers in fitness/education/business niches — while mid-stream strategy switching correlates with the worst outcomes. Diversification data from creator surveys is about *income-stream* risk, not about posting everywhere.

### Cited Findings
- Buffer analysed 52M+ posts across 10 platforms (Buffer users only; data through 3 Dec 2025). Top-10% accounts post more often *and consistently*; accounts that skipped a week fell below their own baseline follower growth ("no-post penalty"); posting once or twice a week was meaningfully better than not posting; reach per post declines at higher frequencies; no single optimal cadence identified. Format performance varies by platform, so "content that works on one network may not work on another" — [Buffer, State of Social Media Engagement 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/)
- Buffer 2025 median engagement rates by platform: LinkedIn ~6.2%, Facebook ~5.6%, Instagram ~5.5%, TikTok ~4.6%, Threads ~3.6%, X ~2.5%; YouTube Shorts median views rose from ~86 (2024) to ~268 (2025). Buffer warns platforms define engagement differently — [Buffer 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/)
- AIR Media-Tech study of 18,000 English-language YouTube channels (Mar 2025–Mar 2026; ≥40 videos, ≥10 long-form; median subscribers as outcome): a 25–40% Shorts share of uploads was the best range; above 55% Shorts long-form performance weakened. "Mix" channels had more median subscribers in Fitness (~+41%, but 10M–50M tier based on 15 channels), Business (~+39%), Education (~+37% at small size, ~+12% mid-size). Long-only won in Gaming (~+85%), Beauty (~+45–52%), Entertainment. **"Transitional" channels that changed strategy mid-year had the lowest medians** (e.g. Entertainment 100K–1M: 51,600 vs 72,400 Mix vs 73,600 Long-only). Source states correlation not causation, quality not controlled, promotes its audit service — [AIR Media-Tech, 28 May 2026](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)
- YouTube Dec 2025 homepage change: long-form recommendations reportedly fell from ~12 slots to 2 (observation by Mario Joos, not confirmed by YouTube). Alphabet stated 29 Oct 2025 that Shorts reached revenue parity with long-form per watch-hour in the US. Dataslayer's other figures ("mixed-format channels grew 40–60% faster", "organic long-form discovery down 30–50%") are unsourced — treat as unverified — [Dataslayer, Dec 2025](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/youtubes-december-2025-algorithm-update-browse-feed-cut-long-videos-by-80)
- Creator-survey diversification stats (aggregated by Archive, 16 Feb 2026; underlying sources undated): 65% of creators fear a platform ban would hurt income; 49% increased presence on alternative platforms; 67% make platform-specific content for multiple channels; 27% of creators rate email as their best engagement channel vs Instagram 15%, LinkedIn 8%, YouTube 8% (InBeat) — [Archive.com](https://archive.com/blog/creator-diversification-strategy-statistics)
- Metricool LinkedIn 2026 study (673,658 posts, 63,108 accounts, published 16 Apr 2026): posts per week fell ~10% while clicks per post rose 4.9% — fewer, better posts held up — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/linkedin-trends/)
- Amy Suto (42k Substack subs, 3 years): "publishing more often than sustainable" caused her only flat months; recommends choosing a cadence you can hit "on your worst week, not your best one" — [Amy Suto, 2026](https://www.amysuto.com/desk-of-amy-suto/how-to-grow-a-substack)
- Cross-posting effectiveness (positive case): The Introverted Networker (full-time job, family) says the summer-2023 growth streak came from repurposing newsletter issues into LinkedIn carousels; LinkedIn was "the best source" of newsletter subscribers; Substack discovery helped only during active periods — [Introverted Networker, 28 May 2025](https://theintrovertednetworker.substack.com/p/how-i-grew-my-newsletter-to-2500)
- Justin Welsh's repurposing loop: reviews his own best-performing social posts (LinkedIn/X) each Tuesday as the seed of the weekly newsletter; claims 45 min per issue vs 2+ h before, plus 15 min/day idea capture — [Justin Welsh, 6 Apr 2024](https://justinwelsh.me/newsletter/my-secret-to-writing-a-great-newsletter-in-45-minutes)

### Inferences
- With 5–7 h/week, the data supports one "home" platform posted consistently (the no-post penalty is real at Buffer scale), plus a second *format* that is cheap to derive (Shorts from long-form on YouTube; carousels/text from newsletter on LinkedIn). Adding a second *platform* with its own native format is a different cost class.
- Switching strategy mid-stream is the documented loser (AIR "transitional" cohort, Suto's overcommit → flat months). Pick, hold for ~12 months, then review.
- The "67% make platform-specific content" stat implies raw cross-posting is uncommon among surveyed (mostly professional) creators — repurposing means re-cutting, not re-uploading.

### Gaps
- No controlled study on raw cross-posting (same clip to TikTok/Reels/Shorts) vs native-per-platform in 2025–26; Buffer explicitly did not study it. I found no official 2025–26 Instagram/TikTok statement on watermark penalties (the Lindsey Gamble piece only covers Mosseri on paid-partnership labels, Nov 2024).
- Colin & Samir / Jay Clouse / Nathan Barry / Dan Koe: their "one platform" positions are in podcasts (Tropical MBA ep. 19 Dec 2025 lists "channel viability" as a topic, no transcript fetched; Ali Abdaal's Colin & Samir episode 8 Oct 2023 page has no advice text). Nothing quotable with numbers was retrievable.
- Kit's State of the Creator Economy: only the 2024 edition's promo page surfaced (1,000 creators; no platform-count or hours figures visible). No 2025/2026 edition found in search.

## KQ2. Owned audience: newsletter benchmarks, small-writer growth, discovery in 2025–26, start before 1,000 followers?

### Takeaway
2025 platform data says newsletters open at ~41–44% (beehiiv/Substack), half of Substack's new free subscriptions come from in-network discovery (recommendations + app), and beehiiv reports a median 482 subscribers in month one and 8,314 at end of year one for 2025 launches — but that median is from a self-selected platform cohort and is contradicted by first-person accounts (100 subs after 4 months; 2,500 after 3 years part-time). The case for starting the list before 1,000 followers rests on on-platform discovery existing now (Substack recommendations, beehiiv Recommendations 2.75x faster growth), not on external social reach.

### Cited Findings
- beehiiv State of Newsletters 2026 (published 6 Jan 2026; 65,000+ newsletters, 28B emails in 2025): avg open rate 41.24% (2024: ~37.7–38%); avg CTR 3.23% (down from 4.74% in 2024); median subscribers first month 482; median by end of year one 8,314; newsletters using Recommendations grew 2.75x faster; Boosts add avg 84 subs/month; median time to first dollar for 2025 launches 66 days; 46.7% of newsletters send weekly, 27% monthly, 1.4% daily; paid subscriptions on beehiiv $19M in 2025 (+138%). Internal inconsistencies noted (two 2024 open-rate figures) — [beehiiv](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/beehiiv-the-state-of-newsletters-2026)
- Substack official (22 Feb 2024): recommendations together with the Substack app drive 50% of all new subscriptions and 25% of new paid subscriptions; 3M+ paid subscriptions network-wide — [On Substack](https://on.substack.com/p/substacks-recommendations-network). Earlier (Jun 2022, **[pre-2024]**): "Substack generates 1 in 3 new subscriptions"; commenters self-reported 12%–59% of their subs from the network — [On Substack](https://on.substack.com/i/57659198/why-is-this-a-big-deal)
- Late 2025: Substack moved recommendations to "sequential modelling" (head of data Mike Cohen); recommendations still ~50% of new free subs; on-platform arrivals convert 3x faster; the platform now prioritises on-platform behaviours (Notes, restacks, comments). Secondary source citing Substack; no primary link — [Let's Data Science, 17 Mar 2026](https://letsdatascience.com/news/substack-rewards-ecosystem-activity-with-discovery-8fcfd144)
- Substack scale (secondary aggregator, mixed dating): ~50M active subscriptions (Apr 2026, counts duplicates); ~5M paid (2025) vs ~2M (2024); ~100k paying publications (Apr 2026) vs 50k (May 2025); typical Substack open rate ~44%; median creator earnings ~$4,000/yr, top quartile >$16,000 (undated) — [bestwriting.com](https://bestwriting.com/substack-statistics)
- Substack Notes, 6-month all-in experiment (Amy Suto, 42k subs, published 14 Jul 2026): Notes drove 1,459 free subs in the window vs 8,366 from onboarding/recommendations; a 15,800-like viral Note yielded 378 free and 0 paid subs; she now posts daily, mostly resharing, ~20% of effort on Notes, ~15 min/day — [Amy Suto](https://www.amysuto.com/desk-of-amy-suto/substack-notes); [Suto, how-to-grow](https://www.amysuto.com/desk-of-amy-suto/how-to-grow-a-substack)
- Small-writer reality checks: The Creator Playbook — launched ~Jul 2024, 100 subs by ~Oct 2024, 2,000 by late Jun 2025; >80% of growth in the final 6 months; failures: no niche, inconsistent cadence (5 posts one week, 0 the next), no offer for months, burnout from posting+selling+emailing+freelancing — [Creator Playbook, 30 Jul 2025](https://thecreatorplaybook.substack.com/p/celebrating-1-year-on-substack). The Introverted Networker — ~2,500 subs after 3 years, weekly Saturday 06:00, full-time job + family; LinkedIn daily posting was the best source; creator shout-outs gave the biggest single-day spikes; LinkedIn verification (summer 2024) boosted reach — [Introverted Networker, May 2025](https://theintrovertednetworker.substack.com/p/how-i-grew-my-newsletter-to-2500)
- Counter-case with an existing audience: Justin Welsh (738k LinkedIn followers) joined Substack 4 May 2026 and hit 8,300 free + 400 paid in 7 days with 1 post and 11 Notes (~$72k annualised); a commenter attributes it to the pre-existing audience — [Finntropy, 2026](https://finntropy.substack.com/p/from-zero-to-8300-subscribers-in)
- Ali Abouelatta (First 1000): full-time job at Duolingo + master's; 1,000 subs in 79 days; ~55k by start of year three; best channel was tweets from well-known accounts, then Hacker News, Product Hunt, cold email — [Hustlers, 27 Jan 2023 **[pre-2024]**](https://hustlers.beehiiv.com/p/ali-abouelatta-newsletter-growth)
- Emma Gannon (full-time Substack writer): thousands of paying subs after 2 years; recommendations brought "thousands"; stopped posting on Instagram and growth continued — [The Hyphen, 10 Mar 2024](https://thehyphen.substack.com/p/two-magical-years)

### Inferences
- beehiiv's 8,314-at-year-one median is almost certainly survivorship-biased (only launches that kept sending; a platform with paid growth tools). First-person solo accounts cluster at 100–2,500 in year one. Plan on the lower range.
- A newsletter started before 1,000 social followers is defensible in 2026 *only if* it lives on a network with built-in discovery (Substack recommendations/Notes or beehiiv Recommendations) and is sent on a fixed weekly cadence; a Kit/standalone list without social reach has no discovery engine.
- Notes/short-form social on Substack converts poorly to paid (378 free, 0 paid from a viral Note) — fine for free growth, not a monetisation lever.

### Gaps
- No Kit/ConvertKit 2025–26 open-rate or growth benchmark for small lists found (Kit's email-statistics page surfaced only a 2024 edition).
- No official Substack 2025/2026 growth report with percentage split for Notes specifically; the 50% figure bundles recommendations + app.

## KQ3. Personal website/blog and SEO in 2026 — discovery channel or archive?

### Takeaway
Ahrefs' Dec 2023→Dec 2025 comparison (300k keywords) shows position-1 CTR down 58% where AI Overviews appear, with informational queries hit hardest; corroborating studies report 47–65% declines. For a solo creator, written long-form on a personal site is now primarily an archive/credibility layer and a feed for repurposing, with slow evergreen trickle — not a primary discovery channel.

### Cited Findings
- Ahrefs (4 Feb 2026; 300,000 keywords, 150k with AI Overview vs 150k informational without; GSC desktop CTR, Dec 2023 vs Dec 2025): position-1 CTR −58% attributable to AIO (prior Apr 2025 study: −34.5%); position 2 −50.8%, position 5 −32.6%, position 10 −19.4%. Raw position-1 CTR for AIO keywords fell from 7.3% to 1.6%; informational keywords without AIO also fell 7.6%→3.9%. Corroboration cited: Seer Interactive −49.4% to −65.2%, Authoritas −47.5%, Kevin Indig >−50% — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update); press release 18 May 2026 — [Business Wire via Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20260518322756/new-research-googles-ai-overviews-now-cost-websites-58-of-their-clicks)
- Amy Suto: evergreen posts on her own site "bring subscribers every single day for years" (unquantified) and act as "doors" to the Substack — [Suto, 2026](https://www.amysuto.com/desk-of-amy-suto/how-to-grow-a-substack)
- 88% of (surveyed) creators maintain their own websites (Archive aggregate, undated) — [Archive.com](https://archive.com/blog/creator-diversification-strategy-statistics)

### Inferences
- Even informational queries *without* AIO lost ~half their position-1 CTR in two years, so "write SEO blog posts to be discovered" is a weak bet for a 5–7 h/week creator in English in 2026. The site's job becomes: canonical home, email capture, long-form archive that short-form points to. Portuguese-language SEO may be less affected (AIO rollout and competition differ) but I found no data.

### Gaps
- No 2025–26 Semrush/Similarweb figure on blog/creator-site traffic specifically (searches returned agency summaries, not the primary reports). No data on Portuguese-language AIO impact.

## KQ4. LinkedIn as a creator platform for a senior corporate professional (2025–26)

### Takeaway
LinkedIn is the highest-median-engagement network in Buffer's 2025 data and personal profiles out-engage company pages (Metricool 2.60% vs 1.60%); personal storytelling and leadership reflection outperform company news, so a personal (non-product) voice can live there. The employer risk is governance, not reach: check policy, keep it off work hours, avoid contentious stances, and expect that content "adjacent" to the day job is the norm.

### Cited Findings
- Metricool (673,658 posts, 63,108 accounts, 16 Apr 2026): overall engagement 12.21% (2025) → 13.90% (2026); personal profiles 2.60% vs company pages 1.60% engagement; carousels avg 1,451 impressions/post vs video 606; personal-profile posts with links get 27% fewer impressions; personal profiles post 3.05x/week vs 2.34 — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/linkedin-trends/)
- Buffer 2025 median engagement: LinkedIn ~6.2%, highest of 10 platforms (Buffer users only; metric definitions differ) — [Buffer 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/)
- Executive-post analysis (8,000 posts by C-level/founders, 37M views; vendor-published, methodology undisclosed, 6 Sep 2025): posting 2–3x/week yields 57% more engagement per post than weekly; weekly posters "unseen ~85% of the time"; executive-written posts earn 2x the engagement of reshares; educational content +42% vs promotional; personal storytelling and leadership reflections dominated; company announcements performed poorly; polished TikTok-style video underperforms — [Executive Presence newsletter](https://executivepresence.beehiiv.com/p/ep-064-the-data-that-s-changing-how-smart-executives-approach-linkedin)
- The Introverted Networker (corporate job): ~daily LinkedIn posts for a year before launching the newsletter; LinkedIn remained the best subscriber source over 3 years — [Introverted Networker, May 2025](https://theintrovertednetworker.substack.com/p/how-i-grew-my-newsletter-to-2500)
- Employer risk (Globe and Mail, undated, expert interviews): employers may want staff to represent only the corporate brand; political/social stances clash with corporate neutrality; managers worry about posting on company time; practical rules — read policy first, talk to manager if none, follow colleagues' norms, schedule posts outside work hours, use a separate name/no employer mention if full separation is wanted, go through conflict-of-interest process if contract requires; "side projects tend to be more acceptable to employers than strong personal stances" — [Globe and Mail](https://arc-dev.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-building-a-personal-brand-even-if-your-employer-doesnt-want-you-to)

### Inferences
- For a Senior Director at a PE-backed firm, a LinkedIn voice built on leadership reflection, learning-in-public and athlete discipline is within what the data says performs; product/competitor commentary and politics are the risk zones. Links in personal posts cost reach (−27%), so the newsletter CTA belongs in comments or profile, not the post body.
- 2–3 posts/week is the engagement sweet spot cited; at ~20–30 min per text/carousel post that is ~1–1.5 h/week — fits inside a 5–7 h budget as the secondary channel.

### Gaps
- No LinkedIn-published 2025–26 reach data; all figures are third-party tool datasets. No documented case of a senior corporate leader disciplined for a *non-political* personal brand; the risk literature is about politics and company-time.

## KQ5. Case studies with numbers (fitness/running/martial arts, learning-in-public, build-in-public, ADHD/mental health), 0→10k–100k in 12–36 months, plus failures

### Takeaway
Documented cases with real cadence/hours are thin; the recurring pattern across the ones found is one home platform held for 12–36 months, weekly minimum cadence, and growth concentrated in the back half. The documented stalls/failures all share the same causes: inconsistent cadence, no niche, overcommitting, and burnout from doing everything at once.

### Cited Findings
**Grew**
- Jacob Alava (fitness, Philippines): posting on YouTube from mid-2019 while a stockbroker; 100k subs "a little over a year" later; 222k by Feb 2022; editing after work, cadence not stated — [Spin.ph, 13 Feb 2022 **[pre-2024]**](https://www.spin.ph/life/active-lifestyle/jacob-alava-from-banker-to-fitness-youtuber-a1373-20220213)
- Ali Abdaal (doctor, learning-in-public/productivity): started YouTube 2017 while working 50–60 h/week in hospital + 2 h daily commute; ~18 months to 100k subs; hours per week not stated — [Ali Abdaal academy page](https://academy.aliabdaal.com/about)
- Ali Abouelatta (First 1000, build/startup learning-in-public newsletter): full-time job + master's; 1,000 subs in 79 days (2020), ~55k entering year 3; growth from Twitter amplification by big accounts, HN, Product Hunt, cold email — [Hustlers, Jan 2023 **[pre-2024]**](https://hustlers.beehiiv.com/p/ali-abouelatta-newsletter-growth)
- Katie Xu (creator-advice niche, TikTok): 70–80k TikTok followers; raw low-effort talking videos beat polished ones; one platform (TikTok) + link-in-bio; took a gap year — [Buffer, 20 Apr 2023 **[pre-2024]**](https://buffer.com/resources/creators-unlocked-katie-xu/)
- Amy Suto (writing/creative-career): 42k Substack subs in 3 years; first 1,000 "the slowest"; most growth from a handful of forwarded posts + recommendations; weekly cadence — [Suto, 2026](https://www.amysuto.com/desk-of-amy-suto/how-to-grow-a-substack)
- Introverted Networker (corporate + family): 2,500 subs in 3 years; LinkedIn daily → weekly newsletter — [May 2025](https://theintrovertednetworker.substack.com/p/how-i-grew-my-newsletter-to-2500)

**Stalled / quit**
- The Creator Playbook (Substack): 100 subs after 4 months, then 2,000 at 12 months; author names the failures — no niche ("writing to see what sticks"), cadence swinging 5→0 posts/week, no offer for months, burnout from posting+selling+freelancing simultaneously — [30 Jul 2025](https://thecreatorplaybook.substack.com/p/celebrating-1-year-on-substack)
- "GYT" YouTube channel (anonymous, Substack "week 100", 2025): reached YPP in 2024 by focusing solely on YouTube; burned out in 2025, editor quit, savings low, slowing release schedule — [rahrah.substack.com](https://rahrah.substack.com/p/week-100)
- Vanessa Lau (800k+ YouTube subs, team of editors/copywriters/sales): quit in Jan 2023 from burnout after "copying other creators' methods" and feeling inauthentic; returned solo — [14 Oct 2024](https://vanessalau.substack.com/p/why-i-quit-youtube)
- Amy Suto: only flat months came from publishing more often than sustainable; "share graphics" produced nothing — [Suto, 2026](https://www.amysuto.com/desk-of-amy-suto/how-to-grow-a-substack)

### Inferences
- Across cases, 0→10k+ took 12–18 months on one video platform (Alava, Abdaal) or ~2–3 years for a newsletter started without an audience (Suto to 42k; Introverted Networker to 2.5k). Nobody documented reached 10k by spreading across platforms from day one.
- The failure signatures (no niche, erratic cadence, overcommit) are exactly the ADHD-risk pattern — the data argues for a cadence floor you can hit on a bad week, not a ceiling.

### Gaps
- No documented running or martial-arts solo creator case with cadence + hours per week found in 2024–26 sources (search results were pre-2024 fitness profiles or course sales pages). No ADHD/mental-health creator case with growth numbers found.
- Hours per week are almost never stated in creator write-ups; "learning in public" and "build in public" results were listicles/playbooks without numbers.

## KQ6. Time budgets per unit of content

### Takeaway
Rule-of-thumb figures: 30–60 min of editing per finished minute of long-form video (so a 10-min talking-head video ≈ 5–10 h editing before filming/scripting); batched Reels ≈ 8–10 filmed in a 1–2 h block plus separate 1–2 h blocks for scripting/editing; weekly newsletter issues range 45 min (templated, Welsh) to 3–7 h (original long-form), with 2–4 h the modal answer among solo writers. A 5–7 h week therefore fits roughly: one newsletter + 2–3 LinkedIn posts + a few derived shorts, OR one short long-form video every 1–2 weeks, not both.

### Cited Findings
- Tasty Edits (vendor rule of thumb, updated 17 Sep 2026; no methodology): 30–60 min editing per finished minute; 5-min video ≈ 3–5 h, 20-min ≈ 10–20 h — [Tasty Edits](https://www.tastyedits.com/how-long-does-it-take-to-edit-a-video/)
- Gling (20 Oct 2026) illustrative talking-head example: 110–180 min hands-on editing total, explicitly "invented to demonstrate the arithmetic", not measured — [Gling](https://gling.ai/blog/how-long-does-it-really-take-to-edit-a-youtube-video)
- Reels batching (Social Media Examiner / Elise Darma, 20 Jul 2023 **[pre-2024]**): film 8–10 reels per batch; block 1–2 h per week each for ideation, scripting, filming, editing, posting — [SME](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/how-to-batch-instagram-reels-content-efficiently/)
- Newsletter hours (Indie Hackers thread, 26 Dec 2020 **[pre-2024]**, 19 self-reports): curated issues 2–3 h; original weekly issues mostly 3–6 h; one essayist ~21 h/week; several add 1–2 h promotion per issue, one adds 8–10 h/week promotion — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-many-hours-a-week-do-you-spend-on-your-newsletter-4f7c7c6715)
- Justin Welsh: 45 min per issue with a 4-step template (was 2+ h); 15 min/day idea capture from his own best social posts — [Welsh, 6 Apr 2024](https://justinwelsh.me/newsletter/my-secret-to-writing-a-great-newsletter-in-45-minutes)
- Substack Notes: ~15 min/day (Suto); Notes ≈ 20% of her effort — [Suto, Jul 2026](https://www.amysuto.com/desk-of-amy-suto/substack-notes)
- LinkedIn: 2–3 posts/week is the cited engagement optimum for executives — [Executive Presence, Sep 2025](https://executivepresence.beehiiv.com/p/ep-064-the-data-that-s-changing-how-smart-executives-approach-linkedin)
- Dataslayer's recommended response to the Dec 2025 YouTube change is 3–5 Shorts/week, which it concedes "requires more production capacity" — [Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/youtubes-december-2025-algorithm-update-browse-feed-cut-long-videos-by-80)

### Inferences
- Worked budget for 6 h/week: (a) video-first — one 6–8 min long-form every 2 weeks (≈2 h script/film + 4–6 h edit spread over two weeks) + 2–3 Shorts cut from it (≈1 h) ≈ 5–6 h/week; or (b) writing-first — one weekly newsletter (2–3 h) + 2–3 LinkedIn posts derived from it (1–1.5 h) + 15 min/day Notes/comments (≈1.5 h) ≈ 5–6 h/week. Running both tracks is ~10–12 h/week.
- The AIR data (Shorts at 25–40% of uploads) is achievable in track (a) only if Shorts are clips, not native productions.

### Gaps
- No surveyed, dated (2024–26) hours-per-video dataset for solo creators exists in what I found; all figures are vendor rules of thumb or 2020–23 self-reports. No per-Reel minutes figure.
