# Niche landscape: 35+ corporate man documenting self-directed journeys (karate, running, weight loss, self-taught history, AI side projects, open ADHD), Portugal-based, English-first — as of October 2026

Scope note: all figures below are dated. Third-party stat aggregators (vidIQ, CreatorDB, OutlierKit, FeedSpot, Modash, Scrumball, Heepsy) disagree with each other by 5–50% on the same channel; where two numbers exist both are given. No creator is named whose numbers I could not verify on at least one dated page. Several direct Social Blade / vidIQ pages were blocked (allowlist) or returned errors; those gaps are flagged.

## Key question 1 — Comparable creators by category: crowding, growth curve, best examples, year one

### Takeaway
Every adjacent category has a clear leader at 1–4M subscribers (running: Total Running Productions 807K; karate: Jesse Enkamp 1.6–1.67M; learning-journey: Mike Boyd 2.7M, Goal Guys 560K; ADHD: How to ADHD 1.9M, ADHD Chatter 318–408K; build-in-public: Pieter Levels ~600K on X), but the mid-tier (10K–200K) is wide and still being filled, and nobody I could verify combines all of Pedro's threads. The one structural pattern across the best examples is that year one was a single, specific, documented pursuit (Hellah Sidibe's run streak, Scott Young's MIT Challenge, Ben Felton's lockdown training logs, Mike Boyd's kickflip), not a multi-topic persona.

**Baseline growth data for any new channel (2025 cohort)**

- vidIQ study of 448,413 channels created Jan–Jun 2025 (137,355 long-form): median 30-day views of a long-form channel's 1st upload = 180, 10th upload = 272 (+51%); best-of-first-10 median = 1,948; 57.8% of long-form channels got one video past 1,000 views, 30.8% past 10,000; Shorts medians ~1,300/video but flat (published 28 Jul 2026) — [vidIQ Research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study/)
- Benchmarks for channels under 5,000 subscribers: 150–500 views in first 48 h is "typical" for educational channels, ~2,000+ for creator/entertainment; the algorithm's profiling period "typically takes 90 days and 12 to 15 videos"; back-catalogue views overtake new-upload views around month six with 24–26 videos (9 Mar 2026) — [Humble&Brag](https://humbleandbrag.com/blog/new-youtube-channel-average-views)
- r/NewTubers: 736K members, +146K (+24.8%) in a year — [GummySearch](https://gummysearch.com/r/NewTubers)

### Inferences
- Running and ADHD are the most crowded of Pedro's categories (both have 1M+ leaders and dozens of 100K+ channels); karate and "learning history in public" are far less crowded; build-in-public is saturated on X but lives on text platforms, not video.
- The verified mid-tier running channels that reached 150–400K (Felton, Lewis, Sidibe, Parkes) all began as one-person training logs with a personal stake (lockdown, a streak, being unfit at 30+), and took 4–6 years to get there; Lewis's negative 28-day subscriber delta in Oct 2026 suggests the mature channels are plateauing.
- Nobody verified here pairs ADHD with athletic/competitive achievement; the closest is Alex Partridge (founder + late diagnosis at 34, talking-head podcast), which grew to ~100K downloads/month within roughly a year — a signal that "high-achiever diagnosed in his 30s" is a working angle in the UK.
- Year-one realism from the cohort data: ~200–300 median views per long-form video, one breakout at ~2K; a committed weekly uploader with an existing network (the academic case) can reach ~20K subs in year one but that is well above median.

## Key question 2 — Does the "journey / season" format (countdown to an event, then the next) have documented success?

### Takeaway
Yes in the sense that the largest comparable channels are built on repeated, bounded pursuits (Goal Guys since 2015, Mike Boyd since 2015, Nick Bare's "Hyrox Prep" series, Hellah Sidibe's streak, Fergus Crawley's event documentaries), but no source isolates the event-countdown structure and gives it a growth number; evidence is by example, not by study.

### Cited Findings
- Goal Guys: 560K subscribers / 86.2M views over a channel created 15 Jul 2015 whose entire format is bounded goals in fitness and productivity — [Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/goalguys/realtime), [BoxRox](https://www.boxrox.com/why-this-guy-regrets-getting-shredded/)
- Mike Boyd: compressed-timeframe skill challenges from 2015 to 2.7M+ in 2025; evolved into a higher-production "Average Mike" series in 2025 — [yespress.io](https://yespress.io/mike-boyd.md)
- Nick Bare, 1.46M, "episodic, cinematic 'Hyrox Prep' series" named as the model Hyrox content (9 Jan 2026) — [Tatler Asia](https://www.tatlerasia.com/lifestyle/sports/hyrox-youtube-channels)
- Hellah Sidibe: an open-ended streak rather than a countdown; the first video came 163 days in and "became an instant hit"; 276K subs by Sep 2022 — [CNN/LocalNews8](https://localnews8.com/sports/cnn-sports/2022/09/22/when-his-hopes-of-a-soccer-career-faded-hellah-sidibe-started-running-every-day-hes-still-going-five-and-a-half-years-later-2/)
- Fergus Crawley: single-event documentaries (double triathlon, Feb 2023, Gymshark-produced) — [BarBend](https://barbend.com/fergus-crawley-289-mile-double-triathlon); channel at 151K (Oct 2026) — [FeedSpot](https://videos.feedspot.com/running_youtube_channels/)
- Scott Young's MIT Challenge: one-year public challenge; "Be public in your quest" credited for accountability — [GreaterWrong](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/ihZkuKz4wBiy2rzkx/mit-challenge-complete)
- Format-level data: long-form views grow with upload count (+51% from upload 1 to 10 in the 2025 cohort) while Shorts are flat, which favours serialised long-form — [vidIQ Research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study/)

### Inferences
- The "season" structure works as a retention device (returning viewers follow a stake with a deadline) rather than as a discovery device; discovery in these examples came from one breakout video (Boyd's kickflip, Sidibe's "why I run every day", DeWolfe's "superpowers").
- Athens 2026 → half marathon 2027 maps cleanly onto the Goal Guys / Nick Bare pattern of consecutive bounded arcs.

### Gaps
- No study or platform data isolating "countdown to event" as a format; all evidence is case-based.

## Key question 3 — Is there a real audience for men's mental-health / ADHD content from a high-achiever angle, and which formats?

### Takeaway
There is a verified audience and it prefers long-form talking formats: ADHD Chatter (interview podcast, ~1.2 uploads/week, 83K avg views/video, 408K subs) and HealthyGamerGG (3.49M, long talking-head/livestream, +157K subs in 90 days) are the two biggest male-led examples, while short-form ADHD content is enormous but increasingly flagged for misinformation and sells to an 18–24 audience.

### Cited Findings
- ADHD Chatter: 408K subs, 815 videos, ~5 uploads/month, 83.3K avg views per video, 166 views per subscriber (21 Sep 2026) — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/adhd-chatter-podcast); host is a founder diagnosed at 34 — [Hachette](https://www.hachette.co.uk/titles/alex-partridge/now-it-all-makes-sense-how-an-adhd-diagnosis-changed-my-life/9781399817813/)
- HealthyGamerGG: 3.49M YouTube, +51K in 30 days, +157K in 90 days, YouTube ~91% of audience (29 Sep 2026) — [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/healthygamergg/)
- ADHD Love: YouTube 550K growing (+25K/yr), TikTok 1.3M stalled at 0 growth across all windows (30 Sep 2026) — [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/adhd_love/)
- Short-form ADHD content: top 100 ADHD TikToks had ~500M combined views; fewer than half of their claims matched DSM-5 criteria; heavy viewers overestimated ADHD prevalence at ~30% vs ~5–6% actual; sample was ~850 college students (NPR, 24 Mar 2025, citing PLOS One / UBC) — [NPR via WLRN](https://www.wlrn.org/npr-breaking-news/2025-03-24/tiktok-is-full-of-adhd-advice-just-dont-trust-it-for-a-diagnosis)

- "Men mental health" search volume ~61K/month, +14% YoY (March 2025); discussion concentrated on Facebook, low on YouTube/Instagram/TikTok — [Glimpse](https://meetglimpse.com/trend/men-mental-health/)
- Fergus Crawley (fitness creator, 151K) openly discusses a suicide attempt at 20 as the origin of his endurance content — [BarBend](https://barbend.com/fergus-crawley-289-mile-double-triathlon)

### Inferences
- The growth is in long-form male talking formats (podcast interview, livestream Q&A), not in relatability Shorts, whose two biggest exemplars (ADHD Love TikTok, DeWolfe) show flat growth or deliberate retreat from the format.
- The high-achiever/late-diagnosis angle is validated by exactly one large case (Partridge); it is not yet a crowded sub-niche on YouTube.
- Mental-health disclosure embedded in an athletic journey (Crawley) is a documented pattern, but Crawley is at 151K, not millions.

- No Portuguese-language ADHD creator was found or verified.

## Key question 5 — Karate / WKF audience online; biggest karate creators; can a small Portuguese karate-gi brand reach buyers through creator content?

### Takeaway
Karate online is a narrow but deep audience: WKF has ~818K YouTube subscribers (Jan 2026) and ~2.1M total social followers, Jesse Enkamp 1.6–1.67M and growing ~20–30K/month, and only a handful of channels above 100K; Enkamp himself built a karate-gi brand (Seishin) on top of his audience, which is the existing proof that creator content sells gis to this audience.

### Inferences
- The addressable karate audience on YouTube is roughly the WKF + Enkamp overlap (~1–2M adult males, US/UK-heavy), and a creator at even 10–20K subscribers would rank inside the FeedSpot top 10 for karate — the category is thin above the top three.
- Creator-led gi sales are a proven path (Seishin), but it was built on a 1M+ audience over ~15 years; micro-influencer rates of ~$240/post from 85K-follower athletes suggest a small brand could seed product through athletes cheaply.
- Enkamp's Instagram is flat-to-declining while YouTube grows, indicating long-form YouTube is where karate attention is accruing in 2026.

### Gaps
- No public sales or revenue figures for Seishin, Arawaza, Hayashi or any gi brand's creator marketing were found.
- No Portuguese karate creator verified; no WKF audience demographic (age/country) data beyond "young" and Karate Combat's male 18–34 claim.
- Playboard and FeedSpot disagree on Enkamp (941K vs 1.6M) — Playboard's figure appears stale; vidIQ/CreatorDB (1.62–1.67M, 2026) are the figures to use.
